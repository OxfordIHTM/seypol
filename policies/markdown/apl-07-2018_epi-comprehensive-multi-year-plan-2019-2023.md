Executive Summary
Immunization has been one of Seychelles’ greatest public health success stories. However, in
order to ensure that all children of Seychelles benefit equitably from this intervention, a
strategic, i.e., long-term approach to planning and implementation is essential. This third
comprehensive Multi Year Plan (cMYP) provides a framework for planning activities to
achieve the major objectives of the national immunization programme. It also includes the
associated costing and funding plan for the national EPI. An extensive situation analyses
showed that they country has made significant progress in some key socio-demographic
indices.
Several achievements have been attained including sustainability of immunization coverage
above 99% for all antigens, absence of mortality due to vaccine preventable diseases (VPDs),
100% procurement of vaccines and injection materials by the Government and attainment
of polio certification level indicators with acceptance of the country documentation by the
Africa Regional Certification Commission (ARCC) in October 2006. The high level of
commitment by the Government and its people, the stable macro-economic environment
allowing for availability of free and accessible child health services to all people, active follow
up and tracing of Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) target groups to ensure
maximum coverage, a high literacy rate, access to the media and information, and the absence
of cultural or religious obstacles to immunization, have all contributed to the success of the
immunization programme and health sector in general. The delivery of immunization services
is integrated with other child survival interventions. It is therefore not surprising that with
these achievements and conducive environment, the country has made progress towards
Universal health coverage.
However, sustaining high immunization coverage, prevention of the risk of importation of
VPDs particularly in view of the numerous tourists visiting the country and high procurement
costs are among the challenges and areas of concern of the programme currently and in the
future. There is a need to review the Human resources at national level to ensure the required
evidence is generated for the sustenance of the programme. In addition, there is a need to
address emerging vaccine hesitancy which has just been noted.
The EPI Comprehensive Multi Year Plan 2018-2023 has been developed through a
consultative process and is based on priorities in the National Strategic Framework 2016-
2020, as well as the Global Vaccine Action Plan, and the Africa Regional Strategic Plan 2014-
2020 and the Global Vaccine Action Plan. Over the next 5 years, the Immunization
Programme will aim to maintain 100% coverage for all antigens, introduce Pneumococcal
vaccine, maintain polio-free status, while exploring possibilities for other new vaccines such
as vaccine, and advocate for negotiated affordable price for procurement of vaccines in a bid
to reduce on the expenditure on vaccines, among other priorities. As a new National Health
Strategic planning cycle will commence in 2021 for the period 2021 – 2025, the cMYP will
be updated following a review to be conducted in 2021 and projections will then be aligned
with the period 2021-2025.
Advocacy at all levels to ensure sustainability of the investments in the programme and of the
achievements will continue, Finalization of the EPI Policy will be done to define the

operational framework of the programme and incorporate any advancements planned. The
projected cost of the programme in the planning period (2019 - 2023) to include health
systems shared cost as well as disease surveillance is approximately $4,881,975.
The major components of this plan includes the mission, strategic goals, strategic objectives,
key activities and costing/financing plan, they were developed based on a comprehensive
situation analysis taking into consideration the global goals and national priorities.

1. INTRODUCTION
1.1 Country Profile
The Republic of Seychelles comprises 115 islands in the Indian Ocean. Out of this number,
only 22 are inhabited. It is situated between 4 and 9 degrees south of the equator and
between 45 and 56 degrees east, covering an exclusive economic zone of more than 1.3
million square kilometres. The largest island is Mahe where 90% of the country population
lives and where the capital city, Victoria, is located. Three other islands of major economic
importance and have health facilities are Praslin and La Digue and Silhouette.
The socio-economic indicators since achieving independence in 1976 are improving
continuously. Sub- Saharan Africa. Nevertheless, Seychelles, as a tiny island country, is not
exempted from the socio-economic problems faced by the Small Island Developing States
(SIDS). Seychelles is a multiparty democratic small state where national unity, pride and
aspirations override ideological and economic differences.

During the planning period of 2019 – 2023, Seychelles economic performance is expected to
continue on the strong trajectory of growth. Further to the macroeconomic performance
continuing to be strong in 2016 and 2017 with economic growth reaching 4% (2016) and

current account deficit remained largely unchanged, while gross international reserves at end-
2016 reached 4 months of prospective imports of goods and services. Supported by lower
than budgeted capital outlays and strong tax revenue growth, the 2016 primary fiscal surplus
reached 3.4 percent of GDP, exceeding the target by 0.4 percent.

1.3 National Health System
The Government provides universal access to free primary health care, which results in very
good indicators of health outcomes such as infant mortality at 10.9 per 1000 live births, a
maternal mortality of zero in most recent years, fully immunised children of 100%, and 99%
of birth deliveries assisted by skilled health workers. HIV prevalence is less than 1% and is
characterised as a concentrated epidemic among high risk groups.
In recent years, the burden of disease has shifted mainly to non-communicable diseases.
Education is fully subsidized up to secondary school level, and partially subsidized at the
tertiary level. The gross enrolment in primary and secondary education is close to 100%.
The Government provides free primary health care to assure universal access to health care,
universal access to safe drinking water, good sanitation and housing provision, and an
advanced social safety net to support the most vulnerable to address Social Determinants of
Health. The health emergency policy and contingency plan has catered for emerging
important public health priority conditions such as dengue, chikungunya and leptospirosis
and considers Ebola, influenza and SARS as potential threats for national health security in
the context of IHR and are all included in the national surveillance system.
The State recognizes the right of every citizen to protection of health and to the enjoyment
of the highest attainable standards of physical and mental health1. Traditionally, Seychelles
has adopted a tier system for its health care delivery. The national health system operates on
three main levels of care:
1. Primary/ community care
2. Secondary care at hospital and specialized facilities
3. Tertiary care which includes overseas treatment.

1.3.1 Seychelles Expanded Programme on Immunization
The National Immunization Programme (NIP)) in Seychelles was initiated in 1808 with
obligatory small pox vaccination, in 1956 with introduction of DPT and OPV, and was
formally established in 1974 to provide the basic antigens against the six childhood killer
diseases which are BCG, DPT, OPV and Measles with the support of the World Health
Organization. The National EPI expanded to include new antigens over the years such as
BCG, OPV, MMR, DT, DPT, ATT, Yellow Fever, Hepatitis B and Haemophilus Influenza Type
b, IPV, HPV, Rotavirus Vaccine. Over the years, the immunization schedule has expanded
to include new antigens such as MMR in 1989, Yellow Fever and Hepatitis B in 1995,
Haemophilus influenzae type b in 2010, HPV vaccine in 2014, IPV in 2015, Rota in 2017 (Fig

with support from the Director to coordinate the planning, monitoring and documentation
of the EPI programme and its performance, the forecasting, procurement, supply and
distribution of vaccines and related equipment, and the routine and sentinel surveillance for

2.1 Situation analysis of the Immunization programme
2.1.1 Service Delivery
High quality immunization services are provided by qualified nurses on scheduled
appointment basis in all public health facilities. The system allows tracking defaulters if any,
through phone call or home visit until the eligible gets the service.
The Seychelles DTP1, DPT3 and MCV1 coverage shows a high reported coverage above 95%
for all antigens that is in conformity with the WHO UNICEF estimate where difference
between reported and estimated is below 5% for all antigens. Among the challenges, the
migration to other health districts, with limitation of reliable denominator estimate provides
lower estimate for some areas and higher for others making it challenging to understand sub
national level performance due to the very few populations.
The DPT1-DPT3 drop-out rate has improved from 3% to 1% in the three years. DPT1-MCV1
drop-out rate was 0% in 2016.
As part of strategies to sustain high reported coverage, the programme has embarked on
adapting the revised AFRO RED approach that is already drafted for finalisation and will be
disseminated to all health districts by end 2018. This will help to standardise health facility
microplanning development by catchment areas. The Pneumococcal vaccine introduction will
be used to train and update the Immunization health workforce in each health district.
In order to further strengthen service delivery, the EPI program continues to address the
recommendations from the comprehensive EPI review (2017), the KAP study findings (2017),
the cold chain inventory (2018) to develop rehabilitation plan and the coverage survey
findings
2.1.2 EPI Coverage performance
The EPI programme in Seychelles has achieved considerable success in preventing and
controlling most vaccine-preventable diseases. The programme has sustained high coverage
levels of all antigens and has subsequently reduced more than 90% of morbidity and
mortality from diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, neonatal tetanus and measles in comparison
with the period prior to the implementation of the EPI programme. This has contributed to
the continued decline in infant and child mortality.
The reported coverage of all antigens in Seychelles has been high. As per the WHO/UNICEF
Joint Reporting Form (JRF) reports, it is noted that in 2014, 20% of the districts reported
coverage below 80% for DTP3 coverage but in the period 2005-2015, the estimated DTP3

2.1.3 Accelerated Disease Control & VPD surveillance
a) Progress towards Polio Eradication
The last, virologically confirmed, case of poliomyelitis in Seychelles was reported in 1966.
Seychelles’ polio National Certification Committee (NCC) was formed in 2001 and the
country documentation for polio free certification has been accepted in 2005 by the Polio
Eradication to the African Regional Certification Commission (ARCC). The country is
conducting active AFP surveillance visits bimonthly. In case an AFP case detected, the stools
specimens will be shipped to the South Africa polio Laboratory at National Institute of
Communicable Diseases (NICD). As expected, the NCC has been submitting the annual polio
progress report documents to the ARCC every year. However, despite active case search
conducted regularly, no AFP case has been notified for over 10 years.
The National coverage for the major antigens including OPV3 has been above 99% for the
past 5 years. The immunization coverage for OPV3 is 100% and as per the WHO UNICEF
estimates (WUENIC) the coverage of OPV3 is above 90% from 2001-2015. Since the
immunization coverage is 100% for children at the age of one year, supplementary
immunization activities are not needed nor done in Seychelles.
In addition, to strengthen the population immunity, IPV was introduced in September 2015.
The switch from tOPV to bOPV was successfully implemented in April 2016. The polio
outbreak response plan is completed which will be called into action in case of need.
b) Progress towards Measles/Congenital Rubella Elimination
Seychelles introduced measles vaccine in 1967 followed by change to MMR in 1989, that was
introduced for first and 2nd dose at fifteen months of age that was shifted to 6 years in 2001.
Seychelles has experienced measles outbreaks in 1967, and in 1974 that were responded to
through vaccination campaigns. The outbreak of 1982 to 1983, affected babies who were
too young to have been immunized. The latest outbreak of measles was in 2006 that affected
children below 15 months and young adults up to 18 years of age resulting in 600 cases in
16

island of Mahé. A vaccination response targeted 6 months to 18 years with no documented
measles deaths.
Noting the high reported and estimated coverage of the two doses, the delayed establishment
of the fever rash surveillance limited the timely assessment of progress but currently
documentation has started for elimination verification since 2018. The coverage of MCV1
(MMR1) & MCV2 *MMR2) has been high and sustained above 95% as per the WHO UNICEF
estimates from 2001 to 2015 (Figs 2.1).

The national laboratory has the capacity to test and confirm through serology measles and
rubella but is yet to submit the proficiency panel for testing so that the lab can be accredited
by WHO for quality assurance since 2015.
c) Sustaining Maternal Neonatal Tetanus Elimination
Seychelles has sustained elimination of Maternal Neonatal Tetanus (MNT) from early 2000.
There is high skilled hospital delivery over 99% and the WHO UNICEF estimate of protected
at birth coverage for 2014 and 2015 is 100%. The surveillance system monitors neonatal and
general tetanus and there were no reported cases of neonatal Tetanus or general tetanus since
2002 when they were considered as having achieved MNT Elimination Status.
Each pregnant mother is scheduled for an average of 10-13 Antenatal care visits during her
pregnancy and will be given the booster doses of TT vaccine during pregnancy. The country
needs to update the TT schedule as the country as it shifts to using Td.
d) Other Key VPDs of importance & other key VPDs of importance under the near horizon
The Rotavirus sentinel surveillance established since 2013, is documenting evidence that
guided the introduction in 2017. Although there is no sentinel surveillance for Paediatric
bacterial meningitis, the specific pneumonia cases are monitored using the IDSR.

generated Hepatitis B infection trend is useful if it can be integrated with the serological result
to identify the risk and determine the introduction of Hep B birth dose. The seasonal influenza
and respiratory infectious disease are also routinely notified and confirmatory tests are being
done. The dengue outbreak has been recurrent and surveillance is monitoring trend for
effective control. The DSRU unit supported by the Public Health laboratory will continue
generating required evidence to provide guidance for the control, elimination and need for
future vaccines by contributing to the local evidence of disease burden.
To monitor the vaccine safety, a national AEFI committee was established in February 2017.
AEFI though notifiable under IDSR framework, the current web-based surveillance reporting
system does not include AEFI reporting that may have resulted in the notification of reported
AEFI that is sub optimal. Notified cases currently are investigated and NIP is tracking the
notified cases. Seychelles has not attained the target of AEFI notification yet.

2.1.6 Vaccine Preventable Diseases surveillance
Seychelles has adopted the AFRO IDSR strategy framework for disease surveillance and
response since 2003. At the national level, the NIP manager is responsible for VPD
surveillance while Disease Surveillance and Response Unit (DSRU) head is responsible for the
remaining surveillance, and these two units are housed in two different agencies of the MoH
the Clinical Care Agency &Public Health Authority respectively.
The VPD surveillance operations are coordinated within the DSRU under the Public Health
Authority of the MoH with designated surveillance focal persons at all levels (six at central
level and over 100 in all HFs). There is web-based weekly reporting of 47 priority diseases
conditions that is robust with web based automated data analysis and outputs that the team
found as exemplary and bringing efficiency for timely availability of information for action.
The absence of scheduled technical coordination meeting between the two Departments/units

sometimes results in inefficiencies in data collection, and in review of data for meeting

National Public Health Laboratory for Vaccine Preventable Diseases
The Acute Flaccid Paralysis (AFP) surveillance system has reported the latest AFP case in 2005
and sample was processed in South Africa at National Institute of Communicable Diseases.
The system is complemented by scheduled annual 6 visits conducted every two months to
ensure suspected AFP cases are not missed by the passive reporting system.
The national Public health laboratory that supports measles /rubella surveillance has the
capacity to test for measles /rubella serology however is yet to test suspected cases of
measles/rubella to effectively support the surveillance system, the latest confirmed outbreak
of measles was in 2006. However, it is yet to test the required samples to quality for quality
control and join the WHO network. The shift to fever-rash surveillance for elimination mode
measles surveillance to verify measles elimination is established in 2018. The samples from
Rota sentinel surveillance are tested in the national laboratory.
2.1.7 Demand generation, communication, Advocacy
Interpersonal communication is the major communication strategy used to sustain demand
for the Immunization services. The Nurses provide information on the vaccine, the procedure,
any anticipated effect to be observed after the vaccination, next appointment that is diligently
done with professionalism.
There is free airtime allocated by the national media for broadcasting health messages that
the immunization programme uses to mobilize the community or raise awareness on vaccines
and immunization whenever needed. However, the incident of vaccine hesitancy noted
during the HPV introduction, non-consenting care givers emerging is a pointer to potential
risk of accumulation of susceptible and needs targeted communication strategy The revision
of public health to include a regulation to make vaccination mandatory is in process.
The communication plan needs to include a risk communication component for anticipated
new vaccines to be introduced including addressing severe AEFI event, so that proactive
mechanism is in place to mitigate such incidents. There is no focal person for health
promotion in the Family health and nutrition. There is a need for all the IEC materials to be
consolidated together as guide for the Nurses as job aid for use noting the recent inclusion of
antigens and the result of the KAP study that indicated knowledge of care givers was limited
to the vaccines that their children are receiving despite good inter personal communication.

in the programmatic section of the cMYP. As this is a follow on cMYP to that of 2013 – 2017, the data
for 2017 was used as base line for planning and projections. Standard programme inputs such as
vaccines, injection materials and cold chain equipment were costed using the prices that Seychelles pays
as a self-procuring country. This is because all the EPI supplies in Seychelles are purchased through
Government. Operational costs for routine and the new antigens introduces were based on past
expenditure. The staff cost is based on the integrated government salary scale grid with input from staff
members themselves. The financing information was obtained from past expenditures of the
Government and the only Immunization partner in the country, which is the World Health
Organization (WHO).

7. CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATION
The process of producing this third cMYP has been beneficial to the management of EPI in
highlighting key areas where sustained effort is needed from 2018 and beyond to further
improve the EPI programme. The EPI through the Directorate of Family Health and Nutrition
will submit this plan to the Senior Management of MoH Seychelles to secure the additional
resources needed for the for the smooth functioning of the EPI in general including the
required verification surveillance information to provide evidence for elimination of targeted
diseases that are due.
It seeks commitments from the WHO to support the efforts to enhance the effectiveness of
the EPI, School Health, DSRU, national Laboratory, Child health, Maternal Health and other
relevant support units like HR which impacts directly, on the reduction of child mortality,
and improvement of the health of the target population.
The management of EPI is committed to the improvement of the service delivery. This single
aim is reflected in the efforts of all our personnel on a daily basis. We will strive to achieve
the goals set. We thank the Senior Management for their past support and we look forward
to their continued support in the achievement of the objectives set out in the cMYP of
Seychelles 2019-2023.
