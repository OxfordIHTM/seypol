Forward
Statistics are important in helping policy makers allocate scarce resources, monitor national
progress and make government more transparent and accountable. The National Bureau
Statistics (NBS) is the apex agency responsible for the production of statistics in Seychelles,
and provides key statistics on a wide range of economic and social issues.
A wide range of statistics are being produced across government through statistical
collections, administrative and transactional data. To get the best value from these assets
requires a holistic and coordinated approach to the production of national statistics. The NBS
plays an important leadership and coordination role in relation to the statistical activities of
other official bodies, both within Seychelles and internationally.
For the NBS to perform better, it is essential that it determines its future hence the “raison
d’être” of this Strategic Plan 2014-2020. This Plan will enable NBS to be more efficient and
effective in the management of statistical activities so as to better meet the increasing needs
of it users.
This Plan has provided a view of where NBS is and where it wants to be by 2020.
Never has so much been expected from statistics; never have statisticians had such means at
their disposal; and never has there been so much willingness to learn from each other and
share internationally the outcomes of that learning.
In order to keep pace with the rapid transformation of the structure of the socio-economic
environment of the Seychelles and to be able to respond to the increasing demand for
statistics, in particular for monitoring Government of Seychelles on-going reforms, it is
essential to plan for the long-term while at the same time maximizing performance in the
short-term. Having a corporate plan enables NBS to ensure that day-to-day decisions align
with the long-term goal.
Laura Ahtime
Chief Executive Officer
National Bureau of Statistics

Introduction
The National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) is Seychelles official national statistical agency. It was
formerly known as the National Statistics Bureau (NSB) which was created on 12 February
2005, as a semi-autonomous organisation, following enactment of the National Statistics
Bureau Act 2005. The Act was revised in 2010 and the name changed to the National Bureau
of Statistics.
History of Statistics in Seychelles
The compilation of statistics dates back to the early seventies when a small statistics unit
operated within the Ministry of Economic Planning. Prior to independence it was called the
Economic and Statistics Unit in the Office of the President.
Over the years, it had been subject to a number of changes in terms of name, location and
parent Ministry. Over the 25 year period (1980-2005), it changed parent Ministry 5 times until
it finally became a semi-autonomous organisation in February 2005.
In 1980 it was known as the “Statistics Division” in the Department of Finance under the
President’s Office and was headed by the Chief Statistician.
In January 1986, it was transferred from the Ministry of Finance to the President’s Office and
located in the President’s Private Office to be more precise.
In August 1986, with the re-organisation of the President’s Office, the Statistics Division was
renamed the Information Systems Division (ISD) which was made up of two sections each
headed by a Director. The sections were named: (i) Statistics and Database Administration
Section (SDAS) and (ii) Systems Development Section (SDS).
In January 1989, the ISD was renamed Management and Information Systems Division (MISD)
and had as its parent Ministry, the Ministry of Administration and Manpower. The SDAS of
the MISD was headed by the Director General.
On 1 April 1998, the MISD was moved to the Ministry of Information Technology and
Communications (MITC) until December 2003.
In January 2004, the MISD was moved to the Ministry of Economic Planning until early 2005.
On 12 February 2005, the NBS was created as a semi-autonomous organisation and is headed
by the Chief Executive Officer.
In July 2010 with the revision of the Act, the name changed to National Bureau of Statistics
(NBS)

Role of the NBS
The NBS provides statistics on a wide range of economic, social, population and
environmental matters, covering government, business and the community. It also has an
important coordination function with respect to the statistical activities of other official
bodies.
Mission:
Continuously build and develop a coherent, reliable, efficient and demand driven “National
Statistical System” to encourage and support informed decision and policy making.
Vision
Become the centre of excellence in production and dissemination of statistics in Seychelles.
Objectives
1. Strengthen and assist in the development of the National Statistical System (NSS) to meet
the challenges of emerging data needs;
2. Improve the quality of our products and services and be the primary source of “official
statistics”; and
3. Improve data dissemination.

Values – ‘QUALITE’
Quality: We focus on high quality products in accordance with
international standards. We value professionalism and aim for excellence
in our products and services. We are committed to deliver results in a
timely cost effective manner.
User-friendly statistics: We support and maintain a responsive national
statistical system by cooperating with our users and suppliers and are
committed to meeting the expectation of users.
Accessibility: We endeavor to make our statistics accessible, relevant and
reliable. We show initiative and ensure that the statistics meet the
requirements.
Learning organisation: We are a learning organisation, constantly
improving our capacity and our skills to bring innovation to our products
and organisation.
Integrity: Statistics are our responsibility and as such we promote the
principles of objectivity, impartiality and independence in the production
of statistics.
Trust: We work to maintain trust of our respondents and users through
consistency, honesty and the reliable application of fundamental
principles.
Employees: We value our human resources and promote their general
welfare and well-being. It is our people that will ensure we are a
successful organisation. Preparing our people for the future will enable us
to deliver on our mission and work towards achieving our vision.

Legislation
The National Bureau of Statistics Act 2010, provides the CEO with the authority to conduct
statistical collections and, when necessary, to direct a person or an organisation to provide
statistical information. The Act imposes obligations on the NBS to maintain the
confidentiality of information collected under the Act.
The National Bureau of Statistics Act 2010 sets out the
responsibilities of the Bureau:
(a) planning, authorising, coordinating and supervising all
official statistical programs undertaken within the National
Statistical System, and establishing standards, and
promoting the use of the best practices and methods in the
production and dissemination of statistical information;
(b) collecting, compiling, analysing, abstracting, publishing and
disseminating statistical information and related material;
(c) assisting Government Ministries and Departments in the
collection, compilation and publication of statistical
information, including statistics related to their activities;
(d) eliminating the duplication of collection and compilation of
information within the National Statistical System and
promoting and developing a body of integrated social and
economic statistics;
(e) subject to section 18, sharing micro data with Government
Ministries, Departments, researchers and academics on
payment of the prescribed fee;
(f) providing advice and assistance to official bodies in relation
to statistics; and
(g) co-ordinating the activities of Seychelles and other countries
and international organisations in the field of statistics and
other related matters.

The Act also establishes the Board of Directors on the discharge of the functions of the
organisation.
The National Bureau of Statistics Act 2010 sets out functions of the
Board which shall be —
(a) to formulate policy and monitor the programs;
(b) to approve the annual budget and action plans of the Bureau;
(c) to make senior staff appointments;
(d) to determine from time to time the Bureau's structure, staffing
levels and terms and conditions of service; (e) to establish rules
and procedures for —
(i) the appointment, career development and discipline of staff;
(ii) the purchase of goods and services and the disposal of assets;
(iii) the proper management of the Bureau's finances and assets;
(f) to keep the Minister informed of the activities and progress of the
Bureau at quarterly intervals;
(g) to perform any other functions assigned by the Minister in
consultation with the Board.

Organizational Structure
NBS is headed by the Chief Executive Officer (CEO) with provisions in the Act for the
appointment of a Deputy CEO.
A Deputy CEO was appointed on 1st November 2016 by President Danny Faure.
The Bureau is organized in three broad technical sections:
 Economic Statistics (National Accounts, Prices, Trade, Tourism, Employment and
Production);
 Census, Surveys and GIS unit [Data Acquisition & Census, and Demography and
Geographical Information System (GIS)]
 Social Statistics (Quarterly Labour Force, Crime and Justice, Gender Statistics,
Poverty and SDGs)
The three technical sections are supported by the Corporate Support Section which includes
Human Resource, Administration & Accounts and Information Technology.

What is the National Statistical System (NSS)?
The National Statistical System (NSS) is a community of government agencies working to
build a rich statistical picture in order to better inform Seychelles. The NSS embraces all
government agencies and official bodies involved in the production and use of official
statistics. It also includes custodians of administrative datasets which contribute to official
statistics.
The NBS has legislative responsibility for planning, authorizing, coordinating and supervising
all official statistical programs undertaken within the NSS.

Special Data Dissemination Standard
The Government of the Republic of the Seychelles plans to build a world-class statistical
organisation. NBS aims to comply with the IMF Special Data Dissemination Standards (SDDS)
by 2015. The SDDS is a set of standards to enhance availability to the public of timely and
comprehensive statistics, and therefore contribute to the pursuit of sound macroeconomic
policies, and the improved functioning of financial markets.
Adherence to SDDS is a core element of the ‘second generation’ reform program to ensure
the sustainability of the recent macroeconomic recovery. Compliance to the SDDS will require
actions across, and coordination between, various institutions (including MFTI, CBS,
Seychelles Revenue Commission, DICT and other organisations with developed information
systems). However, the majority of the required work and process reforms will need to take
place within the NBS.

1. The data: coverage, periodicity, and timeliness: Comprehensive economic and financial data,
disseminated on a timely basis, are essential to the transparency of macroeconomic
performance and policy.
2. Access by the public: Dissemination of official statistics is an essential feature of statistics as a
public good. Ready and equal access are principal requirements for the public, including market
participants.
a. Advance dissemination of release calendars
b. Simultaneous release to all interested parties
3. Integrity: To fulfill the purpose of providing the public with information, official statistics must
have the confidence of their users. In turn, confidence in the statistics ultimately becomes a
matter of confidence in the objectivity and professionalism of the agency producing the
statistics. Transparency of its practices and procedures is a key factor in creating this confidence.
a. Dissemination of the terms and conditions under which official statistics are produced,
including those relating to the confidentiality of individually identifiable information.
b. Identification of internal government access to data before release.
c. Identification of ministerial commentary on the occasion of statistical releases.
d. Provision of information about revision and advance notice of major changes in
methodology.
4. Quality: A set of standards that deals with the coverage, periodicity, and timeliness of data must
also address the quality of statistics. Although quality is difficult to judge, monitorable proxies,
designed to focus on information the user needs to judge quality, can be useful.
a. Dissemination of documentation on methodology and sources used in preparing statistics.
b. Dissemination of component detail, reconciliations with related data, and statistical
frameworks that support statistical cross-checks and provide assurance of reasonableness.

Millennium Development Goals
It was at the September 2000 Millennium Summit that Seychelles along with 189 other UN
member states signed the “Millennium Declaration” and committed to work towards
achieving the Millennium Development Goals (MDGs) by 2015. These goals clearly spell out
what the member states intend to do so as to eradicate the many dimensions of poverty
and achieve sustainable human development.
Associated with these goals are a set of 18 targets and more than 48 indicators. All the 18
targets are to be met by 2015. The 48 plus indicators were identified so as to help in
monitoring progress towards these goals, which are as follows:
 Goal 1 – Eradicate Extreme Poverty and Hunger
 Goal 2 – Achieve Universal Primary Education
 Goal 3 – Promote Gender Equality and Empower Women
 Goal 4 – Reduce child Mortality
 Goal 5 – Improve Maternal Health
 Goal 6 – Combat HIV/AIDS. Malaria and other Diseases
 Goal 7 – Ensure Environmental Sustainability
 Goal 8 – Develop a Global Partnership for Development
To measure and monitor the performance towards the achievement of these set goals and
targets, the availability of numerical indicators, based on data of integrity is crucial. This
process requires the collaborative partnership of institutions mainly responsible for
delivering on these objectives.
In Seychelles, a national committee was set up comprising of members from the National
Bureau of Statistics (NBS), government ministries & departments, the private and parastatal
sectors as well as representation from non-profit institutions. The national committee
headed by a national consultant tasked with the writing of the report met regularly under
the auspices of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.
With strong leadership and accountability to meet commitments, the Seychelles progress
can be viewed at:
http://www.nbs.gov.sc/files/Reports/MDG%20Report%202010_Seychelles.pdf
Internationally work has commenced in accelerating MDG progress and preparing for the
post-2015 UN development framework. Looking beyond the MDGs, much remains to be
done to build the future we want. Globally continuing gaps – on poverty, hunger, health,
gender equality, water, sanitation and many other issues – will still need attention after
2015. The UN sustainable development agenda for the period after 2015 will build on the
progress achieved through the MDGs while addressing persistent inequalities and new
challenges facing people and the planet.

At the Summit for MDGs at the United Nations General Assembly in New York in September
2010, the then Vice-President, Mr. Danny Faure, presented the outcomes of Seychelles’ hard
work towards achieving the MDGs. He commented that a decade after the commitment,
Seychelles was well on the way to achieving the MDGs, but that however, more effort was
needed at the international level to provide mechanisms which matched the development
needs of small island developing states.
The Vice President also stated Seychelles’ commitment to pursue with the MDG Plus (MDG+)
which sets new ambitious targets that go beyond the original goals and targets. Mr Faure
added that “Seychelles is one of the first countries in the African region to make this
commitment”.

Sustainable Development Goals
It is to be noted that the MDGs agenda came to an end in 2015. On September 25th 2015,
countries adopted a set of goals to end poverty, protect the planet, and ensure prosperity
for all as part of a new sustainable development agenda. It came into effect in January 2016
and each goal has specific targets to be achieved over the next 15 years.
The SDGs which has 17 goals, 169 targets and over 300 indicators is expected to transform
the World by 2030.
This global agenda has placed an enormous responsibility on NSOs, and partners in the NSS
to assist in the monitoring of the indicators which will gauge whether the SDGs are effectively
transforming the world. This data-intensive agenda will inevitably require extra resources and
will be a test on the capacity of NSOs to compile and deliver the required indicators for
monitoring the achievements and successes of countries in transforming the world.
The SDGs will requires focusing on areas of priority:
