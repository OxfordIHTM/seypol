**A BILL**

**FOR**

**AN ACT to provide for the regulation of quality, safety and efficacy of medical products and related substances; the operations of pharmacies, the repeal of the Pharmacy Act (Cap 169) and for other connected or incidental matters.**

**ENACTED** by the President and the National Assembly.

**PART I – PRELIMINARY**

**Short title and commencement**

**1.** This Act may be cited as the Medical Products and Pharmacy Operations Act, 2022 and shall come into operation on such date as the Minister may, by notice in the *Gazette*, appoint.

**Application**

**2.**(1) This Act applies to all medical products and operations of pharmacies.

(2) Where there is any inconsistency between any provision of this Act and provision of any other law relating to medical products or pharmacy operations, the provisions of this Act shall prevail.

**Interpretation**

**3.**  In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires—

“administration” means giving or applying to a human being or animal, whether orally, by injection or by introducing into the body in any other way, or by external application, whether or not by direct contact with the body, a substance in its existing state or after it has been dissolved or dispersed in, diluted or mixed with some other substance;

“advertisement” in relation to a medical product, means a pictorial, visual or other descriptive matter appearing in a notice or announcement brought to the notice of members of the public, with the intention to directly or indirectly advise of the existence and benefits of a medical product and “advertise” shall be construed accordingly;

“analyst” means an analyst appointed under section 19;

“authorised officer” means an officer or employee of the Authority or any other person appointed as an authorized officer under section 17(1);

“Authority” means the Public Health Authority established under section 3 of the Public Health Authority Act, 2013;

“authorised person” means a person who is either, a medical practitioner, dentist, nurse, veterinarian, or pharmacist authorised to prescribe medicines;

“batch” means a defined quantity of medical products assembled in a single process or series of processes so that they are expected to be homogeneous;

“batch number” means a distinctive combination of numbers or letters which uniquely identifies a batch;

“Board” means the Public Health Authority Board established under section 6 of the Public Health Authority Act, 2013;

“certificate of marketing authorisation” means a certificate providing authorisation to market a medical product and related substances in the Seychelles;

“Chairperson” means the Chairperson of the Committee appointed under section 8;

“clinical trial" means an investigation in respect of a medical product for use in humans or animals that involves human or animal subjects and that is intended to discover or verify the clinical, pharmacological or pharmacodynamic effects of the medical product, identify adverse events, study the absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion of the medical product or ascertain its safety or efficacy;

“Commissioner” means the Public Health Commissioner appointed under section 7(1) of the Public Health Authority Act, 2013;

“Committee” means the Medical Products and Pharmacy Regulatory Committee established under section 7;

“compassionate use” means access to unregistered medical products in special or emergency situations or in cases of rare diseases for which specialist medicines do not have a local market authorization, or in cases where the medical products are in an experimental stage, or at any rate unproven and the government is not obliged to fund their supply;

“container” means a primary, secondary or transportation container and includes the material used in the packaging of such container;

“dentist” means a person who is qualified to practice as a dentist and registered as a dentist under the Medical Practitioners and Dentists Act, Cap 126;

“Director” means the Director of the Service as appointed under section 5 of the Act;

“dispense” means to prepare and supply to a patient a course of treatment on the basis of a prescription;

“dispenser” means a person other than a pharmacist who is permitted to dispense medical products, and includes pharmacy technicians under supervision of a pharmacist, medical practitioners, dentists, nurse practitioners, optometrists and veterinary surgeons;

“distribution” means division and movement of pharmaceutical products from the premises of the manufacturer of such products, or another central point, to the end user , or to an intermediate point by means of various transport methods, and via various storage or health establishments;

“donation” means the gift of a medical product to government, a non-governmental organisation or private entity by a manufacturer, wholesaler or retailer, and any other distributor or supplier of such product;

“Ethics Committee” means the Health, Research and Ethics Committee, established by the Authority, and responsible for reviewing research for safeguarding the dignity, rights, safety and well-being of the actual or potential participants, and which reports to the Commissioner;

“expiry date” means the date provided on an individual container usually on the label of a product, up to and including the date by which the product is expected to remain within specifications, if stored under recommended instructions;

“export” includes to deliver or supply within the country for dispatch to a destination outside the country;

“health professional” means any one of the categories of health professionals listed in Schedule I of the Health Professionals Act, 2006;

“import” means the act of bringing into the country;

“information management system” means a database and transaction management system that is designed to facilitate the storage, organisation, and retrieval of information within the Medical Products and Pharmacy Regulatory Service;

“insert” means a document included in the package of a medical product that provides information about the medical product and its use;

“interchangeable pharmaceutical product” means a pharmaceutical product which is therapeutically equivalent to a reference product;

“geodemographic criteria” means the taking into account of the analysis of the population and the availability of pharmacy services in an area in relation to an application for registration under section 36;

“label” means the process of identifying a medical product by means of label on the medical products container and medical products package inserts and includes the following information —

(a) name of the product;

(b) active ingredient;

(c) dosage form;

(d) batch number;

(e) expiry date;

(f) special storage conditions or handling precautions;

(g) directions for use, warnings and precautions;

(h) names and addresses of the manufacturer or the supplier, and the address of the manufacturing site of the product;

“magistral formula” means any medicinal product prepared in a pharmacy in accordance with a prescription for an individual

patient;

**“managing pharmacist”** means a pharmacist, with a minimum of five years of professional experience, appointed as the managing pharmacist under section 39;

“manufacture” means all operations of purchase of materials and starting materials, preparation of the active pharmaceutical ingredient and of the pharmaceutical product, including packaging and repackaging, labelling and relabeling, quality control, release, storage and distribution and related controls;

“market” includes a variety of systems, institutions, procedures, social relations and infrastructures for medical products sale, and barter or exchange or supply or disposal to a person;

“mark-up” means the amount or value added to the cost price of goods to cover expenses and profit;

“medicine” means any substance or mixture of substances used or purporting to be suitable for use in—

(a) the diagnosis, treatment, mitigation, modification or prevention of disease, abnormal physical or mental state or their symptoms in humans; or

(b) restoring, correcting or modifying any somatic or psychic or organic function in humans,
and includes any veterinary medicine;

“medical device” means any instrument, apparatus, implement, machine, appliance, implant, in vitro reagent or calibrator, software, material or other similar or related article —

(a) intended by the manufacturer to be used, alone or in combination, for humans or animals for—

1. diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, treatment or alleviation of disease;
2. diagnosis, monitoring, treatment, alleviation of or compensation for an injury;
3. investigation, replacement, modification or support of the anatomy or of a physiological process;
4. supporting or sustaining life;
5. control of conception;
6. disinfection of medical devices; or
7. providing information for medical or diagnostic purposes by means of *in vitro* examination of specimens derived from the human body; and

(b) which does not achieve its primary intended action in or on the human or animal body by pharmacological, immunological or metabolic means, but which may be assisted in its intended function by such means;

“medical practitioner” means a doctor who is registered to practice under the Medical Practitioner and Dentists Act (Cap 126);

“medical product” includes medicines, vaccines, vitamins, complementary medicine products, cosmetics, food supplements, diagnostics and medical devices;

“Minister” means the Minister responsible for Health;

“mutual recognition” means the acceptance of one national medical products regulatory agency’s certification of standards and procedures for medical product regulation by another national medical products regulatory agency;

“narcotics” or “narcotic substances” means drugs listed in the 1963 Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs as ratified by Seychelles on 27th February, 1992;

“nurse” means a person registered under the Nurses and Midwives Act (Cap 150);

“pharmacy” means the science and technique of producing and dispensing medical products to ensure the safe and effective use of medicals products and includes any premises where, any medical product may be dispensed, sold, exposed or offered for sale;

“pharmacy technician” means a person registered as pharmacy technicians by the Council under the Health Professionals Act, 2006;

“pharmacist” means a person registered as pharmacist under the Health Professionals Act, 2006;

“pharmacovigilance” means activities relating to the detection, assessment, understanding and prevention of adverse effects or any other drug-related problem;

“premises” includes building or other permanent or temporary structure connected to the land vehicle, conveyance, vessel, ship, aircraft, floating craft, street, place, open space or place of public resort, bicycle, tricycle, motorised or not;

“prescribe” means to issue an instruction in writing or electronically for a medical treatment or a medical product for a specific patient or animal by an authorised person for the collection of such treatment or medicine from a pharmacy;

“prescription” means an instruction in writing or in electronic form for a medicine by an authorised person or in electronic form;

“professional experience” means the experience gained through full-time employment in a field in which the person intends to be or is licensed or registered to practice, but does not include internship or community service;

“promotion” means all information and persuasive activities by manufacturers and distributors, the effect of which is to induce the prescription, supply, purchase and or use of medical products, and includes advertising;

“psychotropic substance” means a substance listed in the 1971 Convention on Psychotropic Substances as are required for reporting to the International Narcotics Control Board;

“qualified technical person” means a person responsible for the release of batches of finished products for sale;

“Quality Management System” means a set of policies, processes and procedures required for planning and execution of the core business area of a national or supranational marketing authorisation authority;

“quality control” means the process covering all measures taken, including the setting of specifications, sampling, testing and analytical clearance, to ensure that starting materials, intermediates, packaging materials and finished medical products conform with established specifications for identity, strength, purity, shelf-life, storage conditions and other characteristics;

“recall” means a process for withdrawing or removing a pharmaceutical product from the pharmaceutical distribution chain;

“reference product” means a medical product which has been granted a marketing authorisation by a competent authority on the basis of a complete dossier;

“referencing” means taking into account the marketing authorisation decisions of a country with a comparable control system for medical products when considering an application for marketing authorisation;

“related substance” means any natural or artificial material in the form of a solid, liquid, gas or vapour or any active or inactive substance or pharmaceutical ingredient which bears similarity to a medical product and which is used or sold for such a purpose;

“reliance” means relying on the assessment and inspection reports and other work done by a trusted regulatory authority in its evaluation of the same product for which marketing authorisation is sought locally;

“re-scheduling” means a review and update of the national classification system that controls how medicines are made available to the public;

“sampling” means the process of obtaining a representative portion of a pharmaceutical product, based on an appropriate statistical procedure, for a defined purpose;

“scheduled substance” means any medicine or other substance described under section 23;

“scheduling” means the national classification system that controls how medicines are made available to the public;

“sell” means to sell by wholesale or retail, and includes to offer, advertise, keep, expose, transmit, consign, convey or deliver for sale or authorise, direct or allow a sale, or prepare or possess for purposes of sale, barter or exchange or supply or dispose of to a person, whether for a consideration or otherwise, and also includes offering or attempting to sell, or receiving for sale, or having in possession for sale, or exposing for sale, or sending or delivering for sale, or causing or allowing to be sold, offered or exposed for sale; and “sale” and “sold” shall be construed accordingly;

“Service” means the Medical Products and Pharmacy Regulatory Service constituted under this Act;

“shelf-life” means the period during which a pharmaceutical product, if stored under the recommended conditions, is expected to comply with the intended effect of the product specification as determined by stabil­ity studies on a number of batches of the product;

“storage” means storing of medical products up to their point of use;

“substance” means a natural or artificial substance, whether in solid or liquid form or in the form of a gas or vapour;

“substandard or falsified medical product” means the like-named product as defined by the World Health Organisation, and refers to all products which are not reference products or approved generics in terms of the standards of safety, quality and efficacy, and in terms of international best practice in the processes of clinical testing, manufacturing, and distribution;

“supply” includes having in possession for the purpose of supply;

“technical working teams” means the teams established in terms of section 9(d);

“treatment” in relation to disease, includes anything done or provided for alleviating the effects of the diseases, whether it is done or provided by way of cure or not;

“wholesale “ means sale of goods in large quantities, such as for resale by a retailer.

**PART II – ADMINISTRATION AND GOVERNANCE**

**Establishment of the Service**

**4.**(1) There is established for the purposes of this Act, a Medical Products and Pharmacy Regulatory Service.

1. The Service shall be functionally independent and accountable to the Authority.
2. The Service shall consist of—
3. the Medical Products and Pharmacy Regulatory Service ;
4. the Medical Products and Pharmacy Regulatory Committee; and
5. units or departments;

**Director of the Service**

**5.**(1) There shall be a Director of the Service who shall be appointed by the Minister on the recommendation of the Committee, and shall hold suitable qualifications in pharmacy or industrial chemistry;

1. The Director shall be accountable to the Committee for the management of the affairs of the Service.
2. The heads of units shall be appointed by the Committee on the recommendation of the Director.

(4) The Director shall be responsible for—

1. management of the affairs of the Service;
2. implementation of this Act regarding activities of the Service and reporting to the Authority through the Committee;
3. execution of the decisions and directives of the Committee and making periodic reports to the Committee;
4. any other responsibilities assigned to him or her by the Committee;

(5) The Director may delegate some of his or her responsibilities to another suitably placed staff member, but will be ultimately responsible for any decisions and actions taken on his or her behalf.

**Functions of the Service**

**6.** The function of the Service shall be to —

1. regulate the manufacture, import and export, distribution, sale and use of medical products;
2. recommend for the approval or withdrawal of a certificate of registration of manufacturers, wholesalers, retailers, importers, exporters and distributors;
3. regulate, monitor and inspect personnel and premises that are involved in the manufacture, import and export, distribution, sale, use and disposal of medical products;
4. maintain a register of medical products for which marketing authorisation has been granted;
5. regulate and ensure the oversight of clinical trials of medical products;
6. test medical products regulated under this Act;
7. conduct post-marketing surveillance of safety and quality of medical products;
8. regulate the promotion, advertising and marketing of medical products;
9. regulate the use of unregistered medical products for trial purposes or for compassionate use, including use in emergency situations such as epidemics, pandemics and to promote national health programmes;
10. disseminate information on quality and safety of medical products to health professionals and the public;
11. disseminate specific information on medical products to health professionals and the public in order to promote their responsible use;
12. establish and promote training programmes for the general public, and private organisations or institutions in relation to the quality and safety of medical products, pharmacovigilance, post-marketing surveillance and the reporting of adverse events;
13. collaborate with other national, regional and international institutions on medical products regulation;
14. recall products from the market;
15. levy, collect and utilise fees for the services it renders;
16. prescribe the standards appropriate for new medical products; new uses, dosages and formulations of existing medical products; and for such other categories as may be appropriate;
17. investigate conduct related to the manufacture, import, export, storage, distribution, sale and use of medical products;
18. formulate regulations and guidelines for regulating the manufacture, import and export, distribution, sale and use of medical products; and
19. perform such functions as may be assigned by the Committee.

**Establishment of Medical Products Regulatory Committee**

**7.** There is established a Medical Products Regulatory Committee.

**Appointment of members to the Regulatory Committee**

**8.** (1) The Minister shall appoint members to the Committee and shall designate one of the members as the Chairperson and Vice-Chairperson of the Committee.

1. The Chairperson of the Committee shall be accountable to the Commissioner for the discharge of the functions assigned to him or her by the Commissioner.
2. The salary and allowances and other terms and conditions of service of the Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson and Members of the Committee shall be in accordance with Government policies.
3. The Committee shall consist of the following members—

(a) the head of the Public Pharmacy Service;

(b) the head of the Quality Control Laboratory;

(c) a Veterinary Surgeon;

(d) the Director of the Medicine Regulatory Service;

(e) a pharmacist from the private sector;

**Functions of the Regulatory Committee**

**9.** The functions of the Committee shall be to—

1. provide strategic guidance to the Service in the discharge of its functions;
2. review the annual reports presented by the Director of the Service;
3. monitor and evaluate the activities of the Service;
4. establish technical working teams to undertake specific technical tasks as it deems necessary for the functioning of the Service;
5. recommend, to the Minister, persons for appointment as the Director of the Service; and
6. perform such other functions as maybe assigned by the Authority.

**PART III – NATIONAL REGULATORY SYSTEM**

**Market authorisation**

**10.**(1) All medical products, circulating in the area of jurisdiction of this Act must be registered and have a valid marketing authorisation and certificate of conformity or analysis.

(2) The Service may determine that a medical product or category of medical products or part of any class or category of medical products shall be subject to exemption from marketing authorisation in under this Act.

(3) The Minister shall publish an authorization or determination under subsection (1) in the *Gazette* and shall come into operation on the date stipulated in the notice.

(4) In the case of a medical product which was available for sale immediately prior to the date of commencement of this Act and which is subject to marketing authorisation under this Act, sub-section (1) shall come into operation, if no application for marketing authorisation of such medical product had been made within the period of twelve months from the date of such publication, on the expiry of such period.

(5) Sub-section (1) shall not apply in respect of the sale of any medical product compounded by a pharmacist for a particular patient in the course of carrying out professional activities in a quantity required for treatment as determined by an authorised person or the pharmacist if —

1. such medical product does not contain any component the sale of which is prohibited by any law nor any component in respect of which an application for marketing authorisation has been rejected; and

(b) the active component of such medical product appears in another medical product which has been authorised under this Act.

**Applications for market authorisation**

**11.**(1) Every application for marketing authorisation of a medical product shall be submitted to the Director in a prescribed form and shall be accompanied by the prescribed particulars, samples of the medical product, particulars of a qualified technical person and the prescribed application fee.

1. The Service may prescribe the standards appropriate for new medical products; new uses, dosages and formulations of existing medical products; interchangeable pharmaceutical products also known as generic equivalents; and such other categories as may be appropriate.
2. The Service may prescribe standards and procedures for referencing, including recognition and relying upon or other weighing, marketing assessments and approvals of other medical products or regulatory authorities or assessment mechanisms.
3. The Service, shall approve a medical product if it is satisfied—

(a) that the product is suitable for the intended purpose in respect of its quality, safety and efficacy; and

(b) that marketing authorisation is in the public interest.

1. If the Service is not satisfied, it shall notify the applicant in writing of the reasons of refusal for granting the market authorisation.
2. A person who has been refused a market authorisation under subsection (5) shall have one month to make representation with regards to the refusal and if no response is submitted by the applicant within that period, or if after consideration of the response, the Service maintains its decision, it shall reject the application.
3. The Service shall publish the list or register of approved and registered medicines in the *Gazette*.
4. Where the Service considers that circumstances may exist which would render necessary the consideration of whether the registration or authorisation should be varied, suspended or revoked, the Service may serve on the applicant a notice requiring him or her, within such time as may be specified in the notice, to furnish it with any information specified in the notice.

**Registration of manufacturers, importers, exporters**

**12.**(1) No person shall manufacture, import, export, supply, store, distribute or sell at wholesale any medical product, unless the person is registered by the Authority.

1. The conditions for registration for the manufacture, import, export, wholesale and distribution of medical products shall be as may be prescribed by the Service.
2. Every manufacturer, importer, exporter, wholesaler and distributor shall comply with Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) and Good Distribution Practice (GDP) and any other codes of good practice as stipulated in guidelines or as may be prescribed by the Authority.
3. The Authority shall designate ports of entry for medical products imported into Seychelles.
4. The Service shall maintain a register of all licensed premises and shall publish it in the *Gazette.*

**Labels on medicines**

**13.**(1) All medicines imported into Seychelles shall bear a label printed or affixed to the container legible in one of the national languages.

(2) The label provided under subsection (1) shall be the original label produced by the manufacturer of the medical product.

(3) Notwithstanding subsection (2), a label printed or affixed after repackaging shall be permitted if done under licence of the manufacturer.

(4) Subsections (1), (2) and (3) shall apply *mutatis mutandis* to inserts of any medical products.

(5) Any medical product imported into Seychelles which does not conform with this section shall be liable to confiscation by a customs officer and to be disposed of by the Authority.

(6) Whoever contravenes a provision under this section commits an offence and shall be liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding level 6 on the Standard Scale, or to imprisonment not exceeding 6 months or to both.

**Control of clinical trials of medical products**

**14.**(1) No person shall conduct clinical trials of medical products in humans or animals without the authorisation from the Health Research and Ethics Committee of the Ministry.

1. All clinical trials shall be conducted in accordance with the guidelines issued by the Service, including the provisions for Good Clinical Practice (GCP) and Good Laboratory Practice (GLP).
2. A person shall not sell, dispense, supply, assemble or manufacture products for the purpose of a clinical trial or medical research on a medical product unless that person is authorised to do so or has been granted an exemption by the Authority.
3. The Service shall maintain a register of all clinical trials conducted under this section, and such register shall include all relevant information necessary for the control of such clinical trials.

**Promotion and advertisement of medical products**

**15.**(1) The Service shall issue guidelines relating to the promotion and advertising of medical products and for an enforceable code of marketing practice.

**National Pharmacovigilance Programme**

**16.**(1) The Service shall established a National Pharmacovigilance Programme to monitor and report on the safety, quality and efficacy of medical products.

1. The Pharmacovigilance Programme shall—
2. monitor and analyse the adverse effects or events relating to medical products;
3. identify and report adverse events relating to clinical trials;
4. establish causality, take remedial actions, and report to international safety monitoring systems;
5. take appropriate action when necessary, including revising the marketing authorisation or labelling or warning requirements of the affected medical product.
6. The Service shall—
7. issue guidelines to provide for reporting and submission of periodic safety updates by the manufacturers, distributors, the health professionals and the public;

(b) institute a risk-based testing scheme consisting of the sampling of medical products to identify the products most at risk or likely to be falsified or sub-standard, and take appropriate action to protect public health, including enforcement measures under this Act.

1. Where any medical product does not conform to the standards of identity, strength, quality and purity, or any other requirement specified under section 14(2) and (3), the Director shall—
2. instruct the registered person and any person or facility selling such medical product to discontinue the sale of the remainder of the batch;
3. recall any portion of the batch already sold, so far as is practicable; and
4. provide the Service of proof of destruction or disposal of such medical products in the manner approved by the Service.
5. The Director shall—
6. by order published in the G*azette*, withdraw and strike off from the register, a medical product which on the latest available scientific evidence is shown to be hazardous to public health and welfare, or is unsafe, inefficacious or of unacceptable quality; and
7. issue a notice to the public regarding the affected medical product withdrawn from the market, and disseminate such information as widely as possible.
8. If the Director is of the opinion that it is not in the public interest that a medical product be made available to the public, the Director may direct that such product be withdrawn from the market and disposed of in the manner approved by the Service.

**Appointment of authorised officers**

**17.**(1) The Service shall—

(a) appoint authorised officers with relevant qualifications in pharmacy or related sciences, and with knowledge and experience in the inspection of medical products and facilities for the manufacture, sale, storage and transportation of medical products: and

(b) authorise such authorised officers to perform such functions as are stipulated under this Act.

1. All authorised officers appointed under this Act shall carry valid identification during the performance of their duties.

**Powers of authorised officers**

**18.** An authorised officer appointed under this Act may—

1. at all reasonable times, enter any—
2. premises which are listed in the register of premises;
3. other premises in respect of any person who is registered under this Act;
4. premises used in the manufacture, marketing, or distribution of a medical product that is the subject of a marketing authorisation or registration request; or
5. premises suspected of or dealing in products regulated under this Act;
6. at a reasonable time—
7. examine or inspect any certificate of marketing authorisation, registration , book, electronic information storage system or other document on the premises and, for that purpose, may do such other things, including the taking of extracts from documents in the possession of the person as may be necessary to effect the examination or inspection; or
8. take samples for analysis, or for other examination, of any medical product or of any substance capable of being used in the manufacture of a medical product;
9. seize any medical products or related substances, articles or anything —
10. consisting of, or containing, any prohibited substances which the authorise officer has reasons to suspect the substance is liable to forfeiture under this Act; or
11. any records or documents in any form which appear to constitute or contain evidence of a contravention of any provisions this Act;
12. close the premises found to be in contravention of this Act; or
13. recommend any contravention under this Act for administrative, civil and or criminal proceedings.

**Donation of medical products**

**19.**(1)Unless the Commissioner, by order exempts, all donations of medical products to government, non-governmental organisations and private entities shall—

1. be based on an expressed need and be relevant to the disease pattern of the country;
2. not be sent without prior consent by the recipient;
3. be approved for use in the country, and appear on the national list of essential medicines or, if not available, on the World Health Organisation Model List of Essential Medicines, unless specifically requested by the recipient;
4. comply with the presentation, strength and formulation as much as possible to those commonly used in the country;
5. be obtained from a reliable source and comply with quality standards in both the donor and recipient countries;
6. exclude products that were issued to patients and subsequently returned, or those given to health professionals as samples;
7. upon arrival in the country, have a remaining shelf-life of at least one year, and the date of arrival and the expiry date be communicated to the recipient well in advance;
8. be labelled in a language that is easily understood by health professionals in the country, and the labels on individual containers state the International Non-Proprietary name (INN), or generic name, batch number, dosage form, strength, name of manufacturer, quantity in the container, storage conditions and expiry date;
9. as far as possible, be presented in large-quantity units and hospital packs;
10. be packed in accordance with international shipping regulations and be accompanied by a detailed packing list that specifies the contents of each individual numbered carton by INN, dosage form, quantity, batch number, expiry date, volume, weight, and any special storage conditions; and medicines shall not be combined with other supplies in the same carton;
11. require, as far as practicable, that the costs of international and local transport, warehousing, port clearance, and appropriate storage and handling be paid by the donor agency.

(2) Any donations being proposed to any non-governmental organisations and private entities shall notified to the Service in the prescribed form.

**Quality control laboratory**

**20.**(1) There shall be established by the Service, a  National quality control laboratory.

(2) The Commissioner shall, in consultation with the Service, appoint a person who has special knowledge and experience in the analysis of medical products and related substances as the head of the laboratory and such number of analysts as may be necessary for the analysis of medical products or related substances.

(3) The head of the laboratory shall perform all functions relating to the quality of   products and substances regulated under this Act and shall in particular —

(a) analyse medical products, related products or any other regulated product that may be deemed to constitute a medical product for the purpose of this Act;

(b) conduct research and training;

(c)  manage all the operations of the Laboratory;

(d)  train analysts in the analysis of medicines;

(e)  analyse products which are being sold, manufactured, or advertised as medical products; and

(f) undertake such other function as may be determined by the Authority.

(4) In performing its functions, the quality control laboratory may utilise or allow the reliance on and or recognition of, regulatory decisions in the area of laboratory testing of any accredited Laboratory within or outside of the Republic for the analysis of medical products and attendant functions.

**Scheduling and classification**

**21.**(1) The scheduling, re-scheduling, and classification of any medical product or substance shall be determined by the Service and be published in the *gazette*.

**Control of medical products**

1. Control of medical products shall be based on the scheduling status of substances as determined by the Service based on the following categories —
2. scheduled substances that will be available for general sales, in any retail outlet;
3. scheduled substances that will be available on the professional advice of a pharmacist, without a prescription from an authorized person , and available only in registered pharmacies;
4. scheduled substances that will be available only on the prescription of an authorised person , and dispensed by a pharmacist or registered dispenser;
5. scheduled substances that will be available only on the prescription of an authorized person , and dispensed by a pharmacist or registered dispenser, subject to the control measures prescribed in accordance with either the Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs, 1961, the Convention on Psychotropic Substances, 1971, and the UN Convention against Illicit Traffic Drug and Psychotropic Substances, 1988; and
6. scheduled substances that may not be sold, except in accordance with a permit for the purposes of education, analysis or research, or for individual patient purposes.

**PART VI - INTERNATIONAL COOPERATION AND HARMONISATION OF REGULATION OF MEDICAL PRODUCTS**

**Collection of data**

1. The Service shall collect such data as may be necessary on the importation, exportation and manufacture of psychotropic or narcotic substances as are required for reporting to the International Narcotics Control Board.

**International cooperation**

**24.**(1)The Service shall cooperate in matter relating to the discharge of its functions with other national, regional and continental medical products regulatory agencies.

1. The Service shall share pharmaceutical intelligence on products that pose public health risks with other agencies at the regional, continental and global levels.

**Regulatory harmonisation initiatives**

**25.**(1) The Service shall participate in regional and continental medical products regulatory harmonisation initiatives.

1. The Service shall take such measures to ensure effective co-operation with similar agencies in other countries to—
2. harmonise registration of medical products, inspections, quality management systems, information management systems, joint evaluations, joint inspections and any other regulatory activities as may be appropriate;
3. provide for the use of accredited quality control laboratories within the harmonisation framework;
4. provide for the recognition of regional, continental and other international technical guidelines;
5. provide for harmonisation of the data requirements for evidence of quality, safety, and efficacy of medical products and the grounds on which authorisation for distribution shall be granted within the region;
6. provide for mutual recognition of marketing authorisation decisions;
7. share summary evaluation and inspection reports;
8. participate in common post-marketing surveillance conducted in accordance with nationally and internationally recognised standards;
9. provide for cooperation with other regulatory agencies or authorities for the purpose of strengthening national regulatory capacity;
10. establish networks with other regulatory agencies or authorities and collaborate in protecting public health through enforcement activities;
11. establish exchange programmes with other medical products regulatory agencies/authorities so as to keep abreast of evolving scientific development in the field of medical products;
12. provide for any necessary legal mechanisms for regulatory harmonisation; and
13. provide for transparency and information-sharing through—

(m) establishment of a quality management system based on common regional and continental requirements to ensure efficiency; and

1. the creation of a national information management system which allows for sharing information at regional and continental levels in accordance with national laws, bilateral and multilateral agreements.

**PART VII - MONITORING AND EVALUATION**

**Monitoring and evaluation of regulatory system**

**26.**(1)The Service shall create a monitoring and evaluation system charged with reviewing and assessing the performance of the Service.

1. The Service shall prepare periodic reports and submit them to the Commissioner who shall present them to the Board.
2. The Minister or his or her representative shall report on the performance of the Service to relevant governing bodies at regional and continental levels.

**Prohibition on import, export, manufacture etc. of medical products of scheduled substance without registration**

**27.**(1) Any person who manufacture, import, export, supply, dispense, store, distribute or sell any medical products unless in possession of a registration or authorisation issued by the Service, commits an offence.

(2) A person who commits an offence under subsection (1) shall be liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding level 6 on the Standard Scale or to imprisonment not exceeding four years or to both.

**Prohibition on import, export, manufacture of substandard or falsified products**

**28.**(1) Any person who manufacture, import, export, supply, store, distribute or sell any substandard or falsified medical products commits an offence.

(2) A person who commits an offence under subsection (1) shall be liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding level 6 on the Standard Scale, or to imprisonment no exceeding five years or to both.

**Obstruction of authorised officers**

**29.**(1) Any person who obstructs or hinders any authorised officers in the exercise of his or her powers or the performance of his or her duties under this Act commits an offence.

(2) A person who commits an offence under subsection (1) shall be liable on conviction be l a fine not exceeding level 6 on the Standard Scale, or to imprisonment not exceeding two years or to both.

**Fraudulent actions and misleading statements**

**30.**(1) Any person who, with fraudulent intent, tampers with any sample taken under this Act or makes any false or misleading statement in connection with any medical product or scheduled substance sells, supplies or distributes any medical product or scheduled substances upon the container of which a false or misleading statement in connection with the contents is written, commits an offence.

(2) A person who commits an offence under subsection (1) shall be liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding level 6 on the Standard Scale, or to imprisonment not exceeding three years or to both.

**Other offences not provided for**

**31.**(1) A person who contravenes any provision of this Act for which no penalty has been specifically provided for commits an offence and shall be liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding level 6 on the Standard Scale, or to imprisonment not exceeding six years or to both .

**Appeals**

**32.** A person who is aggrieved by a decision of the Service may lodge an appeal with the Appeals Board established under Part VI of the Public Health Authority Act, 2013.

**PART X - PHARMACIES AND RELATED ACTIVITIES**

**Ownership of pharmacy business**

**33.**(1) A person shall not own or operate a pharmacy business unless authorised and registered to do so by the Authority under this Act.

(2) The application form for authorisation to own or operate a pharmacy business shall be in such form and manner as may be prescribed.

(3) A person who contravenes subsection (1) or (2) commits an offence and shall be liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding level 6 on the Standard Scale, or to imprisonment not exceeding two years or to both.

**Persons eligible to own a pharmacy**

**34.**(1) Subject to section 38, the following persons are eligible to own or to have property interest in a pharmacy business—

1. a registered pharmacist; or
2. a company registered under the Companies Act 1972-
3. whose directors are all registered pharmacists; and
4. in which all the shares and the beneficial and legal interest in those shares are held by registered pharmacists;
5. a lawful partnership whose partners are all registered pharmacists;
6. a registered pharmacist together with other persons approved by the Committee, who does not already have a proprietary interest in other pharmacies.

(2) The pharmacist referred to under subsection (1) (b), (c) and (d) shall have the majority share giving him or her effective control over the business.

(3) The holder of a license of a private clinic or hospital shall not be eligible to own or have a property interest in a pharmacy business.

(4) The holder of a license of a private hospital shall not be eligible to own or have a property interest in a pharmacy business but, may stock an emergency supply or medication, provided that, the dispensation is only for forty-eight hours supply of medicine to a patient at any time.

(5) Subsection (4) may be amended by an Order made by the Minister and published in the Gazette.

(6) For the purpose of this section a person shall not be eligible and be authorised to own or have a property share in more than 2 pharmacy businesses.

(7) Any person who contravenes subsection (3) and (4) commits an offence and shall be liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding level 6 on the Standard Scale, or to imprisonment not exceeding two years or to both.

(8) Notwithstanding subsections (1), (2) and (3), subsection (6) shall not apply to a person who—

1. is the executor, administrator or trustee of the estate of a pharmacist who has died and who carried a pharmacy business at the time of his or her death;
2. is appointed or authorized under the law of bankruptcy to administer the property of the pharmacist who carried on a pharmacy business and is bankrupt; or
3. assumes the administration of a property of a person under a mortgage bill of sale or security interest of that person’s pharmacy business,

and continues to carry on the pharmacy business for a period of 6 months, or for any further period permitted by the committee, if the pharmacy services of the business are provided by a registered pharmacist in accordance with this Part.

**Requirement to provide information about pharmacy ownership**

**35.**(1) The Service may require a person to give to the Service any information or produce any documents relating to the ownership or propriety interest in a pharmacy business.

(2) A person who fail or refuse to give to the Service any information required under subsection (1) or fail or refuse to produce any documents required under subsection (1) or wilfully mislead the Service when giving the information commits an offence and shall be liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding level 6 on the Standard Scale, or to imprisonment not exceeding two years or to both.

**Approval of pharmacy businesses**

**36.**(1) The Authority may approve the premises of a pharmacy business if the Authority is satisfied that the premises are suitable for the provision of pharmacy services.

1. In approving a premises under subsection (1), the Authority shall have regard to—
2. the facilities, equipment, security, management and operation of the pharmacy business at the premises;
3. that the premises comply with good pharmacy practice; and
4. any other requirements as stipulated under this Act or that may be prescribed.
5. Without limiting the generality of subsection (2), the Authority may impose any condition it thinks appropriate for the approval of premises for a pharmacy business.

**Cause for disciplinary action**

**37.** There shall be cause for disciplinary action against a registered person under this Part, if the person —

1. registration or license renewal was improperly obtained;
2. fails to comply with any condition imposed by virtue of this Act on person’s registration;
3. commits unprofessional conduct; or
4. is for any reason no longer a fit and proper person to be registered on the register or to be licensed.

**Body corporate disqualified from authorisation to own or have a proprietary interest in pharmacy business under certain circumstances**

**38.**(1) If a body corporate authorised to own or have a propriety interested in a pharmacy business —

1. is convicted of any offence under this Act or any other law; or
2. any member of the board of directors or any officer of that body or any person employed by that body in carrying on the business has been convicted of a criminal offence, or been guilty of any misconduct, as in the opinion of the Committee—
3. renders him or her guilty of unprofessional conduct;
4. or would render him or her guilty of unprofessional conduct if he or she was a registered pharmacist,

the Committee may enquire into the matter and may, subject to the provisions of this Act, direct that the body cooperate is no longer authorised to own or have a proprietary interest in a pharmacy business and is disqualified from such authorisation for such period as it is specified in the direction.

**Managing pharmacist**

**39.**(1) Every pharmacy shall be managed by a registered pharmacist with a minimum of five years professional experience.

1. The managing pharmacist shall—
2. act as the managing pharmacist of a registered pharmacy including any other premises used as storage by the said pharmacy;
3. ensure that he or she or another registered pharmacist sells or supervises the sale of medical products present in the pharmacy and keep a record of the pharmacist who was present at all times while the pharmacy was open;
4. keep any documents, information or evidence in the manner as may be required by or under this Act;

(d) carry out such obligations pertaining to a managing pharmacist as may be established by or under this Act or under the Public Health Authority Act;

(e) nominate a substitute managing pharmacist when he or she will be absent and cannot carry out his or her duties for a period of five or more consecutive days, and shall give notice to the Service of this substitution:

Provided that in exceptional cases, such as the sudden absence on account of illness or other reason, the registration holder may nominate a replacement and notify the Authority, as soon as possible thereafter;

1. comply with regulations or guidelines relating to good practice in the dispensing of medical products as may be provided for by or under this Act or any other law; and
2. dispose of medical products as provided for by or under this Act or any other law.

(h) no pharmacist may act as a managing pharmacist for more than one pharmacy.

**Dispensing of medical products**

**40.**(1) A medical product shall be prepared or dispensed only from a pharmacy and by a registered pharmacist:

Provided that a pharmacist may permit medical products to be prepared or dispensed by a registered pharmacy technician under his or her personal supervision as regulated by or under this Act.

(2) In carrying out his functions in the preparation and dispensing of medical products from a pharmacy, a pharmacist shall adhere to such standards as may be provided under this Act or any other law.

**Sale of medical products**

**41.**(1) A pharmacy shall sell or dispense only such medical products and related substance as may be prescribed by regulations.

(2) Unless otherwise provided for by or under this Act, a medical product shall be sold only from a registered pharmacy:

Provided that the Service may, in special circumstances relating to the provision of services to the public, by guidelines prescribe that a medical product or groups or classes or schedules of medical products therein specified may be sold from a licensed premises other than a registered pharmacy.

**Dispensing of prescription medical products**

**42.**(1) A pharmacist shall prepare or dispense any medical product required by any person presenting a prescription, unless he or she has a justifiable concern that the prescription is false, that the person is misusing the prescribed medical product, that the medical product is not available, or if he or she has a valid professional reason for not preparing or dispensing the prescription.

(2) When, in dispensing any medical product, a pharmacist discovers that there is a reason why the medical product should not be dispensed to the patient or that the dosage regimen indicated on the prescription goes beyond what can be considered a safe therapeutic dose, the pharmacist shall be bound to draw the attention thereto of the person prescribing the same and may require such person to write out in ink or in other indelible manner on the prescription a statement assuming responsibility for the prescription.

(3) Upon presentation of a prescription for a medical product, unless the prescriber specifically requests a particular branded product by writing "branded" on the prescription, a pharmacist shall dispense the medical product prescribed or an equivalent medical product having the same chemical entity, dose, dosage form, formulation and dosage frequency as the medical product indicated on the prescription.

(4) The pharmacist shall assume full responsibility for the dispensing of medical products which do not require a prescription for dispensing to patients.

**Dispensing without prescription**

**43.** A pharmacist shall not dispense any medical product except on the prescription of a medical or dental practitioner, veterinary surgeon or other person authorised to prescribe under this Act or any other Act, unless the medical product is deemed not to require a medical prescription by the service.

**Form of prescription**

**44.** The Service may prescribe by regulations the format, content and presentation of a prescription.

**Labelling of dispensed medical products**

**45.** The pharmacist shall label each medical product or magistral formula or official formula dispensed in accordance with the regulations or guidelines made under this Act.

**Prohibition on sale and disposal of imperfect medical products**

**46.**(1) A managing pharmacist shall not sell, allow the sale of, dispense or supply in any other manner —

(a) any imperfect, deteriorated or harmful medical products or substance;

(b) any medical product bearing an expiry date which has expired;

(c) any patient returns of medical products.

(2) The managing pharmacist shall be responsible for the disposal of the products described under subsection (1) in the manner and form as may be prescribed by the Service.

1. Any person who contravenes subsection (1) and (2) commits an offence and shall be liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding level 6 on the Standard Scale, or to imprisonment not exceeding two years or to both.

**Storage of medical products in pharmacy**

**47.**(1) A managing pharmacist shall not keep or store within the pharmacy any medical product in any container or under such conditions as are not suitable to the nature of the product, and which do not protect it from alteration, deterioration or contamination.

(2) A managing pharmacist shall not permit any medical product

under his or her charge to be kept or stored outside the pharmacy under his or her control, and shall ensure that the pharmacy has the facilities to store the medical products in accordance with approved storage requirements as may be prescribed by regulations.

1. Any person who contravenes subsection (1) and (2) commits an offence and shall be liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding level 6 on the Standard Scale, or to imprisonment not exceeding two years or to both.

**Premises to be kept in accordance with requirements under this Act**

**48.** Premises, facilities, records and equipment used for the storage, preparation and dispensing of medical products are to be kept in accordance with the requirements and standards as may be prescribed under this Act.

**Preparations, dispensing, and administration of medical products to be in accordance with regulations**

**49.** The preparation of magistral and official formulas, and the pre-packing, reconstitution, dispensing and administration of medical products and any other activity related to medical products and their use shall be in accordance with regulations made under this Act.

**Inspection of pharmacies**

**50.**(1) The Service shall inspect pharmacies and any premises registered for use as stores of medical products at all reasonable times.

(2) An inspection shall be carried out by an authorised officers in the presence of the managing pharmacist or of the pharmacist for the time being in charge of the pharmacy.

(3) At the time of the inspection, the authorised officer shall draw up a list of deficiencies that may have been identified and shall sign the list, and such list shall be countersigned by the managing pharmacist or by the pharmacist for the time being in charge of the pharmacy.

(4) The authorised officer shall draw up a report of the inspection within seven working days of the inspection and shall forward a copy of such report to the Service, the holder of the certificate of registration and the managing pharmacist.

(5) The managing pharmacist or pharmacist for the time being in charge of the pharmacy may make comments or otherwise make reservations in respect of the contents of the list or report.

(6) If the managing pharmacist or the pharmacist for the time being in charge of the pharmacy refuses to countersign the list referred to in subsection (3), the authorised officer shall record such fact on the said list together with the reason given, if any, for such refusal.

**Opening times of pharmacies**

**51.** The Commissioner may by notice in the Gazette specify the business hours of pharmacies and may also require pharmacies in specified localities or districts to open for the service of customers on such days and for such duration as may be specified in such notice.

**Wholesale distribution of medical products for human use**

**52.** (1)No person shall engage in the wholesale distribution of any medical product unless he or she is the holder of a wholesaler’s registration issued in accordance with the provisions of this Act unless the medical product has been granted a marketing authorisation under this Act.

(2) The wholesale distribution of a medical product by a wholesaler shall be carried out only from the place specified in, and in accordance with the conditions of, the registration.

**Application for a wholesaler’s registration**

**53.** Any application for the grant of a wholesaler's registration shall be made to the Authority and shall contain such information, documents, and other material as may be prescribed by regulations.

**Granting of a wholesaler’s registration**

**54.**(1) The Authority shall, before determining an application received under section 54, inspect the premises indicated in the application and shall not issue a registration until it is satisfied that such premises conform to the requirements of this Act and the Public Health Authority Act.

(2) The wholesaler’s registration shall specify the premises and the

activities to which it applies.

(3) The holder of wholesaler’s registration holder shall ensure that the activity is carried out in accordance with the provisions of this Act and any regulations made thereunder.

**Notice requiring further information**

**55.** Where the Authority considers that circumstances may exist which would render necessary the consideration of whether the registration should be varied, suspended or revoked, the Authority may serve on the holder of a wholesaler’s registration a notice requiring him, within such time as may be specified in the notice, to furnish it with any information specified in the notice.

**Duties of the responsible person in a wholesale**

**56.**(1) There shall be appointed by the holder of wholesaler’s registration a pharmacist with five year’s professional experience and two year’s store management experience as the responsible person.

(2) The responsible person shall ensure that standards of good practice in wholesale distribution, as maybe prescribed, are complied with at all times.

(3) The responsible person shall be permanently and continuously

available at the wholesale:

Provided that the responsible person may nominate another pharmacist to act as his representative in his absence.

(4) When the responsible person has nominated a representative, he shall immediately inform the Authority of such nomination.

**Inspection in relation to wholesale**

**57.**(1) The Service shall ensure that the requirements of this Act in relation to wholesaling of medical products are complied with.

1. For the purpose of subsection 1 the Service shall—
2. inspect the wholesale establishment and any other location it may deem necessary;
3. examine any documents relating to the inspection;
4. take any samples it may deem necessary;
5. draw up a report of the findings, which shall be communicated to the holder of registration or the applicant for a registration in relation to such inspection, and to the responsible person;
6. carry out any other activity it may deem appropriate for the proper execution of its duties as provided for by or under this Act.

**Other requirements**

**58.** Without prejudice to the provisions of this Part, the Service may, by regulations establish additional requirements for the wholesale distribution of—

(a) narcotic or psychotropic substances;

(b) medical products derived from blood;

(c) immunological medical products;

(d) radiopharmaceuticals; and

(e) such other medical products or groups or classes of medical products as the Minister may prescribe by regulation.

**Application of this part will not apply to public hospital and pharmacies**

1. Notwithstanding section 2(1), the provisions of section 36 to 42 shall not apply to a government owned public hospital or a government owned public pharmacy.

**PART VII - MISCELLANEOUS PROVISIONS**

**Declaration and conflicts of interest**

**60.**(1) A member of the Committee or staff of the Service shall declare any interests related to any medical products, or any other interest which may be relevant to any decision-making.

(2) Any member of the Committee who has indicated a conflict of interest in any matter that has come before the Committee for a decision shall not take part in any decision or vote in that matter.

**Restriction of liability**

**61.** The Service, a member of the Committee or staff of the Service are not liable for any loss or damage arising from any decision made or act carried out in good faith in the exercise of their powers or the performance of their functions under this Act or any other applicable law.

**Protection of and access to information**

**62.**(1) No person who is employed by the Service, or is a member of the Committee shall disclose to any other person or institution any information acquired in the exercise of his or her powers or the performance of his or her functions under this Act and relating to the business or affairs of any person, or use such information for self-gain or for the benefit of his or her employer.

(2) A person may be permitted to disclose information—

1. for the purpose of the exercise of powers or the performance of his or her functions under this Act with the written authority of the Commissioner;
2. when required to do so by any competent court or under any law.

**Regulations**

**63.**(1) The Minister may make regulations for the purpose of carrying out the provisions of this Act.

(2) Without prejudice to the generality of the power under subsection (1), such regulations may provide for —

1. the requirements for a legal prescription for a scheduled substance;
2. the recognition of categories of authorised person;
3. the registration of dispensers other than pharmacist;
4. the records to be kept in relation to scheduled substances sold on the professional advice of a pharmacist or on prescription of an authorised person;
5. the control measures to be implemented in relation to substances scheduled as psychotropic or narcotic substances;
6. the process of obtaining permits for access to scheduled substances for purposes of education, analysis or research or for individual patients’ purposes;
7. the registration of importers, exporters and manufacturers of psychotropic or narcotic substances and the reporting requirements for such substances; and
8. the permissible mark-up of prices for medical products by retailers and wholesalers;
9. the handling of any substandard, expired or falsified medical products in collaboration with other relevant institutions;
10. for offences and provide penalties therefor to a fine not exceeding that of level 6 on the standard scale or to imprisonment not exceeding 5 years or both a fine and imprisonment;
11. for the number of pharmacies that can be permitted to operate within a prescribed area based on geodemographic criteria.

**Repeal and savings**

**64.**(1) The Pharmacy Act (Cap 169) is hereby repealed.

(2) Notwithstanding the repeal of the Pharmacy Act (Act 19 of 1899) all acts done, decisions taken, authorisations or permissions granted, regulations made thereunder which were validly done, taken, or granted under any written law or under and in accordance with the repealed Act shall continue to have effect in accordance with their terms or until amended, annulled or withdrawn in accordance with the written law.

**Transitional provisions**

**65.**(1) On the commencement of this Act —

1. any licences already issued that is in contravention of section 36 and 37 shall have 3 years to conform to the provisions of section 36 and 37.
2. if, after the 3 years stipulated under subparagraph (a) the licences have not complied with the provisions of section 36 or 37, their licence shall continue for the remaining period it is still valid for but shall not be renewable after the end of that period until the provisions of section 36 and 37 has been complied with.
