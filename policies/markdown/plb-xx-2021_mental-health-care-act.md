This brief document is an overview of the content of Section 5 of the new Mental Health
Care Act, 2020. More specifically, the Section that deals with the modalities for care and
treatment of persons with mental health illnesses. The brief has implications for clinical
practice and shall keep the mental health nurses/workers abreast with the relevant part
of  the  legislation  that  directly  relate  to  the  support  and  assistance  they  provide  to
persons living with mental health illnesses. This document has been compiled by the
Policy Unit, Department of Health, Maison Hermitage.

SECTION 5: CARE AND TREATMENT OF PERSONS WITH MENTAL ILLNESS

1. Admission of Persons with Mental Illness

A person with mental illness may be treated, as far as possible, at their home or near to
their  home  without  requiring  care  and  treatment  in  a  mental  health  facility.  In  the
instance that a person with mental illness requires treatment in a mental health facility,
treatment  shall  be  provided  on  a  basis  of  voluntary  admission.  This  also  applies  to
persons that lack the capacity to make decisions for his or her own mental health care
and they may be treated on facilitated admission.

2. Voluntary Admission

All  admissions  in  the  mental  health  facility  shall,  as  far  as  possible,  be  voluntary
admissions  except  where  conditions  for  a  facilitated  admission  exist.  Any  adult  who
believes that he or she has a mental illness and wishes to be admitted to a mental health
facility  for  treatment  and  care  can  make  a  referral  to  the  Consultant-in-charge1  and
thereafter a Medical Officer or a Psychiatrist shall examine the person and provide the
Consultant-in-charge with a report. The Consultant-in-charge shall admit the person to
the mental health facility if he or she is satisfied that the person has a mental illness of

A person who is voluntarily admitted shall not be treated without his or her informed
consent. The person has the right to discharge himself or herself from the mental health
facility. That is, if the Consultant-in-charge of the mental health facility or his or her
designated  representative  is  of  the  opinion  that  the  person  meets  the  criteria  for  a
facilitated admission, the Consultant-in-charge may prevent self-discharge for a period
not exceeding 24 hours to allow for assessment.2

3. Discharge Reports

The Consultant-in-charge of a mental health facility shall, in a prescribed form, issue a
discharge report to the person with mental illness who was admitted for receiving care,
treatment and rehabilitation services.

5. Treatment on Facilitated Admission and Further Steps

A facilitated admission of a person to a mental health facility under (Point 4)  above is
limited to a period of 14 days. The person with mental illness shall receive treatment as
prescribed  by  the  mental  health  professional  with  the  consent  of  the  nominated
representative or on advance directive (if any). In instances where the  Consultant-in-
charge is of the opinion that the person admitted under facilitated admission, on expiry
of the 14 days, no longer meets the criteria for admission, he or she may discharge the
person or may continue the admission under voluntary admission.

On the other hand, if on the expiry of the 14 days, the Consultant-in-charge is of the

6. Admission of Minors

Admission of a minor with mental illness shall be made on application of the parent or
guardian  or  the  person  having  custody  of  the  minor.  The  procedure  for  facilitated
admission under shall mutatis mutandis7 apply to admission of minors. Minors admitted
to the mental health facility shall be accommodated separately from adults.

The parent/guardian or the person having custody of the minor or a person appointed
by the parent or legal guardian shall stay with the minor in the mental health facility
for the period of their treatment in the mental health facility. Minors shall be treated
only  with  the  consent  of  the  parent  or  guardian  or  the  person  having  custody  of  the
minor.

7. Person with Mental Illness at Home

In the instance a person at home is suffering from mental illness, of such a degree that
in the opinion of the nominated representative warrants care and treatment, and that
person is not willing to go to a mental health facility for that purpose, the nominated
representative may make a request to the nearest health facility for an assessment at
home. A subsequent visit by a mental health professional shall be made to assess the
person with mental health illness.

If  the  mental  health  professional  on  assessment  is  of  the  opinion  that  the  person  is
suffering  from  mental  illness,  and  is  neglecting  himself  or  herself  to  an  extent  which
puts their own lives or the safety of others at risk, the mental health professional may
request a police officer for assistance in moving the person to the nearest mental health
facility.

8. Person with Mental Illness Wondering on Streets

A police officer shall, on being informed by a member of the public or on seeing a person
suspected of having mental illness wandering in a public place, convey such person to
the nearest mental health facility for assessment.

9. Emergency Treatment

Notwithstanding  other  provisions  of  the  Mental  Health  Care  Act,  any  treatment  for
mental illness may be provided by any registered medical practitioner to a person with
mental illness, either at a mental health facility, or in the community with or without
the consent of the person with mental illness, if it is immediately necessary to prevent
death  or  irreversible  harm  to  the  health  of  the  person,  or  the  person  from  inflicting
serious harm to self or others. Emergency treatment under includes transportation of
the person with mental illness to the nearest mental health facility for assessment and
any emergency treatment shall be limited to 24 hours.

10. Seclusion and Restraints

A person with mental illness shall not be subjected to seclusion and to physical restraint
unless  it  is  the  only  means  available  to  prevent  immediate  or  imminent  harm  to  the
person concerned or to others. Physical restraints shall be done in an accredited mental
health  facility  and  shall  be  authorised  by  a  Psychiatrist  or  medical  practitioner  with
expertise in mental Health.

Furthermore,  restraints  shall  not  be  used  as  a  means  of  punishment  or  for  the
convenience  of  the  staff.  The  reasons  and  duration  of  each  shall  be  recorded  in  a
database and shall be made available to the Board8 on a regular basis. Physical restraint
in  each  instance  shall  be  restricted  to  a  maximum  period  of  three  hours.  The  family
members, caregiver or nominated representatives shall immediately be informed, when
the person with mental illness is physically restrained.

Nominated representatives, relatives and caregivers of a person with mental illness shall

12. Leave to Persons Admitted as Facilitated Admission

The  Consultant-in-charge  of  the  mental  health  facility  may  grant  leave  to  a  person
admitted as a facilitated admission in a mental health facility for a period not exceeding
seven days at a time. The Consultant-in-charge of the mental health facility may, at any
time revoke the leave if he or she is satisfied that it is necessary for the improvement of,
or to prevent deterioration of the mental health of the person.

In the instance a person with mental illness who has been granted leave from facilitated
admission refuses on revocation of the leave or on the expiry of the leave to come back
to  the  mental  health  facility,  the  Consultant-in-charge  may  follow  the  procedures
specified below (Point 13) for his or her return to the mental health facility.

13. Absence without Leave of Persons Admitted as Facilitated Admission

In  the  instance  a  person  with  mental  illness  admitted  as  a  facilitated  admission  is
missing from the mental health facility without being granted leave, the Consultant-in-
charge of the mental health facility shall inform the police and the person who had made
the application for admission of that person. The police shall have the responsibility to
convey the person back to the mental health facility.

15. Prisoners with Mental Illness

Where an officer in charge of a prison is of the opinion that a prisoner is suffering from
mental illness, the officer shall make an application to the Consultant-in-charge of a
mental  health  facility  for  transfer  of  the  prisoner  to  the  mental  health  facility  for
assessment  by  a  psychiatrist.  On  the  directions  of  the  Consultant-in-charge  of  the
mental  health  facility,  the  psychiatrist  shall  assess  the  person  and  may  recommend
outpatient treatment and send the prisoner back to prison or admission to the mental
health facility either as voluntary admission or a facilitated admission, as the case may
be.

In the instance a prisoner is admitted to the mental health facility, the time spent by
the prisoner at the mental health facility shall be treated as time spent in prison. When
a prisoner is discharged after treatment in the mental health facility, he or she shall be
sent back to prison for continuation of his or her sentence or shall be discharged by the
prison service if the sentence has been served.
