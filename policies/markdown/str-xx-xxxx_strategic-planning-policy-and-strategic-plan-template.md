1.  Introduction and Rationale

Since the 2008 crisis, the Government has embarked on a series of reforms with the aim of
improving the state of the economy and public sector performance.  Initially the aim of the
public  sector  reform  was  to  reduce  the  size  of  the  public  sector,  but  subsequently  also  to
improve  service  delivery.  In  2013,  the  Government  adopted  Results  Based  Management
(RBM) as it recognised the importance of moving to outcomes orientated approach to drive
the Public Sector Reform strategy.  The RBM is a life –cycle approach to management that
integrates  strategy,  people,  resources,  processes  and  measurements  to  improve  decision-
making, transparency and accountability. The RBM Policy, which was approved by Cabinet in
2013  comprises  of  four  pillars:  (i)  Strategic  Planning  (SP),  (ii)  Program  Performance  Based
Budgeting  (PPBB),  (iii)  Performance  Monitoring  and  Evaluation  (PM&E),  and  (iv)
Performance  Management  System  (PMS).  The  RBM  Policy  is  currently  being  implemented
with PPBB taking the lead and piloting is taking place in a number of MDAs since early 2014.
It is expected that by 2019 all MDAs will be PPBB compliant.

The  Government  recognizes  the  importance  of  Strategic  Planning  as  the  foundation  on
which other pillars are developed and is committed to  adopt a Strategic Planning Policy to
engage all MDAs in the strategic planning process as part of their mandate.

Strategic  planning  is  an  organizational  management  activity  that  is  used  to  set  priorities,
focus  energy  and  resources,  strengthen  operations,  ensure  that  employees  and  other
stakeholders  are  working  toward  common  goals,  establish  agreement  around  intended
outcomes/results,  and  assess  and  adjust  the  organization's  direction  in  response  to  a
changing  environment.  It  is  a  disciplined  effort  that  produces  fundamental  decisions  and
actions that shape and guide what an organization is, who it serves, what it does, and why it
does it, with a focus on the future. Effective strategic planning articulates not only where an
organization is going and the actions needed to make progress, but also how it will know if it
is successful

A strategic plan is a document used to communicate with the organization the organizations
goals,  the  actions  needed  to  achieve  those  goals  and  all  of  the  other  critical  elements
developed during the planning exercise for the medium term (five year period).

The  Strategic  Plan  focuses  on  strategic  outcomes  oriented  goals  for  the  institution  as  a
whole,  and  objectives  for  each  of  its  main  service  delivery  areas  aligned  to  its  budget
programmes and, where relevant, also to its budget sub-programmes.

Strategic  planning  for  public  organizations  is  based  on  the  premise  that  leaders  must  be
effective strategists if their organizations are to fulfill their missions, meet their mandates,
and  satisfy  their  constituents  in  the  years  ahead.  Effective  strategies  are  needed  to  cope
with  changed  and  changing  circumstances,  and  leaders  need  to  develop  a  coherent  and
defensible context for their decisions.

The Department of Economic Planning is responsible to oversee the implementation of the
Strategic  Planning  pillar  within  the  RBM  Framework  hence  it  is  custodian  to  the  Strategic
Planning Policy.

This Policy on Strategic Planning sets out the objectives to be achieved by institutionalising
Strategic  Planning  in  MDAs.  It  provides  guidance  to  MDAs  on  the  strategic  planning
framework,  methodology  and  template  of  linking  resources,  activities  and  results,  and
provides  a  mechanism  for  institutionalising  this  methodology.  First,  the  objectives  of  this
policy  are  described,  then  the  institutional  arrangement  required  for  the  development  of
Strategic Planning is provided, and finally, the approach for implementing Strategic Planning
in MDAs is presented.

2.  Policy Objectives

Strategic planning is a comprehensive and systematic management tool designed to address
the  shortcomings  where  alignment  and  integration  with  other  pillars  are  concerned  as  it
helps define the strategy, system, organizational arrangements, methodologies,  processes,
guidelines, and procedures, as well as conducting and updating national, sector and agency
plans within the critical timeframe.

Strategic  planning  helps  organizations  assess  the  current  environment,  anticipate  and
respond  appropriately  to  changes  in  the  environment,  envision  the  future,  increase
effectiveness, develop commitment to the organization’s mission and achieve consensus on
strategies  and  objectives  for  achieving  that  mission.  The  primary  focus  should  be  on  the
outputs the institution produces, and the outcomes and impacts it seeks to achieve.

Strategic planning offers at least five compelling reasons for its use

  Forces a look into the future and therefore provides an opportunity to influence the

future, or assume a proactive attitude.

  Provides  better  awareness  of  needs  and  of  the  facilities  related  issues  and

environment.

  Helps define the overall mission of the organization and focuses on the objectives.
  Provides a sense of direction, continuity, and effective staffing and leadership.
  Plugs everyone into the system and provides standards of accountability for people,



programs, and allocated resources.
In summary, strategic planning is the key to helping us collectively and cooperatively
gain control of the future and the destiny of our organization.

3.  Institutional Arrangement

The  success  of  the  Strategic  Planning  requires  the  establishment  of  clear,  effective  and
efficient institutional arrangements. Although the effective, on-going implementation of the
Policy on Strategic Planning is primarily the responsibility of Principal Secretaries and Chief
Executive  Officers  (of  Agencies),  the  Government  shall  create  the  following  institutional
arrangement as an integral part of enabling the Strategic Planning framework:

  The  Department  of  Economic  Planning  within  the  Ministry  of  Finance,  Trade  and
Economic  Planning  to  act  as  the  Coordinating  Unit  and  provide  support  to  the
MDA’s;

  Strategic Planning Focal Persons in institutions for coordinating Strategic Plans

elaboration and implementation in MDAs.

ANNEX 1: Government of Seychelles Strategic Planning Template

This  template  proposes  a  common  template  for  designing  strategic  plans  by  sectors  in
Seychelles.

Strategic planning is understood as a management tool to help make strategic choices for
the key goals a sector intends to achieve. A sector is understood as the sum of ministries, in
charge of policy making and the respective agencies, in charge of policy-implementation.

The strategic plan is intended to inform policymaking and implementation over the next five
years.  All  strategic  plans  by  the  sector  are  expected  to  be  aligned  with  the  Seychelles
National  Development  Plan  and  to  be  reviewed  so  as  to  inform  the  preparation  of  the
National Development Plan.

The template proposes a common outline for the sector strategic plans as well as guidance
on the process, both crucial elements to an effective strategic plan. The idea is to keep the
strategic plan as brief as possible, no more than 20 pages total, to ensure focus.

Introduction

II.

Foundational sector analysis

This  section  provides  a  rigorous  analysis  of  the  sector,  focusing  on  the  current  state,
progress made and challenges. Different type of analysis can be used. One common option
is to do a SWOT analysis of the sector, reviewing strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and
threats. Independent of the methodology chosen, it is important that the analysis is candid,
precise and realistic and anchored in the current budget context, and based not only on the
view of the sector staff but also its beneficiaries. Please note that the analysis would need to
include  an  economic  analysis  and  be  data-driven,  including  past  trends,  expected  future
trends, and benchmarking).

1.  Overview of the sector, including core subsectors, embedding the sector within the
broader  country  context  (including,  if  possible,  global  benchmarking  with  same
sectors from other countries);

2.  Strengths (what are the key strengths of the sector, e.g. progress made in key areas-

e.g. access to primary school);

3.  Weaknesses  (what  are  the  key  weaknesses  of  the  sector,  e.g.  quality  of  service-

delivery, teacher training, fragmentation/duplication in sector etc.);

4.  Opportunities (both internal and external to sector and to country, e.g.  new health

policy, new markets) and

5.  Threats  (both  internal  and  external  to  sector  and  country,  e.g.  increase  of  non-
communicable diseases, stronger competitors in core tourism target market, climate
changes)

III.

Vision and mission

This section sets out the vision for the sector and the mission of the sector. The vision is a
statement  of  the  long-term  goals  of  the  sector,  and  is  often  aspirational  and  unifying  in
nature. The mission presents the principles and values of the sector.

1.  Vision (Where does the sector see itself in the medium- and long term? Where does
the sector want to go, what are its priority areas and impact in the long run?); and

2.  Mission  (sector  mandate,  including  ministerial  and  agency  mandates,  (formal-
Organizations  and  functions  of  Government,  Act/informal  –  sector/agency  policy),
description of thematic focus (updated if mandate has changed), key stakeholders);
and

3.  Value (organizational values and standards- e.g. efficiency, transparency, ethics and

when available service-standards);

IV.

Sector objectives

This  section  spells  out  the  core  objectives  that  the  sector  intends  to  achieve.  These
objectives  are  the  medium-term  objectives  for  the  duration  of  the  plan.  They  can  be
expressed  in terms of the  overall  impact  and the  outcomes  the  sector  intends to achieve.
The  objectives  to  be  sector-wide,  thus  encompassing  different  objectives,  but  need  to  be
focused.  In  the  ideal  case,  there  is  one  overarching  objective  in  terms  of  the  impact  the
sector  wants  to  achieve  and  a  limited  number  of  outcomes  to  support  this  overarching
objective.  The  objectives  can  be  aligned  with  international  objectives,  but  it  is  important
that  the  objectives  are  focusing  on  the  national  and  sector  objectives  aligned  with  the
Seychellois priorities and needs.

1.  Sector  objectives  (what  are  the  most  important  objectives  for  the  sector  for  the
duration  of  the  plan,  is  there  one  single  unifying  objective?  What  are  the
assumptions for these objections?);

2.  Impact;  (what  is  the  main  impact  the  sector  intends  to  achieve  (  e.g.  educated

citizens);

3.  Key  Outcomes  (e.g.  improved  learning  outcomes  in  secondary  school-  the  fewer

outcomes the better)

the

projects) This section is crucial and one of the most important parts of the SP);

foundational

2.  Policies  (What  are  the  core  sector  policies,  including  those  to  which  the  sector

3.  Programs (overview of core sector programs in terms of objectives, content, budget

VI.

Structure

This section sets out the ministerial and agency structure in the sector to organize the sector
in such a way that it can most effectively achieve the above mentioned objectives.

1.  Sector organization, with clear roles and responsibilities at all levels (sector structure
and institutional set-up across the sector and within each respective institution, eg.
ministry, agencies, authorities etc. and organigram within institution);

2.  Implementation arrangements for the strategic plan, including use of results-based

management tools, e.g. PPBB, PM&E, PMS);

3.  Staffing: current staffing and projected staffing needs, skills mix and talent attraction
and  retention,  staying  within  the  establishment  list;  and  capacity-strengthening
needs and mechanisms in the sector;

4.  Partnerships (national and international, public and private)

VII.

Budget

This section presents the budget available for the duration of the strategic plans. The budget
needs to be aligned with the MTEF and the PPBB estimates. It is crucial that all objectives,
policies, programs and projects are closely aligned with the fiscal ceiling for the sector, so as
to ensure the strategic plan is a realistic and credible yardstick for the sector’s future and
can be effectively used as a management tool, instead of just bringing a vision to the sector.
Setting  realistic  objectives,  outcomes  and targets  will  thus  be  important  to  ensure  budget
alignment. Detailed tables would be added in the annex.

1.  Current expenditure trends;
2.  Alignment with PPBB (latest sector PPBB statement and projections)
3.  Sector public investment plan; (latest PIP and projections); and
4.  Budget  estimates  for  the  duration  of  the  plan  (MTEF  and  other  estimates  for  the

outer years of the SP)

5.  Staffing Budget

VII Performance monitoring and evaluation

This section includes the sectoral M&E framework that will be adopted to ensure adequate
monitoring and evaluation of the strategic plan, aligned with the overall performance M&E
policy. It also includes a results framework with the core indicators from inputs  to impact.
The  results  framework  needs  to  have  baselines  with  identified  data  sources,  measurable
targets and the institutional set-up to ensure the sector results are measured regularly.

VIII.

Change management

This section sets out the core elements of the necessary change management to make sure
the strategic plan can be implemented as planned. It includes a review of the positions of
key  stakeholders,  a  communications  strategy  to  help  disseminate  the  plan  internally  and
externally with carefully targeted messaging in line with the different audiences, and a risk
management  strategy  that  identifies  potential  risks  ex  ante  and  ex  post  and  proposes

1.  Stakeholder  mapping  (who  are  the  key  stakeholders  in  the  sector,  e.g.  students,

teachers, parents etc.) and their roles and views towards the SP objectives);

each target groups);

3.  Risk management (risk identification and management).

Conclusion:

any  further  implementation  arrangements,  and  an  action  plan  if  needed.  It  would  also
propose dissemination mechanisms.
