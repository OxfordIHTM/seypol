# **Executive Summary**

The National School Health Policy 2017 has been developed with the assistance and contributions from a number of professionals in the health and education sectors. The Policy seeks to create a national school system where all stakeholders (parents, teachers, students and members of the community who interact in any way with schools) are safe and healthy and able to enjoy the highest possible level of holistic health.

The stated mission of the Policy is to have key partners and stakeholders are actively engaged in the processes, procedures and programmes that protect and promote holistic health. The ultimate goal is to protect and promote the physical and mental well-being of all persons attending and / or working in schools, especially the children, through a holistic and comprehensive school health programme.

There are three objectives:

a) Create and foster sustainable and conducive school environments that protect and promote the physical and mental well-being of all persons attending and / or working in schools, with special focus on key issues of concern.

b) Facilitate and improve access and availability of preventive and promotive health services to the children.

c) Educate parents, teachers, school children and the community about key health concerns

The Policy focuses on the physical environment of schools, seeking to make them hygienic, safe and secure for all, especially the school children, with proposed measures to ensure that the highest level of cleanliness is maintained throughout. The second focus is on the Integrated School Health Services, with its screening programmes, assessments and interventions, with policy guidelines to improve on existing activities and engage all key school personnel in the processes. The last focus is on visibility of the Integrated School Health Services, with policy measures to help all stakeholders, especially school personnel, parents and the community to be aware of the programmes delivered at school, and enlist their support.

The Policy also gives an overview of the coordinating mechanism and the monitoring and evaluation structures to use for efficient and effective delivery of services.

## **Geography and demographics**

1. The Seychelles Archipelago, consisting of 155 islands, is located in the western Indian Ocean, four degrees south of the Equator and approximately 1,600 km off the coast of East Africa. The islands are scattered over some 500,000km2 of sea. While the land mass is approximately 455.3 km2, with about 491 km of coastline, the Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) is approximately 1.374 million km2. The country is quite isolated geographically and the islands are highly dispersed.
2. The multi-ethnic population of 94, 000 lives mainly on the four largest granitic islands of Mahé, Praslin, La Digue and Silhouette. Eighty percent of the population, however, lives on the main island of Mahé which accounts for approximately 35% of the total land area and is the economic hub of the country. The capital, Victoria - also the major port - and the international airport are also located on Mahé. Most of the other smaller islands have no permanent population. The national languages are Kreol (a French based creole or *patois*), English and French. The population is also predominantly Christian.

## **Health and Education Systems**

1. The right to health is guaranteed in the ***Seychelles Constitution*** ***Chapter Three: Seychellois Charter of Fundamental Human Rights and Freedoms*** in Article 29 which reads in part: “*The state recognises the right of every citizen to protection of health and to the enjoyment of the highest attainable standard of physical and mental health...*”[[1]](#footnote-1) Furthermore, the Constitution in the same Article (29. a) the State engages itself to undertake “*to take steps to provide for free primary health care In State institutions for all its citizens.*” Other promised actions, amongst others, include taking “*appropriate measures to prevent, treat and control epidemic, endemic and other diseases*;” to reduce infant mortality, and “*to promote individual responsibility in health matters*.”
2. Thus, the main provider of clinical and rehabilitative services is the State. The existing health facilities consist of a central referral hospital, 3 cottage hospitals (1 rehabilitative hospital, 1 mental hospital, 1 youth health centre) and 17 decentralized district health centres located throughout the country. These are further complemented by the non-public health facilities, which cover, amongst others, services such as outpatient care services, emergency and trauma care, mother and child services, as well as laboratory and investigative services. There are some specialised services offered at the central referral hospital.
3. The Ministry of Health provides a comprehensive Maternal and Child Health Programme (MCHP) an all-inclusive and free antenatal and post-natal care, through maternal and child health programmes in the central referral hospital and the district health centres, on the four main islands with permanent populations. All births occur in healthcare facilities, assisted by health professionals. The School Health Programme takes over from the MCHP and aims to provide ‘*comprehensive, preventive and promotive health services to school going children between the ages of 4 to 17 years*.’[[2]](#footnote-2) Services include health assessments, including basic screening procedures, are carried out at four stages in the children’s school life: at the end of crèche, primary year 4, secondary 1 and secondary 4. There is presently one school health nurse deployed in each primary and secondary state school. They seek parental consent at each stage of their interventions and they also provide counselling and social support, as necessary.
4. The right to education is also guaranteed in the Constitution in Chapter Three in Article 33 which “*recognises the right of every citizen to education.*”[[3]](#footnote-3) The key features are free compulsory education for 10 years conducted in a holistic and comprehensive manner, with curricula catering to all types of learning styles and abilities. The Education Act (2004) is the principal law that guides education provision in primary and secondary schools. The Ministry of Education’s vision for education is “*to empower future citizens to contribute positively to the process of building a sustainable, peaceful and harmonious Seychelles society, whilst safeguarding and promoting our unique traditions and cultural values*.”
5. Presently, the education system comprises of state (27 primary and 13 secondary). There are 11 professional centres and one university. The focus of the ***National School Health Policy 2017*** will be on primary and secondary state and private schools.

# **School Health Policy**

## **Vision**

*A school system where parents, teachers, students and other stakeholders are safe and healthy and able to enjoy the highest possible level of holistic health*

## **Mission**

*With key partners and stakeholders, the* ***National School Health Policy 2017*** *seeks**to create, foster and maintain a school environment where parents, teachers, students and other stakeholders are actively engaged in the processes, procedures and programmes that protect and promote holistic health.*

## **Goal**

The aim of the ***National School Health Policy 2017*** is to protect and promote the physical and mental well-being of all persons attending and / or working in schools, especially the children, through a holistic and comprehensive school health programme.

## **Objectives**

The specific objectives are to:

1. Create and foster sustainable and conducive school environments that protect and promote the physical and mental well-being of all persons attending and / or working in schools, with special focus on key issues of concern.
2. Facilitate and improve access and availability of preventive and promotive health services to the children.
3. Educate parents, teachers, school children and the community about key health concerns.

# **Policy guidelines**

## **Principles**

1. The policy rests on principles that will help to implement it with all partners. These are as follows:
2. *Holistic approach to health*
3. *A whole-school approach*
4. *Targeted and timely interventions*, and
5. *Meaningful engagement of parents, students, teachers, the community and other stakeholders*.

### **Holistic approach to health**

1. The Policy recognises that health is an interaction of physical, mental, emotional and social factors. Thus, its approach is improve social determinants of health and to focus on all aspects of health; hence, the need for the school health nurse to work closely with the school counsellor, social worker and other actors and school staff to ensure that health is seen and treated in its widest sense possible.
2. The Policy recognises and seeks to align itself with the international commitments made by the Government through the ratification of human rights instruments, and regional economic groups’ protocols, as well as the Ministry of Health’s own policies, and those of the various sectoral national policies from different ministries and agencies. These international and national obligations are guidelines for the Policy. These are listed in Annex 4. It is essential that proposed guidelines are coordinated and that each national policy finds its place in the overall picture of ensuring that health in the schools is seen, treated and implemented in a holistic manner.

### **Whole school approach**

1. The School Health Programme will also ensure that it is developed and implemented in a comprehensive manner that takes into account the variety of needs of the various partners and stakeholders. Piece-meal programmes do not fully cover the needs of its intended beneficiaries and end up responding to only some of the needs identified and expressed. Programmes need to consider issues such as the culture of the Seychellois, the religious particularities and sensitivities of the community to ensure success in its implementation.

### **Targeted and timely interventions**

1. A well-designed School Health Programme is one that is mostly concerned with early identification of holistic health issues and thus, is preventive in nature. When there are rehabilitative interventions to undertake, these are done where and when necessary and as needed, to avoid a one size fits all approach. Thus, interventions need to be based on identified needs and evidence-informed decisions and they are just enough of what the community needs, rather than an overkill.

### **Meaningful engagement of all partners**

1. Lastly, the well-designed School Health Programme seeks to meaningfully engage all stakeholders in the whole process, from conception to design, from implementation to monitoring and evaluation. It is important to especially engage the parents, teachers, students and the community in the complete process and procedures used to develop programmes and interventions. They must own the programmes to be fully involved.
2. Multilateral and bilateral partners are also essential components of the school health programmes, as they offer donations and grants to improve the physical structures of schools and to support teaching and learning with educational materials. These are greatly appreciated. It is, however, important that the processes involved in these assistance programmes are of a participatory nature to allow school personnel and the Ministry of Health to guide in the planning, selection, and implementation of these programmes. This will ensure more effective use of the donations or grants.
3. The actions and interventions in the School Health Programme must be inclusive and “leave no-one behind.” All students, staff of the school, parents, the community and other stakeholders need to be included in programmes. The Programme will ensure that students with physical impairments or mental health issues will be included as fully involved participants. Programmes will be developed together with them and their inputs are considered primordial to the whole process.

## **The proposed policy guidelines**

**Objective One**: *Create and foster a sustainable school environment that protects and promotes the physical, social and psychological well-being of all persons attending and / or working in school*s, with *special focus on the school children*

1. The policy focuses on the following issues: personal hygiene, cleanliness of the school compound, availability and access to facilities for play and exercise, ensuring that children and staff are protected from vehicles on their way and from school, improving the eating experiences of the children and promoting nutrition, proactive and prosocial measures to maintain optimal mental well-being of all persons in the school, especially school children.

***Therefore, it shall be the policy of the Ministry of Health to ensure that:***

1. The School Health Nurse, the School Dental Hygienist, the school-based counsellor and the Personal, Social and Civic Education Teachers, social workers, amongst others, will work together to develop a programme in all schools on personal hygiene, which includes dental hygiene, bathing and cleaning.
2. The Ministry of Education maintains all parts of the school in the highest possible level of cleanliness.
3. All schools shall endeavour to find, build and organise facilities for children to play securely and safely and to engage in physical activities, under the adequate supervision of staff.
4. All schools shall endeavour to build, improve and maintain the physical and aesthetic aspects of the dining halls, tuck shops and other facilities where food is sold and served to make the eating experience of children more pleasant.
5. The overall experience of the children and staff shall be an extension of what the children have been taught in class, incorporating both the theoretical and practical aspects of health.
6. All schools shall review their compounds to eliminate all physical health hazards such as, amongst others, open gutters, broken handrails, protruding metal bars left over from construction of the schools, sidewalks, physical conditions of playgrounds and other facilities.
7. To prevent injury, all schools shall seek to create ergonomic and sufficient furniture and equipment for children to use.
8. All classrooms shall have adequate ventilation for air circulation and freshness at all times so that teachers and staff can enjoy the highest quality of air.
9. Students and staff have access to potable water through water fountains which are maintained in hygienic and pristine conditions.

**Objective Two**: *Facilitate and improve access and availability of preventive and promotive health services to school-age children*

1. The Policy focuses on the development and delivery of a holistic and comprehensive package of school health services.

***Therefore, it shall be the policy of the Ministry of Health to ensure that:***

1. The complete package of the Integrated School Health Services (ISHS) shall include the following activities and services:
2. Screening for a variety of illnesses and conditions (Annex 5.)
3. Assessment based on developmental milestones
4. Immunisation programme of the Ministry of Health
5. Dental hygiene

**Support to the ISHS**

Schools shall be encouraged to:

1. Initiate health promoting programmes, as and when necessary, to complement the ISHS;
2. Increase the number of assessments, e.g., BMI, done by school personnel, such as the P.E. Teachers, counsellors, PSCE Teachers and others.
3. There shall be one school health nurse for each school, and where this is not possible, have at least one nurse for a maximum of two schools.
4. Where the school health nurse has to work in two schools, he or she shall be supported by a Health Promotion Officer whose duties shall include developing awareness and behaviour change programmes for the schools he or she serves.
5. In collaboration with the Ministry of Education, shall periodically review the package of school health services and together, they will develop an adapted and up-to-date package of Integrated School Health Services (ISHS).
6. The ISHS is promoted to parents, students, teachers and the community to increase uptake and improve level of personal responsibility for health and health-seeking behaviour in children.
7. Funding is sought and secured to implement the ISHS.
8. The Ministry of Education ensures that there are appropriate facilities (offices for administrative duties, one-to-one counselling and storage, screening rooms, furniture, adequate lighting and ventilation, toilets and washing facilities, amongst others), are in place in schools for the implementation of the ISHS.
9. The Ministry of Education shall phase out within two years all non-school-based delivery of refreshments and meals to ensure better control of nutrition at school level and to set standards of nutrition within school premises.
10. The Ministry of Education and the Ministry of Health shall maintain a distance between mobile fast food vendors and schools, and promote their own facilities for lunch and snacks.

**Objective Three**: *Educate parents/guardians/care-givers, teachers, school children and the community about key health issues and how to address these effectively*

1. The Policy focuses on health promotion programmes for schools, which include parents, teachers, school children and the community, as well as key partners.

***Therefore, it shall be the policy of the Ministry of Health to ensure that:***

1. Schools shall use their existing multisectoral and multidisciplinary committees, including students and parents, at school level to conceptualise, design, implement and monitor awareness and health behaviour change programmes for parents, teachers, students and the community to address key issues of physical and mental health, as and when necessary.
2. A schedule, integrated with the school’s strategic plan, is devised for each school for the implementation of the awareness and health behaviour change programmes annually.
3. Awareness and health behaviour change programmes are implemented on a monthly basis.
4. Awareness and health behaviour change programmes are monitored and evaluated to draw lessons from them.

# **Monitoring and Evaluation**

1. The Ministry of Education already has a Coordinating Committee which already oversees the School Health Programme. The committee can serve as the M&E Committee needed at national level to oversee the ISHS (see Figure 1).
2. Mechanisms must be established for monitoring and evaluating the school health policy. Reporting, monitoring and evaluation of the ISHS must be integrated with existing school and ministry levels of health information systems as well as interfacing with the education management information system.
3. Monitoring and evaluation will need to focus on:
4. Coverage of services
5. Types of services offered
6. Demographics of coverage, disaggregated by age, sex and districts
7. The impact of the service on the health of learners and on access to schooling, retention and achievement of learners
8. Quality of services
9. Sustainability of school health services in all districts
10. The M&E framework must also propose a set of national indicators that will be used to monitor the ISHS.
