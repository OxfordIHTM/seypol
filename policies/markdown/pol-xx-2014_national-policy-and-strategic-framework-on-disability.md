# Seychelles’ commitment to the development of its people has always been paramount. The development of this National Policy and Strategic Framework on Disability is evidence of this commitment. Through its development process it has helped to open the eyes of many on the issues that relate to disability and in some ways also helped to change mindsets. For these reason those who have collaborated should be proud.

# This policy and strategic framework is modern and forward looking setting important parameters for the implementation of Seychelles’ commitments towards persons with disabilities and to human rights in general. The challenge is to translate the optimism embodied in the document into realism. A lot is already being done. It is hoped that this comprehensive document will allow for efforts to be better coordinated and increase participation where necessary.

# BACKGROUND

# 1.1 Introduction

Seychelles became a party to the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) in 2009. This has helped to push disability issues high on the national agenda with the convention guiding the way.

This first National Policy on Disability of the Government of Seychelles is aimed at addressing the barriers that have been preventing persons with disabilities from reaching their full potential. It recognises that whilst a lot has been done to give persons with disabilities opportunities a lot still remains to be done to meet the aspirations of the Seychellois disabled population and attain full compliance to the CRPD.

The policy aims to integrate an awareness of disability issues into policies, practices and procedures in all areas of service provision and programme development within the scope of the Millennium Development Goals (MDG) and the CRPD. It also gives some guidance as to how this might be achieved and links to other polices and strategies where appropriate. This policy supports a social model of disability where disability is defined as the loss or limitation of opportunity to take part in society on an equal level due to barriers in society and/or the environment.

For this policy to be successful all sectors will need to pledge their commitment to the mainstreaming of disability within policy agendas. It is hoped that the adoption of this policy will facilitate the empowerment of persons with disabilities to claim their rights as citizens and change public mindset about disability.

This policy document has been compiled through wide consultations with all relevant stakeholders to ensure its ownership from the start.

It is structured in the following manner

1. The programme area
2. The articles of the CRPD which are relevant to the programme area
3. Key Issues( identified in consultation)
4. Policy commitment
5. Policy objective
6. Strategic actions

# 1.2 Policy and legal framework

The Constitution of the Republic of Seychelles (1993) is the country’s supreme law and all other laws and policies are subject to it. Article 5 states that ‘any other law found to be inconsistent with this Constitution is, to the extent of the inconsistency, void.’

The Constitution guarantees the right to equal protection before the law for “*every person without discrimination on any ground”*. The Seychellois Charter of Fundamental Human Rights and Freedoms (Chapter 3 of the Constitution), which incorporates many of the principles of international human rights instruments, guarantees 25 rights. Article 36 of this chapter recognises the rights of persons with disabilities in the following way;

*The State recognises the right of the aged and the disabled to special protection and with a view to ensuring the effective exercise of this right undertakes*

*To make reasonable provision for improving the quality of life of and for the welfare and maintenance of the aged and disabled*

*To promote programmes specifically aimed at achieving the greatest possible development of the disabled*

The Policy is aligned to the principles enshrined in the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities and its optional protocol. The Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) and its Optional Protocol provide the normative framework for its members to address the rights of persons with disabilities and ensure that they are included in all development efforts. The Convention has an explicit, socio-economic development dimension that recognizes the importance of development to the full realization of the rights in the CRPD.

# JUSTIFICATION

The 2010 NSB census recorded a population of approximately 2169 persons with disabilities representing 2.5 % of the population of the Seychelles. They frequently face barriers to participate effectively in all aspect of society. The barriers are spread across sectors and include discrimination in work, accessing quality education, transportation etc.

Given that persons with disabilities are more likely to live in poverty than their non-disabled peers, their inclusion in all development activities is essential, if internationally agreed development goals, including the Millennium Development Goals (MDGs) are to be achieved in an equitable manner.

The Seychelles has shown its commitment in addressing the barriers that people with disabilities face through the ratification of the Convention (CRPD). Its principles and provisions now need to be translated into the everyday planning and implementation processes of the country and this policy is developed to accomplish just that**.**

The policy responds to the request of people with disabilities themselves, who have been calling for a new approach to disability, grounded on the conviction that all persons should be equally valued: an approach focusing on the need to adapt society so as to enable (rather than disable) people with disabilities.

# POLICY FRAMEWORK – VISION, GOAL, OBJECTIVES

## 3.1 Vision

A nation where persons with disabilities enjoy full and equal participation in all aspects of life

## 3.2 Mission

To promote an inclusive society for persons with disabilities

## 3.3 Policy Goal

To provide guidelines for the inclusion of persons with disabilities in all national programmes

## 3.4 Specific Policy Objectives

* Enact appropriate legislations to domesticate the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities.
* Promote the participation of women and men with disabilities in decision-making.
* Ensure the mainstreaming of disability rights into all policies, structures, systems, programmes and activities in order for them to contribute effectively to national development.
* Improve service delivery for persons with disabilities

1. **GUIDING PRINCIPLES**

In order to address the challenges that persons with disabilities face there are a number of interlinked principles that must always be at the centre of all attempts to respond to these challenges

***Human rights based approach:*** All measures to advance the rights of persons with disabilities shall not affect the human rights and dignity of persons especially persons with disabilities.

***Harmonisation:*** Harmonising national legislation, policies, strategies and programmes with the Convention (CRPD) or any other relevant human rights instrument

***Empowerment:*** A human rights-based approach to disability aims to empower persons with disabilities to make their own choices, advocate for themselves, and exercise control over their lives.

***Inclusion and participation:*** Particular attention must be paid to the empowerment of persons with disabilities so that they can claim their own rights.

***Sustainable development:*** Mainstreaming disability is an integral part of relevant strategies of sustainable development

***Multi-sector approach:*** All stakeholders including State and NSAs across all sectors need to work together as partners in a co-ordinated way to achieve equality for persons with disabilities.

***Indivisibility*:** The promotion of one right may not justify the violation of another right. Consequently, there is no hierarchy among rights. The realization of one right often depends, wholly or in part, upon the realization of others.

***Inter-dependence and Inter-relatedness*:** The promotion of specific human rights must be part of a comprehensive effort to realize human rights in a holistic way.

***Poverty eradication:*** The goal of halving poverty (MDG 1) cannot be met if the exclusion of persons with disabilities is not tackled

.

***Equalisation of opportunities****:* Equitable progress in the agreed global development agenda cannot be achieved without the inclusion of persons with disabilities. If they are not included, progress in development will further their marginalization. The state should facilitate an environment where persons with disabilities have equal opportunities to the non disabled

# POLICY PROGRAMME AREAS, OBJECTIVES AND STRATEGIC ACTIONS

## 5.1 Disability and legal rights

Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Article 4, 5

**States shall:**

* Endeavour to adopt all appropriate legislative measures for the implementation of the rights recognised in the CRPD
* Review, amend and or repeal all legislation that discriminate against persons with disabilities

**Key issues**

The Seychelles Constitution guarantees the right to equal protection before the law for “*every person without discrimination on any ground”*. The rights of disabled people are provided for under the Seychellois Charter of Fundamental Rights and Freedoms, chapter III of the Constitution. Article 36 of this chapter recognises the rights of persons with disabilities in the following way;

*The State recognises the right of the aged and the disabled to special protection and with a view to ensuring the effective exercise of this right undertakes:*

*To make reasonable provision for improving the quality of life of and for the welfare and maintenance of the aged and disabled*

*To promote programmes specifically aimed at achieving the greatest possible development of the disabled*

There are no laws that overtly discriminate against persons with disabilities. However the lack of certain specific provisions make it that certain rights are not upheld. Gap is also otherwise evident in the mainstreaming of rights of persons with disabilities in the legislation drafting process on certain topics that may have an impact on persons with disabilities; the harmonisation of laws and domestication of international treaties and Protocols ratified by Seychelles and the enforcement and implementation of the law. Laws that at present do make reference to persons with disabilities for example the Employment Act, fall short of the requirements of the CRPD. There is a need to go further and explicitly prohibit humiliation of and discrimination against people with disability, to provide them economic, social security, and to formulate laws that provide them equal opportunity and provide them with information on existing laws. Without the relevant laws in place the rights enshrined in the CRPD will not be able to be implemented in a fully effective manner.

**Policy Commitment**:

The Government shall ensure that all persons are equal before and under the law

**Policy Objective:**

To ensure a legal environment that offers persons with disabilities the same opportunities as the non disabled

**To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:**

* Assess the need for a specific legislative framework in order to achieve the objectives of full participation and equality of the people with disability.
* Review, amend and or repeal all discriminatory laws as necessary.
* Amend the National Council for Disabled Act 1994 to align it with the role it is playing and the role it aspires to play in promoting the rights of persons with disabilities
* Build capacity of the justice system to systematically and routinely protect vulnerable witnesses and deliver justice sensitively, fairly and swiftly.
* Ensure courts are accessible to persons with disabilities
* review terms used to refer to persons with disabilities in laws and regulations
* Align terms, definitions and classification to international norms and practices

## 5.2 Women with disabilities

Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Article 6

**States shall;**

* Take measures to ensure the full and equal enjoyment by women with disabilities of all human rights and fundamental freedoms
* Take all appropriate measures to ensure the full development, advancement and empowerment of women with disabilities

**Key issues**

Women and girls with disabilities experience double discrimination, due to gender and disability (MDG 3). One of the most important areas of discrimination is reproductive health. Women with disabilities have less access to general information on sexual and reproductive health, to family planning and to prenatal, labour, delivery and post natal services. Not only does information need to be made available in the appropriate format but a general change in mindset is necessary so that women with disabilities can be seen as able to make decisions in regards to their health and family. Women with disabilities are also at higher risk of gender-based violence, sexual abuse, neglect, maltreatment and exploitation.

**Policy Commitment:**

The Government shall promote an equitable society through gender-balanced programmes which integrate persons with disabilities.

**Policy Objective:**

To ensure that no one gender is left behind in efforts to ensure that persons with disabilities fully enjoy their rights

**To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:**

* To create an enabling environment to facilitate an increase in participation of women with disabilities in decision-making which concerns them
* Promote and build capacity of women and girls in civil society to become more active and participate in the movement and development of programmes and campaigns relating to disability.
* Organise training related to leadership development of women with disabilities
* Ensure that national gender policies extends to persons with disabilities

## 5.3 Children with disabilities

Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Article 7

**States shall;**

* Ensure the full enjoyment by children with disabilities of all human rights and fundamental freedoms on an equal basis with other children
* Ensure that the best interest of the child is the primary consideration in all actions concerning children
* Ensure that children with disabilities have the right to express their views freely on matters that concern them

**Key issues**

Children with disabilities and their families constantly experience barriers to the enjoyment of their basic human rights and to their inclusion in society. Their abilities are overlooked, their capacities are underestimated and their needs are given low priority. The daily reality for most children with a disability is that they are often condemned to a ’poor start in life’ and deprived of opportunities to develop to their full potential and to participate in society. They are routinely denied access to the same opportunities. However, the barriers they face are more frequently as a result of the environment in which they live than as a result of their impairment. As such a blind child would not benefit from the same quality of education as other children because mainstream schools are not equipped to cater for him or her. In addition a child with disabilities will not be able to access day care services because none at present is equipped to cater for children with disabilities.

Seychelles has been a signatory to the Convention on the Rights of the Child since 1990. The CRC also promotes rights of children with disabilities. Organisations that run programmes or offer services to children are guided by this convention and are required to ensure that the best interest of the child principle in all matters that relate to children.

In 2011 Seychelles adopted a National Framework for Early Childhood Care and Education. This framework has as its motto ‘winning for children: a shared commitment ’. It lays down the country’s commitment towards a winning start for all children. Whilst it does make reference to vulnerable children and mentions children with disabilities as a group under this label, its strategies do not make specific mention to children with disabilities and it is unclear whether specific issues that relate for example to accessibility to education and care for children with disabilities has been taken into account.

The inclusion of children with disabilities is a matter of social justice and an essential investment in the future of society. It should never be based on charity or goodwill but it should be an integral element of the expression and realization of universal human rights.

**Policy Commitment:**

The Government shall take necessary steps to promote the inclusion of children with disability within society

**Policy Objective:**

To ensure that children with disabilities enjoy the same opportunities as non disabled children

**To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:**

* Ensure that national policies and services that cater for children take into account issues faced by children with disabilities
* Provide for an effective remedy mechanism in case of violations of the rights of children with disabilities and ensure that these remedies are accessible to all children, families and caregivers.
* Make concerted efforts to ensure that the necessary resources are allocated to and for children with disabilities and their families.
* Conduct awareness-raising and educational campaigns for the public, as well as specific groups of professionals, with the aim of preventing and addressing the de facto discrimination of children with disabilities.
* Ensure that ECCE strategies integrate the needs of children with disabilities
* Ensure that the best interest of the child principle is maintained in matters that relate to all children
* Ensure that the right of children with disabilities to live with their family is upheld and that alternative placement is considered only as a last resort.

## 5.4 Disability and Accessibility

Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Article 9, Article 21

**States shall;**

* Ensure that persons with disabilities access on an equal basis with others, to the physical environment, to transportation, to information and communications technologies and systems and to other facilities and services provided to the public.

**Key issues**

Issues of accessibility are some of the biggest barriers faced by persons with disabilities in Seychelles. Access to services is not just about installing ramps and widening doorways for wheelchair users - it is about making services easier to use for all people with disabilities, including people who are blind, deaf or have a learning disability. People with disabilities in Seychelles cannot access public transport and most public buildings. Such situation persists though there has been much sensitisation about this issue. Children with disabilities often cannot continue in mainstream schools because they cannot access the classes due to physical impairments or cannot follow the curriculum due to the lack of appropriate communication technology and learning tools. In light of the principles promoted by the convention such situations are not acceptable. Access has a significant place in the process of equalization of opportunity in every area of society.

Whilst it is generally accepted that due consideration must be given to this fundamental right; at present there is no law that makes provision for all public buildings and services to be made accessible for persons with disabilities and therefore this right is often overlooked. Amendments to the Town and Country Act in the near future is expected to bring some positive change to the execution of this right but a lot more needs to be done to address issues of access that relates to information and communication. Access to information for persons with disabilities is poor across sectors. The national broadcasting agency rarely transmits in sign language except for special reportage about the hearing impaired. A culture of accessibility needs to be adopted overall to empower persons with disabilities and allow them to fully enjoy their rights as citizens.

**Policy Commitment:**

The Government shall remove administrative, procedural and physical barriers that prevent equal access to people with disabilities

**Policy Objective:**

To ensure that persons with disabilities have access to public services and information

**To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:**

* Ensure that the appropriate legal provisions are in place to guarantee that buildings and facilities open to the public are accessible to persons with disabilities
* Ensure that the planning authority consults with the relevant stakeholders especially persons with disabilities and their associations when taking decision in regards to public projects
* Invest in communications technologies for persons with disabilities especially children to facilitate access to education
* Invest in public transport suitable for persons with disability
* Provide training for stakeholders on accessibility issues facing persons with disabilities
* Provide in public buildings and other facilities open to the public signage in Braille and easy to read and understand formats.
* Develop policy to provide discount or waiver to customs tariff as well as tax while importing communication and technology equipment necessary for people with disability.
* Make public information available in different formats
* Make provisions for the national television station to transmit information in sign language and in caption
* Ensure that service providers make 'reasonable adjustments' to the way they deliver their services so that people with disabilities can use them.

## Disability and situations of risk and humanitarian emergencies

Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Article 11

**States shall;**

* Take all necessary measures to ensure the protection and safety of persons with disabilities in situations of risk, including situations of armed conflict, humanitarian emergencies and natural disasters

**Key issues**

Persons with disabilities are recognised as a particularly at risk population in times of humanitarian crisis. They are most likely to be trapped in their homes, receive inadequate medical care, and to be unable to access assistance programs due to the barriers that exist in the environment. In the past decade Seychelles has been affected by the tsunami and more recently heavy rains resulting in floods affecting the South East region. These situations of natural disaster revealed some of the limitations of local disaster response.

The Disaster Risk Management Division (DRDM) is the responsible agency for planning how relief efforts are to take place in situation of emergencies; ensuring disaster preparedness; however there are no specific policies targeting persons with disabilities and national preparedness activities fail to involve representation of organisations for persons with disabilities. Though the country is yet to face a situation of risk or humanitarian emergencies where persons with disabilities especially are greatly affected it is important that the country has a disaster and risk management policy and plan that extends to all people and especially that responds to different types of disabilities.

**Policy commitment**

The government shall recognise the specific needs of persons with disabilities in disaster risk reduction and management.

**Policy objective**

To ensure that the necessary framework to ensure the protection of persons with disabilities during times of risk and humanitarian emergencies are in place

**To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:**

* Review documents and programmes related to risk and disaster management to mainstream disability.
* Build capacity in addressing the needs of persons with disabilities in disaster risk management programmes.
* Ensure the participation of organisations of persons with disabilities and/or persons with disabilities themselves in the development and implementation of programmes related to situations of risk and emergencies

## 5.6 Disability and participation

Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Article 29, 30:

**States shall;**

* Ensure that persons with disabilities can effectively and fully participate in political and public life on an equal basis with others
* Promote an environment in which persons with disabilities can effectively and fully participate in the conduct of public affairs without discrimination and on an equal basis.
* Take appropriate measure to ensure that persons with disabilities take part on an equal basis with others in cultural life and sporting activities
* Enable persons with disabilities to have the opportunity to develop and utilise their creative, artistic and intellectual potential
* Ensure that persons with disabilities are entitled to recognition and support for their specific cultural and linguistic identity, including sing languages and deaf culture.

**Key issues**

Close consultation and involvement of persons with disabilities in any planning that concerns them and in political, cultural, sports and leisure activities is a right recognized in the CRPD, as well as one of its key principles. Thus, it is important, for the country to facilitate the participation of men, women and youth with all types of disabilities – physical, mental, intellectual and sensory - and their representative organizations in national development processes. Such involvement will facilitate better and more inclusive national participation and enhance the effectiveness, impact and sustainability of outcomes and outputs. Active engagement of persons with disabilities is also a way to build on local expertise and support empowerment strategies. At present people with disabilities are consulted in an adhoc manner and hence national programmes often miss out on the opportunity to be inclusive.

Sports and leisure activities bring a lot of benefit for persons with disabilities. Historically people with disabilities in Seychelles have done well in sports but there is a lack of continuity of such programmes. A national chapter of the Paralympics is being launched to offer scope for persons with disabilities to take part in high level sports and it is hoped that the current optimism can be maintained.

Some mechanism exists to facilitate the participation of persons with disabilities in the community; these include different associations for different groups of persons with disabilities. One which regroups all persons with disabilities is the District Support Groups which is present in all districts with the purpose of organising activities in the communities and nationally to allow persons with disabilities to participate in social and cultural life. However, some of these associations face many challenges in fulfilling their roles and therefore need to be strengthened to become platforms that are more active in promoting and advocating for the participation of persons with disabilities in all aspects of society especially in all decisions that concerns them.

**Policy Commitment:**

The Government shall provide appropriate support to ensure the participation of persons with disabilities in national development processes, sports, leisure and cultural life

**Policy Objective:**

To ensure the participation of persons with disabilities and their representative organisation in all aspects of mainstream society

**To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:**

* Facilitate the participation of persons with disabilities who may be marginalized in their own communities, such as women and children with disabilities, persons with mental or intellectual disabilities
* Provide the support, accommodation and capacity building that may be required to facilitate the participation of persons with all types of disabilities – physical, sensory, intellectual and mental- in consultation and decision-making processes
* Ensure that the appropriate structures and resources are available to allow persons with disabilities to participate in sports
* Choose venues for consultations that are accessible and easily reached and ensure that relevant information on the process of consultation is made available to persons with disabilities, including in alternative formats and languages.
* Strengthen the capacity of DPOs to lobby for the participation of persons with disabilities within mainstream society

## 5.7 Disability and Security of person

Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Articles 14-16

**States shall**

* Ensure that persons with disabilities enjoy the right to liberty and security of persons on an equal basis with others. These include taking measures to ensure freedom from torture or cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment and freedom from exploitation violence and abuse

**Key issues**

The Seychelles faces many socials scourges amongst which is an increase in the abuse of drugs and alcohol and an increase in crimes.The President of the Republic in 2011, announced the initiation of a “social renaissance” campaign, beginning with national dialogues to engage the population in efforts to find solutions to the problems that are affecting our society.This campaign which is ongoing has resulted in an action plan which attempts to provide solutions for the social ills that are affecting vulnerable groups.

Crimes and social problems affect the security of all persons. Persons with disabilities are particularly vulnerable because they are often less able to defend themselves. Violence and hostility can be a daily experience for them - in the street, on public transport, at work and at home. Many disabled people accept it as a part of everyday life. Persons with disabilities (including those who have not experienced such behaviours directly) and their families are all too often forced to go to extraordinary lengths to avoid it, thereby limiting their own lives.

**Policy Commitment:**

The Government is committed to make the right of persons with disabilities to safety and security an everyday reality

**Policy Objective:**

To prevent all forms of situations where persons with disabilities feel unsafe

**To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:**

* Provide better protection against sexual harassment in all spheres
* Ensure safety and security in public spaces
* Ensure that services for victims of violence and exploitation are able to cater for persons with disabilities
* Ensure that disability is integrated within the Social Renaissance Action Plan and any other plan that address security and safety of the people

## 5.8 Disability and Health

Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Article 23, Article 25

**States shall;**

* Take all appropriate measures to ensure access for persons with disabilities to health services that are gender sensitive, including health related rehabilitation.
* take effective and appropriate measures to ensure the rights of persons with disabilities to decide freely and responsibly on the number and spacing of their children and to have access to appropriate information, reproductive and family planning education

**Key issues**

The Seychelles government prides itself for its ‘health for all’ policy under which primary health services are provided free for all its citizens. However persons with disabilities face many difficulties in accessing health services. Barriers range from accessibility to infrastructures where services are found, information in inappropriate formats and negative attitudes that perceive persons with disabilities as unable to make decisions about their own health.

Most disabilities result from, among others poor health conditions, lack of immunization against disabling diseases and inadequate care of expecting mothers. Seychelles has a comprehensive immunization programme with a very good coverage. However there is a need to put in place mechanisms that identifies at the earliest parents who are at risk of not following immunisation schedules to ensure that all children are reached. Expectant mothers also need to be provided with the appropriate information so that they are aware of certain risk during their pregnancy and the importance of keeping to the immunization requirements.

Early intervention is very important for the prevention of disability. Early identification followed by treatment has the potential of eliminating onset of disability and or reduce the impact of disability in adult life. Seychelles early intervention package consist of paediatric consultation, speech therapy, physiotherapy, occupational therapy, audiology and hearing test and adaptive aids. People need to be educated on the existence of these services so that they can become more proactive in regards to their own and their families’ health.

Over the years national health policies has had to be reviewed to align with the increase in lifestyle related diseases. In particular there have been increasing trends of non-communicable diseases including cardio vascular diseases, diabetes, cancers and respiratory diseases, and injuries. These also affect people with disabilities. Hence health campaigns on these diseases must also target them and their caregivers.

Reproductive Health also brings about major concerns for persons with disabilities. Persons with disabilities have the same right to relationships with others as nondisabled persons and have similar sexual desires as the non-disabled. They are thus equally at risk of being affected by communicable diseases. Unfortunately awareness campaigns against the spread of these diseases exclude persons with disabilities. Most persons with disabilities have little education to enable them to access information on HIV/AIDS. The available campaigns against the disease are not disabled friendly for example all written information is in ordinary print and non in Braille for the visually impaired. Likewise spoken information is not simultaneously translated into sign language for the benefit of the hearing impaired.

The National Sexual and Reproductive Health policy and the National Policy on HIV and AIDS and STIs of 2012 are guided by principles of respect for human rights, choice in family planning matters and equity. Hence in principle persons with disabilities should have full access to the whole range of health services as the non disabled however problems are evident in implementation because some of the plans and strategies derived from these policies fail to target persons with disabilities and they are hence not able to have access to information which would make them aware that they too can benefit from these services and empower them to make informed decisions.

**Policy Commitment:**

The Government shall ensure that all Seychellois including persons with disabilities enjoy a healthy life

**Policy Objective:**

To ensure access to health services and information by persons with disabilities

**To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:**

* Ensure safe motherhood
* Make available targeted health information for persons with different types of disability
* Raise awareness on the prevention of disability
* Ensure that there is no forced sterilization of women with disabilities
* Educate persons with disabilities on their Reproductive Health rights

## 5.9 Disability, Skills Training and Employment

Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities; Article 27

**States shall:**

* Prohibit discrimination on the basis of disability in regards to all matters relating to employment
* Protect the right of persons with disability to decent work including equal opportunities and equal remuneration for work of equal value
* Ensure that person with disabilities are able to exercise their labour and trade union rights on an equal basis with others
* Enable persons with disabilities to have effective access to technical and vocational guidance programmes

**Key issues**

An individual’s capacity to work and lead an independent life is an expression of one’s dignity. In our society the capacity to work is mostly related to the functional ability of the body. Disability is associated with lack of ability and most people with disabilities see themselves in the same light. The end product of this scenario is the difficulty persons with disabilities find themselves in when accessing the world of work.Excluding persons with disabilities from the world of work has costs for societies, in terms of their productive potential, the cost of disability benefits, and the implications for their families. This exclusion may cost countries between 1 and 7 per cent of Gross Domestic Product. These costs to society can be minimized by dismantling barriers to participation of persons with disabilities, in education, skills development, enterprise development and employment, as well as poverty reduction and development programmes.

Acquisition of skills is an important prerequisite in the life of a person with disability. Skill leads to employment and therefore, self-sufficiency and independent living. The Vocational Unit of the National Council for Disabled provides Seychellois disabled an avenue for accessing vocational training and placements. Due to the barriers in education during the early years, children with disabilities rarely make it to post secondary institutions and those that do often find it difficult to keep up. For the moment it can be said that the skills training opportunities available to persons with disabilities are inadequate and do not provide the competence required to enable people with disabilities to work independently.

In addition the work environment is most often not disabled friendly and work organizations often see the cost rather than the benefit of employing a person with disability. People with disabilities also need to see the benefit of gainful employment in comparison to staying at home. One of the policy issues identified for persons with disabilities sometime refusing to gain employment is the fact that their Invalidity Benefit will be terminated once they have a steady income in accordance to the Social Security Act. It is important for this policy to be reviewed taking into account that persons with disabilities have more expenses than a non disabled.

The Ministry of Labour and Human Resource Development is currently developing a new Policy on Employment. The previous policy is dated 1999 and since then the Seychelles society has undergone dramatic changes. The new Policy will reflect the changes that have taken place in the socio economic arena of Seychelles. It highlights as one of its guiding principle ‘*Eliminating discrimination at work’* and persons with disabilities are identified as one of the groups that face such. It follows to list some of the strategies to address discrimination against persons with disabilities. This is one example of a policy where representatives of persons with disabilities and persons with disabilities themselves have been consulted and the result is commitments that mirror the needs of persons with disabilities locally and is in line with the principles of the CRPD. Strategies from the Employment policy has been merged below to ensure that work is the towards the same goal. It is hoped that the necessary backing is given to its full implementation and that the Ministry for Labour continues to include persons with disabilities in future works to keep the policy relevant.

**Policy commitment:**

The government shall ensure that the relevant structures, systems and provisions are in place to facilitate the entry of persons with disabilities into the world of work.

**Policy objective:**

To create an enabling environment for the employment of persons with disabilities

**To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:**

* Employ more persons with disabilities in the public sector
* Promote opportunities for self employment for persons with disabilities
* Reorganise the Vocational Unit to better meet the needs of the time
* Review the Social Security Act to encourage persons with disabilities to go for employment
* Provide assistance to persons with disabilities in finding , obtaining , maintaining and returning to employment
* Build gender sensitive information and statistics on PWDs engaged in economic activity, through strengthened capacity in data gathering and statistical analysis and sharing of information;
* Raise awareness on PLWDs right to work, targeting families and potential employers;
* Introduce work experience and job placement programmes for PLWDs in the open labour market;
* Ensure that hiring processes do not discriminate against PLWDs;
* Ensure that PLWDs receive equal remuneration for work of equal value conducted, as received by any able individuals;
* Monitor and evaluate discrimination against PLWDs in the labour force, including hiring, firing, training, advancement, remuneration, working conditions, protection from harassment and redress;
* Introduce temporary special measures, incentives and other measures to engage PWDs to enter employment and entrepreneurship and to encourage employing organisation to employ persons with disabiltiies;
* Ensure that reasonable accommodation is provided to persons with disabilities in the workplace where applicable;
* Lobby for more workplace buildings in Seychelles to be accessible to persons with disabilities;
* Ensure that people having accidents or other disability have equal opportunity in terms of skilling and training;
* Ensure that the Employment Tribunal and job agency buildings are accessible to PWDs;
* Strengthen inspection of and compliance to health and safety regulations, especially in the private and parastatal sector, to reduce temporary disability risk amongst men and women.

## 5.10 Disability and Education

Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities; Article 24

**States shall;**

Without discrimination and on the basis of equal opportunity ensure an inclusive education system at all level and lifelong learning directed to:

* the full development of human potential and sense of dignity and self worth , and the strengthening of respect for human rights fundamental freedoms and human diversity
* the development of their personality, talents and creativity, mental and physical abilities
* enabling persons with disabilities to participate effectively in a free society.

**Key issues**

In 2007 Seychelles opened its first ever mainstream school the Baie Ste Anne Praslin Primary school, with a concept for integrating children with disabilities. Children with physical disabilities were able to attend normal classes through the use of ramps and a lift and different adaptations were made on an individual basis in the classrooms to allow children with other types of disabilities to follow the curriculum. The school also has within its setting a centre for children with disabilities who are not able to follow the normal curriculum. In 2010 a class for Children with hearing impairments was integrated within a new primary school at Au Cap. These developments show the genuine effort by the government to facilitate learning for children with disabilities.

There is however still a lot to be done. The general educational system in Seychelles has overall found it difficult to guarantee equal access for children with disabilities. School facilities at some levels are inaccessible and teacher education and school curriculum as well do not incorporate the needs of persons with disabilities. As a result of these issues children with disabilities enrolled in primary schools are fewer than desired and enrollment is even lower in secondary schools and higher learning institutions. Additionally, much as education is essential for the development of a child’s potential, some parents still perceive children with disabilities as needing protection and opt not to send them to school.

The Ministry of Education is developing a policy on inclusive education which is expected to increase access for children with disabilities to mainstream schools.

**Policy commitment:** The Government in collaboration with stakeholders shall provide a conducive environment for inclusive education that takes care of the special needs of children with disabilities

**Policy objective:** To ensure that persons with disabilities have access to quality education

**To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:**

* Finalise and implement a National Policy on Inclusive Education
* Develop an action plan for the implementation of Inclusive Education
* make reasonable accommodation on individual basis within the general education system to facilitate the effective education of persons with disabilities
* provide persons with disabilities the same opportunities as other people to take advantage of the range of education, training and employment opportunities available in the country
* invest in teacher training on special needs education
* invest in the development of appropriate special educational needs modules at university level and in- service training
* invest in resource centres to be used by Special Needs Educators
* invest in training for resource personnel for teachers in ‘inclusive’ class rooms( e.g.: educational psychologist, speech and occupational therapist etc.)
* ensure that expatriate teachers are also trained in Special Needs Education and Disabilities

## 5.11 Disability and Technical Aids

Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities; Article 20

**States shall;**

* take effective measures to ensure personal mobility with the greatest possible independence for persons with disabilities

**Key issues**

One key component to the level of participation of people with disabilities within mainstream society is their use of mobility and technical aids. People with disabilities require these aids to enhance their functional ability. Such include white cane, hearing aids, and writing frame with stylus, hats and sunglasses. Other technical aids are tricycles, motorized three wheelers, modified car and Braillers. Despite their importance to persons with disabilities they are not readily available. It is paramount in this modern age for the people with disabilities of Seychelles to be able to access devices that can facilitate their integration and participation in mainstream society. At the moment most persons with disabilities have little information on what is available in the world and only those with enough means can acquire advanced technologies because they have to be imported into the country.

**Policy commitment:** The government shall facilitate access by persons with disabilities to technical aids

**Policy objective:**

To ensure that persons with disabilities have the relevant support to participate effectively in society

**To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:**

* Ensure that people with disabilities are provided with information on availability of and development in assistive aids.
* Provide technical aids to people with disabilities who have no means to buy them.
* Waive fees for technical aids imported into the country and those manufactured in the country.
* Provide training in mobility skills to persons with disabilities and specialist staff working with persons with disabilities
* Put in place a mechanism to ensure the production of low cost technical aids in the country.

## 5.12 Disability and rights to a home and to live independently

Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities; Article 19

**States shall;**

* Take effective and appropriate measures to allow persons with disabilities to live independently and be included in the community

**Key issues**

It is often overlooked that persons with disabilities have the right to a home and and to live on their own. Society instead often sees them as unable to carry out such responsibilities effectively because they lack the same functional ability as a non disabled. The CRPD promotes the right of persons with disabilities to live in their homes in peace and without interference from the government or any other bodies or person. There are many examples globally including locally were persons with disabilities given the opportunity are able to look after themselves, their family and home. The right to live independently and within and among the community is paramount because it is a precondition for anyone to enjoy all their rights and fundamental freedoms. Living independently does not mean that people with disabilities living a highly individual and self sufficient life without the help of other people. It is rather based on the social model of disability which recognizes that people are not limited in their choices by their disability but by the social and physical environment in which they live.

**Policy commitment:** The government shall facilitate the enjoyment by persons with disabilities of a healthier and more satisfying life on their terms

**Policy objective:**

To ensure that persons with disabilities who are able and willing to have the opportunity available to them to have a home, live independently and participate in the community

**To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:**

* Make general services more accessible
* Promote the concept of living independently
* Use role models to advocate for this right

## 5.13 Disability and awareness raising

Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities; Article 8

**States shall;**

* Take immediate, effective and appropriate measures to raise awareness throughout society

**Key issues**

Negative attitude of the community towards disability and persons with disability is one of the major barriers against the integration and equal participation of persons with disabilities in the life of the community. Often disability is perceived as a problem and a person with disability as unable and dependent. The National Council (NCFD) has carried out a number of awareness raising campaign on the principles of the CRPD and other important issues. However, more NGOS need to get involved. At the moment most organizations working with disabled are not sufficiently equipped to play this role. DPOs need to be empowered to be able to perform this role. The scope for public awareness on the needs, rights, abilities and contribution of people with disabilities in the society is wide. At the moment there is a void in organizations lobbying for issues of disability and persons with disabilities themselves need to be empowered to lead this.

**Policy commitment:** The government shall support initiative to promote the rights of persons with disabilities

**Policy objective:**

To raise awareness on disability and get more partners involved in raising awareness

**To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:**

* Maintain awareness campaigns that promote the capabilities and contributions of persons with disabilities
* Promote the use of the Creole version of the CRPD
* Develop a child friendly version of the CRPD to be used to sensitise children
* Implement awareness campaign in schools aimed at fostering an attitude of respect for the rights of persons with disabilities
* Encourage the media to portray persons with disabilities in a manner consistent with the principles of the CRPD
* Establish awareness training programmes on persons with disabilities and their rights in relevant educational institutions
* Use specific days to promote specific issues that relate to persons with disabilities and their rights
* Seek funding from local and international donors to create and maintain awareness campaign on specific issues relating to persons with disability and their rights

## 5.14 Disability and Rehabilitation

Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Art26

**States shall;**

* Take effective and appropriate measures to enable persons with disabilities to attain and maintain maximum independence, full physical, mental, social and vocational ability and full inclusion and participation in all aspects of life.

**Key issues**

Institutional care of people with disabilities has many disadvantages. It targets fewer people, uproots people from their areas of usual domicile, separates people with disabilities from mainstream community life and evokes negative attitude. On the other hand provision of rehabilitation services in the community has many advantages; it serves a large number of people with disabilities of various categories at one and the same time, it is based in the community, it integrates the non-disabled and builds solidarity between people with disabilities and the non-disabled.

Seychelles has for some years been promoting the community based rehabilitation approach and some positive strides have been made in that direction. In recognition of the advantages of the community based rehabilitation approach government policy encourages persons with disabilities to be cared for in the home and the relevant support by the state through the home care scheme are provided for this to happen. A few who are found unable to be cared for in a home setting are accommodated at the North East Point home for the elderly.

Health services that cater for persons with disabilities such as occupational therapy and physiotherapy have been decentralised bringing these services closer to the people. Skills training have been organised in different communities for persons with disabilities. Social workers are permanently based in every district and schools are also based in the districts. However, community based skills training are at present for short periods and depend on the funding that can be secured. Additionally, since there is one main centre that provides skills training, people with disabilities who want to benefit have to leave their communities everyday to attend the courses as schools within the communities are often inaccessible to children with disabilities.

**Policy commitment:** The government shall take measures to ensure that community based rehabilitation (CBR) is adopted as a strategy and method of service delivery to people with disabilities

**Policy objective:**

To ensure access by persons with disabilities to rehabilitation services and programmes that is comprehensive and multi disciplinary

**To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:**

* Create awareness on the benefits of CBR
* Establish clear guidelines on the roles of different agencies in CBR
* Establish mechanism for multi disciplinary assessment of individual needs and strength so that persons with disabilities can benefit in the best way possible from CBR
* Provide training for caregivers
* Provide continuing training for professionals and staff working in rehabilitation services
* Seek funding from local and international donors to support community based skill training initiatives
* Establish and maintain participation of private sector and NGOs in CBR

## 5.15 Disability, adequate standard of living and social protection

Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Article 28

**States shall:**

* take appropriate steps to safeguard and promote the right of persons with disabilities to an adequate standard of living for themselves and their families, including adequate food, clothing and housing without discrimination on the basis of their disability.

**Key issues**

Persons with disabilities are more likely to experience poverty than their non-disabled peers. Enjoyment of the right to an adequate standard of living and social protection by persons with disabilities is highly relevant to the achievement of the Millennium Development Goal of eradicating hunger and poverty. Denial of the right to an adequate standard of living and social protection can negatively impact the further enjoyment of many other human rights, including the right to health.

In terms of social protection the government of Seychelles provides statutory benefits to persons with disabilities. Additionally households who have difficulty making ends meet may also be assisted financially by the Agency for Social Protection under the welfare scheme. Financial assistance and benefits are reviewed periodically to better meet the needs of the recipients. Other social support from which persons with disability can benefit is social housing. Special accommodations are made for persons with disabilities on a case by case basis. The Social Services division of the Social Affairs Department also offers services such as counseling and home visits.

It is important to ensure that the social protection framework does not encourage persons with disabilities to become dependent on the state but instead empowers them to seek to live independently and participate actively within mainstream society.

**Policy commitment:** The government shall ensure a sustainable social protection system that caters for persons with disabilities

**Policy objective:**

To ensure that adequate social support are in place for persons with disabilities

**To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:**

* ensure more effective access by persons with disabilities, in particular women and girls with disabilities and older persons with disabilities, to social protection programmes and poverty reduction programmes
* ensure access by persons with disabilities and their families living in situations of poverty to assistance from the State, including relevant training, counselling, financial assistance and respite care;
* reduce dependency by encouraging persons with disabilities who are able to work to do so
* review provisions in Social Security Act which terminates Invalidity Benefit to persons with disabilities who gain full time employment taking into account that they may have more expenses than a non disabled
* Review the way disability is assessed from purely medical perspective to a more holistic approach
* ensure access by persons with disabilities who are in need, to public housing adapted to meet their needs
* ensure access to persons with disabilities to clean water
* ensure equal access by persons with disabilities to retirement benefits and programmes.
* ensure that social protection services and programmes are accessible to persons with disabilities
* consult organisations of persons with disabilities in the development, monitoring and evaluation of social protection policies***.***

## 5.16 Disability, information, statistics and research

Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Article 31

**States shall:**

* Undertake to collect appropriate information, including statistical and research data, to enable them to formulate and implement policies to give effect to the convention

**Key issues**

Reliable information is an important tool in the provision of services to persons with disabilities. Unfortunately there is a serious lack of data and information in this service area and it is one of the reasons why often services and programmes overlook their needs. For the first time in 2010 the National Census conducted by the NBS recorded information on this group. However this information is still limited and does little to guide the development and delivery of programmes and services.

**Policy commitment:** The government shall sensitize government agencies, individuals and non-governmental organizations on the importance of collecting data on persons with disabilities

**Policy objective:**

To ensure the availability of data on persons with disabilities for evidence based planning

**To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:**

* Make available relevant tools for collecting data on persons with disabilities
* Develop a database on people with disabilities
* Ensure that data specific to persons with disabilities are collected in national data collection initiatives e.g. Census and other relevant surveys
* Ensure the dissemination of available statistics and research and their accessibility to persons with disabilities and others
* Encourage research in specific areas of disability
* Ensure timely reporting to the UNCRPD committee

## IMPLEMENTATION FRAMEWORK

## 6.1 Institutional framework

The Social Affairs Department within the Ministry of Social Affairs, Community Development and Sports has the responsibility to oversee disability issues in the country. Nonetheless disability is a cross cutting matter and different sectors have differing responsibilities. In the implementation of this policy the Ministry will work closely with all partners to achieve the set objectives. The ministry will set up a multi sectoral committee to oversee the implementation of this policy.

The National Council for Disabled is a budget dependant civil society organisation with the important role of advising the government on issues that relate to disability. It has been the key agency behind the development of this policy and in its implementation endeavours to support all stakeholders in achieving the targets.

### *6.2 The Social Affairs Department’s Core Function*

The mandates of the Social Affairs Department are as follows:

* To sensitize and educate individuals and families on personal and social responsibility
* To provide support to individuals and families to restore social functioning
* To provide relief to and secure the protection of vulnerable groups
* To rehabilitate and re-integrate marginalised groups into mainstream society
* To carry out research and social impact assessment and advocate for evidence-based recommendations in policy planning.
* To strengthen co-operation with all stakeholders and partners to maximise benefits for social development
* To ensure the availability of adequate and appropriate resources for the effective functioning of the Department

***6.3 The National Council for Disabled Core Function***

The National Council for Disabled is governed by an Act which outlines the following as its functions:

a) to co‑ordinate the activities of public or private organisations and other persons engaged in the welfare of disabled persons;

(b) in collaboration with public or private organisations and other persons ‑

(i) to provide care and assistance to disabled persons;

(ii) to promote, develop and organise services and programmes for rehabilitation and employment of disabled persons;

(iii) to provide and secure employment for disabled persons;

(c) to cooperate with public or private organisations and other persons in furtherance of the welfare of disabled persons;

(d) to assist public or private organisations and other persons in promoting and organising projects and income generating activities for the benefit and welfare of disabled persons;

(e) to advise the Government on education, sports, training programmes, employment and vocational training courses for disabled persons;

(f) to assist public or private organisations and other persons in organising sporting activities for disabled persons

(g) to assist in the provision of facilities considered by the Council to be necessary or desirable for the welfare of disabled persons;

(h) to undertake any other activities in furtherance of the functions of the Council.

It is important to highlight here that the functions outlined for the council has been proposed for review.

**6.4 The Multi Stakeholder Committee on the implementation of the National Policy on Disability**

The Social Affairs Department in collaboration with the NCFD shall decide on the specific mandate of this committee. Its core purpose will be to:

* Ensure that all relevant stakeholders are involved in the implementation of the policy
* Ensure that stakeholders are held accountable for the implementation of specific actions that will give effect to this policy
* Ensure that the policy remains a ‘living’ document

**6.5 Stakeholder responsibilities**

In order to improve outcomes for people with disabilities in the implementation of this policy, the following shall be the responsibilities of principal stakeholders.

**Government/Ministries**

The Government shall create an enabling environment for the implementation of this policy by:

* Putting in place relevant legislations aimed at promoting the rights of persons with disabilities
* ensuring the availability of the appropriate administrative structures, programmes and services to give effect to this policy
* making available funding to support the implementation of this policy
* ensuring participation, integration and equal opportunity for persons with disabilities
* mainstreaming disability within all relevant policies, programmes and services
* strengthening capacity of the National Council for Disabled to monitor and evaluate the implementation of this policy and assist stakeholders in implementing the policy

**National Council for Disabled**

The National Council for Disabled shall in promoting the rights of persons with disabilities lobby for compliance to this policy by:

* Monitoring and evaluating the implementation of this policy
* Assisting stakeholders in their endeavors to implement the policy
* Organizing sensitization sessions with stakeholders on their responsibilities
* Advising government on any revision work required on the policy to ensure that it remains relevant

**NGOs/ Civil Society**

NGOs shall ensure the participation of people with disabilities and their families in the development and implementation of this policy and other policies that can impact on this one by:

* Sensitizing persons with disabilities and their families on the policy and other issues relating to disability
* Lobbying government to implement the relevant legal and administrative framework to give effect to this policy

**Families/ communities**

Families and Communities shall support and protect persons with disabilities and the implementation of this policy by

* Facilitating their participation in mainstream activities
* Helping persons with disabilities to advocate for their own rights

## RESOURCE MOBILISATION

## Evidently the actions required to enforce this policy and our international commitments are wide in scope, and responsibilities touch on many stakeholders. For this policy to succeed it requires various resources; such as specific human capacity, technical aids but most importantly it requires financial backing. The key funding party is of course the government through the national budget but the policy also calls for private sector and NGO support. Continued collaboration with development partners is also essential, especially to be able to fill in the gaps in knowledge and experience on disability issues and rights of persons with disabilities.

## This policy shall be reviewed every 5 years or earlier upon the decision of the Multi stakeholder Committee.
