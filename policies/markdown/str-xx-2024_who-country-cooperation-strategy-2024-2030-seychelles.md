MESSAGE FROM THE WHO REGIONAL DIRECTOR FOR AFRICA

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The Republic of Seychelles and the World Health Organization (WHO) have collaborated to develop
the fourth Country Cooperation Strategy (CCS-4) for the period of 2024 to 2030. The CCS-4 outlines
the strategic directions and priorities for WHO's partnership with the Government and other
stakeholders in the health sector over the next seven years. It is in line with Seychelles' Vision 2033,
the National Development Strategy 2024-2028, and the National Health Policy 2015. The CCS-4 is
also consistent with the Seychelles National Health Strategic Plan (NHSP) 2022-2026, which aims to
achieve the highest level of physical, social, mental, and spiritual health for all individuals in
Seychelles while living in harmony with nature. The plan focuses on increasing life expectancy,
achieving universal health coverage (UHC), preparing for and responding to health emergencies, and
promoting healthy populations.
The CCS-4 is also aligned with the UN 2030 Sustainable Development Agenda goals, the Seychelles
UN Sustainable Development Cooperation Framework (UNSDCF) 2024-2028, and the Africa Health
Strategy (2016-2030) of the African Union. It reflects the goals of the Thirteenth General Programme
of Work 2019-2023 (GPW13) and the Fourteenth General Programme of Work 2025-2028 (GPW14)
of the WHO Secretariat, which aim to achieve UHC, address health emergencies, and promote health
and well-being for all populations.
The development of the CCS-4 was based on the recommendations from the final evaluation of CCS-
3 (2016-2021) and extensive consultations with the Ministry of Health, other ministries,
departments, and agencies involved in broader health determinants, non-governmental
organizations, development partners, and other health stakeholders. The CCS-4 aims to strengthen
national institutional and systemic capacities to facilitate WHO's transition from certain forms of
support in the future.
Since the late 1970s, the government of Seychelles has been committed to providing free health
services through a network of community health centers and well-equipped hospitals, supplemented
by overseas evacuations for complex cases. This has led to the expansion of UHC for essential health
services and the achievement of positive health indicators, making Seychelles one of the best-
performing countries in the WHO African Region.
The collaboration between the Republic of Seychelles and the World Health Organization (WHO)
recognizes several important factors. Given the shift from communicable to non-communicable
diseases as the leading causes of morbidity and mortality, the cooperation will prioritize "whole-of-
government" and "whole-of-society" approaches to address the broader determinants of health. This
includes supporting the reorientation of the national health system to provide equitable health
promotion and disease prevention services through the Primary Health Care (PHC) system, with a
focus on cost-effectiveness and sustainability.
Additionally, there will be an emphasis on institutionalizing performance assessments of the national
health system and national health research system. This will generate relevant evidence to inform
decision-making processes. Considering the potential increase in acute public health emergencies
associated with climate change, the cooperation will also focus on strengthening multi-level
capacities for prevention, preparedness, rapid detection, and response.
To maximize the effectiveness and efficiency of cooperation, resources will be allocated to areas
where WHO has a comparative advantage and optimizes synergies with other development partners
and stakeholders. The fourth Country Cooperation Strategy (CCS-4) outlines four strategic priorities.

The WHO Country Cooperation Strategic Agenda for Seychelles, spanning from 2024 to 2030,
outlines the mission, vision, and strategic priorities for improving the health and well-being of the
population. The mission is to create a Seychelles where all individuals achieve the highest level of
physical, mental, and social well-being while living in harmony with nature. This aligns with the goals
of the National Health Sector Strategic Plan 2022-2026 and the national Sustainable Development
Goals (SDGs).
The vision is for the people of Seychelles to experience better health, well-being, and dignity at all
stages of life throughout the country.
The strategic priorities are as follows:
1. Enhance healthy living and accelerate actions to address the risk factors and determinants of
health.
2. Strengthen universal health coverage (UHC) and service quality and address priority
communicable and noncommunicable diseases.
3. Safeguard the health and well-being of the population against public health emergencies.
4. Elevate WHO health leadership, advocacy, and innovations for health.
To achieve these strategic priorities, the following strategic deliverables have been identified:
1.1: Enhance institutionalization of health and well-being in all sectoral policies to tackle risk
factors of priority noncommunicable & communicable diseases and injuries.
1.2: Enhance healthy living across the life course, mental health, and control substance abuse.
2.1: Improve the performance of the health system, including the organization and quality of
health services, as well as reducing inequities in access to essential health services and
interventions across all stages of life.
2.2: Enhance programs, regulatory frameworks, and interventions to address priority
noncommunicable and communicable diseases.
3.1: Strengthen preparedness and integrated surveillance systems to prevent and mitigate
emerging health risks, such as epidemics, pandemics, and hazards related to climate change.
3.2: Enhance the capacity to respond effectively to all health emergencies and improve timeliness
and effectiveness of health emergencies responses.
4.1: Strengthen WHO’s presence, leadership, advocacy, and strategic communications to drive
health impact in Seychelles.
4.2: Improve capacities for health research, innovation & digital transformation, information
systems, and use of data for health decision-making.

NEXT SEVEN YEARS: KEY HEALTH SYSTEM ISSUES AND AREAS
OF FOCUS
Seychelles needs to prioritize several key areas in the next seven years to protect and improve
population health and the performance of its health system. These areas include enhancing the
national health system's performance and resilience by adopting a primary health care approach,
strengthening leadership and governance, engaging non-state actors, ensuring an adequate and
skilled workforce, developing climate-sensitive financial frameworks, and improving the quality of
services across different life stages.
The country also needs to focus on addressing infectious diseases such as pneumonia, sexually
transmitted infections, HIV/AIDS, tuberculosis, dengue fever, and leptospirosis. This can be achieved
through improved disease surveillance, preparedness, and response, as well as the implementation
of international health regulations and a One-Health approach that integrates human, animal, and
environmental health.
In addition, Seychelles should prioritize addressing the determinants of health through promoting
healthy lifestyles, addressing socioeconomic and environmental factors that impact health outcomes,
and fostering health-promoting environments in various settings like homes, schools, workplaces,
and communities. The country should also collaborate across different sectors to create policies that
positively impact health and strengthen its resilience against climate-related health risks.
Furthermore, Seychelles needs to focus on tackling non-communicable diseases and their risk factors
like tobacco use, unhealthy diets, physical inactivity, and alcohol consumption. It should prioritize
mental health promotion, prevention, and treatment, as well as combat substance misuse through
prevention programs and support services. Additionally, leveraging technology for efficient health
information management and exploring digital solutions will also be crucial for improving the health
system.
DEVELOPMENT PARTNERSHIPS: MAIN HEALTH DEVELOPMENT
PARTNERS IN SEYCHELLES

GOVERNMENT
In recognition of the complex nature of health issues, the Government of Seychelles established
close working arrangements between the Ministry of Health and other sectors including education,
housing, environmental sanitation, water supply, food security, and community development [73].
This approach was in alignment with the principles in Alma Ata Declaration on Primary Health Care
(PHC) in 1978 and the Ottawa Charter for Health Promotion in 1986 which emphasized the
importance of intersectoral action for the promotion of health, achievement of health equity, and
upholding of health as a human right [74,75].
Seychelles committed to intersectoral action and addressing determinants of health by co-signing the
2009 Ouagadougou Declaration on PHC and Health Systems strengthening in Africa on coordinated
efforts across sectors [76,77]. The global community further reaffirmed this commitment at the
Global Conference on PHC in Astana, Kazakhstan in October 2018 through the adoption of the
Declaration of Astana, which called for addressing economic, social, and environmental determinants
of health and incorporating a Health-in-All-Policies (HiAP) approach [78].
Reflecting its dedication to promoting health and health equity, the Seychelles Government Cabinet
endorsed the HiAP pledge on September 20, 2017, committing to prioritize health in all national
policies [64]. The Ministry of Health (MOH) played a crucial role in guiding and coordinating the
efforts of other sectors in positively influencing the social determinants of health [64]. Recognizing
that health is integral to all 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), the Cabinet acknowledged the
need for a comprehensive, intersectoral approach to achieve these goals.
As a result, the Seychelles HiAP Policy Network, comprising all relevant ministries, departments, and
agencies (MDAs) listed in Annex 4, was established in 2022 to facilitate the integration of health
considerations into all sectors' policies and actions [79]. The MOH established the HiAP Task Force to
support the work of this policy network. The current Government of the Republic of Seychelles
(GoRS) has outlined its plans to revitalize HiAP in its National Development Strategy (2024-2028) [6]
and National Health Strategic Plan 2022-2026 [7]. The World Health Organization (WHO) is
committed to supporting in institutionalization of HiAP to promote the health and well-being of the
population.
THE CIVIL SOCIETY, PRIVATE SECTOR, AND ACADEMIA
Civil Society: In November 2014, the Citizens Engagement Platform Seychelles (CEPS) was established
as the national umbrella organization for civil society in Seychelles. It currently consists of 72
accredited non-governmental organizations from various sectors. The CEPS has organized these
organizations into seven commissions based on different themes, such as environment and natural
resources, faith-based organizations, and gender rights. The goal of the CEPS is to promote the active
involvement of its member organizations in national and international development. Specifically, it
aims to engage in national decision-making, policy formulation, and law-making, as well as advocate
for civil society's role in development. Many health-related non-governmental organizations,
including the Cancer Concern Association and the HIV and AIDS Support Organization, are members
of the CEPS. However, there is currently no formal agreement or collaboration between the CEPS and
the Ministry of Health (MOH) to enhance coordination between health-related civil society
organizations and MOH entities.
Private Sector: The involvement of the private sector in the health sector is still in its early stages but
is growing. Currently, there are 64 general medical practitioners, 16 dentists, and 63 pharmacists
practicing in the private health sector. The Ministry of Health recognizes the importance of private

sector organizations in improving the health and well-being of the population. The private sector has
the potential to contribute to the improvement of health through various avenues, such as providing
private health services, ensuring occupational safety and health of workers, contributing to corporate
social responsibility schemes, and supporting environmental and public health initiatives. However,
to optimize the contributions of the private sector, a formal framework for coordination,
collaboration, participation, and accountability needs to be established. The MOH's National Health
Strategic Plan 2022-2026 aims to ensure cooperation and policy synergy between the public, private
sectors, and civil society.
Academia: There are two main academic institutions in Seychelles involved in health-related research
and education. The National Institute of Health and Social Studies (NIHSS) was transferred from the
Ministry of Education to the Ministry of Health in 2014. Its objective is to provide tertiary-level
training in health, allied health, and social services. It currently offers certificate and diploma courses,
and not provide degree-level programs. The NIHSS is also involved in conducting basic research and
publishing academic literature. The University of Seychelles (UniSey) was established in 2009 and
consists of two faculties and several research institutes. The faculties and research institutes
collaborate on research projects and aim to develop research with practical applications for
Seychelles' transitioning into a knowledge-based economy. UniSey has a research journal called
Seychelles Research Journal (SRJ), which publishes articles twice a year. However, at the time of the
assessment, there was no evidence of a research policy or a research ethics review board at UniSey,
although the establishment of a robust research governance framework is planned in its strategic
plan. The Guy Morel Institute (TGMI) was formerly affiliated with the Seychelles University before
becoming independent. It provides professional development in logistics and contract law and
financial services through collaborations with international institutions. It collaborates with the
Ministry of Health on capacity building of healthcare professionals, research and policy development.
SOUTH-SOUTH COOPERATION
South-South Cooperation in Seychelles dates back to the late 1970s when the post-independence
government recognized the central role of health in national development and proactively promoted
equitable healthcare access. Cooperation agreements were established with countries such as Cuba,
China, Nigeria, and India, enabling the recruitment of medical doctors. Additionally, agreements
were made for overseas training of doctors and dentists in former Eastern Europe, Cuba, and
Zimbabwe. Seychelles has had a long-standing bilateral cooperation with Cuba since April 1978,
which extends to various sectors including health, education, environment, sports, culture, economy,
and social spheres.
SOUTHERN AFRICAN DEVELOPMENT COMMUNITY (SADC)
Seychelles joined the Southern African Development Community (SADC) on 31 July 1998, becoming
the 14th member. In August 1999, Seychelles signed the SADC Protocol on Health. As a member of
SADC, Seychelles has actively contributed to the development of the SADC Vision 2050, with a focus
on social and human capital development. Key cross-cutting issues in the vision include gender,
youth, environment and climate change, and disaster risk management. The SADC Health Ministers
have developed a Health Policy Framework that addresses ten areas including health research and
surveillance, health information systems, health promotion and education, HIV and AIDS,
communicable and non-communicable disease control, disabilities, reproductive health, health
human resources development, nutrition and food safety, and violence and substance abuse.
INDIAN OCEAN COMMISSION (IOC)

Seychelles is a member of the Indian Ocean Commission (IOC), an intergovernmental organization
consisting of five member states - the Union of Comoros, France (on behalf of Réunion), Madagascar,
Mauritius, and Seychelles. The IOC was established in 1982 and institutionalized in Seychelles in
1984. The IOC governance structure includes the Summit of Heads of State and Government, the
Council of Ministers of Foreign Affairs, the Committee of Permanent Liaison Officers, and the General
Secretariat based in Mauritius. The General Secretariat is responsible for implementing decisions,
managing cooperation projects, mobilizing resources from development partners, and entering into
agreements with specialized agencies. The IOC Strategic Development Plan (2023-2033) focuses on
resilience, peace and security, human, cultural and economic development, and institutional and
partnership architecture. The IOC collaborates with various health technical partners including the
World Health Organization (WHO).
AFRICA SMALL ISLANDS DEVELOPING STATES (SIDS)
Seychelles has actively participated in the African Small Islands Developing States (SIDS) Network
since its establishment in 2006. The network includes five African countries - Cabo Verde, Comoros,
Mauritius, Sao Tome and Principe, and Seychelles. The SIDS Network addresses various recurring
themes such as universal health coverage (UHC), health system strengthening, pooled procurement
of quality medicines, noncommunicable diseases, human resources for health, communicable
diseases (especially HIV and malaria), illicit drug use, health security and emergencies, and climate
change. In 2011, the SIDS Network adopted a Plan of Action for the health sector's adaptation to
climate change, which was implemented from 2012 to 2016. The SIDS also actively participated in
the Third Global Conference on Health and Climate Change, where the "Climate Change and Health
in Small Island Developing States - Regional Plan of Action for SIDS in the African and South-East
Asian Regions" was adopted.
BILATERAL AND MULTILATERAL DONORS
In 2015, Seychelles was declared a high-income country. Consequently, during the 2017 review
conducted by the Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) Development
Assistance Committee (DAC) on the list of Official Development Assistance (ODA) Recipients,
Seychelles was classified as a graduating country and this classification took effect from 1 January
2018 [90].
According to the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), countries that graduate from the
Least Developed Country (LDC) status may face potential challenges such as losing access to
preferential trade benefits targeted at less developed countries. This includes unilateral duty-free
market access and relaxed rules of origin requirements in major importing countries. In addition,
concessional development financing provisions can also be affected [91].
According to the Ministry of Health (MOH) National Health Accounts, in 2018, Seychelles allocated a
total of SCR 1,166,736,420 to health expenditure. Of this total, 74.7% was provided by the
government, 23.2% came from households, 1.3% from corporations, 0.7% from other countries
(donors), and 0.05% from non-profit institutions serving households (NPISH) [92]. Therefore, donor
funding for health accounted for less than 1% of the total health expenditure in Seychelles.
As a result of its graduation from the OECD DAC list of recipient countries, Seychelles is no longer
eligible for direct funding from GAVI, the Vaccine Alliance, and the Global Fund to Fight HIV, TB, and
Malaria. Consequently, Seychelles must continue to expand domestic funding for health in order to
meet its healthcare needs.

COLLABORATION WITH THE UNITED NATIONS SYSTEM AT THE COUNTRY LEVEL
The United Nations Multi-Country Office for Mauritius and Seychelles is located in Port Louis,
Mauritius. The United Nations Country Team (UNCT) for Seychelles consists of FAO, IFAD, ILO, IOM,
ITC, ITU, UNAIDS, UNCTAD, UNDESA, UNDP, UNDRR, UNECA, UNEP, UNESCO, UNFPA, UN-HABITAT,
UNHCR, UNHR, UNICEF, UNIDO, UNITAR, UNODC, UNOPS, UNWOMEN, and WHO. Four of the
agencies are based in in Mauritius while the rest are based in Madagascar, South Africa and Kenya.
WHO is the only agency with a representative level physical presence in Seychelles. WHO has at
times facilitated the work of other agencies without a physical presence [93].
On August 30, 2018, the Government of the Republic of Seychelles (GoRS) and the UN in Seychelles
jointly signed the first UNSDCF called the "Strategic Partnership Framework (SPF) 2019-2023." This
framework had four strategic priority areas, including economic transformation, environmental
sustainability and resilience, human capital development and data development, and science,
technology, and innovation. The SPF evaluation conducted in 2022 noted that it consolidated the
work of the UN system in a single coherent strategy, providing a platform for common policy work,
sharing of resources, strong coordination of programs, and timely delivery of results. The SPF had a
strong collaborative link with the GoRS national development priorities, sustainable development
goals, international and regional commitments, and obligations. The evaluation rated overall
progress towards the outcome of human capital development and quality of life as fair.
On March 24, 2023, the GoRS and the UN in Seychelles jointly signed the UNSDCF 2024-2028. This
framework has three priorities (people, prosperity, and planet), three outcomes, and eight inter-
agency outputs. The outcomes include empowering all people in Seychelles, particularly vulnerable
groups, and providing improved and sustainable services, protection, and opportunities; benefiting
all people in Seychelles, especially vulnerable groups, from a resilient, sustainable, and inclusive
economy; and better preparing people and institutions for disaster risks and strengthening natural
ecosystems.
The World Health Organization (WHO) made implementation commitments within the UNSDCF
2024-2028 for Seychelles to contribute to all its three priorities: PEOPLE, PROSPERITY, and PLANET.
Under PEOPLE, WHO will focus on improving access to health services, promoting healthy lifestyles,
and strengthening occupational safety. Under PROSPERITY, WHO will contribute to a resilient
economy, access to affordable and nutritious food, and investment in care economies. Under
PLANET, WHO will focus on enhancing resilience to disasters and pollution, promoting a healthy
environment, and improving waste management. WHO will also harness science, research, and
innovation, strengthen global health leadership, and collaborate with various partners to protect and
advance people's health.
The 4th Country Cooperation Strategy for Seychelles (CCS-4) aligns well with the strategic
commitments made in the UNSDCF. The CCS-4 will facilitate the implementation of health outcomes
defined in the UNSDCF.

SEYCHELLES’S PATH TO SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT GOALS
The sustainable development of Seychelles is strategically guided by Vision 2033, spanning the
period from 2018 to 2033. This visionary framework was meticulously crafted, incorporating global
commitments such as the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) 2, the Paris Agreement on
climate change 3, the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) 4, and the African
Union Agenda 2063.
Vision 2033 aims to transform Seychelles into a resilient, responsible, and prosperous nation where
healthy, educated, and empowered Seychellois coexist harmoniously with nature and engage with
the wider world by 2033. Among its nine core missions, one is to cultivate a highly skilled, healthy,
and productive population that supports the realization of Vision 2033. In all, the vision rests upon
six pivotal pillars of 1) Good Governance, 2) People at the Center of Development, 3) Social Cohesion,
4) Innovative Economy, 5) Economic Transformation, and 6) Environmental Sustainability &
Resilience.
The health sector agenda is reflected in the “People at the Center of Development” pillar. The health
sector’s vision, as outlined in Vision 2033, is to achieve the highest level of physical, social, mental,
and spiritual health for all Seychellois, while fostering harmony with nature. Guided by the principles
of “Health for all, Health by all, and Health in all”, the health sector is expected to contribute
significantly to the overall well-being of Seychelles’ population.
The National Development Strategy (NDS) 2024-2028, approved by the cabinet in December 2023,
serves translates Vision 2033 into implementation programmes in the medium-term. The NDS 2024-
2028 has six key priority areas:
1. A Modern Public Service
2. Economic Transformation and Diversification
3. Social Cohesion and Inclusion
4. Environmental Sustainability and Resilience
5. Innovative Economy
6. People-Centered Development
Within this context, the health sector is expected to play a pivotal role as part of the “People at the
Center of Development” pillar while also tangentially contributing to the other priorities. The health
sector’s strategic focus, as outlined in the NDS 2024-2028, is to achieve the highest level of health
and wellbeing for all Seychellois by:
1. Pursuing effective health protection and promote empowerment that involves fostering
personal and societal responsibility for holistic health.
2. Building a high-quality integrated healthcare system that focuses on people-centered care.
The health sector is additionally expected to develop a communication strategy to manage risk
factors related to non-communicable diseases (NCDs) and communicable diseases and strengthening
health services across the life course

Though the Seychelles National Health Policy (NHP) 2015, which has no expiry date, was developed
sometime back, its focus on promoting, protecting, and restoring the health, quality of life and
dignity for all Seychellois resonates adequately with what is reflected in the NDS 2024-2028. The
National Health Strategic Plan (NHSP) 2016-2020, served as a pivotal bridge, advancing the shared
vision of the NDS 2024-2028 and the NHP 2015. In alignment with NDS 2024-2028, the NHSP
emphasizes comprehensive, high-quality integrated health care services towards the NDS’s objective
of economic transformation and social cohesion. Additionally, the NHSP’s commits to research, data
management, and innovation in line with the NDS’s vision of an innovative economy. The NHSP
concomitantly operationalizes the NHP’s mission by relentlessly promoting health, quality of life, and
dignity for Seychellois. It translated these principles into tangible actions of preventive, promotive,
curative, and rehabilitative health programs including the provision of free primary health care at the
point of use to exemplify the NHP’s commitment to equitable access and quality services.
The Health Annual Performance Report 2022 charted implementation progress of the NHSSP during
the year 2022 and highlighted the continuing key priorities for the sector in the coming years as
follows
 Enhancing Preventive Measures: The focus shifted toward proactive health promotion, risk
factor management, and disease prevention.
 Integrated Care Systems: Strengthening the integration of health services across the life
course emerged as a critical goal.
 Human Resources Development: Efforts to achieve self-sufficiency in health workforce
continued, emphasizing skill development and retention.
 Sustainability and Innovation: The sector explored innovative approaches to ensure long-
term sustainability.
 Public-Private Partnerships: Collaboration with the private health sector gained prominence,
aligning with the NDS’s vision.
In summary, the NHSP 2016-2020 laid the groundwork for a healthier Seychelles, bridging policy
aspirations with practical implementation towards the shared vision of a resilient, empowered
population living in harmony with nature.

Strategic Priority 3: Safeguarding the health and well-being of the population against public health
emergencies.
This strategic priority ensures that Seychelles is fully prepared to prevent and mitigate emerging
health risks, such as epidemics, pandemics, and other hazards; and has the capacity to timely detect
and effectively respond to public health emergencies or infectious disease threats. The increasing
interaction between people and animals, rapid urbanization, environmental degradation,
international travel, natural disasters, foodborne diseases, antimicrobial resistance, and climate

change all pose significant threats to human health. Therefore, it is crucial for every country and
community to identify and understand health threats, assess strengths and vulnerabilities, mitigate
risks, and coordinate effective responses as needed [10]. The following strategic deliverables have
been identified to achieve the goals of Strategic Priority 3:
1. Strengthen preparedness and integrated surveillance systems to prevent and mitigate
emerging health risks, such as epidemics, pandemics, and hazards related to climate
change..
Strategic Approaches
Enhancing the implementation and monitoring of International Health Regulations (IHR) core
capacities, with a specific focus on monitoring antimicrobial resistance and promoting the
One Health approach.
Strengthening systems for multidisciplinary and multisectoral Integrated Disease Surveillance
and Response (IDSR) performance, as well as improving data reviews, monitoring, and
evaluation processes.
Strategic Actions
- Evaluations (JEE) of country capacity to prevent, detect, and respond to infectious diseases
and other public health threats.
- Establish and make functional a multi-sectoral coordination mechanism for IDSR, IHR core
capacities, One Health and antimicrobial resistance
- Conduct health emergency risk and health sector climate change vulnerability assessments
plus risk mapping
- Develop multi-hazard health emergency risk mitigation plus climate change resilience and
adaptation plan
- Develop and monitor National Action Plan for Health Security, including incorporation of a
component for health sector climate change resilience and adaptation.
- Improve early outbreak detection through strengthened real-time case/outbreak
investigation, EIOS, disease surveillance and reporting.
- Develop and monitor a National Action Plan to combat antimicrobial resistance.
- Strengthen capacity of the national laboratory system, through development of standard
operating procedures, training of staff, provision of kits and regional networking for
collection and testing of surveillance samples.
- Update Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) tools plus in-service training curriculum, build
capacity of health workers on IPC and monitor IPC implementation
- Build capacity for monitoring and reporting on Global Antimicrobial Resistance and use of
Surveillance System (GLASS) indicators.
- Strengthen capacity for implementing and monitoring the revised 3rd generation IDSR
guidelines through training among the human and animal health workforce.
2. Enhance the capacity to respond effectively to all health emergencies and improve
timeliness and effectiveness of health emergencies responses.
Strategic Approaches
Strengthening sectoral coordination and alignment to optimize emergency response
strategies and operations. This includes coordinating different sectors like health, education,
transportation, and communication to effectively address disaster challenges by establishing

a robust coordination mechanisms to foster collaboration between sectors and ensure
seamless coordination during emergency response efforts.
Enhancing national capacities for managing identified disaster risks and vulnerabilities to
improve overall emergency response by investing in readiness and mitigation measures for
priority risks plus vulnerabilities; and strengthening healthcare systems to provide quality
clinical care and medical countermeasures during health emergencies including ensuring
that demands are met and negative impacts on the population and healthcare infrastructure
are minimized.
Strategic Actions
- Maintain functionality of a national Health Emergencies Operations Centre, with standing
rapid response teams, and working linkages with law enforcement and other sector rapid
response.
- Preposition health emergency response contingency capacities in all locations that can be cut
off or can not be reached timely
- Build capacity for health emergency situation response readiness and grading assessment
and preparation of initial response plan
- Develop and periodically test strategies for scalable clinical care capacities and maintain
capacities for critical medical countermeasures.
- Strengthen partnerships and networks for a whole-of-society approach to collaborative

- Implement selected recommendations from the Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk

IMPLEMENTING THE CCS
This chapter outlines cooperation pathways that will be utilized to support the implementation of
CCS-4 strategic priorities at the country level. It provides specific details about national-level
agreements, which will be coordinated by the Ministry of Health, between the three levels of WHO
and various partners, including non-state actors. The mandate, platforms, range of expertise of
WHO’s three organizational levels (country, regional, and headquarters) coupled with the
organization’s reputation as an impartial convener of diverse partners; steward of global standards,
frameworks, and conventions; and trusted source of health information; all well positions WHO to
effectively marshal the cooperation partnerships that will fully implement the jointly agreed strategic
agenda in Seychelles leverage the organization’s continually improving assets (such as expertise,
norms and standards, evidence-based guidelines and recommendations, scientific evidence, and
data) to ensure impact on public health [127,128].
In line with WHO's constitutional mandate and core functions [127,128], the following means of
implementation will be used:
LEADERSHIP: DIPLOMACY AND CONVENING
Achieving Seychelles' health sector vision requires a renewed commitment from all relevant
stakeholders, including the public sector, private sector, civil society organizations, bilateral and
multilateral agencies, to respect and empower national structures and processes established for
health leadership and governance [10]. In the implementation of CCS-4, WHO will employ its
diplomacy, advocacy, and convening roles to support national health leadership in aligning a broad
array of health actors with national priorities and systems.
LEADERSHIP: ADVOCACY AND STRATEGIC COMMUNICATION
WHO's work on promoting healthy lives and well-being, including universal health coverage, health
emergencies, and healthier populations, involves both technical and political aspects [128]. WHO will
advocate for health at the highest political level and engage with various non-state actors to support
Seychelles' health development vision. WHO will promote a "Health in All Policies" approach,
emphasizing a comprehensive and collaborative approach involving all sectors of government and
society. WHO will engage political leaders, government members of cabinet, and other relevant
actors to champion a coordinated multisectoral agenda addressing the key determinants of health in
Seychelles. WHO will also emphasize coordination, teamwork, and synergy across its three levels and
with other partners, such as the African Union, Africa CDC, IOM, SADC, UNCT, World Bank, and
bilateral agencies, in implementing, monitoring, and reporting on CCS-4 [10].
LEADERSHIP: STRATEGIC POLICY DIALOGUE
At the national level, the Ministry of Health (MOH) and WHO will establish a policy dialogue agenda
for each strategic priority area, as outlined in the implementation pathway. To translate this agenda

into action, the WHO Secretariat will focus on country needs and relevant resolutions and decisions
of WHO governing bodies (WHA, EB, and RC) [128,10]. Strategic policy dialogue will involve political
leaders, policymakers from multiple government agencies, and non-state actors, such as the private
sector, civil society, and academia, to garner support for MOH's efforts to promote the health and
well-being of the Seychellois population. Leveraging its trusted role as a source of knowledge and
data, WHO will effectively support and advocate for policy actions aligned with GPW14 priorities
[10].
TECHNICAL COOPERATION: STRATEGIC SUPPORT AND LEVERAGING OF PARTNERSHIPS
The WHO Secretariat will provide strategic support to enhance Seychelles' national health system,
focusing on maximizing robustness, system performance in terms of health level and equity,
responsiveness, financial risk protection, and efficiency. This support will include advice on various
aspects of health system building blocks and universal health coverage [10,128]. In collaboration with
the Government of Seychelles Ministry of Health, other government agencies, the UN system,
development partners, and local non-state actors, such as civil society organizations, WHO will
provide advocacy materials, coordinate partnerships, develop strategies, and mobilize resources. The
delivery of strategic support will be tailored to the specific context, depending on the presence of
WHO at the national level, regional office (AFRO, MCAT, Emergency Hub), or headquarters.
TECHNICAL COOPERATION: TECHNICAL ASSISTANCE AND CAPACITY BUILDING
Technical cooperation between WHO and the Government of Seychelles (GoRS) will focus on
strengthening individual and institutional capacities and providing technical assistance. WHO will
offer integrated and coordinated technical guidance, including the contextualization of norms,
standards, and guidelines, research coordination, and translation into health interventions [10,128].
SERVICE DELIVERY AND OPERATIONAL SUPPORT
Given the maturity of the national health system in Seychelles, it is assumed that the country does
not require support with service delivery, except in acute emergency situations, such as the COVID-
19 pandemic, where WHO might provide catalytic support with equipment, medicines, vaccines, or
supplies [11,62]. Additionally, WHO may provide operational support necessary for facilitating the
provision of other modalities of support indicated above

CCS MID-TERM EVALUATION
The mid-term evaluation (MTE) aims to assess the results achieved by 2027 in comparison with the
outcome indicators outlined in the monitoring, evaluation, accountability, and learning framework.
This evaluation will generate evidence for use in accelerating progress towards the achievement of
CCS-4 strategic priorities and strategic deliverables, reprogramming in subsequent biennium, and
enhancing learning and mutual accountability. It might be prudent to undertake the CCS-4 MTE
jointly with the final evaluation of the Ministry of Health National Health Strategic Plan (NHSP) 2022-
2026 to maximize efficiency. Specific objectives of the CCS-4 MTE are as follows:
1. Assess progress towards the achievement of strategic priorities and deliverables, using
predefined milestones.
2. Determine the need for reprogramming in subsequent work plans.
3. Identify challenges that may hinder the effectiveness, efficiency, and impact of the CCS-4
implementation.
4. Take necessary actions to address identified challenges and expedite progress towards
achieving strategic priorities and deliverables.
FINAL EVALUATION OF CCS
The final evaluation (SE) of the CCS-4 strategic agenda will be conducted at the end of 2030 to review
the outcomes and impact of the implementation. The SE will assess the contribution of WHO in
achieving national health development targets and those outlined in the CCS-4 results framework
and monitoring framework.
The evaluation will be based on the OECD/DAC criteria of relevance, coherence, effectiveness,
efficiency, impact, sustainability, and cross-cutting features such as equity, human rights, and gender
mainstreaming. The SE will seek to answer the following questions:

1. Relevance: Did the CCS-4 implementation align with the country's needs and priorities?
2. Coherence: How well did the CCS-4 strategic agenda fit with other interventions?
3. Effectiveness: Did the CCS-4 implementation achieve its objectives?
4. Efficiency: How effectively were resources used?
5. Impact: What difference did the CCS-4 implementation make?
6. Sustainability: Will the gains from the CCS-4 last beyond the implementation period?
The evaluation team will use a mixed methods approach, as described in the WHO Evaluation
Practice Handbook, to answer the evaluation questions. The SE will also identify key achievements,
factors that facilitated or constrained progress, lessons learned, missed opportunities, and priorities
for the next CCS cycle. The findings and recommendations of the SE will inform future collaboration
between WHO and Seychelles and guide the development of the next CCS
