1. Executive Summary
1.1. A brief description summarizing the nature of the problem addressed by the Industrial Policy
(IP).
Seychelles Economy has seen a positive growth post the global Covid-19 pandemic. However, the
vulnerabilities that we were exposed to during the Covid-19 pandemic, and including the climate
change issues continue to hover around the country’s economic stability. To that effect the
government via its National Development Strategy has prioritized a Transformative Economic
Agenda with the key theme being economic resilience, diversification and environmental
sustainability, and climate change resilience. This Industrial policy (IP) will look to directly to
support these two objectives as whilst promoting a healthier nation. This IP goes even a step further,
especially taking into account Seychelles SDG commitments and would take the form of a “Green
Industrial Policy” keeping sustainability as one of the core themes of the IP. By focusing on these
themes, the IP has set out objectives to achieve the economic transformation in a sustainable manner
using the manufacturing industry as a tool to achieve these goals.
1.2. A list of the main objectives of the Industrial Policy:
Based on extensive data analysis and consultation with the support of the EQuIP Methodology the
following IP objectives were developed:
• Objective 1: Increase higher value-added productive activities: In order to diversify the economy,
the Industrial policy wants to create an ecosystem that reduces reliance on service-based activities
and promote an increase in the higher value-added productive activities.
• Objective 2: Diversify exports to higher value-added products to increase resilience: As a small
island state there is a need for us to diversify on the number of high value-products that we export
instead of having to depend on a few specific sectors.
• Objective 3: Reduce import dependency by promoting local made products on the domestic market:
Maximize domestic benefits by empowering our population to venture into businesses using the
already existing local demand for products that are currently being imported e.g.: cinnamon powder.
• Objective 4: Increase the quality of manufacturing jobs: The current manufacturing jobs in
Seychelles are not attractive for our local workforce especially the youths. The workforce is
composed mainly of foreigners in those fields, the IP would like to address this issue so that the
return of this sector can remain in the country.
• Objective 5: Promote the emergence of Green Industry/Circular Industry: Though we are promoting
increase in the manufacturing sector we want to do it in a sustainable manner, as the protection of
our environment remains a priority for every Seychellois

2. Introduction
2.1. Brief overview of the previous IP
Seychelles has had to review its IP since the current was developed for a period of 2015-2020. This policy
was developed with the objective of addressing the cost of doing business, access to land, inclusion of STI,
quality of production, export competitiveness and intellectual property protection to support industrial
competitiveness and development.
2.2. How does the IP relate to the Government's priorities?
This Industrial Policy has been developed based on its contributions to the achievement of the National
Development Strategy (NDS) of the Seychelles. Amongst the six pillars of the NDS, the three priority areas
to which this Industrial Policy can relate the most are the Transformative Economic Agenda (TEA),
Environmental Sustainability and Climate Change Resilience and a Healthy Nation. Firstly, the IP
contributes to the TEA component of the NDS by promoting high-value activities to transform the economy
into a more diversified production structure and allow for larger benefits for the country. The IP will
contribute specifically to two elements of the TEA; diversification and inclusive growth. Secondly, the IP is
key for the achievement of the priority area of Environmental Sustainability and Climate Change Resilience
as this strategy will only promote productive activities that do not come at the expense of the environment.
And thirdly, the IP relates to the government´s priority of a Healthy Nation through the promotion of locally
produced goods, as well as ensuring safe working conditions and avoiding pollution from industry that could
have negative impact in the population´s health. In order to contribute to the above-mentioned Government´s
priorities, the IP vision has been designed as follows:
“This new Industrial Policy aims at promoting a sustainable economic transformation by diversifying into
higher value added & green industries that offer attractive employment for Seychellois.”
2.3. Methodology applied
The process for designing this Industrial Policy was guided following the “Enhancing the Quality of
Industrial Policies” (EQuIP) methodology. The methodology places emphasis on evidence-based and
participatory policy design and aims at enabling policymakers to formulate, design, review and implement
strategies for inclusive and sustainable industrial development. The methodology was developed under a
collaboration between the United Nations Industrial Development Organization (UNIDO), and the German
Development Cooperation (GDC) to support the deployment of effective industrial policy support services.
By providing a comprehensive toolkit that can support efforts in the areas of industrial diagnosis, strategy
and policy design, EQuIP aims to strengthen the self-determination of countries in the policy design process,

resulting in policies that are fully “owned” by the government and that reflect local values, capacities and
objectives.
The EQuIP methodology provides a systematic step-by-step approach to structure the IP design process. It
provides a framework for policy makers to build an Intervention Logic that maps each of the main
components of the IP with a goal-oriented approach (See Figure 1). The first step is to look into the National
Development Strategy (NDS) and understand in which way can the IP most contribute to their achievement
(Section 3). Then, based on the NDS, we defined the Industrial Policy Objectives which are the direct and
specific goals of the IP (Section 4). Once the objectives are clear, we defined Intervention Areas which are
the concrete changes that are required within the economy in order to achieve the Policy Objectives (Section
5). And last, we selected the Policy Instruments which are ultimately the specific activities and interventions
that the government will implement to achieve the changes in the Intervention Areas (Action Plan)

2.4. Process for the elaboration of this policy
The development of this Industrial Policy was done based on a participatory approach. In total, 4 national
stakeholder consultation events took place between October 2022 and April 2023. Representatives from
various national institutions were invited to discuss the different perspectives and provide relevant feedback
to ensure a realistic and context appropriate strategy. The stakeholder group included civil society, private
sector and public institutions representatives. The draft of this Policy document was shared with all
stakeholders and a validation workshop took place on the 27th of March 2021. A list of the institutions that
participated in the design process of this Industrial Policy can be found in Annex 2.

environmental facets are the focus, ensuring synergies between various policies and programs that contribute
to common goals. Furthermore, the NDS takes into account the international development commitments
such as the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and the Agenda 2063. The six priority areas for

right balance between economic growth and the preservation and conservation of natural resources must be
struck, in the short, medium, and long terms, pushing for "green," climate-smart, and sustainable thinking
throughout.

Environmental Sustainability and Climate Change Resilience and in a less direct manner, a Healthy Nation.
It is expected that this IP will contribute to TEA for a social and economic transformation by stimulating
diversification into new productive activities that offer better returns for the economy as well as better jobs
for the population.
The new IP can also contribute to Environmental Sustainability and Climate Change Resilience. Any action
undertaken by the IP to promote new industrial activities will need to ensure that environmental impacts are

3.2.1. Transformative Economic Agenda
Within the context of the IP, the aim is to contribute to the TEA through the promotion of high-value
activities that will lead to larger benefits for the country. The IP can contribute to two elements of this agenda,
namely green diversification and inclusive growth. Diversification will contribute directly to the resilience
component of the overall TEA and inclusive growth is to ensure that the future growth of the economy avails
to all stakeholders, with emphasis on the lower income bracket equal opportunity to grow and improve their
standard of living. Beyond looking at new areas of industry, the IP also seeks to create synergies with existing
sectors such as the tourism, construction, agriculture, and fisheries sectors and ensure maximum yield is
generated from the limited resources of the country. The IP will focus on fostering comprehensive, strategic
and sustainable growth across all sectors by emphasizing cross-sectoral eco-friendly practices and forging
strong, value driven connectivity across industries.
3.2.1.1. IP contribution to Diversification
Currently, the tourism sector is the main driver of the economy with most value generation being
concentrated in the service sector. This concentration creates vulnerabilities, as the heavy reliance on a single
sector leaves the country highly vulnerable to external shocks. For example, the effect of the Covid-19
pandemic on Tourism had a very heavy impact on the economy that could have been cushioned by other
economic activities, had the economy been more diversified. while fisheries sector did provide some support
to the economy during COVID and shows great potential to be one of the sectors that can support the
diversification. In addition, the future growth potential of the tourism sector will eventually reach its natural
capacity limit even after expanding and diversifying towards other forms and segments of the sector, making
Tourism an unlikely candidate to remain the engine of growth of Seychelles in the long run. Hence,
diversification into other activities is highly important to ensure stable sustainable growth and overall
resilience of the economy.

3.2.1.2. IP contribution to Inclusive growth
The TEA makes reference to the need for an Inclusive and Sustainable economic strategy to deliver
transformational change. Inclusive economic development ensures that growth benefits are not just captured
by the few but are distributed fairly across society, providing opportunities to all. If well designed and
implemented the IP can contribute to this component of the agenda by ensuring equal and decent
opportunities for Seychellois in the manufacturing sector, whether as investors and business owners or
employees in decent and well-paid jobs

3.2.2. Contributions to environmental sustainability and climate change resilience
This priority sets the scope of the nature of Industrial development that the Seychelles can look to undertake.
Through this pillar, it is expected that the IP will only promote productive activities that do not come at the
detrimental expense of our natural environment. Preservation is important as our natural capital is very
valuable to the nation given, as it currently stands, that the tourism and fishery sectors are the main pillars
of the economy and they are in turn highly dependent on a sane environment. Furthermore, vulnerabilities
as a Small Island Developing State (SIDS) include climate change, an ever-pressing threat, and so it is
imperative that we do not, in any way, contribute towards environmental degradation which will have
adverse climate change implications.
Hence, a broad consensus has been reached that this new industrialization agenda should take the form of a
“Green Industrial Policy”. The IP can therefore particularly contribute to greening existing industries, to help
reduce our economy's current environmental footprint to support the work towards our commitment to
reduce economy wide GHG emission by 26.4% by 2030. .
When looking at the aspect of industry and environment it is necessary to understand the current economy
and its effect on the environment. While emissions are relatively low in Seychelles, the topic that stood out
the most is the growing material extraction. Domestic material extraction is defined as the annual amount of
raw material extracted from the natural environment for the production of goods and services. High levels
of extraction contribute to soil degradation, water shortages, biodiversity loss, damage to ecosystem
functions and global warming exacerbation.

The biomass extraction is largely determined by wild fish catch. The depletion of bio marine life can have
long term negative impacts on livelihoods as well as the environment. Furthermore, the recent dynamics of
the fishery sector implies that there will be a need to extract more fish from the sea to expand under the
current structure that focuses on quantity rather than quality. With depleting fish stocks due to climate
change, the long-term viability of this sector is at risk. It is hence important to identify an adaptation
approach that allows for the generation of more value from a lower quantity of fish. This IP hence targets
the Greening of the Fishery sector by promoting a shift towards higher value-added products that reduce the
pressure on the fish stock. an idea which is well aligned with the new approach of fisheries ministry.
While the largest positive environmental impact of the IP will come from its contribution to the greening of
other sectors, it is also targeting environmental sustainability within the manufacturing sector. Hence, at the
interface of diversification and sustainability, this policy will promote manufacturing activities that do not
imply environmental trade-offs, e.g. by expanding the capacity for manufacturing environmentally friendly
products. These initiatives include converting bi-products from industries (e.g. waste based products) and
the production of other green products as well as an expansion of renewable energy and energy efficiency
within industry.

4. Industrial Policy Objectives
The vision of this Industrial Policy can be summarized in the following way:
This new Industrial Policy aims at promoting a sustainable economic transformation by diversifying into
higher value added & green industries that offer attractive employment for Seychellois.
Based on an in-depth industrial performance diagnostic as well as after several discussions and consultations
with the relevant stakeholders, the Ministry has identified 5 key objectives as the main priorities for the
implementation of this policy:
i. Increase higher value-added productive activities
This Industrial Policy aims at promoting high value-added productive activities to increase the
overall benefits that the manufacturing sector creates in the country.
ii. Diversify exports to higher value-added products to increase resilience
To reduce economic vulnerability, this Industrial Policy seeks to diversify exports by shifting
the current dependency on a few resource-based activities to a larger range of higher value-
added export products.
iii. Reduce import dependency by promoting locally made products on the domestic market
Seychelles aims at maximizing domestic benefits by empowering the local manufacturing
sector to venture into activities using the existing local demand for products that are currently
being imported.
iv. Increase the quality of manufacturing jobs
This Industrial Policy seeks to promote manufacturing activities that can attract the local
population and provide inclusive opportunities by offering high quality jobs in the productive
sector with better salaries and working conditions.
v. Promote the emergence of Green Industry/Circular Industry
To foster sustainability, this Industrial Policy aims at promoting manufacturing activities that
not only do not harm the natural capital, but that actively contribute to reducing the negative
impacts of other sectors in the country.

5.1. Intervention Area 1: Financing for New Industries Available
Why are the kind of new high-value added, diversified and green industrial activities for export or the

considering new manufacturing investments in the country. It is believed that, if this constraint is tackled, it
will significantly contribute to how the industrial sector could transform the country.
Recognising that resources are limited; it is not expected that through this policy the financing situation will
improve for all companies at the same time. Furthermore, spreading a limited amount of financial resources
“too thin” among too many companies is unlikely to bring about transformative impacts. Instead, it is
intended to strategically focus this intervention area on two specific groups of companies that are most likely
to bring about the change that is envisioned. On the one hand, companies that already exist and are willing
to venture into new innovative productive activities and/or shift towards new processes and products that
ensure a much higher degree of value generation than what is currently in place will be targeted. These
companies might currently be active in low value-added manufacturing or in non-manufacturing activities
(e.g. tourism), but they can be incentivized to consider broadening their engagement into modern high value-
added industrial production. On the other hand, improvements will be made for the financing for new
investors that are not yet established but are interested in starting a company in Seychelles within the areas
that are aligned with this Industrial Policy.
The focus will not be on initiatives to generally improve the access to finance through the banking sector,
instead, the idea is for the government to proactively encourage companies and to provide active support for
the kind of investments to happen in a strategic and goal-oriented manner. In the ideal scenario, anybody
that is already interested in investing into the kind of projects that this policy wants to promote will be
enabled to pursue those plans, while additional investors will be sensitized and incentivized to consider
investing into high value-added industries.
This shift in the provision of financing will contribute in particular to the policy objective 1: Increase higher

5.2. Intervention Area 2: Technology, Innovation and R&D support for New Industries
Based on the consultation with relevant stakeholders, we have identified that there is a need for improving
the existing capacities with regards to technology, innovation and R&D for new industries. Currently, most
of the manufacturing activities such as fish processing and food production taking place in the Seychelles
are rather focused on labour intensive sectors with low value-addition. These sectors tend to have more
simple production processes with limited technological capabilities and are known to be less competitive in
the international markets. Currently there is very limited dynamism in terms of companies adopting new
technologies, innovating or conducting R&D as their focus is on maintaining their current business structure.
However, in order to stimulate the emergence of new productive sectors that offer higher value creation, the
country must put efforts on R&D for innovation, technological development and product development. This
Intervention Area aims at providing the conditions and knowledge for companies that are willing to invest
into new, innovative higher value-added activities.
This Intervention Area can specifically support the achievement of Objectives 1, 2, 3,4 and 5 by providing
companies with support in the process of adopting new technologies, introducing product and process
innovations and conducting R&D activities that will lead to higher value added and innovative green
activities both for exports and for the domestic market. There is a strong interconnection between this
intervention area and intervention area 1, as financing support is one of the key elements required to facilitate
the shift and encourage more businesses to upgrade.

5.3. Intervention area 3: National Value Chains Developed
One of the key intervention areas that was prioritized through the stakeholder consultation process is the
development of national value chains. Currently, most industrial activities in the Seychelles are strongly
influenced by foreign factors, be it in the form of foreign investors, targeting foreign markets or using foreign
suppliers for the required inputs in production. As one example, because of a lack of collaboration with local
farmers and food producers and the lack of local support systems, the tourism sector largely relies on
purchasing imported food items, despite the availability of higher quality domestic alternatives. It is
recognized that this strong dependency on outside factors reduces the degree of potential domestic benefits
that the country could derive from its economic activities, in particular from the tourism sector.

New Industrial Policy Draft
The key change that this intervention area targets is the establishment of interconnections between a variety
of local actors and businesses that can multiply domestic economic linkages. This can take the form of
producer collectives that consist of a range of national primary producers (e.g. farmers) as well as processors
(e.g. food manufacturers), which are directly connected to relevant customers (e.g. hotels). One of the
frequently stated reasons for the lack of these national value chains is the lack of sufficient scale, consistency
and reliability of production. This can be tackled by government interventions and the private sector that
pool the available local resources, standardize the final products and ensure reliable logistics.

on the achievement of objective 3 with respect to the reduction of the import dependency by promoting local

consumers will be served by strong national value chains that have replaced imported goods in a significant

establishment of stronger national food value chains that are linked to the agriculture sector could particularly
boost the income opportunities of the lower income bracket of the country and hence contribute to enhanced
inclusiveness through higher wages and better working conditions.
5.3.1. Current status / performance (analytical findings)
The strong import dependency of Seychelles in terms of manufactured products was assessed in detail in
chapter 4. It is particularly noteworthy, that a large amount of the imports consists of processed food items
as well as goods that are required in the tourism industry. While the country lacks detailed data on this topic,
it is evident, that there is a lack of local products in the hotels and the service industry for the consumption
of the tourism sector.
Overall, locally made products face strong competition from imported alternatives. For example, furniture,
toiletries and food products, as those imported goods are significantly cheaper than the locally produced
ones. This pattern can also be explained by a lack of value that the market places on local products.
Accordingly, the local entrepreneurs complain about the lack of opportunities to sell their produce. Another
topic that is evident, is the fact that local producers can often not compete with imported alternatives in terms
of branding, packaging and marketing. In many cases this leads to local products being side-lined, while
foreign equivalents take their space in supermarkets and hotels.
Finally, the lack of collaboration among local businesses has been highlighted in the stakeholder
consultations. Firstly, there is limited cooperation between primary producers (e.g. farmers), which leads to
a strongly segregated supply of many goods that could find a market if they would be pooled into larger
quantities. Secondly, there is a lack of interactions between local farmers on the one hand and food
processors on the other hand. Thirdly, the main potential customers (tourism companies) are not connected
to the local producers. These three links will need to be established by this industrial policy in order to
overcome the import dependency.

reliability and at consistent quality level. Accordingly, these products will penetrate the market more easily,

objective 3. If the price differences between imported and domestic products can also be reduced through
government support, further scaling up of local production can be expected, which could in turn contribute

ANNEX 4: CRITERIAS FOR SECTOR SUPPORT
1. Products with high value added (input – output) . A value-added product is a saleable commodity
that has been enhanced with additional qualities that make it worth a higher price than the
raw materials used to make it.
2. Products that make use of local raw materials
3. Products that are developed that is not manual labor dependent (E.g.: jewelry technical
craftsmanship)
4. Products that are eco-friendly and products developed from the waste we generate (E.g.: Using by-
products from a current industrial process, recycling as well as waste to energy production)
5. Products that support the use of resources. (E.g.: solar panels)
6. If they are raw products then high-grade value products (E.g.: Sashimi grade fish, vanilla). This
would mean that less will need to be extracted from nature to generate more returns.
7. High value food products (incl. processing of agro products & high value fish)
8. Products that support the greening of a sector (E.g.: eco-friendly paints or fertilizers)
9. Products that can be used by the local tourism sector (E.g.: High-end toiletry, cosmetics and spa
products)
10. Marine biotechnology
11. Furniture and interior decoration
12. Sustainable construction material that can reduce the extraction of resources
