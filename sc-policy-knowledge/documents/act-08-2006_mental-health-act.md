1. This Act may be cited as the Mental Health Act shall be deemed to have come into operation on the 16th February, 2009

**2.** (1) In this Act —

*   **“Board”** means the National Mental Health Board established under section 11;
*   **“care”**, in relation to persons who are mentally ill, means diagnostic and therapeutic intervention for their benefit including institution-based treatment and other measures for the social integration and rehabilitation of such persons;
*   **“Commission”** means the Mental Health Commission established under section 8;
*   **“consultant-in-charge”** means a specialist psychiatrist or physician appointed to be in charge of one or more mental health facilities;
*   **“Court”** means the Supreme Court or Magistrates’ Court;
*   **“Director of Health Services”** for the purpose of this Act means the person in charge of all consultant doctors of the Victoria Hospital;
*   **“emergency admission”** means a case in which it is necessary for the welfare of a patient or in the public interest that the patient should forthwith be placed under assessment, care or control;
*   **“forensic patient”** means a person who is unfit to stand trial by reason of mental illness or is detained pursuant to an order under sections 136, 138 or 275 of the Criminal Procedure Code;
*   **“medical officer”** or **“medical practitioner”** means a qualified person licensed to practise medicine in Seychelles;
*   **“mental health facility”** means a mental health hospital or a part of a hospital or a psycho-geriatric home for mentally ill elderly persons or a clinic or health centre or other place for in-patient or out-patient treatment of individuals with mental illness, which has been declared, authorised or established under section 14;
*   **“mental illness”** means a significant occurrence of a mental or behavioural disorder exhibited by symptoms indicating a disturbance of mental functioning, including symptoms or disturbance in thought, mood, volition, perception, orientation or memory which are present to such a degree as to be considered pathological;
*   **“Minister”** means the Minister responsible for Health;
*   **“next of kin”** means spouse, child, father, mother, brother, sister, grandparent, grandchild, uncle, aunt, nephew or niece and includes any person with whom an individual suffering from a mental illness ordinarily resides or under whose responsibility or control that individual lives or has been placed;
*   **“patient”** means any individual who is suffering from mental illness and is undergoing treatment at a mental health facility;
*   **“treatment”** means medical treatment, nursing and care under medial supervision.

## PART II – ADMINISTRATION OF THE ACT

**3.** This Act shall be administered by the Minister.

**4.** The Minister shall ensure that persons performing functions under this Act —

(a) provide all patients with the best possible treatment and care; and

(b) minimise restrictions upon the liberty of patients and interference with their rights, dignity and self-respect so far as is consistent with the proper protection and care of the patients and the protection of the public.
**5.** The Minister shall appoint a consultant-in-charge for every mental health facility or for more than one such facility.

**6.** The consultant-in-charge shall report to the Director of Health Services on the admission, treatment and discharge of patients in the mental health facility.

1. The consultant-in-charge of each mental health facility shall keep a complete and up-to-date register of patients who are admitted, discharged, transferred to another facility, mechanically restrained or confined in seclusion, and of those who have died at the facility.

**8.**
(1) There is established a Mental Health Commission for the purposes of this Act.

(2) The Commission shall consist of —

(a) the Director of Health Services who shall be the chairperson of the Commission;

(b) a medical specialist in the field of psychiatry;

(c) a magistrate or a legal practitioner with at least 3 years’ experience;

(d) a person with the qualifications and experience in the field of psychiatric nursing; and

(e) a representative of the Ministry responsible for Social Affairs.

(3) The members of the Commission referred to in subsection (2), other than the Director of Health Services, shall be appointed by the Minister to hold office for a period of 3 years and shall be eligible for reappointment.

(4) The Commission shall meet at least once in every month.

(5) Three members, at least one of whom shall be a medical practitioner, shall constitute a quorum.

(6) The decisions of the Commission shall be taken by a majority of votes of all the members present and voting at any meeting and, in the event of an equality of votes, the chairperson or the member presiding in the absence of the chairperson shall have a casting vote.

(7) No member of the Commission shall vote on any matter in relation to any patient who has been treated by that member.

(8) The members of the Commission shall be paid such fees or allowances as the Minister may determine.

**9.** The functions of the Commission shall be to —

1. review matters relating to the overall management of patients;
2. inspect with sufficient frequency every mental health facility to ensure that the conditions, treatment and care of patients comply with the provisions of this Act;
3. bring to the notice of the Board any breach or suspected breach of discipline, professional misconduct or violation of patient’s rights;
4. refer any suspected criminal offence under this Act to the police;
5. report to the Board any problem relating to the living conditions and the standard of care at any mental health facility.

**10.**
(1) Where the Commission receives a complaint that a person has been admitted to or kept at a mental health facility against the person’s will the Commission shall enquire into the complaint immediately.

(2) The Commission shall keep a record of its proceedings and decisions.

(3) The Commission shall furnish to the Board for every period of 3 months a report of its decisions and such other information as the Board may from time to time require.

**11.**
(1) There is established, for the purposes of this Act, a National Mental Health Board.

(2) The Board shall consist of —
- (a) the Principal Secretary of the Ministry or Department of Health who shall be the chairperson of the Board;
- (b) the Principal Secretary of the Ministry responsible for Social Affairs or the representative of the Principal Secretary;
- (c) a medical specialist in the field of psychiatry;
- (d) the Director of Health Services;
- (e) a Judge nominated by the Chief Justice;
- (f) a person qualified in the field of clinical psychology or occupational therapy;
- (g) a nurse of not less than 7 years’ experience in the field of psychiatric care;
- (h) a person having adequate qualifications and experience in the field of social work.

(3) The members of the Board referred to in subsection (2) (c), (e), (f), (g) and (h) shall be appointed by the Minister to hold office for a period of 3 years and shall be eligible for reappointment.

(4) The members of the Board shall be paid such fees or allowances as may be determined by the Minister.

(5) The Board shall meet at least once every three months or upon the written request of not less than 3 members.

(6) Four members of the Board shall constitute a quorum.

(7) Subject to this section, the Board may regulate its meetings and proceedings in such manner as it thinks fit.

**12.** The functions of the Board shall be —

1. to plan and manage mental health care in Seychelles;

(b) to promote standards of good practice and the efficiency of mental health services;

(c) to promote the physical and mental health of patients and to protect their rights;

(d) to make recommendations regarding measures for the occupational health care of patients and for their rehabilitation;

(e) to investigate complaints and grievances of patients, next of kin, visitors and the staff.

**13.** The Board shall —

1. furnish a report of its activities in each year to the Minister and such other information with respect to the activities of the Board as the Minister may require;
2. inspect each mental health facility at reasonably regular intervals and interview any person in the facility if necessary.

**14.**(1) The Minister may, upon the recommendation of the Board, declare by notice in the Gazette any hospital, clinic, health centre or home or other premises to be a mental health facility for in-patient or out-patient treatment.

(2) The Supreme Court may authorise places within prisons to be places for the reception and treatment of remandees or convicts who are mentally ill during the term of their remand or imprisonment.

(3) The Minister may, on receipt of a written application and after consultation with the Board, authorise the establishment of a private mental facility which shall then be conducted in conformity with the provisions of this Act.
# PART III – EXAMINATION, ADMISSION AND REVIEW

**15.**(1) A physician or a psychiatrist shall examine a person where —

1. The person appears to be suffering from a mental illness;
2. The person’s case necessitates an examination,

if that person or the person’s next of kin consents to the examination.

(2) Where the physician or psychiatrist is informed or has sufficient reason to believe that a person has been brought to a facility against the person’s will, the physician or the psychiatrist shall not examine, admit or treat the person unless the physician or psychiatrist has reasonable ground to believe that the person constitutes a danger to himself or to other persons due to mental illness.

**16.**(1) A person may be admitted as a patient to a mental health facility on the person’s own written request if the person has attained the age of 18 years.

(2) A person who is mentally ill and is incapable of expressing consent to receive treatment may be admitted as an involuntary patient to a mental health facility on the application of the person’s next of kin.

(3) A request for admission to a particular mental health facility may be refused if —

1. the consultant-in-charge is not satisfied that the person has a mental illness that requires immediate treatment;
2. the consultant-in-charge is of the opinion that the person has a mental illness that is not amenable to treatment at all or in that facility; or
3. the accommodation available at that facility is insufficient or unsuitable:

Provided that arrangements shall be made, where it is necessary to admit the person to another facility.

(4) The consultant-in-charge may refer an untreatable person who is a danger to himself or others to the Court with a recommendation for preventive confinement.

**17.**(1) A person who has gained admission voluntarily to a mental health facility may leave the facility at any time upon giving to the consultant-in-charge an oral or written notice of intent to leave.

(2) Subject to validation by the Court, an order for the continuation of the detention of an in-patient who has stayed for a continuous period of 12 months in a mental health facility may be made by the consultant-in-charge if the consultant-in-charge is satisfied that the patient requires further in-patient treatment and the continued detention is in the interest of the patient’s health and safety or of the protection of other persons.

(3) The admission of a patient who is not a citizen of Seychelles shall be on a written application approved by the Minister.

**18.**(1) A police officer may take and convey to a mental health facility, through the accident and emergency department at any time any person who, the officer reasonably believes, is suffering from mental illness and —

1. is not under proper care and control, or is being cruelly treated or neglected by any relative or other person having charge of that person;
2. is dangerous to himself or others;
3. due to such illness is acting in a manner which is offensive to public decency; or
4. is dependent on alcohol or drugs.

(2) The Police shall assist in the conveyance of a person to a mental health facility where —

1. the next of kin of the person has reasonable grounds to believe that the person is suffering from mental illness and is resisting removal to a mental health facility; or
2. a request for the conveyance is made by a mental health facility in respect of a person who has escaped therefrom or has failed to return to the facility at the expiry or cancellation of his leave of absence or has failed to comply with a condition subject to which leave was granted.

(3) For the purpose of subsections (1) and (2), police officers may use reasonable force to gain entry into any premises or to apprehend the person concerned.

(4) The consultant-in-charge of a mental health facility to which a person is taken under this section shall admit that person for a period not exceeding 72 hours for the examination or treatment of the person.

(5) The consultant-in-charge may, after the examination, hand over the person admitted into the mental health facility to the care of any relative, or detain the person as an involuntary patient subject to validation of the detention by the Court.
(6) Where the police officer takes a person to a mental health facility under this section, the officer may, if requested, render such assistance to the physician or psychiatrist carrying out the examination or treatment as may be necessary for the purpose.

**19.** (1) Where a patient is admitted to a mental health facility, the psychiatrist or physician admitting the patient shall —

1. perform a general medical examination of the patient;
2. whenever practicable, inform the patient or the patient’s next of kin of the reasons for the patient’s admission.
3. whenever practicable, inform the patient of the patient’s rights and duties under this Act in the language with which the patient is most familiar;
4. enter the specific reasons for admission in the patient’s medical record;
5. except in the case of a person ordered by the Court to be confined either on account of unfitness to stand trial or following an acquittal on grounds of insanity, file an admission report in the patient’s record;

(f) keep a complete record in respect of the patient including adequate information about the patient’s mental condition, any treatment administered or any refusal of examination, treatment or admission.

(2) Following the admission or continuing detention of a person as an involuntary patient, the consultant-in-charge shall, except where the patient is discharged under section 20 —

1. in all cases except those of forensic patients, forward a copy of the admission report to the Court within 72 hours of the admission together with a request for validation of the admission or continued detention of the person;

(b) enter the name and date of birth of the patient and such other particulars as the consultant-in-charge deems appropriate in a register kept for that purpose.

(3) Where the Court is satisfied that a request for validation of admission or continued detention is in order, it shall issue an order for validation of the admission or continued detention and cause a copy of the same to be forwarded to the consultant-in charge.

(4) Where the Court is not satisfied that the procedures under this Act have been followed or that the admission or continued detention of a person as a patient is in conformity with the provisions of this Act, it may make such inquiry as it thinks fit and thereafter validate the admission or continued detention or refuse to validate it, in which case the person will stand discharged.

**20.** (1) Subject to subsection (2), where upon admission a patient is attended on by a physician or a nurse, a psychiatrist shall within 24 hours of the patient’s admission, re-examine the patient to review the patient’s admission and treatment and shall either confirm the admission or discharge the patient.

(2) Subsection (1) shall not apply to a forensic patient.

**21.** (1) The Commission shall review every case of a patient –

(a) referred to the Commission by the consultant-in-charge who has treated the patient;

(b) who has been in a mental health facility for more than 6 months; or

(c) in respect of whom any interested party has requested that a review be carried out.

(2) Upon such a review, the Commission shall determine whether —

(a) the patient should be transferred or discharged;

(b) the treatment or living conditions of a patient should be changed:

*Provided that a forensic patient shall not be discharged without a Court order or the order of the Minister, as the case may be.*

**22.** (1) Where a Court in the course of a trial or preliminary inquiry finds that a person is unfit to stand trial and makes an order for the detention of that person in a mental health facility, the order shall be sufficient authority for the police to convey the person forthwith to the facility specified in the order.

(2) On receipt of an order under subsection (1), the consultant-in-charge shall immediately inform the Commission of the order and shall detain the person referred to in the order as a forensic patient for the period specified in the order.

(3) The Director of Health Services shall, subject to such order as may be made by the Court, forward at the end of every detention period a report to the Court assessing the person's ability to stand trial.

(4) Where during the detention of a person under subsection (1) the Commission is satisfied that the person is able to stand trial, the Commission shall inform the Court accordingly.
**23.**(1) Where a person is detained pursuant to a Court order upon being found not guilty of a crime but insane, the consultant-in-charge shall immediately inform the Commission of the order and the person shall be detained as a forensic patient.

(2) The Commission shall submit to the Minister a report on the mental health of the person as early as possible but not later than 12 months after the commencement of the detention and thereafter at intervals of 12 months.

(3) Such a person shall be discharged on the written order of the Minister following a report from the Commission that the person has fully recovered from the person's mental disorder and no longer constitutes any danger to himself or other persons.

# PART IV - PROTECTION OF PATIENTS AND THEIR PROPERTY

**24.**(1) There shall be as far as practicable a separate unit or premises for each of the following categories of patients —

1. forensic patients;
2. patients suffering from acute mental illness;
3. psycho-geriatric patients;
4. mentally retarded patients;
5. patients other than those listed above.

(2) There shall be for all categories of patients separate units for male and female patients.

(3) The consultant-in-charge of a mental health facility shall ensure that every patient —

(a) is provided with health care and living conditions that are conducive to human dignity and proper treatment; and

(b) is prevented from causing harm to himself or others.

**25.** Where a person lacks capacity to exercise the right to consent to treatment or experimentation, or the right to confidentiality or access to records, such right may be exercised on behalf of such person by a next of kin.

**26.**(1) Any patient or the patient's next of kin who alleges a violation of the patient's rights or who is dissatisfied with the admission, refusal to admit, treatment or discharge of the patient or the living conditions of the patient at a mental health facility may lodge a complaint with the consultant-in-charge of the mental health facility.

(2) Where the consultant-in-charge fails to attend to the complaint within one week of receiving it or where the patient or the patient's next of kin is dissatisfied with the decision of the consultant-in-charge, the patient or the patient's next of kin as the case may be may, within 30 days of making the complaint or the decision whichever is the later, apply to the Commission for a review of the failure to attend to the complaint or the decision of the consultant-in-charge.

(3) An application to the Commission under subsection (2) shall be heard at its first sitting following the receipt of the application.

(4) Where the patient or the patient's next of kin is dissatisfied with the decision of the Commission, he may within 21 days of the decision, apply to the Supreme Court for a review of the decision.

**27.**(1) The consultant-in-charge of a mental health facility shall ensure that an individual account for each patient admitted to the mental health facility is maintained giving details of the following —

1. any money belonging or any monetary benefit accruing to the patient including any funds made available for the patient by his next of kin or any other person;
2. any remuneration paid to the patient in consideration of any work performed by the patient during his stay at the mental health facility.

(2) The consultant-in-charge of the mental health facility shall ensure the transfer of any balance standing in the account of a patient to the patient personally or to the patient's next of kin on the patient's discharge from the mental health facility, or to the legal representative of the patient if the patient dies during the patient's stay at the mental health facility.

(3) On or before 31st January of each year, the consultant-in-charge of a mental health facility shall send to the Commission an annual report on patients' accounts, specifying in respect of each account the opening and closing balance and any transaction on the account.

(4) The Commission may call for further information from the consultant-in-charge concerned and where the Commission suspects that an irregularity has been committed in respect of a patient's account, it may refer the matter to the Board.

**28.** On receipt of any report or information that a person who is mentally ill is being wrongly or cruelly treated or neglected in any manner, the Commission shall make necessary inquiries, whether by itself or through a psychiatric nurse, social worker, clinical psychologist or a police officer, and thereafter cause the person to be admitted to an appropriate mental health facility, as it sees fit, for care and treatment.
# PART V – TREATMENT, LEAVE AND DISCHARGE

**29.**(1) No person, including a forensic patient, shall be administered treatment at any mental health facility unless the person has been diagnosed by a physician as suffering from a mental illness and the person or the person's next of kin has consented to the treatment.

(2) Where a person is unable to give the consent and the next of kin cannot be traced or refuses to give the consent, the treating psychiatrist shall pursue a treatment plan to the best of his clinical judgment.

(3) No treatment by way of psychosurgery or electroconvulsive therapy or any non-psychiatric treatment shall be administered to any patient without the consent of the patient or the patient's next of kin and the advice of the treating psychiatrist:

> Provided however that where the patient is under the age of 18 years, or is unable to give consent, the consent of the next of kin and the advice of the treating psychiatrist shall be sufficient.

(4) Details of every psychosurgical, electroconvulsive or non-psychiatric treatment administered to a patient and of any consent shall be entered in the record of each patient.

**30.**(1) The treating psychiatrist shall draw up an individual treatment plan in respect of every patient as soon as practicable after the patient's admission.

(2) The patient or the patient's next of kin may participate in the formulation of the treatment plan which shall include —

1. the nature, side effects and expected duration of the treatment;
2. (b) the nature and duration of any other non-psychiatric treatment that may be required.

(3) Where a treating psychiatrist considers that it is urgently necessary to administer treatment to a patient under section 29(2) in order to prevent immediate harm to the patient or other persons, as the case may be, the psychiatrist may administer such treatment prior to the submission of a treatment plan under this section.

**31.**(1) A patient may be transferred or referred for treatment from one mental health facility to another where —

(a) the patient requests the transfer or referral and the available resources allow the transfer or referral;
(b) a given facility is over-crowded or is inadequately equipped; or
(c) the consultant-in-charge of the mental health facility concerned deems it necessary.

(2) Subsection (1)(a) shall not apply to a forensic patient.

**32.** A patient who has been admitted to a mental health facility may be removed for treatment abroad with the approval of the Minister.

**33.**(1) A person who wishes to take a patient into that person's custody and care may apply to the consultant-in-charge of the mental health facility who may release the patient into the custody and care of that person upon such terms and conditions as may be specified in writing by the consultant-in- charge or upon a Court order.

(2) A person who takes a patient from any mental health facility under subsection (1) but is subsequently unable or unwilling to continue to take care of the patient shall report the matter to the consultant-in-charge of that mental health facility and the consultant-in-charge may admit the patient back to the mental facility on the terms and conditions on which the patient had been admitted before the patient's release.

**34.**(1) The consultant-in-charge of a mental health facility may, after consultation with the Director of Health Services, grant leave to a patient other than a forensic patient for a period of more than 3 months where the Director is satisfied that —

1. the patient does not present a danger to any person; and
2. adequate family or community support is available for the patient.

(2) The Director of Health Services may, at any time, cancel any leave of absence granted under this section and recall the patient.

(3) Where a patient fails to return to the mental health facility after the expiry of the patient's leave or after having been recalled by the Director of Health Services, the Police shall, on the written request of the consultant-in-charge, apprehend and convey the patient to the facility.

**35.**(1) Every patient admitted to a mental health facility under this Act shall remain admitted until the patient is granted temporary leave of absence or is removed, released or discharged in accordance with this Act.

(2) Where a patient escapes from or dies in a mental health facility, the consultant-in-charge of the facility shall notify such occurrence to the patient's next of kin and where applicable to the Police or the Court.
(3) A patient who has escaped or disappeared may be apprehended and conveyed back to the mental health facility.

(4) All persons who had previously been notified of the escape of a patient shall be notified of the readmission when the patient is readmitted to the facility.

**36.** (1) The consultant-in-charge of a mental health facility shall discharge a patient admitted to the facility as soon as reasonably practicable where —

1. the consultant-in-charge is satisfied that the patient has recovered;
2. the Court has under section 19(4) refused to validate the admission of the patient; or
3. The consultant-in-charge has obtained an order from the Director of Health Services or the Court for the discharge of the patient.

(2) The consultant-in-charge shall, where it is intended to discharge a patient who is suspected to have committed an offence, notify the Police at least 24 hours prior to the time of such discharge.

**37.** The consultant-in-charge shall, through its staff trained in dealing with patients suffering from mental illness, keep in touch with, and monitor the health and welfare of, any patient discharged from the mental health facility.

# PART VI - OFFENCES

**38.** Any person who knowingly makes a false declaration as to the state of mind of any other person with intent to cause the admission or treatment of that other person in a mental health facility or the removal of that person from such a facility shall be guilty of an offence and liable on conviction to a fine of R50,000 or to imprisonment for 5 years.

**39.** (1) Any medical officer, psychiatrist, officer or other employee of a mental health facility, or any guardian or carer of a patient who commits any act of cruelty to, or abuse or willful neglect of, any patient shall be guilty of an offence and liable on conviction to a fine of R50,000 and imprisonment for 5 years.

(2) Nothing in this section shall make any member of the staff of a mental health facility or any guardian or carer of a patient liable for an offence if such member of staff or guardian or carer acts in good faith taking steps considered necessary in the interest of a patient or to prevent the patient from causing physical injury to others.

(3) In this section, "abuse" means any act or omission which causes or may cause injury or death and includes —

1. the use of excessive force when placing a patient in bodily restraints or in seclusion;
2. the physical assault of a patient;
3. the sexual assault of or consensual sexual connection with a patient.

**40.** Any person who allows, induces, facilitates or connives at the escape of a patient being conveyed to or from, or while under care and treatment in, a mental health facility or who knowingly harbours any patient who has escaped from a mental health facility shall be guilty of an offence and liable on conviction to a fine of R50,000 and imprisonment for 5 years.

**41.** (1) Any person who, not being a psychiatrist or a physician, intentionally signs a medical certificate for the purpose of any of the provisions of this Act shall be guilty of an offence and liable on conviction to a fine of R25,000 and imprisonment for 1 year.

(2) Any psychiatrist or physician who intentionally states anything in a certificate under this Act which he knows to be untrue shall be guilty of an offence and liable on conviction to a fine of R25,000 and imprisonment for 1 year.

(3) A psychiatrist or physician who signs any certificate or order for the purposes of this Act without having seen and personally examined the person to whom the certificate or order relates shall be guilty of an offence and liable on conviction to a fine of R25,000 and imprisonment for 1 year.

**42.** Any person who, without the consent of the consultant-in-charge of a mental health facility, gives or sell any article or commodity of any kind to any patient shall be guilty of an offence and liable on conviction to a fine of R5,000.

**43.** (1) Any psychiatrist, physician or nurse who conducts any clinical trials or experimental treatment on any patient without that patient's informed consent or carries out any mode of treatment having no psychiatric or therapeutic purpose and not having been approved by the Minister shall be guilty of an offence and liable on conviction to a fine of R50,000 and imprisonment for 2 years.

(2) Any person who carries out any tubal ligation or vasectomy of a patient without the consent of patient's next of kin shall be guilty of an offence and liable on conviction to a fine of R50,000 and imprisonment for 5 years:

*Provided however that where the patient is unable to give consent, the consent of the patient may be dispensed with.*

**44.** Any psychiatrist or physician who signs any order or certificate under this Act in respect of any patient of whom that psychiatrist or physician is the next of kin shall be guilty of an offence and liable on conviction to a fine of R25,000 and imprisonment for 1 year.

**45.** (1) Any psychiatrist, physician, nurse or other health worker or any other person discharging functions under this Act who divulges any personal information relating to a patient obtained in the course of performing professional duties or the administration of this Act shall be guilty of an offence and liable on conviction to a fine of R50,000.
(2) Subsection (1) does not prevent a person from —

1. divulging information if authorised or required to do so by law or by the person's employer; or

(b) divulging statistical or other data that could not reasonably be expected to lead to the identification of any person to whom it relates.

**46.** Any consultant-in-charge who fails to deliver an account as required under section 27 shall be guilty of an offence and liable on conviction to a fine of R10,000 and imprisonment for 6 months.

**47.** Any psychiatrist, physician, nurse or other health worker who without just cause refuses to provide needed professional services to a patient shall be guilty of an offence and liable on conviction to a fine of R50,000 and imprisonment for 2 years.

# PART VII - MISCELLANEOUS

**48.** The Minister may, in appropriate cases, require patients to pay part of the cost of their treatment and care.

**49.** (1) Any person who does any act in pursuance of this Act shall not be under any civil or criminal liability in respect thereof if the Court is satisfied that the person acted in good faith and with reasonable care.

(2) No proceedings under this Act shall be commenced after the expiry of six months from the act complained of, or in the case of a continuance of injury or damage, after the expiry of six months from the discovery thereof.

**50.** (1) Patients in a mental health facility and discharged patients who are being monitored under section 37 shall —

1. comply with agreed care and treatment programmes;
2. refrain from assaulting any staff member; and
3. be of good behaviour.

(2) Where a patient repeatedly frustrates the purposes of the patient's admission by unreasonably withholding consent to, or refusing to follow, treatment or by repeatedly being violent, the consultant-in-charge shall submit a report on the case to the Director of Health Services who may, after considering the matter, discharge the patient or order that he be detained in a unit for the mentally ill in a prison or in any high security facility as deemed fit.

**51.** (1) The Minister may, in consultation with the Board, make regulations —

1. for the control and management of mental health facilities and the standards to be maintained by them;
2. that are necessary or expedient for giving effect to he provisions of the Act.

(2) Regulations made under subsection (1) may provide that any person who contravenes them shall be guilty an offence and liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding R20,000.

**52.** (1) Wherever in any written law any reference to a lunatic or to an insane person or to lunacy or to insanity or to any mental asylum is contained that reference shall be read and construed as a reference to a patient or individual with mental illness within the meaning of this Act, or, as the case may be, to mental illness or to a mental health facility.

(2) The provisions of this Act and any regulations made thereunder shall apply to any person received or admitted into a mental hospital or otherwise dealt with under the Mental Treatment Act (Cap 127) prior to its repeal, whether pursuant to a Court order or otherwise, as if such person were admitted or dealt with in a mental health facility under this Act and such admission has been validated or sanctioned by a Court order, as the case may be, under this Act.

(3) Any act or thing done or document executed under the Mental Treatment Act (Cap 127) prior to its repeal shall be deemed to have been done or executed under this Act and shall not be invalidated by reason of the repeal.

**53.** The Mental Treatment Act (Cap 127) is hereby repealed.

**54.** Notwithstanding section 53, any subsidiary legislation made under the repealed Act shall continue to have effect until repealed or revoked by orders or regulations made under this Act.

# MENTAL TREATMENT ACT

**SUBSIDIARY LEGISLATION**

## SECTION 7

### MENTAL TREATMENT (MENTAL HOSPITAL) REGULATIONS

[25th June, 1951]

**1.** These regulations may be cited as the Mental Treatment (Mental Hospital) Regulations.

**2.** The Superintendent shall, subject to these regulations and the orders of the Chief Medical Officer and any medical officer by him deputed, have full authority over the care, treatment, lodging and dietary of the patients in the Mental Hospital.

**3.** The scale of rations shall be such as may from time to time be communicated in writing by the Chief Medical Officer to the Superintendent with the approval of the Minister.

**4.** Every person admitted to the Mental Hospital shall pay such charges, if any, as the Chief Medical Officer may prescribe.

**5.** (1) The visiting days and hours shall be such as may be published by the Chief Medical Officer in a notice exhibited at the entrance to the Mental Hospital. The Superintendent may in his absolute discretion exclude any visitor.

(2) Visitors shall comply with all reasonable requests made to them by the Hospital staff.
