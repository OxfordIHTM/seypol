# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Available data suggests that Seychelles’ current demographic feature will result in a set of crucial population concerns that has the real potential to dominate the local development agenda in this century. Some of the key challenges highlighted in the policy include an ageing population and its subsequent socio-economic impacts, the impact of social ills on populations in their prime ages of productivity, and the current rate of migration. All these trends will require the development of comprehensive, innovative and integrated strategies to ensure sustainable development.

This document describes the policy on population and proposes specific strategies for the attainment of its goals. It builds on a previous policy that spanned from 2007 to 2012. It is influenced by current engagement of the Government in the implementation of the **Seychelles Vision 2033** and the development of the first **National Development Strategy 2019 – 2023**. The national development strategy is intended to become an enduring mechanism for guiding and coordinating national efforts and initiatives for sustainable development in Seychelles, through multiple, diverse and wide stakeholder participation. On this very same premise, the Population Policy addresses relevant issues in the relationship between population and Socio Economic Development. In addition, it looks at the interface between population and other cross cutting issues namely gender and community participation. As was the case in the previous policy emphasis remains on the mainstreaming of population factors into sectoral policies and strategies with the core role of coordinating and influencing other policies, strategies and programmes that ensure sustainable development of the people. At the heart of the policy is the realization that sustainable growth cannot be achieved unless population issues are integrated into a variety of efforts.

Nations are increasingly judged by the wellbeing of their people; by levels of health, nutrition and education; by the civil and political liberties enjoyed by its citizens, by the protection guaranteed to children and by provision made for the vulnerable and disadvantaged. The revised policy has as one of its goal the achievement of the **SDGs**. Ultimately the effectiveness of this policy depends on the commitment to these issues by the stakeholders and intended beneficiaries.

# 1.0 BACKGROUND

In 2007, the first Seychelles National Population Policy was published. This policy came in the aftermath of the World Population Programme of Action based on the 1994 Cairo International Conference on Population and Development (ICPD) and the African Common position as expressed in the Dakar/ NGOR Declaration on Population Conference(DND) and was greatly influenced by the principles being promoted therein. The thrust of this first National Population was to provide a framework and guidelines for the integration of population variables into the development process with the purpose of harmonizing population dynamics with socio economic dynamics. This is particularly important for hastening the attainment of sustainable and equitable development in the country.

In the process of implementation of the 2007 National Population Policy over a period of 5 years, some successes have unmistakably been made. One of which has been the increased awareness amongst stakeholders of population issues. Additionally, population issues such as poverty, migration, women equality and empowerment, family, sexual reproductive health, HIV/AIDS, people with special needs, population ageing and environment sustainability was integrated for the first time in a 5-year plan of action, and was further integrated into other national policies and development plans.

However, some constraints have remained and new ones have emerged. The revision of this policy has identified certain key shortcomings including inadequate commitment to the intervention strategies laid out in the National Population Plan of Action 2007 and inadequate integration of population issues within sector polices and plans. This is linked to the major challenge of creating better understanding of the linkages between population and development issues. Other challenges include the strengthening of institutional frameworks to facilitate the implementation of population and development programmes; strengthening
of the Population Unit; building local capacity for data collection and analysis, including sex-disaggregated data; and strengthening the monitoring and evaluation framework to improve programme implementation and sustainability. These remain foreseeable challenges of which new dynamic strategies need to be adopted from the onset of the development process of this second National Population Policy to allow every prospect for an effective implementation.

New developments that have taken place nationally and internationally in the 12 years that has passed require that this new policy respond to current priorities in population and development programmes. To ensure that this becomes a reality several consultations with stakeholders have taken place from the time the initial policy reached its expiry and throughout the process to develop this new one with the aim that the final product responds to the need of the time.

This policy strongly recognizes that one of the overall objectives of developing population policies is to create synergies between the various sectoral interventions, in order to achieve the different goals. Various stakeholders including the Government, Civil society and even the private sector have a leading role in the promotion of the policy and its strategies.

## 2.0 INTRODUCTION

Population Policy has been defined as actions taken explicitly or implicitly by public authorities in order to prevent, delay or address imbalances between demographic variables on one hand and the social economic and political goals on the one hand (May, 2012).

The Government of Seychelles has consistently upheld the need to protect and nurture its people and ensure the development of a sustainable and equitable society. In the Constitution of the Republic of Seychelles (1993), fundamental rights have been enshrined to ensure this; such as the right to education and the right to adequate and decent shelter.
Furthermore, the Government is a party to various international Conventions and Resolutions which reinforce its commitment to sustaining a human centered development, including: The **Conventions on the Rights of the Child (1990)**, the **Convention on the Elimination of all forms of Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW - 1992)**, the **ICPD Programme of Action (1994)**, the **Copenhagen Declaration on Social Development (1995)**, the **Beijing Platform for Action (1995)**, and the **Millennium Declaration (2000)**. In 2006, Seychelles also ratified the **Convention of the Rights Persons with Disabilities**. These instruments place important obligations on its member states to act in the interest of the groups whose rights these instruments aim to protect.

The 2019 Population Policy is formulated in the context of economic growth and sustainable development. It emphasizes a systematic integration of population variables into development planning. It represents a broad recognition that the population is the nation’s most valuable resource and is both the instrument and objective of national development.

## 2.2 Guiding principles
The implementation of the population policy will be guided by the following principles:

1. Respect for the fundamental human rights and freedoms relating to social, cultural, political, and religious beliefs and practices.
2. Recognition of the cyclical and dualistic nature of social and economic development and its influence on population dynamics and vice versa.
3. Recognition of the critical role of gender equality and equity in national development
4. Reaffirmation of the rights of all vulnerable groups
5. Prudent exploitation of the country’s non-renewable resources taking into consideration the needs of future generation
6. Recognition of the multi sectoral nature of population issues and the critical need to have a multi sectoral approach for successful implementation
7. The family as the basic unit of society
8. Couples and individual right to decide freely and responsibly the number and spacing of their children as well as to have access to information, and education to do so.

## 2.3 Policy Focus

The strategic orientations of the policy are based on:

* Enhanced awareness across all levels of the linkages between population, resources, the environment, poverty eradication efforts and sustainable development.
* Prioritization of population concerns and their accompanying actions
* Increasing the capacity of planners in mainstreaming population issues in development.
* Active involvement and commitment in initiating, implementing and financing actions to address population concerns.
* Strengthened efforts across sectors in the collection, processing, analysis and dissemination of disaggregated population data
* Alignment with international commitments

The NHSP aims to:

1. Increase expectation of life at birth
2. Reduce incidence, prevalence and mortality associated with priority non-communicable and communicable diseases
3. Increase level of satisfaction of the people and of health professionals with the existing health services and
4. Improve overall wellbeing of all people in Seychelles
# The Seychelles National Gender Policy 2016
The Seychelles National Gender recognises that to achieve sustainable development and full empowerment, it is vital that all emerging risks and long term challenges are analysed ad addressed with a gender lens. Gender equity is one of the cross cutting themes to be mainstreamed within this policy.

# The Family Policy 2018
In 2015, the Social Affairs Department undertook to develop a National Family Policy. The policy is a direct initiative of the Social Renaissance Programme aimed at adding new impetus in the quest for a more stable and functional society. The policy attempts to strike a balance; one that respects the right of everyone to form a family while preserving the family unit as the natural and fundamental element of society as is laid down in the Constitution of the country. It recognizes that the primary responsibility for care giving lies with parents and other family members. However, when and where necessary, Government will intervene to ensure the protection of family members against all forms of abuse and discrimination.

# The Ageing Policy 2018
Declines in fertility and increases in longevity are producing very substantial shifts in the population age structure, such that the share of children is shrinking while that of older persons continues to grow. This trend is expected to continue and even accelerate in the next few decades. The goal of the National Ageing Policy is to maintain adequate living standards for people, as they grow older, while recognizing and harnessing their skills and experience and encouraging harmonious intergenerational transfers.

# National Human Resource Development Policy and Strategies 2018
This document is expected to support the country’s effort to develop the necessary skills and competencies to support its growing economy, which depends largely upon the services sector, namely tourism, ICT, and real estate. The Policy and strategies aims to ensure that the conditions necessary for the development of human capital is available.
# 3.0 THE SOCIO ECONOMIC SETTING

## 3.1 Seychelles as a small island developing state

As a small island developing state, Seychelles is characterized by its small population and geographic size, remoteness from major markets and limited natural resources which leads to a heavy reliance on external resources linked to tourism, consumer products and capital input, costly public administration per person, climate change and sea level rise. Seychelles’ narrow resource base and other vulnerabilities as a SIDS have resulted in a heavy dependence on external financial flows for its economic and social development. The special case of Small Island Developing States (SIDS) has been given prominence by numerous global meetings and recommendations including the Rio + 20 in 2012 and the conference declarations, the future we want (2012) and the 2014 Samoa, UN Conference on SIDS, which resulted in the Samoa Pathway. These recommendations acknowledge amongst other things the need to integrate economic, social and environmental issues by recognizing their linkages for the achievement of sustainable development.

Seychelles is ranked 62nd out of 189 countries and territories in the Human Development Report 2017 placing it in the high human development category. This ranking was based on the progress made by Seychelles in each of the HDI (Human Development Index) indicators; between 1990 and 2017. It reflects Seychelles’ life expectancy at birth, which has increased by 2.9 years, mean years of schooling increased by 2.1 years and expected years of schooling increased by 3.4 years and Seychelles’ GNI per capita increased by about 78.0 percent.

Seychelles has achieved seven of the eight Millennium Development Goals (MDGs) and is now fully engaged in the pursuing the SDG agenda. The Population Policy direction of the country needs to be aligned to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDG), which is supported by a Global Indicator Framework expected to improve data availability on certain key population concerns.

Investment in human development has been an essential element of all policies and National Development Plans (NDP) in Seychelles since independence. Nevertheless, Seychelles, as a
Small island state is not exempted from the many socio-economic problems faced by many countries. Seychelles high per income status has affected the country’s ability to obtain support from the international community, especially long-term loans and over the years, the country has had to revert to short-term loans to ensure that priority projects are realised.

The small population base, brain drain and the cost of tertiary education all result in a shortage of skilled manpower in every sector. There is the continuous need to build up capacity in terms of infrastructure, facilities, equipment and material; a need, which the local market cannot respond to adequately. The increase in foreign unskilled labour, has serious implications not only economically but also socially and culturally. Low productivity, poor work ethics amongst Seychellois workers and a psychology of dependence, threatens to jeopardize the achievements reached so far. Sound people-centred policies managed in a judicious manner on the basis of clear and focused priorities with specific measurable targets and outcomes are key to sustaining development.

During the early 2000’s Seychelles was experiencing macroeconomic difficulties that affected the country’s economic development. In 2008, the economy plunged to near collapse. Through the intervention of IMF and the World Bank, the government conducted prudent macroeconomic policies and implemented comprehensive and bold structural reforms. The economy steadily improved but economic growth continued to fluctuate. Real GDP per capita (USD) growth rate was at 0.6% in 2017. The Government has adopted several strategies such as moving the economy significantly away from being centrally planned to a market economy with increased participation of the private sector, streamlining Government workforce and better debt management. These measures have shown result in recent years with an increase of economic growth.

The relationship between population growth and economic development is complex. In the absence of a strong economy, any country will have difficulty in maintaining decent quality of life and improve in sectors such as health, education, and environment. Furthermore, to be able to sustain economic growth the country requires the full and productive employment and decent work for all its citizens. An ideal, which is severely, challenged by a reality of social
ills especially the scourge of substance abuses and its consequences on the working age population.

## 3.4 Population projection

The population of Seychelles is expected to increase by 15.9% from 90,825 in 2010 to 108,034 in 2045. The population for the age groups 0-14 and 15-64 is projected to decline from 22.4% to 16.8% and 70.2% to 63.3% respectively for the period 2010 to 2045. However, the population in the age group 65+ is expected to increase to nearly threefold from 7.4% to 19.9% by 2045. This observation points to the ageing structure of the Seychelles’ population following the trend of other developing countries and based on the UN definition of an ageing society. An ageing population will have several implications especially on the provision of healthcare and social welfare system. In the same vein, the age dependency ratio which has continuously declined from 689 in 1990 to 440 in 2018 is projected to start rising around 2025.

## 3.5 Mortality Levels and Trends

Mortality levels in Seychelles have declined over the years and this has been attributed to the free and improved medical services and the rise in standards of living in the country brought about by economic and social progress. Whilst death rate significantly decreased from 1971 to 1980, from 1990 onwards the rate has tended to range from 7.4 to 8.0. According to the Ministry of Health statistics, the main causes of deaths are non-communicable diseases (e.g. diseases if the circulatory systems – heart diseases, cerebrovascular disease, diseases of the respiratory systems and neoplasms).

Infant mortality has also declined significantly from 1970 (33.3) to 1990(13.0) thereafter remaining low, despite certain fluctuations between the year. Significant increase was recorded in 2018; among 817 deaths registered at the Seychelles Hospital, 31 were the deaths of babies a substantial increase from the previous year where it stood at 18 infant deaths.

## 3.6 Fertility trends

The Total Fertility Rate (TFR) started to decline sharply in the mid-1970s from 5.9 children per women. This is attributed to the improvement of the family planning service, improvement in socio-economic factors such as high adult literacy rate, high school enrolment levels, high female employment rate, and women empowerment. Since 2008, TFR has remained within the 2.o to 2.4 ranges but made a significant increase in 2017.

## 4.0 CROSS CUTTING THEMES

### 4.1 Population Momentum

A “population momentum” is the tendency for population growth to continue beyond the time that fertility has declined below replacement level. This is because past high growth rates of the population during the baby boom era of the 1960’s to 1970’s, have produced an age distribution with a relatively high proportion of women in the reproductive ages (15-49). Even though there has been a significant decline in the total fertility rate in Seychelles in the last 25 years. The existence of a large number of child bearers every year means that the population will continue to grow even when the current total fertility rate is below replacement level. The existence of a large proportion of people in the working age as a result of past high levels of fertility and low levels of mortality could be a demographic “dividend” or “liability” depending on the ability of the economy to absorb and utilise the increased supply of labour.
## 4.2 Data and research
Accurate, disaggregated and comprehensive data on population and population issues are essential to effective planning and decision-making. There is growing recognition with ministries becoming increasingly aware of the need for in depth studies and with visible pull of resources and collaboration with the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) to collect data on certain key population concerns such as poverty and labour outside of the Population census every 10 year. These efforts need to be sustained to allow for the measurement of progress made at the expiry of this policy as well as others.

## 4.3 Integration of population issues in plans and programmes
The integration of population into all spheres of development is currently not being addressed fully. The utilisation of available data for planning and policymaking is low. There is a need to integrate population variables into sectoral and national development plans, policies and programmes. For this to happen, involvement of policy makers, government commitment at all levels, and strengthening of institutional capacities for undertaking integration are essential. If sectors fail to integrate population issues into their development plans they may fail to fulfil their mandates and achieve their objectives, as population issues directly or indirectly influence activities of most organisations.

## 4.4 Gender equality and equity
The Seychelles is signatory to the **SADC Protocol on Gender and Development** and the **Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women (CEDAW)**

Gender characteristics of households and population at large have profound influence on many development frontiers including health, education, and poverty. Females in Seychelles according to the 2010 National Census head a substantial proportion of households (51%).

The Government put a lot of emphasis on the promotion of services for both men and women, such as education and health for all. The life expectancy in Seychelles stands at 70.3 for males 78.5 for females. The literacy rate for women in Seychelles is 92% compared to 91% for men. Enrolment of girls in primary and secondary schools is 100%. Girls are equally well represented at tertiary and post-secondary levels of education and compete on an equal basis
with men for scholarships for university studies. Women are well represented at local government level and making up 73 of the district administrators. The number of women magistrate has increased from 1 in 2002 to 3 out of 6 in 2019. The percentage of women in Cabinet of Ministers has increased from 14% in 2005 to 42 % in 2019.

In spite of these numerous achievements, equality of access and outcomes for women is not the norm in all spheres of life and there are many pockets of inequalities that will continue to need consistent action. As such, the number of women in top positions in the defense forces and the police remain very small. In Parliament, they have declined from 24% to 21% of the Members. It is clear that although many more women are taking up employment, women continue to have lower status jobs, are clustered in unskilled, and lower paid occupations.

According to the ‘Seychelles Biological and Behavioral Surveillance of Heroin Users of 2017’; illegal drug use which has increased at an alarming rate is greatly affecting the male population. Other areas of concern related to the male population is the male dominated prison population which is closely linked to the drug problem in the country and another evident problem occurring even before males reach adulthood is the under achievement of boys in school. All these issues need concerted efforts by multiple stakeholders to address.

## 4.5 An Ageing population

Improved life expectancy is leading to an increase in the absolute number and proportion of persons aged 60 years and above and this phenomenon is expected to continue. When viewed in the context of significant weakening of traditional support systems, the elderly is increasingly vulnerable, needing protection and care. The Ministry of Family Affairs launched in 2018 a **National Policy on Ageing** which addresses the changes and impacts as well as challenges and opportunities of ageing.

Mainstreaming Population issues in sectoral plans means building geriatric health concerns, designing programmes to make the elderly economically self-reliant, providing and routinizing health screenings and exploring avenues to encourage grown up children to look after the aged.
## 4.6 National Development

The overriding objective of economic and social development is to improve the quality of lives that people lead, to enhance their wellbeing, and to provide them with opportunities and choices to become productive assets in society.

A new vision dubbed **Vision 2033** has been established through wide consultation. The vision reads as follows:

> “A resilient, responsible and prosperous nation of healthy, educated and empowered Seychellois living together in harmony with nature and engaged with the wider world.”

This vision echoes the desire to see Seychelles develop further, with the people owning and actively participating in the development process. The vision will be operationalised through the implementation of a series of three five-year National Development Strategies (NDS), beginning with the first NDS for 2019-2023, which takes into account Global, and Regional Commitments including the SDGs and Agenda 2063. The NDS’ pillars have a direct link to the population and population issues.

# 5.0 IMPLICATIONS OF THE POPULATION SITUATION FOR DEVELOPMENT

## 5.1 POPULATION AND EMPLOYMENT

Seychelles active labour force amounts to an estimated 48,437 with a labour force participation rate of 70.5% in 2017. Accommodation and food service activities employ the largest proportion of the working population, followed by public administration, construction, manufacturing, transportation and storage, and wholesale and retail.

The national unemployment rate is low, but has risen over the past decade, reaching an average rate of 3.9% in 2017. Youth unemployment is significantly higher, affecting 11% of the working-age population. Underlying causes of youth unemployment appear to be linked primarily to a mismatch between the skills demanded by employers and the skills and
preferences of job seekers. Substance abuse among youth has also increased in recent years, and is linked to many social problems. The male population of 15 to 50 years who are supposed to be actively productive is particularly affected by this scourge.

Registered job seekers are concentrated mainly in services and sales, followed by clerical support workers; with very few seeking jobs as managers, in skilled agricultural and fisheries sectors, or crafts.

As a SIDS, the Seychelles inherently lacks the workforce required to support its modern economy. This is true across all sectors but production sectors like Tourism, Fisheries, Agriculture are severely affected. Other sectors like construction, education, health, and many businesses also lacks sufficient qualified workforce. Manpower planning therefore, needs to be pragmatic, relying on solid assessment of the economics - employment and education interface.

Social development in particular, the education of young women over the years, in combination with effective family planning has brought about a change in values with more women joining the labour force. However, although women are well represented in key positions, women continue to do less well that men on the labour market with women forming a higher rate of overall unemployment and occupying a lesser share of senior and middle management (44.5%) compared to men.

## 5.2 POPULATION AND THE ENVIRONMENT

Protected areas form a major gem for the Seychelles and the tourism industry which it relies on. The natural resources base of the country is continuously in jeopardy due to pollution, the threat of loss of biodiversity, inadequate environmental awareness, inadequate integration of environmental concerns in human, technological and planning processes.

Although environmental pollution is influenced by population and industry, the number of people does not directly affect the environment, rather the kind of technology they use, their
Life styles and many other fundamental things that are quite different for different parts of the world are what affects the environment. Population growth in Seychelles is resulting in increased consumption, greater need for space and an increase in pollution loading of the environment. In view that the social and economic spheres is embedded in and surrounded by the environment; they are therefore directly affected by changes to it. The key to sustainable development is the realisation that although humans need services and products for better quality of life, this should not be at the at the expense of the environment. It is therefore, prudent for the government to consider the linkages between population and environment and how effective policies can be developed to ensure that there are limits to growth and wise use of natural capital. Much effort is already being injected in ocean based sustainable development through the **Seychelles Blue Economy Strategic Framework and Roadmap 2018 - 2030**. The success of these endeavours relies on the awareness and commitment of the population to the interrelationships between the population and the environment.

## 5.3 POPULATION, FOOD AND NUTRITION

Over the years, the performance of the agriculture has lowered and remained below desirable national levels. Data shows that local poultry production which was 10% in 2012 went down from 80% in 2008, pork production stood only at 27% in 2008 having down from 50% in 2007 and production of local fruits and vegetables which was only at 50% in 2012 went down from 65% in 1995. As a result, 80% of food consumed in Seychelles is imported.

The Seychelles population has also experienced significant changes in dietary and lifestyle patterns which has been linked to a rise in non-communicable diseases like diabetes, heart disease, cancer and renal failure. Overweight and obesity in school children and adolescents have doubled between 1998 and 2014 and is a great concern to the Ministry of Health (School Screening Program 2014).

With the projected rise in the population combined with the number of tourists increasing annually and an increasing number of expatriate workers and long-term migrants so will the food requirement for the country. The Seychelles National Agriculture Investment Plan
(SNAIP) is the main operational plan expected to rejuvenate the agriculture, livestock and fisheries sector. This plan aims to increase the share of total consumption of livestock products that is produced locally from 20% to 50% by 2020 and the share of total consumption of fruit and vegetable products that is produced locally from 46% to 56%. Despite these efforts, Seychelles remains a long way from self-sufficiency due to its small size and lack of human resource.

## 5.4 POPULATION, AND EDUCATION

The Government has continued to invest in the development of human capital through the provision of education, the major part of which is free and compulsory. As a result, the working population is highly educated with 90% of workers having attended at the least secondary level education and 40% post-secondary education or training. Better educated parents have also led to fewer children whilst more people especially women have joined the world of work. Nonetheless, the education sector is marked by a number of challenges which hinges on the quality of the package of education being offered.

Additionally, population growth has placed pressure on existing educational resources, creating a situation of unmatched resources to satisfy needs. National forecasts of the specific needs of higher education, e.g. how many biologists (or even scientists generally), civil engineers (or even technologists generally), is greatly required so that the provision of education can be matched to these needs. Additionally, educational planning has to take into consideration the changing demographic scenarios, particularly the trend in the age distribution of the population in order to fulfil school, teacher and other educational requirements.

The primary and secondary school going population stands at 15,957. The education invested in today’s children is expected to determine the human capital skills of tomorrow’s labor force. However, the participation of students at Professional Centers is particularly worrisome partly due to the higher participation of girls and the high dropout rates being experienced by some centers. In 2017 there were 1622 (889 females and 733 males) enrolled in the 8 state education and training institutions. In the same year, Seychelles Maritime Academy (SMA)
recorded 65% drop outs, followed by 46% drop outs at SIAH, 41% at STA and 29% at NIHSS.
These professional centers are supposed to produce work force for key sectors in the country.

## 5.5 POPULATION AND POVERTY REDUCTION

The Social Welfare system in Seychelles aims to ensure that all Seychellois enjoy an adequate standard of living. Special emphasis is given to those who are considered as less fortunate to ensure that they do not live in poverty. The range of benefits and services provided that directly or indirectly benefit Seychellois families include old age pensions, sickness benefits and special assistance programmes for disadvantaged groups, such as the chronically ill, and the disabled. The Agency for Social Protection also support programmes geared towards encouraging recipients of benefits to gain and maintain employment. Women feature predominantly among the beneficiaries of the Social Security Schemes, the Means Tested Financial Assistance, and the Unemployment Relief Scheme, to name but three.

Access to education, health services, safe drinking water and sanitation have all been part of the country’s efforts over the years to alleviate poverty, encourage social integration and uphold a high standard of living for its citizens. However, despite these efforts and the Seychelles elevation to high income status, poverty in the small island nation remains a real challenge.

From the 2013 Household Budget Survey an estimated 39.3% of the population was found to be below the poverty line. The face of poverty was identified as the young, single, and / or less educated and the unemployed.

A wide range of policies that impact on the cost of living have been implemented including; a 13th month salary for worker since 2015, review of the minimum wage, public sector long service allowance and continuous increase of social security benefit amounts in a bid to improve individual incomes. However, these measures fail to address the poverty issue in a holistic and final manner.

In recent years’ poverty alleviation has gained mileage on the national agenda with the setting up of the Department of Poverty Alleviation which has contributed to the push for research
in this area. The organisation has established itself as the central structure for poverty
alleviation in Seychelles tackling the issue through a multidimensional approach.



The challenges of using an income based poverty rate determination is being addressed
through the development of a **Multidimensional Poverty Index for Seychelles** of which a pilot
was launched in the last quarter of 2018. The Seychelles’ MPI comprises 4 dimension: living
standard, health, education and employment. Prelimniary findings of the pilot show that
deprivation in terms of the highest level of education attained and informal employment
contribute the most towards the MPI in Seychelles.



## 5.5 POPULATION AND MIGRATION
With a limited resource base, a small domestic market and an isolated location, Seychelles is
to a large part sustained by its migratory linkages to the wider world. Under such
circumstances the social and economic dynamics that are common to many countries across
the developing world which depend to a certain extent on migration stands out.


Over the past decade, the Seychelles has experienced a significant rise in the in-flows of
migrant workers. Today, migrants represent an estimated one-third of the country's
workforce. The country does not have a comprehensive migration system but rather labour
migration is on a case by case basis upon application by the prospective employer. Migrant
workers originate mainly from India, Madagascar, the Philippines, Sri Lanka and Mauritius,
with new migration flows emerging from countries such as Nepal, Bangladesh and Pakistan.
The number of migrants from Western European countries has also increased in recent years.


While primarily concentrated in low- and semi-skilled jobs in the construction, tourism, and
manufacturing sectors, migrant workers also occupy highly-skilled positions in the tourism,
financial and public service sectors. The importation of labour has wide ranging implications and is politically sensitive to locals who see foreigners getting better jobs, and or better remuneration than what they get with similar qualifications and experience. At the same time, emigration of Seychellois continues towards high-income countries, suggesting out-flows of highly-skilled workers.

## 5.6 POPULATION AND HEALTH

Free primary health care at the point of use is a Constitutional right in Seychelles. Overall the country provides a good health care package. Infectious and communicable diseases, such as HIV/AIDS, Malaria, Leptospirosis still pose a challenge and call for concerted and integrated actions for control and prevention. Lifestyle related diseases, cardiovascular and diabetes are also proving to be serious challenges.

### HIV/ AIDS

The Government continues to be committed to improving the national response to the HIV/AIDS epidemic through the appropriate allocation of resources to support interventions at various levels. One concrete development has been the promulgation of the **National AIDS Council Act in 2013** which establishes a Council with specific responsibilities to oversee and take actions to reverse the epidemic.

Preventive Mother to Child Transmission (PMTCT) services for HIV were introduced in 2001. All pregnant women attending antenatal clinics are offered HIV testing using a routine opt-out approach, and the majority of women accept to be tested. All pregnant women infected with HIV are managed at the Communicable Disease Control Unit (CDCU) and currently all HIV infected pregnant women are eligible for Anti-Retroviral Therapy (ART) (Option B+) and HIV-exposed infants receive appropriate ARV prophylaxis for free. For early infant diagnosis (EID) of HIV, blood samples are sent overseas. All women living with HIV (WLHIV) have access to sexual reproductive health services including family planning.
A total of 134 HIV positive pregnancies were reported from 1988 to December 2017, of which 88 (68%) benefited from the PMTCT program since its introduction in 2001. Prior to the introduction of PMTCT, 8 out of the 23 babies born from HIV positive mothers were infected with HIV representing a mother to child transmission rate of 35% compared to 4 out of the 106 babies since the introduction of PMTCT program representing a mother to child transmission rate of 3.7%. To note, 2 babies were diagnosed HIV positive at 9 months old after birth though their mothers tested HIV negative throughout their pregnancies.

From 2012-2016, 47 pregnant women infected with HIV were reported by the CDCU, some of the women were newly diagnosed, whereas a few pregnancies occurred in women known to be living with HIV. Three MTCT transmissions were reported during the last five years with one infant death from AIDS. Partner testing is offered for partners of pregnant women, however, uptake of testing by partners is very low.

Sadly, the majority of the HIV infected cases are in the reproductive and economically active age group. This has a direct implication on resource allocation to the health sector.

## Reproductive Health

Reproductive health implies the rights to have a safe and satisfying sex life, the capacity to reproduce safely and the freedom to decide when and how often to do so. * Care during child birth

Seychellois women have control over their family planning rights and all indications show that they are the main sexual and reproductive health seekers. The contraceptive prevalence rate for modern contraceptive method use among all women aged 15-49 stands at ……. Condoms are supplied free by the Ministry of Health and sold at private pharmacies and several shops. The number of users is not really known hence this can make a difference to the overall estimate for contraceptive prevalence.
All health centers and private clinics have facilities to confirm a pregnancy. A **Termination of Pregnancy (TOP) Board** formed by three medical practitioners exists to consider applications for termination of pregnancy. The woman wanting recourse to a termination of pregnancy is allowed on very strict criteria relating to the health of the baby or the mother. The parents are counseled prior to and after the intervention and is followed up by the family planning staff in primary health care.

Unsafe abortions comprise of all incomplete, septic abortions. While septic abortions are not always induced, many attempts are made to interrupt an unwanted pregnancy and often in unsafe conditions. These can include introduction of foreign and abortive objects vaginally as well as ingestion of abortive substances. The ongoing practice of illegal and unsafe abortions is an issue of serious concern which needs to be investigated further. The total abortions recoded in 2017 was 521 of which only 80 had been carried out under the Termination of pregnancy guidelines.

## 5.7 SPECIAL GROUPS
Children, the youth, the elderly and people with disabilities are among the groups in society that need special protection to ensure their full participation in the development of the country.

Another group where particular attention is required and much resource is being allocated is drug users. Illegal drug use in Seychelles, especially heroin, has increased to an alarming rate. A survey in 2017 estimated 4,800 heroin users which equals to 5.6 percent of the population above 15 years of age. This has considerable implications to the country’s limited resources. Different sectors identify different groups that they consider vulnerable or at risk and all them have to be taken into account when developing policies, plans and programmes.

# 6.0 POLICY GOAL, OBJECTIVES, TARGETS AND STRATEGIES
## 6.1 Goal
The primary goal of the National Population Policy is to promote the sustainable development of the country through appropriate policies, plans and programmes which take into demographic changes

### 6.2 Objectives

The objectives of the National Population Policy are to:
1. Mainstream population concerns into all policies, plans and programmes.
2. Support couples and individuals to decide freely and responsibly on the number of children, and to have dignified access to information, education and services.
3. Sustain low Infant, Child and Maternal Morbidity and Mortality rates.
4. Contribute to the prevention and treatment for HIV/AIDS, STIS and Non Communicable disease.
5. Mitigate the impact of migration.
6. Contribute towards addressing the special needs of vulnerable groups.
7. Ensure the availability, quality and timeliness of population-related data and research for use in policy and planning.
8. Ensure the quality and enhancement of human resources.
9. Ensure a healthy population.
10. Sustain nutrition and food security.
11. Mitigate the impact of Social ills.
12. Contribute towards efforts to alleviate poverty.

### 6.3 Targets

The major population-related targets of the Policy by 2029 are to:
1. Achieve full capacity for integration of population concerns in policies, plans and programmes.
2. Improve the availability and use of data and research on population.
3. Maintain the current low population growth rate and total fertility rate.
4. Make quality reproductive health services accessible, and acceptable to all sexually active persons including vulnerable groups of society.
5. Improve the availability, quality and accessibility of population, gender, HIV/AIDS and environment-related IEC.

# 7.0 INSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK FOR IMPLEMENTION, COORDINATION, MONITORING AND EVALUATION

The policy will be implemented through a multi sectoral and multi-dimensional, integrated approach. The government will collaborate with NGOs, the private sector, communities and other agencies in implementing the policy. Indeed, individuals, political parties and other organized groups in civil society are expected to play an active role to ensure the attainment of policy goals and objectives.

## 7.1 Research, Policy and Societal Development Division

The Research, Policy and Societal Development Division in the Ministry of Family Affairs consist of a Population Unit. The Population Unit in collaboration with the National Population Group will coordinate, monitor and evaluate the implementation of Population Policy

The Population Unit will submit annual progress reports to the National Population Group for monitoring purposes, which will enable the National Population Group to make informed decisions pertaining to any changes in the implementation of the policy.

## 7.2 The National Population Group

The National Population Group will comprise of persons occupying posts of Director Generals and above from the public, civil and private sector. The Committee will be chaired by the Principal Secretary of Family Affairs. The National Population Group’s duties are to ensure that cooperation and coordination deemed appropriate in the implementation of policies and programmes related to the livelihoods, development, prevention and improvement of the welfare and participation of the population.

The Committee will meet quarterly to discuss on issues, share ideas/information and propose what actions need to be taken. Its mandate will be to co-ordinate implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the population policy and report to the Minister for Family Affairs who will subsequently report to the Cabinet of Ministers.
### 7.3 Sector Ministries

The sector Ministries are responsible for implementing their respective tasks for the integration of population issues in their sector plans and programmes.

### 7.4 Non-Governmental Organisations/Civil Societies

NGOs, including both local and international with focus on population and other population related issues, are key partners in monitoring the NPP. NGOs should provide useful data, information, and qualitative analysis from their micro-level studies and assessments.

### 7.5 Information, education and communication

Information, education and communication (IEC) on this policy and population issues must be clear, focused and disseminated everywhere. This will ensure that the messages are effectively conveyed. The Policy and its strategies needs to be supported by a national communication strategy on population related issues.

### 7.6 Roles and responsibilities of stakeholders

The implementation of the NPP requires a multi sectoral approach so as to ensure that population and development are mutually supportive and fully integrated. This relies on the collaborative role of all sectors, ministries, department and agencies, including the private sector and civil society is essential is necessary in achieving the goals of the policy.
