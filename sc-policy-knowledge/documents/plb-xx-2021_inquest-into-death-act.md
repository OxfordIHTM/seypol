# HEALTH IMPLICATIONS: THE INQUEST INTO DEATH ACT, 2021
*Gazetted on 8th November, 2021*

## WHY WAS THE LAW ENACTED
To promote justice through investigations, and the identification of the causes and circumstances, of sudden or unexplained deaths, or deaths that occurred in certain circumstances and make recommendations to reduce the chances of similar deaths to occur. The Act clarifies clearly the purpose of an inquest into death and address issues relating to post-mortem examination, the release of a body for transportation out of Seychelles, burial and cremation and in certain instances exhumation.

## IMPLICATIONS FOR CLINICAL PRACTICE
The Inquest into Death Act, 2021 has implications for clinical practice and as such relevant health professionals should get acquainted with the following inferences:

### #1: Purpose of Inquest
To enquire into the cause of and circumstances connected to the cause of death. The proceedings and evidence at the inquest is directed to ascertaining the identity of the deceased person; how, when and where the deceased person came by that person’s death and the cause of and circumstances of the death.

### #2: Obligation to Report
Any person who becomes aware of a death which appears or qualifies for an inquest shall report the death to the police as soon as reasonably practicable. Contravening the above without reasonable excuse is seen as an offence and is liable on conviction to a fine of level 2 on the standard scale (a fine not exceeding SCR25,000) or imprisonment for a term not exceeding 1 year, or both.

### #3: Prohibition of Issuing a Certificate of Death
If a death qualifies for an inquest, it shall not be removed or alter its position unless authorized by a police officer, pathologist, forensic pathologist or medical practitioner. This is necessary to prevent any destruction or damage to the body.

### #4: Directives and Powers of Forensic Pathologist
The forensic pathologist may view the body where it is lying or order its removal to a more appropriate setting. The forensic pathologist is given powers to access the medical files of the deceased person and may require any medical practitioner, within reasonable time, to provide a detailed medical report on the treatment and care before the person was deceased or any other medical information which is deemed necessary for the purpose of investigation. The medical practitioner shall comply with the request and shall not so doing be treated as acting in contravention with any written law.

### #5: Questioning a Witness
Where at any inquest a report is received in evidence (e.g. medical report), the magistrate may summon the marker of that report as a witness.

### #6: Post-Mortem Examination
A Magistrate may order a post-mortem examination to be conducted if the Attorney General makes an application to the Magistrate or after consultation with a forensic pathologist, pathologist, or an approved medical practitioner to establish the cause of death. Post-mortem shall be performed by a pathologist, forensic pathologist, a medical practitioner under the supervision of a pathologist or forensic pathologist; or an approved medical practitioner. The post mortem includes any operations on the body that is necessary and retaining any part, content, substances or thing for the purpose of determining the cause of death. Furthermore, the medical person conducting the post mortem can appoint a qualified person to make a special examination by way of analysis, test or otherwise. In completing the post mortem, a report of the findings including the conclusion drawn from the report and the certified cause of death shall be made and forwarded to the appropriate Magistrate.

### #7: Public Health Implications
A Magistrate may order an exhumation of a body or the remains of a body after consulting with the Public Health Commissioner or from an application by the Attorney General. A Magistrate may order the release of a body, whether or not the Magistrate considers that an inquest is necessary, subject to such direction from the Public Health Commissioner for burial or cremation, transportation of the body outside of Seychelles and for retention of the body for use in any manner in accordance with the law. Furthermore, a Magistrate shall issue a certificate of an order of release of the body where the Public Health Commissioner determines that it is in the interest of public safety.
#8: Magistrate Certificate

The Magistrate shall send a copy of a signed certificate setting out the cause of death as determined at the inquest to the Principal Secretary for Health and the Commissioner of Public Health within 48 hrs after conclusion of the inquest.

In the event that a death has occurred in Seychelles and the Magistrate has decided not to hold an inquest into the death after considering the results of the investigations, the same certificate shall be forwarded within 48hrs to the Principal Secretary for Health and the Commission of Public Health.

#9: Adjournment of Inquest

If, before the conclusion of an inquest, any person is charged with any offence under the relevant laws which is the subject of the inquest, the magistrate shall adjourn the inquest until after the conclusion of the criminal proceedings in that matter. The same process of adjournment shall apply in the event that a commission is appointed under the Commission of Inquiry Act or any other body appointed under the law.

Depending on the findings, the Magistrate shall resume or shall discontinue its investigations if a cause of or the circumstances of death has been established.

## SCHEDULE 1 OF THE ACT
### DEATHS QUALIFYING FOR INQUEST

1. Death in Seychelles of a person whose identity is not known.
2. Any death in Seychelles that was unnatural or violent.
3. Any death in Seychelles that resulted or is suspected to have resulted, directly or indirectly, from an accident.
4. Any death in Seychelles that occurred, directly or indirectly, as a result of any medical treatment or care.
5. Death in Seychelles of a person where the person was, before the person's death, in official custody and where the death was related, or suspected to be related, to that custody.
6. Any death in Seychelles occurring apparently or possibly as a consequence of any law enforcement operation.
7. Any death in Seychelles involving a public vehicle, commercial transport vehicle or other mode of public transport.
8. Any death of a person in possession of an immigration permit.
9. Any death on board a Seychelles-registered vessel or a Seychelles registered aircraft.
10. Upon the authority of the Attorney General to hold an inquest, a death occurring outside Seychelles on or from an aircraft or a vessel, not being a Seychelles-registered vessel or a Seychelles-registered aircraft.
11. Any death in Seychelles that was caused or suspected to have been caused by an unlawful act or omission.
12. Any death in Seychelles the manner or cause of which is unknown.
13. Any death in Seychelles that occurred under suspicious circumstances.

## SCHEDULE 2 OF THE ACT
### DEATH FOR WHICH INQUEST SHALL BE HELD

1. Death in Seychelles of a person whose identity is not known.
2. Death in Seychelles of a person where the person was, before the person's death, in official custody and where the death was related, or suspected to be related, to that custody.
3. Any death in Seychelles occurring apparently or possibly as a consequence of any law enforcement operation.
4. Any death in Seychelles that was caused or suspected to have been caused by an unlawful act or omission.
5. Any death in Seychelles the manner or cause of which is unknown.
6. Any death in Seychelles occurred under suspicious circumstances.

## SCHEDULE 3 OF THE ACT
### PERSONS WHO MAY BE APPOINTED TO HOLD AN INQUEST

1. An Attorney-at-Law with at least 3 years standing;
2. An expert who conducts inquests in another country.
