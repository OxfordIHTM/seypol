## Background

The current Ebola outbreak is centred on six countries in West Africa: Guinea, Liberia, Nigeria, Sierra Leone, the since March 2014. There is the potential for further spread to neighbouring African countries.) Senegal became the sixth country in the region to register cases.

The World Health Organization has reported that as of 31 August 2014, 3685 (probable, confirmed and suspected) cases and 1841 deaths have been reported in the current outbreak of Ebola virus disease by the Ministries of Health of Guinea, Liberia and Sierra Leone.

In Nigeria, there have been 21 cases and 7 deaths.

In Senegal, one case has been confirmed and there have been no Ebola deaths or further suspected cases.

Ebola currently does not pose a significant risk to the Seychellois public. Nonetheless in view of the on-going situation and the latest development in DRC and Senegal, the Public Health Authority has updated measures in place so as to further decrease the chance of introducing Ebola virus in Seychelles. These measures include travel restrictions to the affected and neighbouring countries, as well as intensified surveillance at the ports of entry.

Ebola virus is the cause of a viral haemorrhagic fever disease. Symptoms of Ebola include fever and additional symptoms like severe headache, muscle pain, vomiting, diarrhoea, stomach pain, or unexplained bleeding or bruising. Symptoms may appear anywhere from 2 to 21 days after exposure to Ebola virus, although 8-10 days is most common. This is known as the incubation period.

The virus is spread through **direct contact** (through broken skin or unprotected mucous membranes in, for example, the eyes, nose, or mouth) with the blood or body fluids (urine, faeces, saliva, semen, and other secretions) of a person who is sick with Ebola, or with objects like needles that have been contaminated with the virus, or infected animals.

Ebola is **not** spread through the air or by water or, in general, by food; however, in Africa, Ebola may be spread as a result of hunting, processing, and consuming infected animals (e.g., bushmeat).

## Introduction

Haemorrhagic Fever outbreaks, particularly Ebola are of serious public health concern, as they have the potential to cause large number of deaths, and have significant economic and social impacts. For a highly lethal disease like Ebola, which has no vaccine or cure, information aimed at behavioural change becomes the principal preventive measure.

Public awareness and appropriate response are vital for prevention and control of outbreaks of Haemorrhagic Fever. The mass media is probably the single most important source of health information for the general public, and influences policy-makers and collaborators. It is important to raise awareness and educate the public on the risks, dangers and preventive measures to adopt to prevent the spread of Ebola in Seychelles. Therefore, specific strategies, messages and tools, particularly adapted to the needs of Seychelles, are needed for a systematic and strategic engagement with the general population and stake holders

This Communication Strategic Plan is an essential and integral component of the national prevention and response programme being put in place and led by the Ministry of Health to decrease the chance of the introduction of the Ebola virus in Seychelles.

To accelerate the process, existing information and experiences gathered during previous consultations and engagements with the public, partners and stake holders on similar issues have been used in the formulation of the plan.

Meetings and consultations with the Health Promotion Unit, the Disease Surveillance and Response Unit and other Departments and desk reviews have been done based on the urgency of the situation.
The Plan has been developed using a combination of standards, guidelines, best practices and advisories from the World Health Organization (WHO), Centre for Disease Control (CDC), Disease Surveillance and Control Unit (DSRU) and Public Health Authority in the Ministry of Health as well as guiding documents such as the International Health Regulations, WHO Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response Guidelines, WHO Strategic Action Plan for Ebola Outbreak Response July-December 2014 and the WHO Roadmap – 29th Aug 2014.

## Guiding Principles

The plan is based on five essential practices and guiding principles for effective outbreak communication identified as best practices by WHO: **build trust**, **announce early**, **be transparent**, **respect public concerns**, and **plan in advance**.

**Build trust**

*As the foundation for effective outbreak communication, the most critical objective is to build, maintain, or restore public trust in those responsible for managing the outbreak and issuing information about it.*

**Announce early**

*Early announcement contributes to early containment in a situation where every day counts. Equally important, early announcement wins public confidence that authorities are openly reporting what they know when they know it, setting expectations that information will not be concealed.*

**Be transparent**

*Transparency characterizes the relationship between the outbreak management teams and the public. Transparency can be defined as communication that is candid, easily understood, complete, and accurate.*

**Respect public concerns**

*The public is entitled to information that affects their health and the health of their families. Public concerns should be treated as legitimate, explored, and respected as a force that will influence an outbreak’s impact. Today, effective risk communication is viewed as a dialogue between technical experts and the public.*

**Plan in advance**

*Outbreak communication planning must be a part of outbreak management planning from the start.*

## Risk Communication areas

The following risk communication areas are taken into consideration in drafting the communication objectives, strategies and activities for this plan:

1. **Information and education**, where people are informed and educated about risks and risk assessments in general;
2. **Behaviour change and protective action**, which encourages risk reduction behaviour by trying to influence the perceptions of the audience;
3. **Disaster warnings and emergency information**, which provides direction and behavioural guidance in disasters and emergencies; and
4. **Disaster warnings and emergency information**, which involves the public in risk management decision-making and in resolving health, safety, and environmental controversies.

## Purpose

This communication strategic plan will contribute to maintain a zero local infection rate and 100% detection rate for imported cases of Haemorrhagic Fever (Ebola) in Seychelles.

## Rapid Assessment

A rapid assessment among members of the general population was conducted to have a glimpse of the knowledge and perception of the public on the Ebola virus. It is recommended that additional assessments are carried out to further give a better understanding of the knowledge, attitude and practices of the various target groups among the general public.

The assessment was conducted by face to face, telephone and text interviews. Most of the respondents had heard about the Ebola virus. The majority knew it was infectious and deadly. All of them wanted more detailed information about the virus and the preventive measures that will protect them from infection.

A summary of key responses is in Annex 3.

## Consideration of outrage
The concept that risk perception is a function of hazard and outrage means that in any risk communication effort, the risk communicator cannot afford to ignore any outrage that is felt by the audience. The following conclusions about hazard and outrage are to be taken into consideration and are addressed by the strategies and activities planned:

1. The public responds more to outrage than to hazard;
2. Activists and media may amplify the outrage but they do not create it;
3. Outraged people do not pay much attention to hazard data;
4. Outrage is not just a distraction from considering the real hazard - both are legitimate and must be addressed;

## Advocacy and Partnerships

The success of the communication interventions will depend greatly on the effective involvement of all stake holders and collaborators who have a role to play in the prevention and response to any potential epidemic in the country. So as not to undermine communication efforts, partners should strive to present information in a coordinated and consistent way and advocate constantly to support the surveillance and response plan in place.

The media in particular has essential and specific roles and responsibilities vis a vis the threat of an epidemic. The media can be used very effectively, especially at the start of an outbreak, to inform public, as good reporting translates technical information into lay language and can help the public understand the situation, including its implications for their own health and behaviours. In addition, media coverage can put those in charge of the outbreak response under close public scrutiny, creating pressure for them to be seen as moving rapidly and decisively to protect public health.

# Chapter 3

## Communication Goal

The overall goal of the communication strategy plan is to increase the level of knowledge on the Ebola virus and promote the adoption of preventive practices and hygiene among the general population to prevent the importation and spread of the Haemorrhagic Fever (Ebola) in Seychelles.

## Primary Communication Strategy

Develop and implement a comprehensive communication plan to raise awareness and promote the adoption and practice of preventive measures against the spread of the Haemorrhagic Fever (Ebola) in Seychelles.

## Communication Objectives

Taking into consideration the fact that no study has been conducted to establish a base line on knowledge, attitudes and practices among the population on the prevention and control of haemorrhagic fever, the assumption used for the purpose of this plan is that the majority of the population is not aware of these prevention and control measures.

1. To increased percentage of the general population who are aware of the origin, symptoms, modes of transmission and effects of Haemorrhagic Fever (Ebola) in the next 6 months.
2. To increased percentage of the general population who have adopted and are practicing the recommended preventive measures against Haemorrhagic Fever (Ebola) in the next 6 months.
3. To increased collaboration among specific target groups, partners and stake holders advocating for and participating in the implementation of the surveillance and response plan for Haemorrhagic Fever (Ebola) put in place by the Ministry of Health in the next 6 months.

It is highly recommended that in order to validate key knowledge, attitudes, perceptions, barriers and practices related to haemorrhagic fever, a formative study be carried out.

While communication would be contributing towards the achievement of the above mentioned objectives, they would also depend to a great extent on the effectiveness of the surveillance and response interventions as well as levels of engagement of different stakeholders.

## Communication Strategies

1. Define and use clear key messages targeting specific groups based on their settings, influence, roles and responsibilities.
2. Use mass media as effective channels of communication taking into consideration its accessibility and high literacy rate among the general population to raise awareness and promote the adoption and practice of preventive measures against the spread of the Haemorrhagic Fever (Ebola).
3. Build capacity of health workers, partners and collaborators to advocate and participate in the implementation of the communication plan.
4. Use of social mobilization techniques and community interventions to promote best practices, hygiene and other preventive measures against infectious diseases such as Ebola.
5. Establishment of a crisis information center to provide real time, accurate information on the progress of the outbreak, the public health and measures in place and preventive measures recommended for prevention of Ebola.

## Target Audiences

This communication strategy aims to work with key, predetermined target groups. In order for the strategy to be effective, it has to be relevant to the needs of each of the target groups. Therefore it is important to understand their needs and characteristics so as to design specific strategies to convince them to practice as well as maintain the recommended/desired behaviours. Different communication approaches, messages and content is needed for each group.

Additionally, a great deal of advocacy would also need to be carried out with the **Tertiary Target groups** such as policy makers/implementers and the media as they directly or indirectly influence the intention/ability to act of the secondary target groups as well as primary target groups.

## Key Messages

### Information messages

1. *Ebola virus is the cause of a viral haemorrhagic fever disease.*
2. *Symptoms of Ebola are fever, severe headache, muscle pain, vomiting, diarrhoea, stomach pain, or unexplained bleeding or bruising. Symptoms may appear anywhere from 2 to 21 days after exposure to Ebola virus.*
3. *Ebola virus is spread through direct physical contact with body fluids like blood, saliva, stool, vomit, urine, and sweat of an infected and sick person and or dead body.*
4. *Ebola virus can also be spread through using skin piercing instruments that have been used by an infected person.*
5. *Ebola is not spread through the air or by water.*

### Personal protection messages

1. *Always wash your hands with soap and water and adopt good hygiene practices.*
2. *Report to a health facility if you have any of the following symptoms: fever, head ache, diarrhoea, muscle & joint pain, red eyes, rash and vomiting.*
3. *Avoid contact with a suspected and /or infected person or dead body.*

### Public Health measures and official notices

The following public health measures based on the authority of the Public Health Authority is effective to date and will be updated based on the circumstances and progression of the Ebola outbreak.

1. All Seychellois citizens will not be allowed to travel without the expressed permission of the Public Health Commissioner, to **Sierra Leone, Liberia, Guinea, Nigeria, the Democratic Republic of Congo and Senegal** until the outbreak has been declared over by WHO.
2. Citizens of the countries mentioned at (1) and any other foreign nationals who work in Seychelles, are discouraged from visiting the listed countries. If they do, they will not be allowed to re-enter Seychelles until the outbreak has been declared over by WHO.
3. Citizens of any nationals will not be allowed to enter Seychelles if they were in **Sierra Leone, Liberia, Guinea, Nigeria, Democratic Republic of Congo and Senegal** less than 21 days ago.
4. Passengers and crew of any ship which visit any port of the countries mentioned above **(Sierra Leone, Liberia, Guinea, Nigeria, Democratic Republic of Congo and Senegal)** will not be allowed to disembark in Seychelles, especially if the visit to the affected countries was less than 21 days ago.
5. Seychellois citizens are discouraged from travelling to West African region. This applies not only to countries that border the affected states, but also others in the region. *(Namely: Sierra Leone, Liberia, Guinea-Bissau, Guinea Conakry, Nigeria, Cameroon, Chad, Niger, Burkina Faso, Senegal, Mali, Benin, Ivory Coast, Ghana, Togo, Congo, D.R. Congo, Gambia, Mauritania, Senegal ). C*itizens of these aforementioned countries are required to apply for a VISA prior to being granted permission to enter Seychelles.
6. People are strongly discouraged from attending any gatherings that may put them into close contact with potentially infected individuals.

## Communication Approaches

1. **Advocacy:** Aims to engage the support of influential organizations and individuals and target people in positions of power. Advocacy would help influence policy and to raise the issue preparedness and response in epidemic on the policy agenda and in the minds of the people.
2. **Interpersonal Communication** *(One to one, Door to door, Forums, workshops, meetings etc.):* would be one of the key approaches of this strategy in order to increase knowledge on the importance of hygiene as well as promote behavioral change among families and communities.
3. **Community mobilisation:** would be carried out to strengthen dialogue among community members, NGOs, Churches etc., on issues of hygiene. It would provide the platform to help increase community participation and ownership.
4. **Mass media, outdoor media and folk media:** would be useful to raise mass awareness, bring the issue into the limelight and also help promote critical behaviors and programme information. Simultaneously, they would also provide support and credibility to the interpersonal and community mobilization efforts.
5. **Capacity Building:** In order to be able to carry out communication activities effectively, a significant amount of capacity building activities would need to be carried out at various levels. More importantly, for communicators in direct contact with families and communities.

## The Social Media
Social media has proven to be a very accessible and influential medium for communication during the last ten years. Consequently it has its advantages and disadvantages, the fact that the contents of the social media networks are controlled by users themselves leaves room for misinformation which can fuel public panic and anxiety far out of proportion to the reality of the actual threat to health. This scenario emphasises the need for official communication to be timely, clear, transparent and consistent aiming to reduce rumours and speculations. The use of social media is also recommended to influence the audiences positively and pre-empt rumours and speculation on the issues being addressed.

## Proactive and Effective Communication initiatives

### Establishment of a Crisis Information Center

Rumours, speculations and misinformation can be effective barriers during communication in emergency and crisis situations. Lack of accurate and timely information is the most common cause of speculation and rumours.

The main role of the Center is to be a source of timely and accurate information and to pre-empt rumours and speculation on the Ebola outbreak among the general population and the media. Other key functions will include:

* An effective communication tool to triangulate information from and between the National Authorities, the media and the general population.
* Act as the media monitoring mechanism (*i.e. monitor all information being published and broadcast locally (SBC, PureFM, Seychelles Nation, Today in Seychelles, Les Seychellois, Weekly, Seychelles Independent etc.) and from the main international news channels (CNN, BBC, Aljazeera, TV 5 etc.) as well as social media (Facebook) on Ebola.
* Use as an internal communication mechanism as well to keep all Ministry of Health staff as well as partners and stake holders involved in the implementation of the Ebola response plan well informed on the progress on the outbreak and the activities being implemented as part of the plan. This will be done either through intranet and or emails and phone calls.

The Information Center will be equipped with fixed line, cellular call facilities, television and internet access. It will be staffed with personnel trained in communication with particular focus on risk communication and public relations.

The Center will keep itself updated on the latest information on trends, measures and programmes in place locally and internationally to pass on to the media and the general population based on queries by phone and emails.

The Center will be advertised as the main interactive public information mechanism through which the media and public can address the concerns, queries and suggestions for the duration of the outbreak preparedness and response phase.

### Identification of Communication focal points within key organisations, partners and stake holders

To facilitate the exchange of information, planning, monitoring and evaluation purposes it is important to identify and establish communication focal points within key organisations, partners and stake holders for the duration of the outbreak preparedness and response for three main reasons:

1. To guarantee that all relevant official information reaches the prospective target groups in a consistent and timely manner.
2. Ensure a direct line of communication between the Ministry of Health and its partners/stake holders in the implementation of the communication plan for Ebola.
3. Record feedback from the respective parties in time to mitigate communication barriers and obstacles in the implementation of delegated activities.

In cases where appropriate the Health and Safety Officers or the Human Resource Managers would be the ideal persons for such role.

### Designation of a national spoke person from the Ministry of Health

To ensure a credible and official source of information and directives from the Ministry of Health as the lead agency in the preparedness and response of the Ebola outbreak, it is proposed that a key spoke person is designated to communicate on behalf of the Ministry of Health during the outbreak period. An alternate person should also be identified to stand in in the absence of the designated spoke person.

The identity and role of the designated spoke person will be communicated to the media and all relevant partners/stake holders.

## Communication Activity Plan

The activity plan incorporates activities that are already in progress due to the urgency of the situation and existing plans and protocol for infectious diseases management by the Public Health Authority and the Health Care Agency. The plan also takes into consideration the involvement of partners and stake holders in respect of their involvement in the epidemic response plan for the threat of Ebola importation in Seychelles and the viral Haemorrhagic Fever surveillance guidelines from DSRU.

* Inputs, contributions and participation of partners (local and international), stake holders and other interested parties.
* The eventuality additional financial resources to strengthen the interventions.

# Contingency Crisis Communication Planning

To ensure the sustainability of effective communication in the eventuality that the Ebola outbreak reaches Seychelles, it is wise and important to plan for scenarios that will need extraordinary measures and interventions in relation to communication that may or may not be covered by the Public Health Act and the legal implications of such measures.

These may include but not limited to:

* Priority access to national media channels
* Use of conventional communication approaches (e.g. Bull horns, mobile PA systems, etc.)
* Access to large target groups (such as employees of large organisations, students)
* Dedicated publication on the outbreak
* Blockage or legal action against information sources and promoters of behaviours that may contribute to hamper response efforts and or deterioration of the outbreak (Actions by religious groups, alternative medicine practitioners, anti-social/political groups etc.)

We propose that the multi-sectorial communication team with the guidance of the Public Health Authority undertake the task to draw up crisis communication action plans based on a series of worst case scenarios in the event that confirmed cases of Ebola are detected in Seychelles.

A crisis communication contingency fund should also be established to finance any additional activities needed based on these plans.

## Annex 3: Rapid Assessment on the Ebola Virus Disease

Rapid assessment was done with 20 people of different background and age groups (14-80 year of age) by text messages, phone calls and one to one interviews.

### 1. State 3 things you know about the Ebola Virus Disease

* It is a virus. (70%)
* It is a deadly virus. (50%)
* Ebola is transmitted through blood and coughing. (10%)
* Ebola is transmitted through body fluids. (20%)
* No vaccine has been found for the Ebola virus to date. (20%)
* An Ebola vaccine has been tested on Americans. (10%)
* Ebola is a virus introduced by the Americans as a weapon to be used in the war. (10%)
* Ebola is spread by unprotected physical contact and body fluids.(10%)
* Don’t know anything about the Ebola. (30%)
* It causes bleeding (10%)
* It originates from Africa (10%)

* More information on the possibility of cure?
* How long will take to find a cure?
* How is Ebola contracted/transmitted?
* Is there a cure for Ebola?
* Origin(s) of Ebola?
* What/who transmit Ebola?
* How does one know he/she has Ebola?
* What are the symptoms of Ebola?
* What happens when one gets Ebola?
* What are the possibilities of Ebola getting to Seychelles?
* How one does protects oneself and one’s family against Ebola if it gets to Seychelles?
* As a volunteer what kind of protection will I get to assist others?
