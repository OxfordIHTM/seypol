# 1 INTRODUCTION

Global health inequalities impact access to healthcare in most low-and middle-income countries (LMICs), as well as affecting deprived population groups and/or communities in high-income countries (HICs). These may often mean shortages of the necessary health equipment and health-related supplies. The health sectors of many developing nations are assisted by more economically developed nations or international organisations providing aid, through donations of medical equipment, supplies, or technical assistance in a range of health-related aspects.

Whilst donations of such medical supplies are greatly welcomed and appreciated by the recipients, a quick study of the literature on medical donations will inform any reader of the many challenges that may be brought on by donations, such as poor quality control/standards, weak communication between donor and recipient, and lack of expertise for maintenance.

Many recipient countries may not be able to cope with the incurred costs of medical equipment throughout their life span as the costs of purchasing amount to a mere 20% of the overall costs incurred in the equipment life cycle[^1]. Additionally, a problematic idea sometimes noted with donors, is that the recipient countries’ circumstances mean that anything is better than nothing[^2]. This may lead to the dumping of unwanted equipment and supplies to recipient countries. Several estimates suggest that between 40% to 70% of donated medical devices and equipment in low-resource settings are broken, unused, or unfit for purpose[^3].

Many developing countries lack regulations and policies for the donation of medicines and medical equipment. In efforts to guide donations, the World Health Organization [WHO], has developed several guidelines to ensure that donations of medical supplies are more efficient and effective. These guidelines have been widely recognised as the international

The WHO guidelines for Medicine Donations and guidelines for Health Care Equipment Donations are formed around four underlying core principles of **Good Donations Practice**, developed in pursuit of more effective, efficient, and ethical donations of medical products:

### Core Principles

1. Donations of medicine and health-related products should benefit the recipient to the maximum extent.
2. Donations should be given with respect for the wishes and authority of the recipient.
3. **No double standards in quality**: the standards of donations should be acceptable for use in the donor country/organisation.
4. Effective communication between donor and recipient with all donations made according to a plan formulated and agreed to by both parties.

The Paris Declaration on Aid Effectiveness, adopted by over 100 countries in 2005, commits signatories to improving the quality of aid and its impact on development. The declaration is not officially endorsed by Seychelles to-date, but it is by most global and bilateral partners. It sets out five principles (Table 1). These principles form the basis of guiding donor-recipient roles and responsibilities in donations.

One of the main challenges, however, is that despite the existence of international guidelines on donations, many donors, may not be aware of, or adequately respect, them. This often results in unsolicited donations, which may not adequately address priority needs for the health systems of developing economies. There may be no assistance provided by the donor for upkeep of any machinery or support in training programmes to
build capacity for the Health Care Workers [HCWs] to use the donation effectively.

### Table 1 Principles of the Paris Declaration on Aid Effectiveness

| PRINCIPLE | DESCRIPTION |

| Ownership | Developing countries set their own development strategies, improve their institutions and tackle corruption. |

The mere existence of guidelines and standards on donations are insufficient. Enforcement is required to ensure that such guidelines, whether international or domestic, are respected and adhered to by both donors and recipients. Therefore, while it may seem prudent to develop policies guiding health-related donations, adequate recognition and buy-in from stakeholders is key to successful implementation.

The WHO suggests the following methods to aid in the successful implementation of donations policies:

* The effective and efficient management of donations.
* Ensuring appropriate actions from donor agencies – Donors should respect the four core principles of Good Donations Practice and national guidelines set by the recipient.
* The avoidance of donations of drugs (or other consumables) with short expiry dates.
* Informing the public – the public in the donor country is not always aware of the problems associated with donations. Governments of donor countries should make effort to create more public awareness of Good Donation Practices.
* Establishing donor coordination.

For the purposes of this policy, gifts given to individuals (or groups thereof) in public service are not covered. For acceptance of gifts, refer to Article 61, Order 119 of the Public Service Procedures Manual (2011) for details on what circumstances such a practice is acceptable.

## 1.1 Seychelles & Assistance in Healthcare

Seychelles is a small island state of 115 islands, located in the Indian Ocean, with a large exclusive economic zone of 1,350,000 square kilometres of mostly ocean. The growth of the Seychelles’ economy is largely contributed to, by the tourism and fishing industries. The islands are home to 99,258 people, the majority of whom inhabit the three main islands: Mahé, Praslin, and La Digue.

The Seychellois people enjoy access to a wide range of healthcare services, with a Service Coverage Index of 71, the highest in the African Region, according to the World Health Statistics Report of 2020. The right to health care is incorporated in Article 29 of the Seychelles constitution, as a foundational human right: Seychelles recognizes the rights of its citizens, to protection of health and to the enjoyment of the attainable standard of physical and mental health.

The State, through the Ministry of Health (MOH), is the key health service provider, through a series of primary care centres, cottage hospitals and one tertiary hospital. These are complemented by the (expanding) private
health sector. Three entities exist under the MOH, each responsible for
undertaking a specific set of functions.

**Health Care Agency (HCA)** providing healthcare services; **Public Health
Authority (PHA)** for delivering on public health functions and regulation of
the health sector and the **National AIDS Council (NAC)** for coordinating
and leading the National response to HIV/AIDS epidemic.

The sector works towards achieving the National vision for health: the
“attainment, by all people living in Seychelles, of the highest level of
physical, social, mental and spiritual health and living in harmony with
nature”. Derived from the vision, are the health sector’s goals:

*   Increase life expectancy and healthy life expectancy.
*   Achieve and sustain all dimensions of UHC.
*   Prepare, prevent, detect early, and respond to health emergencies.
*   Promote healthy populations.

## 1.2 The Need for a Policy to Guide Donations

A lack of formal guidelines at National level and the fact that Seychelles
has not endorsed the Paris declaration on aid effectiveness, a gap remains
in terms of guidance around donations, in particular to government
entities.

The MOH receives donation from both local and international donors, but
does not have formal guidance on donations. The development of this
policy was recommended in the Health Task Force Report (2013). The
need is further reiterated in the NHSP 2022-2026 under the Strategic
Direction one, “Strengthen Leadership, Governance, and
Administration”.

In the absence of formal donation guidelines can and has led to a number
of challenges. These include donations not tailored to sector needs, the
lack of quality controls on donated health products, as well as the lack of adequate local technical expertise for optimal use and maintenance of some donated medical equipment.

According to the **National Health Policy (2015)**, almost 90% of medical equipment in use at that time in the sector were donations. Some of these lacked specifications and standardisation, creating challenges for operation, maintenance, repair, and acquisition of spare parts.

There is also some uncertainty about the effectiveness of some donations due to poor reporting. One intervention stated in the **NHSP 2022-2026** is to develop annual reports on grants, technical assistance [TA] and donations; this will help improve the quality and amount of the data available on donations, and build sector knowledge on the effectiveness of the aid.

## 1.3 Purpose and Objectives of the Policy

This is the first policy to guide donations to the health sector. It is well aligned with the wider sectoral goal of fostering meaningful partnership in health. The purpose of this guidance document is to ensure a more formal and managed donation process and improved aid effectiveness.

For the purposes of this policy, donations will refer to cash or in-kind donations to the MOH Seychelles, irrespective of value.

The objectives of this policy are:

* To formalise and standardise the procedures on donations to the MOH;
* To improve transparency and accountability in the process; and
* To improve aid effectiveness.
## 2. RESPONSIBILITIES

MOH receives donations from both local and international partners, and is grateful for their continued support. It is essential that both donors and recipients take responsibility for all donations.

### 2.1 Responsibilities of Ministry of Health

* MOH should ensure that all relevant actors within MOH and its entities, all donors (local and international) and other stakeholders involved in the donation process (including other government Ministries, Departments and Agencies), are aware of the contents of this policy and follow the procedures stated herein.
* MOH should ensure that all offer of donations are carefully considered based on proposed criteria, before acceptance (see Annexes 1 and 2).
* For donation of specialised medical equipment, medical supplies or medicines, the relevant experts in the field should submit relevant MOH specifications/standards and be involved in the process of verifying proposed donations. Where other guiding policies are available for any specific type(s) of donations, MOH must ensure that additional information is shared with donors.
* MOH should ensure effective communication with the donor and intermediary actors throughout the donations process.
* MOH will keep records of the type and value of all donations to the sector and report annually.

## 2.2 Responsibilities of the Donor

* Donors planning to donate to MOH should establish direct contact with the International Co-operation Unit (ICoU) of the MOH at the earliest possible stage of the process. This should always precede any agreement on the nature and timing of any donation.
* Donors should respect the core principles of good donation practices, and the guidance set by this policy.
* Donors should ensure that donations are based on the stated needs of MOH and should be relevant to the health services provided in the Seychelles.
* All donations of medicines should be aligned with the MOH Essential Drugs List and donations of medical equipment and medical supplies should align with specifications set by relevant entities.
* Donors should ensure that donations are dispatched only with the consent of MOH.
* Donors should ensure that donations are obtained from legitimate, reliable sources, complying with the quality standards of both the donor country and the quality standards in Seychelles.
* For donations of medical equipment, donors should also offer the provision of installation assistance, aftercare requirements, and relevant training for local operating and maintenance technicians.
* The donor should ensure that all donated medicines and medical supplies have a shelf-life of at least one year and/or 80% of the shelf-life remaining, whichever is longer.
* After arriving in Seychelles, all donated medical equipment should have a useful life of at least two years.
* The donor should ensure that donated items are appropriately labelled in at least one of the three national languages of Seychelles (English, French or Creole), with relevant support documents to guide use, maintenance, repair, and contacts of the manufacturer.
In the case of medicines and vaccines, the labels should include:

- a. The International Non-Propriety Name (generic name)
- b. Dosage form (where applicable)
- c. Manufacturer name and address
- d. Quantity in container
- e. Storage conditions
- f. Expiry date

* Certificates of Analysis (CoA) should accompany all consignments of medicines, for each batch therein. Where warranted, Certificates of Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) may also be required.

* Donors should ensure that all donations are packed in accordance with international shipping regulations and be accompanied by a detailed packing list which specifies the contents of each package.

  The packing list should specify the contents of each numbered carton by quantity, batch number, expiry date, volume, weight, and any special storage conditions.

## 3 PROCEDURES

Standard procedures for donations are necessary, to ensure that the process is clear and transparent to all parties involved, whilst also ensuring sector accountability.

### 3.1 General Procedures

* All donations to the health sector, whether monetary or in-kind, must follow these procedures.

* This policy is applicable to all donations, both local and international, regardless of estimated value.

* As far as possible, all donations must be channelled through the International Cooperation Unit [ICoU] of the MOH, with the involvement of the relevant entity where indicated.
* The ICoU Unit and respective entity will be responsible for keeping an accurate record of all donations to the MOH ensuring that there are no discrepancies in the data.

## 3.2 Procedures for MOH

### Procedures for Requesting Donations

* The ICoU Unit shall guide the relevant entities throughout the donation process.
* A written request for donation should be submitted to the ICoU Unit to initiate the process.
* All donation requests must be guided by relevant experts in MOH, to determine alignment with needs and conformity to standards and procedures. Available lists of core medical equipment, medicines, vaccines, and medical supplies can be made available as applicable.
* All requests for donations by the MOH shall be approved by the Minister or Principal Secretary.
* All donations from foreign entities must be channelled through the Ministry responsible for Foreign Affairs.

### Procedures for Accepting Donations

The following procedures should be adhered to in the instance of accepting a donation:

* The ICoU Unit, in consultation with the donor, should fill in a pre-donation checklist (see Annex 1) for each donation, detailing the name of the donor, the beneficiary unit for the donation, type of donation, key objective of the donation
* After receiving a donation, it shall be reviewed by respective experts to confirm alignment to what was agreed/expected as technical specifications. A acceptance checklist should be completed by the relevant entity or unit (see Annex 2).
* A written letter of acceptance or refusal (with reasons) of a donation should be issued to the donor once a thorough assessment of the
donation offer has been made.

* Where donation is in the form of credit/cash, ensure currency is exchangeable in Seychelles financial system.
* Upon successful acceptance and receipt of donations (completed forms in Annexes 1 and 2 submitted to ICoU), a letter of receipt of donations, with the appreciation of the MOH, will be issued by the ICoU to the donor.

## 3.3 Procedures for Donors

### Procedures for Unsolicited Donations

* Both local and international donors must contact the ICoU Unit of the MOH when deciding to make a donation.
* The ICoU Unit will provide guidance and make available to the donor:
    * This policy document.
    * Other relevant documents as needed (e.g. Seychelles’ Essential List of Medicines).
* The donation offer will then be reviewed by respective experts or specialised units in the MOH, to determine whether the donation offer is acceptable.
# 4 ACCOUNTING

* Records should note the nature of donation with clear specifications (cash, fixed assets, inventory or other).
* Information on the registered cash equivalent of assets donated for in-kind donations, should be provided.
* Proper accounting procedures for the donation must be followed in accounting systems (ledger system).
* Units/entities and ICoU receiving donations shall keep clear records of their receipt.
* Supporting documents for donations should be maintained in files after receipt.
* A centralised archiving mechanism for donations should be created to maintain adequate records of all donations to MOH.
* Annual reports on donations to MOH will be produced and disseminated by the ICoU, as part of sector accountability.
