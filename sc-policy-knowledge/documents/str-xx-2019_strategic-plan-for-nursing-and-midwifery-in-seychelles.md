# SECTION ONE: SITUATION ANALYSIS

## 1.1 INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND

### 1.1.1 INTRODUCTION

The Republic of Seychelles is a small, high-income island state situated in the western Indian Ocean. The country comprises 115 tropical islands spread over 45,166 hectares. Seychelles is more than 1,500 km from the East Coast of Africa, indicating its remoteness from the mainland continent. The country’s exclusive economic zone covers an area of 1.4 million km2 in one of the world’s major tuna fishing grounds.

The main islands are Mahe, Praslin and La Digue and together they share the bulk of all economic activities. Victoria, the capital of Seychelles, is located on Mahe, the largest of the three main islands. Whilst the three main islands are permanently inhabited, the remaining islands are sparsely populated with a small working population running tourists’ establishments and some are uninhabited.

With gross national income per capita of US$14,760 in 2015, Seychelles is classified as a high-income country. Tourism accounts for 35 percent of GDP, 25 percent of employment, and 70 percent of foreign exchange earnings. Tuna fishing, processing, and ancillary activities represent more than 8 percent of GDP, about 10 percent of jobs, and 93 percent of exported goods. The artisanal fisheries sector is small together with the agricultural sector, its share of GDP is less than 3 percent but it has great socioeconomic significance.

### 1.1.2 THE HEALTH SECTOR

The overarching health sector vision according to the National Health Policy is “the attainment, by all people in Seychelles, of the highest level of physical, social, mental and spiritual health and living in harmony with nature”.

In 2014, following the recommendations of the Health Taskforce to modernize and strengthen the health system, the public health sector implemented a new structure that introduced

Consisting of the health ministry and three public bodies (National Health Policy 2015).

## Health Ministry
With a Secretariat lead by a Principal Secretary, it is responsible for the formulation of health sector policy development, planning, monitoring and evaluation, and oversees the implementation of health strategies by the three public bodies for health care provision, health regulations and training in health care.

## Health Care Agency (HCA)
An autonomous agency that manages the provision of primary, secondary and tertiary care. It provides integrated health care services through community-based care and in-patient care by Seychelles Hospital services. HCA is the main employer of nurses and midwives in Seychelles.

## Public Health Authority (PHA)
An independent entity that regulates the health sector and provides for the protection of the population's health. It regulates health services, health premises, health practitioners as well as the environmental and commercial activities that impact on health. PHA in its prevention programmes employs a small percentage of nurses.

## National Institute of Health and Social Services (NIHSS)
An autonomous entity which is the academic arm of the teaching hospital. It provides pre-service education and continuous in-service education of health workers and is working towards the institutionalization of high level health research. The NIHSS trains the majority of nurses and midwives in Seychelles as well as other health workers.

***

Seychelles has developed a robust network of health facilities with **16 health centres** providing primary health care, supported by:
* One referral hospital
* Four cottage hospitals
* One rehabilitative hospital
* One mental hospital
* One youth health centre

The health centres provide a comprehensive range of services including some nurse led services such as family planning, child health services, school health and domiciliary care. All health centres are staffed by a team of health professionals headed by a nurse manager.

The public facilities are complemented by the private providers, which is on the rise. In 2016, there were:
* 16 general private practitioner clinics
* 10 dentists
* 38 pharmacies

Though private clinics are increasing in number, their size is small and scope of service remains mainly
the provision of primary treatment and limited secondary care, however they are also employers of a small percentage of nurses and midwives. They refer patients to the government-run secondary and tertiary care services when required.

**Seychelles Hospital** is the main referral hospital, offering secondary and some tertiary care, whilst two referral hospitals offer psychiatric and rehabilitative care. The bulk of highly specialized treatment takes place overseas mainly in Chennai, India, Sri Lanka and Reunion Island in hospitals with which Seychelles Government has special agreement.

According to a study on *Service Availability and Readiness Assessment* carried out by World Bank and Seychelles Ministry of Health on health facilities in 2017; Seychelles has 2.6 facilities per 10,000 population, which is higher than the global norm of 2 facilities for that population.

The inpatient bed density of 29.7 bed per 10,000 population is higher than World Health Organization (WHO) global norm of 25 beds and the global average of 27 beds, but lower than the average for high income countries (39 beds per 10,000 population); and with 23.8 maternity beds per 1,000 population, Seychelles exceed WHO target of 10 beds.

### 1.1.3 POPULATION AND HEALTH PROFILE

The population of Seychelles stood at 95,821 as at 31st December 2017, indicating a growth rate of 1.7% over 2016. Almost all its population is settled in three islands (86 percent in the main Mahé Island, 10 percent in Praslin, and 3 percent in La Digue). Only one percent of the population is settled in all the other islands.

In 2017, 1,651 births were registered; the crude birth rate (number of births per thousand mid-year population) was 17.2, and infants <1 year of age comprised 1.7% of the total population. The total fertility rate stands at 3.6. The crude birth rate was slightly lower at 17.2 in 2017 compared to 17.4 in 2016.

There were 748 deaths registered; 18 were among infants which gave an infant mortality rate of 10.9 per thousand live births, a decrease from 13.4 per thousand live births in 2016. Neonatal death constitutes the majority of overall infant mortality, with 9.7 deaths per thousand live births in 2017. The under-five mortality rate was 2.9 per thousand live births. One maternal death
was recorded in 2017, corresponding to a maternal mortality ratio of 60.6 per 100,000 live births (Table 1). The crude death rate continued to decrease from 8.1 in 2005 to 7.8 in 2017.

Life expectancy at births for both sexes stands at 74.8 years. It is 70 years for male and 79 for females. This compares favorably with other small island states with similar economies, and some Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) countries.

The projected population for 2025 is 100,000. The number of births in Seychelles has been reasonably stable over the last five years averaging about 1,601. A breakdown by age group shows that, in 2016, 21.1 percent of the population was under 15 years, 42.4 percent i.e the biggest proportion of the population was between 15-44 years, 27.7 percent 45-64 years and 8.8 percent 65 years and over. The proportion of persons in the over 65 years has been growing over the last five years indicating that there is a shift towards an aging population which will increase the demand for nursing care in future.

By 2035 it is projected that the percentage of children under 15 years would have gone down to 18% and the above 65 years of age would have increased to 16.7%. The percentage of people of working age is projected to have increased slightly in the age group of 45 years age and over.

The burden of disease in Seychelles has shifted to non-communicable diseases, injuries, and mental health problems, for some time now. Preventable risk factors underlie most non-communicable diseases and reflect changing lifestyles and diet - the major risk factors are unhealthy diet, tobacco use, the harmful use of alcohol, and lack of physical activity. Recent figures from WHO indicates that prevalence of smoking is 38.5 percent among men and 5.8 among women.

Non-communicable diseases have been the leading causes of death in the Seychelles since the turn of the century. The three main non-communicable diseases are:
* Cardiovascular (like heart attacks and stroke)
* Respiratory diseases (such as chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and asthma)
* Cancer

Mortality from communicable diseases can be stated as being low. However, in recent years, there has been a rise in mortality from communicable conditions. The key conditions of concern include: human immunodeficiency virus (HIV)/ acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS),
Hepatitis C, leptospirosis, sexually transmitted infections, and vector-borne diseases such as dengue. HIV prevalence amongst the general population is less than 1% and is characterized as a concentrated epidemic amongst the high-risk groups – men-who-have-sex-with-men (MSM), intravenous drug user (IDU), prison inmates and sex workers.

The right to health is enshrined in the constitution and health services are available free of charge at the point of use in all government facilities for all Seychelles citizens. The constitution grants all citizens access to primary care services free of charge, in all public health facilities. Seychelles has attained Universal Health Coverage (UHC) in terms of physical access and absence of financial barriers to health care. The health, nutrition and population outcomes compare favorably with peer economies - 99 percent of deliveries are assisted by skilled health workers.

## 1.2. THE NURSING WORKFORCE

Nursing and Midwifery personnel in Seychelles are the largest group of professional within the health workforce, providing effective nursing care to individuals across the age and health spectrum, working with families, groups, and communities in a diverse range of settings and locations. They play a very significant role in providing and maintaining the health care system. The two professions have been recognized for their crucial contribution to the Millennium Development Goals (MDGs) through several World Health Assembly (WHA) resolutions. Resolution WHA 64.7 on “Strengthening Nursing and Midwifery” recognizes that nurses and midwives constitute a majority of the global health workforce and make significant contributions to improving health outcomes and saving lives through the delivery of wide- ranging nursing and midwifery services, among other important contributions they bring to health service delivery.

Nurses and midwives carry the responsibility of bringing health services to the communities through the spectrum of health care delivery mechanism in the primary health care up to the tertiary levels of health care.

There are currently 811 nurses registered with the Seychelles Nurses and Midwives Council (SNMC). This represents the number of potential nurses and midwives who could practice in Seychelles. According to the SNMC only 660 nurses are practicing in 2017. Of this number 527 or 79.8% are employed in the public sector mainly by HCA and 20% are employed in the private sector. In the private sector, nurses function in hotels, industries and independently. The number of nurses to 1000 population stands at 7. This is comparable to some OECD countries and better than countries in Africa and some small Caribbean island states with similar economies.
The data relating to nursing and midwifery workforce in Seychelles is not readily available in one repository. The three main data sources have been the SNMC, the Chief Nursing Officer’s (CNO) office and Directorate of Nursing Office in the HCA. Different data sets are kept by different groups in nursing management and regulation. It has therefore been difficult to analyze data on the workforce due to the categorization of staff and some inconsistencies in the core data.

Currently, nurses, midwives work in multidisciplinary teams, where individuals have specific tasks and responsibilities, but increasingly they work together as a team to support and care for patients and people in a variety of settings from hospitals, to care homes, private clinics and in hotels.

### 1.2.1 DEPLOYMENT OF NURSES AND MIDWIVES IN HCA

The majority of nurses of nurses and midwives working in HCA are working in the hospital services. A total of 289 nurses or 60.4 % work in Seychelles hospital. 189 or 39.6% work in the community health services manning the health centres and cottage hospitals.

Deployment of nurses and midwives to the different units is done by the directorate of nursing office. Nurses and midwives have very little choice in where they are posted to work. They do not apply to work in one particular unit and can therefore be transferred from acute care to community health based on service needs.

New graduates on recruitment have to work for six months in community health and six months in hospital as part of a programme to consolidate their skills. It is only after the completion of the first year in employment that they are posted to work in one specific unit either in hospital services or community health.

#### 1.2.3 RECRUITMENT AND RESIGNATION

Attracting people to nursing and retaining the nurses that are currently employed is vital to a sustainable health care system. Many countries are facing the problem of shortage of nurses because the supply is not aligned with the demand for nurses. The shortage of nurses in Seychelles is due to a number of factors. The shortage is not only in numbers but just as important is the shortage of skills to meet the changing health landscape. With the advance of more specialism in health and medicine more nurses with specialist skills are needed and generally this evolution in nursing and midwifery in Seychelles has been slow.

The National Institute of Health and Social Studies (NIHSS) is the only institution in the country offering a nursing programme. This is a three year programme leading to a diploma in nursing. Most of the graduates on completion of their studies are recruited to work in HCA. The NIHSS have been not been able to attract the number of students with the right academic level into the programme for a number of years. Lowering the bar for entry into the programme has only resulted in a higher attrition rate among the students and a possible decline in standards.

The number of graduates leaving the NIHSS has not been adequate to meet the demand for nurses in the service area. The demand has also increased due to expansion of services such as addiction therapy, increased number of specialists in acute care services and the increase in private health services. This has also been worsened by the high attrition rate among nurses.
Indeed over the last five years the number of nurses leaving the profession has been more than the number of graduates entering the labour market. Over last five years, according to figures received from the directorate of nursing office, there has been **91 nurses exiting the profession** and NIHSS has produced **81 Graduates** during that period. The numbers have not been adequate to replace the nurses leaving let alone filling existing vacancies and catering for service expansion both in private and public sector. This shows a significant imbalance between the local supply and demand.

The number of nurses recruited over the last five years has steadily increased. The number of resignation has also more than doubled over the last four years as shown in Figure 5. HCA has had to recourse to international recruitment to fill its establishment. Currently there are **74 expatriate nurses** working in the public health system, namely in HCA. The countries of origin are mainly India and Sri Lanka and a few others from Madagascar and Kenya. Active recruitment has only been from India and Sri Lanka, countries experiencing nursing shortages as well. International recruitment of nurses has been a global concern for over a decade with destination countries depleting the stock of nurses through active unregulated recruitment leaving these countries of origin with even bigger shortage. This led the Commonwealth Secretariat and World Health Organization to come up with codes of practice for international recruitment of health workers. Seychelles needs to adhere to the codes of practice in its international recruitment procedure.

### 1.2.6 INTERNATIONALLY RECRUITED NURSES

Seychelles has had to resort to recruiting nurses from other countries to address the problem of shortage of nurses. Recruitment has been mainly from India and Sri Lanka, countries where they have nursing shortages according to WHO. While the number of nurses has increased with international recruitment, there is still shortage of skills in Seychelles hospital.

International recruitment brings its own problems such as culture adaptation and language problem. This is an important consideration in developing any policy for international recruitment as even if a large percentage of people in Seychelles understand English language, they may have difficulty communicating their needs especially when they are ill. Furthermore some of the internationally recruited nurses may have problems with English language as this is not their mother tongue making communication more difficult.
### 1.2.7 Nursing Workforce Data and Information

Nursing data is collected by different nursing units. There is no one unit where comprehensive data on nursing workforce is maintained. Accurate, timely and relevant nursing workforce data should be available from a single, accountable source. This has been the challenge in the preparation of this report. It is a challenge which will underpin any workforce planning and should be addressed as a priority.
### 1.3. STRENGTHS, WEAKNESSES, OPPORTUNITIES AND THREATS IN NURSING

Information was collected through interviews with Chief Nursing Officer, Senior Principal Nursing Officer, Principal Nursing Officers, CEO Health Care Agency, Senior Medical Consultants, Registrar and Chairman SNMC, President Nurses Association, Director National Institute of Health and Social Studies, Nurse Instructors, Nurse Managers from both hospital and community health and two groups of practicing nurses and midwives of different grades, across all levels and units of healthcare delivery. Secondary data was also retrieved from different reports within Ministry of Health and Health Care Agency.

The interviews have helped to identify the strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats that will continue to challenge nursing and midwifery and have also provided a direction for the future of the profession. The findings have been validated at a workshop organized for nurse managers. They indicate that the following are some critical components which are needed for the future:

## 1.3.1 NURSING MANAGEMENT/LEADERSHIP

In this section the term nursing management and nursing leadership will be used interchangeably.

Nursing leadership is required to inform the strategic direction of the national health-care system and play a key role in establishing change that is patient and family centred, evidenced-based and cost-effective. Nursing leadership is about influencing others to accomplish common goals. It is complex and multifaceted - providing support, motivation, coordination and resources to enable individuals and teams to achieve collective objectives. Leadership is therefore seen as an essential component of any nursing role. Understanding and obtaining knowledge of leadership attributes and skills must be an important part of any nurse’s development.

In Seychelles there is a Chief Nursing Officer, who is a senior nurse/midwife working in the Ministry of Health. This is the most senior nursing position and this office advises government on nursing and midwifery issues and brings nursing contributions to health policies and policies impacting on a nursing and midwifery.

The CNO has overall responsibility for providing strategic directions for nursing and midwifery at national level. In delivering on this responsibility the CNO is expected to work with nurse leaders in nursing practice, nursing education and nursing regulations. She has no administrative line of responsibility for these areas of nursing but works with them to develop the strategic direction for the profession and to deliver on the national nursing and midwifery mandate.
The nursing/midwifery services in the HCA, the main employer of the nurses and midwives in the country is headed accordingly by a **Senior Principal Nursing Officer (SPNO)** and assisted by two **Principal Nursing Officers (PNO)**, one responsible for nursing in the hospitals and the second one responsible for nursing services in the community. It is to be noted that the SPNO position is not reflected in the current scheme of service for nurses and midwives. The two levels of management is not explicitly distinct giving rise to overlapping of functions and causing some confusion for nurses as was reported during interviews with nursing personnel.

The next level of nursing management in HCA is **nurse managers** who have dual role of being the administrative managers of units as well as being the clinical leaders. They are responsible for managing people, systems, processes, the environment and resources to enable a high standard of patient care. They are also responsible for ensuring that the patient care in their unit is well co-ordinated and that nursing management of clients and patients are effectively carried out.

In many instances, nurse managers reported that they have had inadequate preparation to assume responsibilities of the role. Many charge nurse managers do not have access to formal leadership education or ongoing mentoring and coaching. Leadership programmes in health and nursing are ad hoc and inconsistent in their programme content and methodology. The management programme when offered has been more of a general management approach and not tailored to health and nursing services.

They reported that feel unsupported as managers and believe that there is no established mechanism to harness their contribution to nursing and midwifery decisions.

They believe that whilst they are responsible and accountable at every level of their practice, profession and organization they lack authority in relation to health-care decision making, distribution of resources and budget control. This situation gives rise to impasses which occur when a leader lacks authority, yet is accountable for results. This situation is impacting the organization causing stagnation, and frustration for the leaders as can be observed in nursing generally.
### 1.3.2 NURSING REGULATIONS

The Seychelles Nurses and Midwives Council is the regulatory authority responsible for the registration of nurses and midwives and its role and responsibilities are outlined in the **Seychelles Nurses and Midwives Act 1985**. Its primary function is to protect members of the public by ensuring that nurses are competent and fit to practice. The SNMC fulfills this function by ensuring that all nurses practicing in the country are registered and have the required competencies and have a current practice license. It checks that those applying to enter the register have the right qualifications and have the right skills to practice safely and effectively. It sets scopes of practice for the professions and standards for education and practice. Its code of ethics sets out the standards for practice and behavior expected of nurses and midwives in Seychelles.

The SNMC investigates complaints about conduct of nurses and takes necessary actions to protect the public. This includes suspension and removal from the register depending on the gravity of the case.

The SNMC is managed by the Registrar, a senior nurse midwife and she is assisted by an Assistant Registrar. The SNMC is made up of 13 council members. 8 of these members are elected by peers and 5 are nominated by the Minister of Health according to criteria sets out in the **Seychelles Nurses and Midwives Act 1985**. The council members serve for a period of two years and members can be re-elected or re-appointed to serve as council members. Apart from the two ex-officio i.e. the Director of Nursing and the nurse in charge of nursing programme in the NIHSS and the representative of Nurses Association of the Republic of Seychelles (NARS), there is no other criteria for other nurses and midwives coming from the service areas to be appointed. Consequently members of council receive no preparation to take on the important role of being a council member when appointed as they are not always conversant with regulatory issues. This obviously impact on their ability to deliver at the level required of them.

The current Seychelles Nurses and Midwives ACT of 1985 is being reviewed to modernize, and it render it more responsive to needs of the professions and to promote a clear vision of professionalism and to be a force for public protection and confidence. The legislation also needs to take into account the development in health and the professions.
The council is currently implementing a programme of relicensing every three years for nurses and midwives. This is based on the premise that as professionals nurses and midwives need to regularly show that they remain fit to practice, which is in line with the culture of life-long learning which needs to be promoted. They are therefore required to follow a number of hours of continuous professional development during that period. The current ACT does not provide for the periodic re-licensing and this has to be regularized soon as it is already being implemented and its legality can be challenged by any nurse or midwife.

The council is also implementing a six month internship progamme for graduates from NIHSS to provide them with necessary skills to build on knowledge gained during their three year education programme. This however is not structured and has not been evaluated to measure its effectiveness.

Nurses and midwives generally do not understand the role of the council, it is perceived as being punitive, as council is seen mostly when matters of a disciplinary nature are referred to them. Its role of benchmarking standards for practice and education has not been fully developed and needs to be stepped up urgently to keep pace with development within the profession. Its supervisory function is seen as being minimal and therefore impacting its function of public protection.

The council is currently working on introducing a licensing examination for nurses and midwives for entry on the register. This will also apply to internationally recruited nurses and midwives. It is believed that this would ensure that only those who are proficient will be able to register with the council and practice in Seychelles.

The role of the council currently does not extend to support workers such as nursing assistants. This cadre is working closely with nurses and midwives and their work is not regulated. There is some concern among trained nurses as the assistants are doing some tasks which traditionally have been seen as nursing duties and their unregulated practice may have some adverse effects on patients’ outcomes.
### 1.3.3 NURSING CLINICAL PRACTICE

Nurses and midwives function in a variety of settings providing care to individuals, families and communities. Their work extends from primary prevention to palliative care. It is believed that nurses and midwives in Seychelles are generally skillful.

Many health prorammes in Primary Health Care are nurse led. These are mainly preventive programmes such as family planning, child health services and domiciliary care. Midwifery care is also generally given by midwives and only cases presenting with abnormality and complications are referred to obstetricians.

Nurses with advanced skills function in some services such as anesthesia, critical care and emergency services.

Nurses and midwives especially those working in HCA feel that they face a number of challenges in the delivery of nursing care. Shortage of manpower and skills has been cited as the main problems. The workload is further increased by the amount of non nursing duties which nurses are currently doing which could be undertaken by less qualified personnel or other health professionals. Demand on nursing and midwifery services is set to increase as health services in the country in both public and private sector continue to develop and evolve.

The lack of staffing norms means that staff distribution is seen as a major concern. Staff distribution is not done according to needs of units and staff rostering is also not done according to workload, patient acuity and unit needs.

The absence of a model of care for nursing in the in-patient facilities and in the community setting is a factor which could compromise care as identification of nursing care needs is not systematically done and patient care can therefore be compromised.

There is a lack of proper monitoring and evaluation system and standards generally. Policies are not always known by all, neither are guidelines nor standard operating procedures available, where they exist they are often not adhered to. Most of the existing guidelines or standard operating procedures have not been reviewed to make them current so as to improve nursing practice. Evaluation of nursing care is not systematically done leading to compromised patient care.
Nurses in interviews referred to unsatisfactory practice environment such as overcrowding in units lack of aids and other resources leading to low morale and de-motivation. This obviously affects their output and ultimately patient care. The workload was reported by nurses to be further burdened by the amount of what nurses considered to be non nursing duties.

### 1.3.4 NURSING EDUCATION

There have been many changes in health and health services in the last few decades. The changes have impacted nursing and midwifery profession and this has necessitated that nurse education moved from an apprenticeship style education to a diploma programme and more nurses are now moving on to a degree programme. This shift has made it possible for nurses to integrate nursing knowledge and clinical practice improving the care they give to clients and patients resulting in more positive health outcomes.

The **NIHSS** is the sole institution in the country providing pre service diploma programme to nurses and post basic education programmes to nurses and midwives. The institution offers Seychelles Qualifications Authority(SQA) validated programmes. The institution has a good ratio of instructors to students and instructors are educated at advanced nursing and midwifery level.

Currently the NIHSS offers a pre-service three year programme in nursing leading to a diploma in nursing qualification. The nursing and midwifery curricula are competency–based. Additionally based on country’s needs, the institute do offer post basic courses in specialized fields of nursing and midwifery. Currently there is a Bachelor of Nursing and a Masters of Nursing programme being offered in collaboration with Chamberlaine University in United States of America.

The institute offers other health related programmes at diploma and certificate level. There is the possibility for inter-professional teaching and learning.

Student nurses and midwives receive their clinical experiences in the government health facilities. These are mainly the Seychelles hospital and the health centres. During their clinical placements students are mainly supervised by senior nurses in the workplaces. The instructors from NIHSS provide some support but this is limited.
Like many training institutions in developing countries, NIHSS have constraints of resources insufficient financial and human resources as well as infrastructure, technology and clinical learning laboratory. Recruitment into the nursing programme and to some extent successful completion of the programme is impacted by poor academic level and problem solving skills of students. The location of the training institution in the north of the country is also believed to influence students’ decision on whether to take up nursing as a career.

The basic competencies of nursing/midwifery programme graduates are often negatively influenced by inadequate clinical supervision and non-practicing, non-expert clinical nursing/midwifery educators and clinicians who do not have adequate preparation and time to provide this function.

The NIHSS is currently offering limited continuing professional development programme for nurses and midwives who are in the service areas. Their capacity to carry out nursing research is also limited. Nursing research is an area which is under developed and requires strengthening and to be further developed.
# SECTION TWO: STRATEGIC PLAN FOR NURSING AND MIDWIFERY

## 2. BACKGROUND

There have been many changes in Seychelles over the last three decades in terms of demographic trends, social developments, changing patterns of illness and technology. The context of health has also changed like in most parts of the world. Most significantly, with people living longer, we have a greater number of older patients and people to support, many with multiple health conditions and complex needs and with higher expectations of what health, care and support can and should deliver.

Ensuring access to quality health services that are responsive to the population needs, and constraints facing health systems in the 21st century is a considerable challenge for governments globally. The Seychelles National Strategic Plan 2016-2020 seeks to address this through strengthening the integration of health services to provide Seychellois citizens with quality services for their holistic well-being in line with their expectations.

Nurses and midwives collectively form the largest component of the health workforce, and have close contact with many people across the age continuum and in all health-service areas. Nursing and midwifery services are vital resources for attaining health and development targets such as strengthening the integration of health services, ensuring clients receive quality services for holistic well-being in line with their expectations. It is therefore imperative that the roles and responsibilities of this large group of the health workforce evolve in parallel with changes taking place in health and in the broader social, epidemiological and economic context if they are to be responsive.

The full potential of this large and important human resource for health needs to be harnessed if the health sector is to deliver on key interventions necessary for the country to address its health priorities as outlined in the SNHSP. It is believed that this will be delivered by the **Strategic Plan for Nursing and Midwifery (SPNM)** which will define the priorities require to address structures and systems to manage, educate, lead and regulate the two professions for their enhanced contribution in improving health outcomes in line with the Seychelles National Health Strategic Plan. The SPNM will provide the road map to guide nursing and midwifery
profession over the next four years. As this is the first strategic plan to be developed for the
profession, it will translate the aspirations of nurses and midwives to maximize its contribution to
health generally develop the profession, builds on the values and principles expressed in the
NHSP 2016-2020 and those outlined in the WHO Strategic Direction for Strengthening Nursing
and Midwifery Services 2018-2022.

The SPNM is the culmination of a range of work which has been undertaken in Seychelles to
maximize nursing contribution in health care, to improve quality of health care to the population,
to assess nursing workload and analysis of patients’ complaints. It has been developed in
consultation with members of the nursing and midwifery family through individual interviews and
a series of workshops. Its development has been overseen by a steering group chaired by the
CNO and a consultant.

## 2.1 VISION STATEMENT

The vision of 2018-2023 for nursing/midwifery in Seychelles is to achieve professional
excellence in education, clinical practice, research, and management, using nursing expertise in
responding to population health needs and shaping health policies through purposeful
engagement with all partners for quality and cost-effective nursing/midwifery care and the
attainment of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals.

This vision statement expresses the desired future for nursing and midwifery in Seychelles. It is
in line with the overall health sector vision as stated in the National Health Policy that is “the
attainment, by all people in Seychelles, of the highest level of physical, social, mental and
spiritual health and living in harmony with nature”. Through professional excellence it seeks to
meet the population health needs and contributes to the continued attainment of universal
health coverage in Seychelles both in terms of accessibility as well as quality care.

## 2.2 MISSION STATEMENT

Nurses and midwives advancing the profession, using professional knowledge, and applying
advanced skills and compassion to provide person and family-centered care based on best
practice for the optimal well being and positive health outcomes. Advocating for all those they
serve and the wider community, while respecting each person's right to safe, timely, effective
and coordinated care, protecting his/her dignity, privacy and confidentiality.
The mission statement places emphasis on the right to health and respect for human dignity. The nursing and midwifery profession aligns itself to the overall mission of the health sector’s mission which seeks to “relentlessly promote, protect and restore health and quality of life and dignity of all people in Seychelles with the active participation of all stakeholders, through the creation of an enabling environment for citizens to make informed decisions about their health”.

## 2.3 GUIDING PRINCIPLES

The guiding principles will help the profession to achieve its vision and mission whilst maintaining maintain a focus on its core values and the goal of improving health, social equity and improving health outcomes. Nurses are committed to providing ethical nursing practice based on an identified code of ethics. As a caring profession we believe:

* In delivering the highest level of care with compassion, sensitivity and competence in an ethical and caring manner across the health-illness continuum to maintain a healthy nation, prevent diseases and hospital admissions, manage acute episodes of illness when they arise, and lead improvement in health care.
* Health is a human right and patients have the right and need to be empowered to participate in their own care whether the goal is quality of life or dignity of death.
* In integrity, respecting the dignity and moral wholeness of every person without conditions or limitation, affirming the uniqueness of and differences among persons ideas, values, ethnicities, culture, political beliefs, religion or sexual orientation.
* In demonstrating competence and commitment to lifelong learning for improving the quality of care we give to clients and patients.
* In being accountable for the care clients and patients receive and have the courage to do the right thing for the people in our care and advocate for them and embrace new ways of working.

The vision and value statements need to be an integral part of the ways of working for the profession. Every member of the profession should make it his/her responsibility to express these values in his daily interactions with clients/patients, peers and colleagues and these should underpin every professional decision.

#### 3.1.4 MODERNIZING NURSING REGULATION

Changes in the health service environment, the changing nature of the health workforce, and increasing specialization by medical practitioners and nurses have led to nurses taking on a range of new practices and decision-making responsibilities. Tasks that may have once been considered nursing are frequently being taken on by nursing assistants and tasks that were once considered the domain of medical practitioners are now part of everyday nursing practice. The flexibility of the nursing workforce in being able to quickly assimilate new practices and be deployed where most needed, has been critical to improving health outcomes for the population.
Increasingly, nurses and midwives as well as other members of health professions are being held accountable for their practice. The primary goal of professional nursing care is improved health outcomes for individuals, families, communities and society. Part of improving health outcomes is to ensure the safety of the public who are the recipients of nursing care. One of the roles of the SNMC is to protect the public by regulating nursing and midwifery practice. SNMC defines the extent and limits of practice of all categories of nurses and midwives. As the two professions evolve in response to changing health landscape, nurses and midwives seek to enhance their capacity to improve health outcomes for people, their scopes of practice must be sufficiently broad and enabling to ensure practice is not limited unnecessarily, yet still ensure patient safety. Legislations need to facilitate this and potential legislative barriers need to be addressed so that nurses and midwives working in advanced roles have regulated scopes of practice.

The Seychelles Nurses and Midwives ACT of 1985 needs to be amended to cater for the changing professions as a result of wider national and global societal developments and practices.

**Goal:** Institute high standards of self governance for quality service and public protection in line with nursing and midwifery development.

### STRATEGIC INTERVENTIONS:

* Ensure the safety of the public through the formulation of legislative frameworks and processes which also support effective and efficient use of all nursing personnel.
* Ensure that the definitions and scope of practice of nursing and midwifery are in line with nursing development and are embodied in legislation and that mechanisms are in place to safeguard the public.
* Define the scopes of practice for nurses and midwives in advanced practice.
* Ensure high level of professional self-regulation.
* Develop national standards for nursing practice, education, and management.
### 3.1.5 Building and Strengthening Leadership, Management and Governance

Nurses and midwives together form the largest group of health professionals in all countries, and they are central to delivering safe, high-quality, effective and efficient health services. It is crucial that this important group of health professionals are well managed to optimize the professions’ contribution to health outcomes through strong management and clinical leadership. Leadership in nursing and midwifery is crucial to the quality of patient/client care and to the development of the professions.

Leaders need to be competent, confident, well motivated and socially skilled. They need to be team players who have the ability to work with others in and across professionals and organizational boundaries. Nursing and midwifery needs to adopt a succession approach to leadership ensuring that leaders are identified and nurtured to provide strategic leaders in the future.

**Goal:** Strengthen nursing and midwifery governance and management at all levels to maximize professional contribution to health policy, planning and development ensuring the delivery of cost-effective, evidence-based, safe programmes and services.

#### Strategic Interventions:

* Develop effective approaches to strengthen nursing/midwifery leadership capacities and the inclusion of nurses and midwives in the development of health policies and programmes at all levels.
* Enhance the contributions of nursing and midwifery services for achieving population health targets in line with the NSP.
* Increase the visibility of nursing and midwifery leadership and input in health programmes at all levels.

## 3.3 MONITORING AND IMPLEMENTATION

The implementation of the SPNM will be coordinated by the CNO’s office within the Ministry of Health. All nursing organizations will be responsible for taking forward the strategy, identifying the key interventions they need to contextualized and develop activities to address the needs.

At an individual level each nurse midwife will be responsible to embrace the perspectives expressed in the strategy.

The Directors of Nursing in HCA, the director of NIHSS, the Registrar of the Nurses and Midwives Council, and the President of the Nurses Association will develop plans of action for the implementation of the strategy. The strategy will run for a period of four years so the implementation of these areas of action will be within this time frame.

At the national level the CNO will work with all partners to move forward the implementation and will support and monitor the progress of the strategy.

### 3.3.1 MONITORING AND EVALUATION

The SPNM will require a strong and effective monitoring and evaluation framework for the successful implementation of the strategy. Having a strong plan is important to ensure that progress is monitored and necessary actions needed are identified and executed when necessary. The plan will also highlight the different reporting mechanisms required to enable nursing and midwifery professionals meet any reporting obligations both locally and internationally.

The CNO will be responsible to have the monitoring and evaluation framework developed and disseminated to all partners and those concerned. This will be done in line with the National Performance Monitoring and Evaluation Policy of the Ministry of Heath.
