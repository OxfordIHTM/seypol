# Guiding Principles

## Alzheimer's Includes Other Dementias

Reference to Alzheimer's disease or dementia in this document is considered to also include other forms of dementia.

## Person and Family Centered

Develop the Plan with the needs of elderly and people with dementia, their care partners, family members, and guardians at the frontline; this includes sensitivity to cultural values and beliefs.

## Life-Course Approach

We are in this together, from before diagnosis through end-of-life care. This Plan recognizes that we can each do something to help individuals and government address this issue holistically.

## The Idea of Healthy Ageing

Seychelles has an ageing population. Good care and support for elderly is a fundamental aspect of health ageing in any society.

Through quality care, the negative outcomes from Dementia and other age related diseases, can be minimized. This will lead to a better life for the sufferer and their care-givers, as well as lower burdens on the health system in the longer-run.

## The Current and Foresseeable Future Impact of Dementia in Seychelles

Dementia is a general term used to describe a group of conditions characterized by the loss of memory, language, ability, number among others. The most common symptom of dementia include changes in language, problem-solving, or the ability to recognize or identify familiar people, objects, or place.

Alzheimer's is the most common form of dementia, accounting for 60-80% of cases, and vascular dementia is the next most common at around 17% of cases, unless

The Alzheimer's Association identifies ten early signs and symptoms of dementia:

1. Memory that disrupts daily life;
2. Challenges in planning or solving problems;
3. Difficulty completing familiar tasks at home, at work, or at leisure;
4. Confusion with time or place;
5. Trouble understanding visual images and spatial relationships;
6. New problems with words in speaking or writing;
7. Misplacing things and losing the ability to retrace steps;
8. Decreased or poor judgment;
9. Withdrawal from social activities and;
10. Changes in mood and personality.

### Risk Factors for Dementia

The impact of Alzheimer's dementia is already being felt across Seychelles - on individuals and families, as well as the government. Emotional and financial responsibilities, on local communities through lost productivity from family caregivers and state-funded beneficiaries, and on local healthcare systems, which incur significant costs for care and services. And, the greatest challenges are yet to come.

While Alzheimer's dementia is not a normal part of aging, the ageist risk factor for developing this condition is age. The prevalence of Alzheimer's dementia increases significantly and exponentially after age 65. With our aging population, the prevalence of Alzheimer's dementia is predicted to increase in coming years.

First-degree relatives (mother and father) and lack of (physical activity) and other co-existing medical conditions (diabetes, hypertension, depression and obesity) are other risk factors for which increases chances of developing Alzheimer's dementia. A family history of Alzheimer's and genetic influences associated with this also increase risk.

## Impact on Individuals

Studies suggest that people guess 45 or over, over an average duration of four to eight years after a diagnosis of Alzheimer's disease, but some survive as long as twenty years. The progressive nature of dementia, the impact of the disease on the person's cognition, and the resulting loss of independence create particular challenges for individuals.

In the early stages, declining memory may lead to challenges in managing finances, work or activities of daily living, and social obligations. As the disease progresses, people with dementia may also experience difficulty in initiating activities, decision making and behavioural and psychological symptoms.

Over time, they depend more and more on family or others, to help them with daily living. More than 60% of people with dementia may wander away from home at some point during the disease. And those who wander are at risk of getting lost or injured. Some of the most common reasons for hospitalizations of people with Alzheimer's. Eventually, people with dementia need total help for washing, dressing, eating and toileting.

The symptoms of Alzheimer's and other dementia, lack of insight, shortcomings in memory, judgment, and the ability to plan and carry out activities, creates greater risks for self-
neglect, malnutrition, injury, medication errors, financial exploitation, uncut nome cares, and nursing home placement – which is often even greater for those who live alone.

### Impact on Families and Caregivers

Dementia affects the whole family. Family members provide the vast majority of care for those living with dementia. Family members caring for a person with dementia in America provide an average of 21.5 hours per week of care.

While most family members provide such caring willingly and experience benefits through this role, for many the experience is stressful and conducive to negative outcomes – stress, health risks, depression, and emotional strain. The greatest impacts on family caregivers are increased stress, depression, anxiety, and caregiver burden. In some cases, caregivers may experience burnout.

Findings also indicate that caregivers of people with dementia are twice as likely, as compared to non-caregivers, to experience depression. Caregivers are also more likely to have a health condition such as depression and/or anxiety. They are also more likely than non-caregiving persons to report their health as fair or poor.
These emotional and physical impacts lead to increased health care costs for dementia caregivers nationwide.

## Impact on Health Care Systems

As the number of people with Alzheimer's and other dementias increases, so will the pressure in health care systems, which will lead to an increase in potentially avoidable emergencies (e.g. accidents or preventable hospitalisations).

People with Alzheimer's and other dementias are more likely to have other (chronic) conditions than those without. Dementia thus influences quality of care these conditions must, as diabetes and hypertension, leading to more frequent visits to clinics and hospitals.

## Impact on Government, Other Payers and Society

Dementia, including Alzheimer's disease is one of the costliest diseases to society. It affects a diverse range of people that have dementia and other dementias. The largest component of costs attributable to dementia is the cost for institutional and home-based long-term supports and services.

Dementia also impacts employment and economy, as more family caregivers need to sing most hours, reduce work hours or leave their jobs completely.

### The Importance of Integrating Alzheimer's/ Dementia Care into the National Health/Services

Proposed state level health care reform should be focused on changes in the health system that include a strengthened patient-centered holistic medical home model concentrating on the services to chronic health care management.

As chronic health care management evolves in Seychelles, it is critical that the emerging dementia and best practices around the identification, treatment and management of dementia and disorders associated with dementia are integrated into the health system and transformations effect at the state and local levels. Improvements must include the capacity to:

* Detect early signs and symptoms of cognitive impairment;
* Make and disclose a timely diagnosis;
* Provide care coordination for adequate decision-making and end-of-life care;
* Coordinate care for wellness, medical and support services needs;
* Provide caregiver education and support for those caring for persons with dementia;
* Promote independent and active engagement of persons with dementia in the community.
