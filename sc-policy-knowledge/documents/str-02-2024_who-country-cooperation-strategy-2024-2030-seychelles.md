# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Republic of Seychelles and the World Health Organization (WHO) have collaborated to develop the fourth **Country Cooperation Strategy (CCS-4)** for the period of 2024 to 2030. The CCS-4 outlines the strategic directions and priorities for WHO's partnership with the Government and other stakeholders in the health sector over the next seven years. It is in line with:

* Seychelles' Vision 2033
* The National Development Strategy 2024-2028
* The National Health Policy 2015

The CCS-4 is also consistent with the **Seychelles National Health Strategic Plan (NHSP) 2022-2026**, which aims to achieve the highest level of physical, social, mental, and spiritual health for all individuals in Seychelles while living in harmony with nature. The plan focuses on increasing life expectancy, achieving universal health coverage (UHC), preparing for and responding to health emergencies, and promoting healthy populations.

The CCS-4 is also aligned with the **UN 2030 Sustainable Development Agenda** goals, the Seychelles UN Sustainable Development Cooperation Framework (UNSDCF) 2024-2028, and the Africa Health Strategy (2016-2030) of the African Union. It reflects the goals of the Thirteenth General Programme of Work 2019-2023 (GPW13) and the Fourteenth General Programme of Work 2025-2028 (GPW14) of the WHO Secretariat, which aim to achieve UHC, address health emergencies, and promote health and well-being for all populations.

The development of the CCS-4 was based on the recommendations from the final evaluation of CCS-3 (2016-2021) and extensive consultations with the Ministry of Health, other ministries, departments, and agencies involved in broader health determinants, non-governmental organizations, development partners, and other health stakeholders. The CCS-4 aims to strengthen national institutional and systemic capacities to facilitate WHO's transition from certain forms of support in the future.

Since the late 1970s, the government of Seychelles has been committed to providing free health services through a network of community health centers and well-equipped hospitals, supplemented by overseas evacuations for complex cases. This has led to the expansion of UHC for essential health services and the achievement of positive health indicators, making Seychelles one of the best-performing countries in the WHO African Region.

The collaboration between the Republic of Seychelles and the World Health Organization (WHO) recognizes several important factors. Given the shift from communicable to non-communicable diseases as the leading causes of morbidity and mortality, the cooperation will prioritize "whole-of-government" and "whole-of-society" approaches to address the broader determinants of health. This includes supporting the reorientation of the national health system to provide equitable health promotion and disease prevention services through the **Primary Health Care (PHC)** system, with a focus on cost-effectiveness and sustainability.

Additionally, there will be an emphasis on institutionalizing performance assessments of the national health system and national health research system. This will generate relevant evidence to inform decision-making processes. Considering the potential increase in acute public health emergencies associated with climate change, the cooperation will also focus on strengthening multi-level capacities for prevention, preparedness, rapid detection, and response.

To maximize the effectiveness and efficiency of cooperation, resources will be allocated to areas where WHO has a comparative advantage and optimizes synergies with other development partners and stakeholders. The fourth Country Cooperation Strategy (CCS-4) outlines four strategic priorities.
# WHO Country Cooperation Strategic Agenda for Seychelles (2024-2030)

The WHO Country Cooperation Strategic Agenda for Seychelles, spanning from 2024 to 2030, outlines the mission, vision, and strategic priorities for improving the health and well-being of the population. 

**Mission:** To create a Seychelles where all individuals achieve the highest level of physical, mental, and social well-being while living in harmony with nature. This aligns with the goals of the National Health Sector Strategic Plan 2022-2026 and the national Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

**Vision:** For the people of Seychelles to experience better health, well-being, and dignity at all stages of life throughout the country.

## Strategic Priorities

1. Enhance healthy living and accelerate actions to address the risk factors and determinants of health.
2. Strengthen universal health coverage (UHC) and service quality and address priority communicable and noncommunicable diseases.
3. Safeguard the health and well-being of the population against public health emergencies.
4. Elevate WHO health leadership, advocacy, and innovations for health.

## Strategic Deliverables

### Priority 1: Healthy Living
* **1.1:** Enhance institutionalization of health and well-being in all sectoral policies to tackle risk factors of priority noncommunicable & communicable diseases and injuries.
* **1.2:** Enhance healthy living across the life course, mental health, and control substance abuse.

### Priority 2: UHC and Service Quality
* **2.1:** Improve the performance of the health system, including the organization and quality of health services, as well as reducing inequities in access to essential health services and interventions across all stages of life.
* **2.2:** Enhance programs, regulatory frameworks, and interventions to address priority noncommunicable and communicable diseases.

### Priority 3: Public Health Emergencies
* **3.1:** Strengthen preparedness and integrated surveillance systems to prevent and mitigate emerging health risks, such as epidemics, pandemics, and hazards related to climate change.
* **3.2:** Enhance the capacity to respond effectively to all health emergencies and improve timeliness and effectiveness of health emergencies responses.

### Priority 4: Leadership and Innovation
* **4.1:** Strengthen WHO’s presence, leadership, advocacy, and strategic communications to drive health impact in Seychelles.
* **4.2:** Improve capacities for health research, innovation & digital transformation, information systems, and use of data for health decision-making.

## PROMOTING A HEALTHIER POPULATION

Life expectancy, a critical indicator of population health, reflects the average number of years an individual can expect to live from birth. In 2020, Seychelles achieved average life expectancy (ALE) of 77.3 years, reflecting substantial progress in health outcomes. However, the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic in 2022 had a discernible impact. The ALE declined to 74.2 years, representing a 4.1% reduction. Specifically, male life expectancy decreased by 4.0% (equivalent to 2.9 years), while female life expectancy declined by 5.1% (equivalent to 4.2 years).

Indeed gender disparities were fully evident, with an 8-year gap existing between male and female life expectancies in 2022. Notably, in 2019, a significant divergence of 10.1 years (15.8%) existed between health-adjusted life expectancy at birth (HALE) and both sexes ALE. This divergence likely reflects socioeconomic determinants impacting health-related quality of life. The pandemic’s disruptive effects on health systems, healthcare access, and overall well-being contributed to the observed decline in life expectancy.

# Health Statistics and Mortality in Seychelles

## Mortality Rates
In 2022, Seychelles recorded a neonatal mortality rate of 8.3 and an infant mortality rate of 15.3 per 1,000 live births. Remarkably, the maternal mortality ratio was recorded to be zero per 100,000 live births.

## Disability-Adjusted Life Years (DALYs)
A total loss of 31,888.4 Disability-Adjusted Life Years (DALYs) was indicated in 2019 due to a combination of morbidities, functional losses, and premature mortality. 

### Demographics of DALYs
*   **Persons aged 14 years and below:** 8.3% of total DALYs lost.
*   **Persons aged 15-64 years:** 64.0% of total DALYs lost.
*   **Persons aged 65 years and above:** 27.7% of total DALYs lost.

### Causes of DALY Loss
*   **Noncommunicable Diseases (NCD):** 76.6% (24,437.06). Primary contributors include cardiovascular diseases, neoplasms (cancers), diabetes, kidney diseases, musculoskeletal disorders, and digestive diseases.
*   **Communicable, Maternal, Neonatal, and Nutritional Diseases (CMNND):** 13.9% (4,429.22). Significant roles were played by respiratory infections, tuberculosis, maternal and neonatal disorders, and enteric infections.
*   **Injuries:** 9.5% (3,022.14). Primary causes were transport-related and unintentional injuries.

**Gender Distribution:** Approximately 59.0% of DALY loss affected males, while 41.0% affected females.

## Leading Causes of Death

### Cardiovascular Diseases (CVDs)
In 2022, CVDs were responsible for 280 deaths (152 males and 128 females), accounting for 58% of total NCD-related mortality and 30% of all registered deaths. Breakdown of CVDs:
*   Ischaemic heart diseases: 41%
*   Cerebrovascular diseases: 15%
*   Hypertensive diseases: 13%
*   Atherosclerosis: 5%
*   Other cardiovascular conditions: 26%

### Cancers (Neoplasms)
Cancers accounted for 159 deaths (102 males and 57 females), representing 33% of total NCD-related mortality and 17% of all registered deaths.
*   **Males:** Prostate cancer (32%), lip/oral cavity/pharynx (13%), trachea/bronchus/lung (9%), colorectal (6%), and others (40%).
*   **Females:** Colorectal cancer (18%), breast cancer (16%), cervical-uteri cancer (12%), ovary cancer (7%), and others (47.4%).

## NCD Risk Factors
*   **Tobacco Smoking:** Age-standardized prevalence (15+ years) declined from 25.9% (43.0% males, 8.8% females) in 2015 to 20.2% in 2020.
*   **Alcohol Consumption:** Per capita consumption (15+ years) increased from 10.8 liters in 2016 to 12.0 liters in 2021.
*   **Childhood Nutrition:** 
    *   **Stunting:** Declined from 7.9% in 2016 to 7.2% in 2022.
    *   **Overweight:** Declined from 10.2% in 2016 to 5.2% in 2022.

### Future Projections
According to the Ministry of Health (MOH), NCDs such as hypertension, diabetes, and cancer exhibit a consistent upward trend linked to poor nutrition, sedentary lifestyles, and substance abuse. By 2030, compared to 2013 estimates:
*   Cancer incidence is projected to double.
*   Hypertension prevalence is projected to rise by 34%.
*   Diabetes prevalence is projected to rise by 49%.
*   Obesity prevalence is projected to rise by 52%.

## Communicable Diseases (CDs)
*   **COVID-19:** In 2022, it was the fourth leading cause of death, claiming 97 lives (51 males, 46 females). Age distribution: 71% (65+ years), 21% (45-64 years), and 8% (25-44 years).
*   **Pneumonia:** Responsible for 77 deaths (42 males, 35 females) in 2022, accounting for 8% of all registered deaths (73% were aged 65+).
*   **Leptospirosis:** Rare, but caused three deaths (all male) in 2022.
In 2021 and 2020. **Dengue Fever** caused 3 deaths in 2022; 1 female (aged 70 years) and 2 males (aged 57 and 66 years). **HIV/AIDS** was responsible for 27 deaths (19 males and 8 females) in 2022. The AIDS-related mortality rate per 100,000 population declined from 29.2 in 2021 to 22.5 in 2022, following a peak of 10.2 in 2020.

There also were external causes of mortality including accidental falls, drowning, homicide, suicide and road traffic accidents. Accidental falls were responsible for 9 deaths (6 males and 3 females) in 2022. Accidental drownings accounted for 16 deaths (13 males and 3 females) in same year. Homicides and suicides were responsible for 6 (4 males and 2 females) and 13 (9 males and 4 females) deaths respectively in 2022. Road Traffic Accidents (RTAs) killed 19 people (14 males and 5 females), thus making RTAs to account for 2% of all deaths in 2022, with a mortality rate of 19.1 per 100,000 population. All in all, the number of deaths from external causes increased by 61.5% between 2018 and 2022 with suicides and RTAs having the highest increases of 30% and 111.1% during this period.

# NEXT SEVEN YEARS: KEY HEALTH SYSTEM ISSUES AND AREAS OF FOCUS

Seychelles needs to prioritize several key areas in the next seven years to protect and improve population health and the performance of its health system. These areas include:
- Enhancing the national health system's performance and resilience by adopting a primary health care approach
- Strengthening leadership and governance
- Engaging non-state actors
- Ensuring an adequate and skilled workforce
- Developing climate-sensitive financial frameworks
- Improving the quality of services across different life stages

The country also needs to focus on addressing infectious diseases such as pneumonia, sexually transmitted infections, HIV/AIDS, tuberculosis, dengue fever, and leptospirosis. This can be achieved through:
- Improved disease surveillance, preparedness, and response
- Implementation of international health regulations
- A One-Health approach that integrates human, animal, and environmental health

In addition, Seychelles should prioritize addressing the determinants of health through promoting healthy lifestyles, addressing socioeconomic and environmental factors that impact health outcomes, and fostering health-promoting environments in various settings like homes, schools, workplaces, and communities. The country should also collaborate across different sectors to create policies that positively impact health and strengthen its resilience against climate-related health risks.

Furthermore, Seychelles needs to focus on tackling non-communicable diseases and their risk factors like tobacco use, unhealthy diets, physical inactivity, and alcohol consumption. It should prioritize mental health promotion, prevention, and treatment, as well as combat substance misuse through prevention programs and support services. Additionally, leveraging technology for efficient health information management and exploring digital solutions will also be crucial for improving the health system.

## DEVELOPMENT PARTNERSHIPS: MAIN HEALTH DEVELOPMENT PARTNERS IN SEYCHELLES
# GOVERNMENT

In recognition of the complex nature of health issues, the Government of Seychelles established close working arrangements between the Ministry of Health and other sectors including education, housing, environmental sanitation, water supply, food security, and community development [73]. This approach was in alignment with the principles in **Alma Ata Declaration on Primary Health Care (PHC)** in 1978 and the **Ottawa Charter for Health Promotion** in 1986 which emphasized the importance of intersectoral action for the promotion of health, achievement of health equity, and upholding of health as a human right [74,75].

Seychelles committed to intersectoral action and addressing determinants of health by co-signing the 2009 **Ouagadougou Declaration on PHC and Health Systems strengthening in Africa** on coordinated efforts across sectors [76,77]. The global community further reaffirmed this commitment at the Global Conference on PHC in Astana, Kazakhstan in October 2018 through the adoption of the **Declaration of Astana**, which called for addressing economic, social, and environmental determinants of health and incorporating a Health-in-All-Policies (HiAP) approach [78].

Reflecting its dedication to promoting health and health equity, the Seychelles Government Cabinet endorsed the HiAP pledge on September 20, 2017, committing to prioritize health in all national policies [64]. The Ministry of Health (MOH) played a crucial role in guiding and coordinating the efforts of other sectors in positively influencing the social determinants of health [64]. Recognizing that health is integral to all 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), the Cabinet acknowledged the need for a comprehensive, intersectoral approach to achieve these goals.

As a result, the **Seychelles HiAP Policy Network**, comprising all relevant ministries, departments, and agencies (MDAs) listed in Annex 4, was established in 2022 to facilitate the integration of health considerations into all sectors' policies and actions [79]. The MOH established the HiAP Task Force to support the work of this policy network. The current Government of the Republic of Seychelles (GoRS) has outlined its plans to revitalize HiAP in its National Development Strategy (2024-2028) [6] and National Health Strategic Plan 2022-2026 [7]. The World Health Organization (WHO) is committed to supporting in institutionalization of HiAP to promote the health and well-being of the population.

## THE CIVIL SOCIETY, PRIVATE SECTOR, AND ACADEMIA

**Civil Society:** In November 2014, the Citizens Engagement Platform Seychelles (CEPS) was established as the national umbrella organization for civil society in Seychelles. It currently consists of 72 accredited non-governmental organizations from various sectors. The CEPS has organized these organizations into seven commissions based on different themes, such as environment and natural resources, faith-based organizations, and gender rights. The goal of the CEPS is to promote the active involvement of its member organizations in national and international development. Specifically, it aims to engage in national decision-making, policy formulation, and law-making, as well as advocate for civil society's role in development. Many health-related non-governmental organizations, including the Cancer Concern Association and the HIV and AIDS Support Organization, are members of the CEPS. However, there is currently no formal agreement or collaboration between the CEPS and the Ministry of Health (MOH) to enhance coordination between health-related civil society organizations and MOH entities.

**Private Sector:** The involvement of the private sector in the health sector is still in its early stages but is growing. Currently, there are 64 general medical practitioners, 16 dentists, and 63 pharmacists practicing in the private health sector. The Ministry of Health recognizes the importance of private
sector organizations in improving the health and well-being of the population. The private sector has the potential to contribute to the improvement of health through various avenues, such as providing private health services, ensuring occupational safety and health of workers, contributing to corporate social responsibility schemes, and supporting environmental and public health initiatives. However, to optimize the contributions of the private sector, a formal framework for coordination, collaboration, participation, and accountability needs to be established. The MOH's **National Health Strategic Plan 2022-2026** aims to ensure cooperation and policy synergy between the public, private sectors, and civil society.

**Academia:** There are two main academic institutions in Seychelles involved in health-related research and education:

*   **The National Institute of Health and Social Studies (NIHSS):** Transferred from the Ministry of Education to the Ministry of Health in 2014. Its objective is to provide tertiary-level training in health, allied health, and social services. It currently offers certificate and diploma courses, but not degree-level programs. The NIHSS is also involved in conducting basic research and publishing academic literature.
*   **The University of Seychelles (UniSey):** Established in 2009 and consists of two faculties and several research institutes. They collaborate on research projects to develop practical applications for Seychelles' transitioning into a knowledge-based economy. UniSey has a research journal called *Seychelles Research Journal (SRJ)*, which publishes articles twice a year. However, at the time of the assessment, there was no evidence of a research policy or a research ethics review board at UniSey, although the establishment of a robust research governance framework is planned in its strategic plan.

**The Guy Morel Institute (TGMI)** was formerly affiliated with the Seychelles University before becoming independent. It provides professional development in logistics and contract law and financial services through collaborations with international institutions. It collaborates with the Ministry of Health on capacity building of healthcare professionals, research and policy development.

## SOUTH-SOUTH COOPERATION
South-South Cooperation in Seychelles dates back to the late 1970s when the post-independence government recognized the central role of health in national development and proactively promoted equitable healthcare access. Cooperation agreements were established with countries such as **Cuba, China, Nigeria, and India**, enabling the recruitment of medical doctors. Additionally, agreements were made for overseas training of doctors and dentists in former Eastern Europe, Cuba, and Zimbabwe. Seychelles has had a long-standing bilateral cooperation with Cuba since April 1978, which extends to various sectors including health, education, environment, sports, culture, economy, and social spheres.

## SOUTHERN AFRICAN DEVELOPMENT COMMUNITY (SADC)
Seychelles joined the Southern African Development Community (SADC) on 31 July 1998, becoming the 14th member. In August 1999, Seychelles signed the **SADC Protocol on Health**. As a member of SADC, Seychelles has actively contributed to the development of the **SADC Vision 2050**, with a focus on social and human capital development. Key cross-cutting issues in the vision include gender, youth, environment and climate change, and disaster risk management.

The SADC Health Ministers have developed a Health Policy Framework that addresses ten areas:
1. Health research and surveillance
2. Health information systems
3. Health promotion and education
4. HIV and AIDS
5. Communicable and non-communicable disease control
6. Disabilities
7. Reproductive health
8. Health human resources development
9. Nutrition and food safety
10. Violence and substance abuse

## INDIAN OCEAN COMMISSION (IOC)
Seychelles is a member of the **Indian Ocean Commission (IOC)**, an intergovernmental organization consisting of five member states - the Union of Comoros, France (on behalf of Réunion), Madagascar, Mauritius, and Seychelles. The IOC was established in 1982 and institutionalized in Seychelles in 1984. 

The IOC governance structure includes:
* The Summit of Heads of State and Government
* The Council of Ministers of Foreign Affairs
* The Committee of Permanent Liaison Officers
* The General Secretariat based in Mauritius

The General Secretariat is responsible for implementing decisions, managing cooperation projects, mobilizing resources from development partners, and entering into agreements with specialized agencies. The IOC Strategic Development Plan (2023-2033) focuses on resilience, peace and security, human, cultural and economic development, and institutional and partnership architecture. The IOC collaborates with various health technical partners including the World Health Organization (WHO).

## AFRICA SMALL ISLANDS DEVELOPING STATES (SIDS)

Seychelles has actively participated in the **African Small Islands Developing States (SIDS) Network** since its establishment in 2006. The network includes five African countries - Cabo Verde, Comoros, Mauritius, Sao Tome and Principe, and Seychelles. 

The SIDS Network addresses various recurring themes such as:
* Universal health coverage (UHC)
* Health system strengthening
* Pooled procurement of quality medicines
* Noncommunicable diseases
* Human resources for health
* Communicable diseases (especially HIV and malaria)
* Illicit drug use
* Health security and emergencies
* Climate change

In 2011, the SIDS Network adopted a Plan of Action for the health sector's adaptation to climate change, which was implemented from 2012 to 2016. The SIDS also actively participated in the Third Global Conference on Health and Climate Change, where the "Climate Change and Health in Small Island Developing States - Regional Plan of Action for SIDS in the African and South-East Asian Regions" was adopted.

## BILATERAL AND MULTILATERAL DONORS

In 2015, Seychelles was declared a high-income country. Consequently, during the 2017 review conducted by the Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) Development Assistance Committee (DAC) on the list of Official Development Assistance (ODA) Recipients, Seychelles was classified as a graduating country and this classification took effect from 1 January 2018 [90].

According to the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), countries that graduate from the Least Developed Country (LDC) status may face potential challenges such as losing access to preferential trade benefits targeted at less developed countries. This includes unilateral duty-free market access and relaxed rules of origin requirements in major importing countries. In addition, concessional development financing provisions can also be affected [91].

According to the Ministry of Health (MOH) National Health Accounts, in 2018, Seychelles allocated a total of **SCR 1,166,736,420** to health expenditure. The funding breakdown was as follows:

| Source | Percentage |
| :--- | :--- |
| Government | 74.7% |
| Households | 23.2% |
| Corporations | 1.3% |
| Other countries (donors) | 0.7% |
| Non-profit institutions serving households (NPISH) | 0.05% |

Therefore, donor funding for health accounted for less than 1% of the total health expenditure in Seychelles.

As a result of its graduation from the OECD DAC list of recipient countries, Seychelles is no longer eligible for direct funding from **GAVI**, the Vaccine Alliance, and the **Global Fund to Fight HIV, TB, and Malaria**. Consequently, Seychelles must continue to expand domestic funding for health in order to meet its healthcare needs.
# COLLABORATION WITH THE UNITED NATIONS SYSTEM AT THE COUNTRY LEVEL

The United Nations Multi-Country Office for Mauritius and Seychelles is located in Port Louis, Mauritius. The United Nations Country Team (UNCT) for Seychelles consists of FAO, IFAD, ILO, IOM, ITC, ITU, UNAIDS, UNCTAD, UNDESA, UNDP, UNDRR, UNECA, UNEP, UNESCO, UNFPA, UN-HABITAT, UNHCR, UNHR, UNICEF, UNIDO, UNITAR, UNODC, UNOPS, UNWOMEN, and WHO. WHO has at times facilitated the work of other agencies without a physical presence [93].

On August 30, 2018, the Government of the Republic of Seychelles (GoRS) and the UN in Seychelles jointly signed the first UNSDCF called the "**Strategic Partnership Framework (SPF) 2019-2023**." This framework had four strategic priority areas, including:
* Economic transformation
* Environmental sustainability and resilience
* Human capital development and data development
* Science, technology, and innovation

The SPF evaluation conducted in 2022 noted that it consolidated the work of the UN system in a single coherent strategy, providing a platform for common policy work, sharing of resources, strong coordination of programs, and timely delivery of results. The SPF had a strong collaborative link with the GoRS national development priorities, sustainable development goals, international and regional commitments, and obligations. The evaluation rated overall progress towards the outcome of human capital development and quality of life as fair.

On March 24, 2023, the GoRS and the UN in Seychelles jointly signed the **UNSDCF 2024-2028**. This framework has three priorities (people, prosperity, and planet), three outcomes, and eight inter-agency outputs. The outcomes include:
* Empowering all people in Seychelles, particularly vulnerable groups, and providing improved and sustainable services, protection, and opportunities
* Benefiting all people in Seychelles, especially vulnerable groups, from a resilient, sustainable, and inclusive economy
* Better preparing people and institutions for disaster risks and strengthening natural ecosystems

The World Health Organization (WHO) made implementation commitments within the UNSDCF 2024-2028 for Seychelles to contribute to all its three priorities: **PEOPLE**, **PROSPERITY**, and **PLANET**.
* **Under PEOPLE**, WHO will focus on improving access to health services, promoting healthy lifestyles, and strengthening occupational safety.
* **Under PROSPERITY**, WHO will contribute to a resilient economy, access to affordable and nutritious food, and investment in care economies.
* **Under PLANET**, WHO will focus on enhancing resilience to disasters and pollution, promoting a healthy environment, and improving waste management.

WHO will also harness science, research, and innovation, strengthen global health leadership, and collaborate with various partners to protect and advance people's health.

The 4th Country Cooperation Strategy for Seychelles (CCS-4) aligns well with the strategic commitments made in the UNSDCF. The CCS-4 will facilitate the implementation of health outcomes defined in the UNSDCF.

# SEYCHELLES’S PATH TO SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT GOALS

The sustainable development of Seychelles is strategically guided by **Vision 2033**, spanning the period from 2018 to 2033. This visionary framework was meticulously crafted, incorporating global commitments such as:
* The UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)
* The Paris Agreement on climate change
* The UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC)
* The African Union Agenda 2063

Vision 2033 aims to transform Seychelles into a resilient, responsible, and prosperous nation where healthy, educated, and empowered Seychellois coexist harmoniously with nature and engage with the wider world by 2033. Among its nine core missions, one is to cultivate a highly skilled, healthy, and productive population that supports the realization of Vision 2033. In all, the vision rests upon six pivotal pillars:
1. Good Governance
2. People at the Center of Development
3. Social Cohesion
4. Innovative Economy
5. Economic Transformation
6. Environmental Sustainability & Resilience

The health sector agenda is reflected in the **“People at the Center of Development”** pillar. The health sector’s vision, as outlined in Vision 2033, is to achieve the highest level of physical, social, mental, and spiritual health for all Seychellois, while fostering harmony with nature. Guided by the principles of *“Health for all, Health by all, and Health in all”*, the health sector is expected to contribute significantly to the overall well-being of Seychelles’ population.

The **National Development Strategy (NDS) 2024-2028**, approved by the cabinet in December 2023, serves translates Vision 2033 into implementation programmes in the medium-term. The NDS 2024-2028 has six key priority areas:

1. A Modern Public Service
2. Economic Transformation and Diversification
3. Social Cohesion and Inclusion
4. Environmental Sustainability and Resilience
5. Innovative Economy
6. People-Centered Development

Within this context, the health sector is expected to play a pivotal role as part of the “People at the Center of Development” pillar while also tangentially contributing to the other priorities. The health sector’s strategic focus, as outlined in the NDS 2024-2028, is to achieve the highest level of health and wellbeing for all Seychellois by:

1. Pursuing effective health protection and promote empowerment that involves fostering personal and societal responsibility for holistic health.
2. Building a high-quality integrated healthcare system that focuses on people-centered care.

The health sector is additionally expected to develop a communication strategy to manage risk factors related to non-communicable diseases (NCDs) and communicable diseases and strengthening health services across the life course.
Though the Seychelles National Health Policy (NHP) 2015, which has no expiry date, was developed sometime back, its focus on promoting, protecting, and restoring the health, quality of life and dignity for all Seychellois resonates adequately with what is reflected in the NDS 2024-2028. The National Health Strategic Plan (NHSP) 2016-2020, served as a pivotal bridge, advancing the shared vision of the NDS 2024-2028 and the NHP 2015. In alignment with NDS 2024-2028, the NHSP emphasizes comprehensive, high-quality integrated health care services towards the NDS’s objective of economic transformation and social cohesion. Additionally, the NHSP’s commits to research, data management, and innovation in line with the NDS’s vision of an innovative economy. The NHSP concomitantly operationalizes the NHP’s mission by relentlessly promoting health, quality of life, and dignity for Seychellois. It translated these principles into tangible actions of preventive, promotive, curative, and rehabilitative health programs including the provision of free primary health care at the point of use to exemplify the NHP’s commitment to equitable access and quality services.

The Health Annual Performance Report 2022 charted implementation progress of the NHSSP during the year 2022 and highlighted the continuing key priorities for the sector in the coming years as follows:

*   **Enhancing Preventive Measures:** The focus shifted toward proactive health promotion, risk factor management, and disease prevention.
*   **Integrated Care Systems:** Strengthening the integration of health services across the life course emerged as a critical goal.
*   **Human Resources Development:** Efforts to achieve self-sufficiency in health workforce continued, emphasizing skill development and retention.
*   **Sustainability and Innovation:** The sector explored innovative approaches to ensure long-term sustainability.
*   **Public-Private Partnerships:** Collaboration with the private health sector gained prominence, aligning with the NDS’s vision.

In summary, the NHSP 2016-2020 laid the groundwork for a healthier Seychelles, bridging policy aspirations with practical implementation towards the shared vision of a resilient, empowered population living in harmony with nature.

## IMPLEMENTING THE CCS

This chapter outlines cooperation pathways that will be utilized to support the implementation of CCS-4 strategic priorities at the country level. It provides specific details about national-level agreements, which will be coordinated by the Ministry of Health, between the three levels of WHO and various partners, including non-state actors. The mandate, platforms, range of expertise of WHO’s three organizational levels (country, regional, and headquarters) coupled with the organization’s reputation as an impartial convener of diverse partners; steward of global standards, frameworks, and conventions; and trusted source of health information; all well positions WHO to effectively marshal the cooperation partnerships that will fully implement the jointly agreed strategic agenda in Seychelles leverage the organization’s continually improving assets (such as expertise, norms and standards, evidence-based guidelines and recommendations, scientific evidence, and data) to ensure impact on public health [127,128].

In line with WHO's constitutional mandate and core functions [127,128], the following means of implementation will be used:

### LEADERSHIP: DIPLOMACY AND CONVENING

Achieving Seychelles' health sector vision requires a renewed commitment from all relevant stakeholders, including the public sector, private sector, civil society organizations, bilateral and multilateral agencies, to respect and empower national structures and processes established for health leadership and governance [10]. In the implementation of CCS-4, WHO will employ its diplomacy, advocacy, and convening roles to support national health leadership in aligning a broad array of health actors with national priorities and systems.

### LEADERSHIP: ADVOCACY AND STRATEGIC COMMUNICATION

WHO's work on promoting healthy lives and well-being, including universal health coverage, health emergencies, and healthier populations, involves both technical and political aspects [128]. WHO will advocate for health at the highest political level and engage with various non-state actors to support Seychelles' health development vision. WHO will promote a "Health in All Policies" approach, emphasizing a comprehensive and collaborative approach involving all sectors of government and society. WHO will engage political leaders, government members of cabinet, and other relevant actors to champion a coordinated multisectoral agenda addressing the key determinants of health in Seychelles. WHO will also emphasize coordination, teamwork, and synergy across its three levels and with other partners, such as the African Union, Africa CDC, IOM, SADC, UNCT, World Bank, and bilateral agencies, in implementing, monitoring, and reporting on CCS-4 [10].

### LEADERSHIP: STRATEGIC POLICY DIALOGUE

At the national level, the Ministry of Health (MOH) and WHO will establish a policy dialogue agenda for each strategic priority area, as outlined in the implementation pathway. To translate this agenda
into action, the WHO Secretariat will focus on country needs and relevant resolutions and decisions of WHO governing bodies (WHA, EB, and RC) [128,10]. Strategic policy dialogue will involve political leaders, policymakers from multiple government agencies, and non-state actors, such as the private sector, civil society, and academia, to garner support for MOH's efforts to promote the health and well-being of the Seychellois population. Leveraging its trusted role as a source of knowledge and data, WHO will effectively support and advocate for policy actions aligned with GPW14 priorities [10].

## TECHNICAL COOPERATION: STRATEGIC SUPPORT AND LEVERAGING OF PARTNERSHIPS

The WHO Secretariat will provide strategic support to enhance Seychelles' national health system, focusing on maximizing robustness, system performance in terms of health level and equity, responsiveness, financial risk protection, and efficiency. This support will include advice on various aspects of health system building blocks and universal health coverage [10,128]. In collaboration with the Government of Seychelles Ministry of Health, other government agencies, the UN system, development partners, and local non-state actors, such as civil society organizations, WHO will provide advocacy materials, coordinate partnerships, develop strategies, and mobilize resources. The delivery of strategic support will be tailored to the specific context, depending on the presence of WHO at the national level, regional office (AFRO, MCAT, Emergency Hub), or headquarters.

## TECHNICAL COOPERATION: TECHNICAL ASSISTANCE AND CAPACITY BUILDING

Technical cooperation between WHO and the Government of Seychelles (GoRS) will focus on strengthening individual and institutional capacities and providing technical assistance. WHO will offer integrated and coordinated technical guidance, including the contextualization of norms, standards, and guidelines, research coordination, and translation into health interventions [10,128].

## SERVICE DELIVERY AND OPERATIONAL SUPPORT

Given the maturity of the national health system in Seychelles, it is assumed that the country does not require support with service delivery, except in acute emergency situations, such as the COVID-19 pandemic, where WHO might provide catalytic support with equipment, medicines, vaccines, or supplies [11,62]. Additionally, WHO may provide operational support necessary for facilitating the provision of other modalities of support indicated above

Although milestone achievements have been realized at global level in having the WHO assessed contribution significantly increased plus the commitment by the organization to channel most of the assed contribution increases to countries; this will not adequately cover the financing needs for CCS-4 in Seychelles. ## MONITORING AND EVALUATION

The overall purpose of monitoring and evaluation is the measurement and assessment of performance in order to manage health development results more effectively [129]. The main objectives of result-oriented monitoring and evaluation of CCS-4 implementation are to support accountability and repositioning of the WHO Country Office (WCO), ensure informed decision-making, enhance organizational and health development learning, and build country capacity in monitoring and evaluation.

Monitoring and evaluation of the CCS-4 Strategic Agenda implementation will be conducted in collaboration with the Government of Seychelles (GoRS) and with the involvement of all three levels of WHO to ensure accountability and encourage joint ownership of results. Simultaneously, the progress of strategic priorities and strategic deliverables linked to the UNSDCF 2024-2028 will be tracked.

### CCS IMPLEMENTATION MONITORING

Monitoring refers to the ongoing assessment of progress towards CCS-4 strategic priorities and strategic deliverables as part of the routine programme management of the WCO, using WHO's Global Service Management system. It is a continuous process of recording, reflecting, and using information.
information related to implementation progress to optimize the probability of attaining CCS-4 priorities and strategic deliverables. Thus, monitoring is the primary responsibility of the WCO programmes, in collaboration with the Ministry of Health and other stakeholders contributing to CCS-4 strategic agenda implementation.

The CCS-4 strategic agenda implementation will be monitored annually to assess the progress achieved, identify and address any bottlenecks. The objectives of the annual monitoring are to:

-   Establish that the focus of WCO programmes and resources is on CCS-4 strategic agenda implementation.
-   Assess progress in the implementation of planned activities and expected outputs that contribute to the eventual achievement of strategic priorities and strategic deliverables.
-   Determine whether CCS-4 strategic priorities-related activities are being implemented timely and efficiently.
-   Identify, analyse, and resolve process bottlenecks in the implementation of planned activities and outputs.
-   Identify strategic priorities related to activities that should be re-programmed under biennial operational work plans.

Progress monitoring will be conducted using existing WHO biennial plan of action monitoring mechanisms which result into preparation semi-annual, annual and biennial monitoring reports. These reports will inform the mid-term and final evaluations of CCS-4.

## CCS MID-TERM EVALUATION

The mid-term evaluation (MTE) aims to assess the results achieved by 2027 in comparison with the outcome indicators outlined in the monitoring, evaluation, accountability, and learning framework. This evaluation will generate evidence for use in accelerating progress towards the achievement of CCS-4 strategic priorities and strategic deliverables, reprogramming in subsequent biennium, and enhancing learning and mutual accountability. It might be prudent to undertake the CCS-4 MTE jointly with the final evaluation of the Ministry of Health National Health Strategic Plan (NHSP) 2022-2026 to maximize efficiency. Specific objectives of the CCS-4 MTE are as follows:

1. Assess progress towards the achievement of strategic priorities and deliverables, using predefined milestones.
2. Determine the need for reprogramming in subsequent work plans.
3. Identify challenges that may hinder the effectiveness, efficiency, and impact of the CCS-4 implementation.
4. Take necessary actions to address identified challenges and expedite progress towards achieving strategic priorities and deliverables.

## FINAL EVALUATION OF CCS

The final evaluation (SE) of the CCS-4 strategic agenda will be conducted at the end of 2030 to review the outcomes and impact of the implementation. The SE will assess the contribution of WHO in achieving national health development targets and those outlined in the CCS-4 results framework and monitoring framework.

The evaluation will be based on the OECD/DAC criteria of relevance, coherence, effectiveness, efficiency, impact, sustainability, and cross-cutting features such as equity, human rights, and gender mainstreaming. The SE will seek to answer the following questions:
1. **Relevance:** Did the CCS-4 implementation align with the country's needs and priorities?
2. **Coherence:** How well did the CCS-4 strategic agenda fit with other interventions?
3. **Effectiveness:** Did the CCS-4 implementation achieve its objectives?
4. **Efficiency:** How effectively were resources used?
5. **Impact:** What difference did the CCS-4 implementation make?
6. **Sustainability:** Will the gains from the CCS-4 last beyond the implementation period?

The evaluation team will use a mixed methods approach, as described in the WHO Evaluation Practice Handbook, to answer the evaluation questions. The SE will also identify key achievements, factors that facilitated or constrained progress, lessons learned, missed opportunities, and priorities for the next CCS cycle. The findings and recommendations of the SE will inform future collaboration between WHO and Seychelles and guide the development of the next CCS
