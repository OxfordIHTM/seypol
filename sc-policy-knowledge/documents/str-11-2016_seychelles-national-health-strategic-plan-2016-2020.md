# Definitions

**Additional insured**: A person or organization named in the insurance policy as an insured on an independent basis outside of the primary insured. This may occur approximately.

**Admitted insurance**: Insurance that is written by an insurance company that is licensed to sell insurance in a state or country outside the limits of the primary insured. As mentioned in the policy, this usually refers to a specific insurance company that is licensed in the state where the insured resides.

**Agent**: A person who is authorized by an insurer to represent it in the sale of insurance and to negotiate insurance contracts. An agent can be an individual or a legal entity. Agents are typically paid a commission for their services.

**Aggregate limit**: The maximum amount an insurance company will pay for all covered losses during a specific period, usually a policy year. Once the aggregate limit is reached, the insurance company will not pay any more claims for that policy year.

**Emergency alloy**: An event-driven close that protects some community's biodiversity to a limiting extent.

**Emergency event**: An unexpected, sudden, and usually unusual occurrence that may cause damage or damage the damaging health effects from an unplanned event. This includes natural disasters, accidents, or other unforeseen events that can result in loss of life, injury, or property damage.

**Environmental pollution**: The contamination of the environment by substances that may cause harm to humans, animals, or plants. This include air, water, and soil pollution. Environmental pollution can be caused by human activities, such as industrial processes, agriculture, and transportation, as well as natural phenomena, such as volcanic eruptions and forest fires.

**Essential Health Services**: The health protection, disease prevention, curative and rehabilitative services and other interventions that are required to maintain the health of the population. These services are usually provided by the government or other public health agencies and are accessible to all members of the population.

**Health**: A state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity. This is the definition of health provided by the World Health Organization (WHO).

**Health care**: A set of services provided by health care professionals to maintain or restore health. This includes preventive, diagnostic, curative, and rehabilitative services.

**Health care professional**: The licensed workers that deliver the specified Essential Health Services. The workforce includes all those registered with the Seychelles Medical and Dental Council, the Seychelles Nursing Council, and the Seychelles Pharmacy Council.

**Health system**: The organization of people, institutions, and resources that deliver health care to meet the health needs of the population. This includes public and private health care providers, as well as government health agencies.

**Health System**: The mechanism for ensuring access to quality health care services as demanded by the population, where all components of the health system act in harmony. This includes the provision of health services, the management of health resources, and the regulation of health care providers. The health system also includes the roles of health workers and intermediaries, the distribution of health resources, and the implementation of health policies and strategies.

**Health worker**: Any person who is engaged in the health sector and provides health services to the population. This includes doctors, nurses, pharmacists, and other health care professionals.

**Incident**: An event or occurrence that results in a loss or injury. An incident can be a single event or a series of events that lead to a loss or injury. Some examples of incidents are accidents, errors, and failures.

**Index**: A measure of the relative value of a variable of interest. An index can be used to track changes in a variable over time or to compare the value of a variable across different groups or regions.

**Injury**: Physical damage to the body caused by an external force. This includes fractures, sprains, cuts, and burns. Injuries can be caused by accidents, violence, or other external factors.

**Insurance**: A contract between an insurance company and an insured party, in which the insurance company agrees to provide financial protection against certain risks in exchange for a premium. Insurance can cover a variety of risks, including health, life, property, and liability.

**Insurance policy**: The legal contract between the insurance company and the insured party. The insurance policy outlines the terms and conditions of the insurance coverage, including the risks that are covered and the exclusions that apply.

**Insurance premium**: The amount of money that the insured party pays to the insurance company in exchange for insurance coverage. The premium is typically paid on a monthly, quarterly, or annual basis.

**Intervention**: A set of actions taken to improve a health outcome or to prevent a disease. Interventions can be individual or population-based and can include screening, vaccination, treatment, and health education.

**Medical services**: The services provided by health care professionals to maintain or restore health. This includes preventive, diagnostic, curative, and rehabilitative services.

**Referral health services**: The health care services whose function is specifically to manage, or facilitate the referral process.

**Routine Health services**: Health care services necessary to protect and manage the damaging health effects from non-emergency situations. This includes preventive services, such as vaccinations, and chronic disease management.

# CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION

## 1.1 Background to the SHSP

The creation of a new national health system at the end of the 80Ts was inspired by the principles adopted by the World Health Organization (WHO) in 1978, that health is a fundamental human right, with a priority on primary health care. Government undertook responsibility to provide health services, financed entirely through taxation, making the services free at the point of use. The strategy was based on the principle of community health centres, providing a comprehensive range of Health services. At the same time, sectoral responsibility for health was decentralised to the district level through the Health Centres.

While the principles underpinning development have remained relevant, and still reinforced the conceptualisation of the Seychelles National Health Strategic Plan, the health system has faced challenges in the past few decades. Demands and expectations from an increasingly educated population, the changing epidemiological profile of the population, the need for more efficient use of limited resources, sophistications in health technologies and the emergence of private health service providers have altered the context in which the health system operates.

In response to these changes, the inevitable increase in demand for resources for health, for the Ministry of Health and Social Affairs, and the need for better alignment of available resources with priority needs, there is a need to re-examine the current health system and its management. This is a requirement for the rolling of grant health care services and regulating services sunk, in line with its role, the Ministry has determined that a new National Health Strategic Plan (NHSP) is required for the period 2022-2026.

Since 2015, The policy emphasises the central role of health in human development, and seeks to mobilise resources and build capacity to improve the health of the population. The NHSP 2022-2026 is a strategic step of development, modest incremental changes are insufficient to address the situation health needs, and relate to the current health system. This project will outline and quantify both quantitative and qualitative in nature.

## 1.2 Rationale for the NHSP

The rationale for the NHSP is derived from the National Health Policy, and from general directions, sets milestones and proporciona the appropriate guide to outline and optimise the investment in health. Health is a fundamental human right. The health of a population is influenced by a complex interplay of social and cultural factors that shape our individuality, outlook and interaction with other entities and the environment. The health of the population is an important indicator of the overall well-being of a country. Many varied and diverse factors can contribute to this and have the potential to influence health. It follows, therefore, that the pursuit of health should be a central objective of any nation, in its holistic sense, with involvement to all sectors of society and all possible partners.

At this juncture, it is necessary to set out priorities that generate a greater awareness that health is a common concern and an understanding that all can and should contribute to the better health of
individuals, families, communities and ultimately the nation at large. At the lowest level of the health system there needs to be a focus on the **primary health care (PHC)** approach for the health and well-being of all citizens.

Throughout the sector, the different actors will need to work together in a coordinated manner. The various actors, they government provincial, civil society, or health professionals, to find common ground and engage in coordinated action to ensure that the health system is responsive to the community for the long term.

To achieve these goals, it is essential to develop a comprehensive and integrated approach that considers the diverse needs of the population, including those of vulnerable and marginalised groups. This includes ensuring that there is adequate capacity, funding and resources to implement the policies and guidelines. It is also important to ensure a productive, formal and informal relationship and the users beneficiaries of the services. Issues of access, utilisation, equity and quality of services are key considerations. In this context, strengthening the governance and management of the health system is critical. The connection between health sector and other development is multidimensional and the **NHSP** recognises the need to collaborate with other sectors to achieve the desired outcomes. This includes the priorities and strategies of other development partners to be in close coherence to enable the synergy.
nutrition and physical activity in the prevention of non-communicable diseases. The NHSP draws from the sustainable development goals, the regional health strategy of the WHO AFRO, and other relevant international and local frameworks for the health sector in Seychelles.

The Seychelles National Health Strategic Plan (NHSP) 2022-2026 sets out the long-term planning framework for the country up to 2026. Taking into account population projections, it maps the main "sandstones" potential for the sector. It provides a high-level strategic direction and outlines the processes for implementing the plan, focusing on the key priority areas of the health sector:

* clinical services
* housing, energy, water, education, health, environment and community while also addressing issues of environment,
* climate change, and disaster risk management

This strategic plan also serves as a framework for the coordination and implementation for ensuring health strategies within the context of developing resilient and healthy communities in the area.

As Seychelles is a Small Island Developing State (SIDS), it is particularly vulnerable to the impacts of climate development in the face of vulnerabilities posed by climate change. Seychelles has furthermore endorsed the SDGs Acceleration Agenda. This ensures that the NHSP is aligned with the overarching goals of the SDGs. The Agenda provides a common framework for sustainable, inclusive and equitable economic growth, while the accelerating the achievement of the SDGs. It also ensures that the implementation of the NHSP integrates many issues of human development and its action for health outcomes any other SDGs and remaining directions by aligning the health sector’s priorities with the Sustainable Development Goals. The NHSP also incorporates the General Assembly Declaration on Non-Communicable Diseases, the UN General Assembly Declaration on Mental Health, and the updated WHO Global Action Plan on Primary Health Care (PHC).

The Sustainable Development Goals and sustainable targets outlined by the UN Member States in 2015 strengthen the NHSP’s ambition to ensure healthy lives and promote well-being for all at all ages. These goals and sectoral policies strengthen, as their targets are aligned with the NHSP. The NHSP adopts a multisectoral approach to achieve these goals. Most of the SDGs are health-oriented or aligned with the health sector objectives. The NHSP provides a strategic framework for the health sector in Seychelles, and ensures that the key priorities in shaping the approach to health development, and ensure that 500-plus goals and the agenda are integrated into the national health planning process.

### The National Immunization Programme

Immunization is one of the most successful and cost-effective public health interventions for reducing morbidity and mortality communities in the country. This is so both at community and health facility levels with the aim of ensuring that all children under 5 years of age are fully immunized. The National Immunization Programme Health Facilities are maximizing its effort to identify and screen the most at risk persons for major NCDs. The current services are as follows:

* The national immunization programme provides all vaccines recommended by the WHO in the expanding program, and are targeted specifically at both the general population and the Most at Risk Populations in specific priorities. These include children under 5 years of age, pregnant women, and elderly persons with HIV/AIDS treatments.
* The programme also provides immunization services aimed at preventing childhood diseases — with a focus on preventing tb, pertussis, diphtheria, whooping cough, hepatitis B, mumps, measles,
rubella, yellow fever and pneumonia in children. In addition, the sector is also providing **IMNCI (Integrated Management of Neonatal and Childhood Illness)** services.

Screening services for migrant workers to detect communicable disease risks, to guide intervention services, are provided in collaboration with the Seychelles Ports Authority.

Nutrition services are provided through the various health facilities and through community-based services, such as growth monitoring and developmental assessments, targeted at each service point.

Tuberculosis and leprosy services are provided as needed and coordinated through the health facilities.

Clinical and rehabilitative services are provided as needed, including physiotherapy.

Services to identify Neglected Tropical Diseases, specifically lymphatic filariasis, together with those for *Strongyloides stercoralis*/*Strongyloides larvae* are also available.

### Conclusion

### 3.6.4 Sustainable financing for health

**Scope**

Sustainable financing aims to ensure that the provision of the essential health services is financed in a manner that is efficient, equitable and sustainable. Seychelles is facing the challenge of continuing to provide high quality health services to a small and ageing population. The increased increase in the burden of non-communicable diseases, together with an ageing population- due to improved longevity, will continue to place additional pressures on the health budgets each year.

**Desired future state**

* Efforts to define expenditure on health through the National Health Accounts have greatly improved in the recent years. Together with the exercise to determine the cost of each and every service provided in the health sector, this has provided the necessary evidence base tools to develop investment plans for health and facilitate the allocation, mobilisation and management of resources. Further efforts are needed to improve the quality of the data used for these calculations and to ensure that the National Health Accounts are updated on these exercises and then prioritized with motivated to be engaged active
* Health sector investment plans should be developed in line with the sustainable development, including financing of health. Cost awareness, capacity for budgeting and priority setting in health are required to ensure that the needed investments are made. To this end, there is a need to further strengthen the capacity of the health sector to provide data and information. Managers also need to be encouraged to be proactive and empowered to take charge of their services, and develop a clear vision of how the services will be provided and delivered.
* The current focus on tax financed health services, while equitable, limits the sector's capacity to finance necessary service extensions. In particular, the budgeting process tends to focus on maintaining current activities or only permits small marginal increases in services developed by existing health services. Other options for financing should be explored, including with other sectors and with the engagement of the population, especially where changes in policy might be necessary. There are also potential opportunities for the private sector to invest, in particular with infrastructure development and provision of clinical services where substantial investment need to be made.

## Objectives

The health sector will aim to introduce innovative approaches towards assuring sustained investment, in order to strengthen the delivery of modern health care in an efficient and equitable manner. In particular, the health sector will promote initiatives that lead to improved quality and efficiency of services. This includes enhancing the capacity of the health workforce, partnerships with the private and other sectors in the provision and purchasing of services.

Investment in health innovations shall be sought, with the support of other sectors and international partners, to enhance the quality and efficiency of health services and the overall of the health sector. In addition, more efficiency be considered, budgeting and financial management shall be improved to ensure that resources are used effectively, with a particular focus on eliminating inefficiencies and reducing waste.

Efforts to improve efficiencies in health services at all levels will be directed to the creation of the culture and mechanisms to ensure greater cost awareness, maximise the use of resources, manage public expenditure more efficiently, and produce better outcomes. This includes the most rational use of diagnostic services and prescribing, and their practices regularly monitored and reviewed. There will also be a focus on the cost of health services, including the cost of the shadow pricing and billing for their individual use of services.

Administrative and management capacities shall be enhanced, using alternative financing approaches, in particular to support the development of new priority interventions. The potential for public Private Partnerships (PPP) in the delivery of health services and key services areas will be investigated and applied in a coordinated and systematic manner.

### 3.6.5 Research and Innovation

**Scope**

The sector aims to develop a culture of research and innovation, aimed at ensuring evidence based decision making is practiced in the health sector. It will coordinate the governance, analysis, statistics and dissemination of evidence to generate the appropriate knowledge needed to inform decision making. The focus shall be on biomedical, biostatistics and systems research work. This involves creating a strong culture of evidence based decision making among healthcare workers.

**Outstanding issues:**

* The research and capacity is still very limited, in relation to its tasks and terms of reference.
* There is no clear strategic direction for research and innovation in the health sector, and there are no incentives for health professionals to conduct applied research and to share information.
* There are no existing policies to direct research and translate findings into clinical and public health practice. There is a need to develop a research policy to provide a clear strategic direction and disciplines a climate for a prioritization of data/publications and results.
* While there are individual practitioners who conduct research, significant resources in terms of staff and finance are granted to abstract research but not linked to health system needs.
* While there is a need for a coordinated approach to research, there is currently no central mechanism to prioritize research based on national health needs and consequently, there is still no coordinated approach to research and innovation in the health sector, and limited access to research and information needs of the health sector.
* The country lacks an integrated system to disseminate research to practitioners and researchers.
* There is no framework to evaluate innovations proposed for implementation in the health sector.

**Objectives**

The overarching goal is towards generating appropriate knowledge from research, which is needed for evidence-based decision making in the health sector. This focus shall be on building the capacity for generating evidence to inform the closest to clinic and community level.

**Research generation:** The sector will focus on improving oversight of research, with a closer relationship between the MOH and the University of Seychelles to ensure a coordinated implementation of the research agenda and building capacity of health workers to conduct the research and of the MOH to supervise its conduct.

**Research synthesis:** The sector will focus on putting in place the required specialised capacities needed to analysis of research findings, both for indigenous research and systematic reviews of
research conducted in other countries to generate information needed to inform the decision making process.

**Knowledge management:** The health sector will explore innovative approaches towards building bridges between research and decision making, to enable interaction between the two fields.

### 3.6.6 Partnership and coordination

#### Scope

The Ministry of Health recognizes the need for collaboration with many actors, for the health goals to be achieved. While the MOH can directly provide public health services, it needs to leverage other resources. This includes partnerships agreements with other actors to obtain other health benefits. These actors include:

* Other government agencies conducting actions that have an impact on health (determinants of health)
* The private sector providers of health services, such as the private sector and NGOs
* The international partners providing support of health services within the Seychelles
* The academic institutions providing support with research and training in health services

#### Outstanding issues

Partnership for health is an essential component of national health development. Many partnerships agreements, formal and informal, have been developed over the past decades.

However, the existing partnerships are not adequately documented. There is a lack of partnership priorities and criteria clearly defined. Moreover, little effort has been made to evaluate the progress of these partnerships. Some of the partnerships are only based on the provision of donations and sponsorships. In order to maximize the positive impact of partnerships, greater effort is needed to identify and prioritize partnerships, and to create maturity, the respective monitors, audit and evaluation partnerships.
Taking a broad view of partnership for health, there is potentially great scope for modelling other sector (public and private) partnerships in engagement with the public sector. Examples are possibilities in health promotion, screening, treatment and rehabilitation. Arrangements can range from simple, ad-hoc collaborations to more permanent, joint programme units, such as the partnerships operated by Health and Education (contracts) and formal MOUs and contracts (e.g. in reproductive health).

In addition to these operational partnerships, there are strategic partnerships. These partnerships focus the efforts of the **Heart of our Nation** movement and its **My Health, My responsibility** campaign. The strategic partnerships are designed to get people on board with the vision for a healthy health system. These will be pursued actively within the existing national policies, standards and priorities.

Partnerships at the operational level is well established through interaction with international organisations and through bilateral and arrangements. Such partnerships, whether technical or in the form of financial support, are usually focused on specific tasks or programmes, rather than contributing to the overall health system.

These mechanisms will be designed to ensure that the partners in health and mechanisms shall ensure that the interventions impacting on the prioritised priorities in these Ministries, which include social development and education, are coordinated and integrated into the national health system. ### 3.4.7 Governance and leadership

**Scope**

Health governance relates to the functioning of the institutions by which the authority of the state is exercised in the health sector. Policy makers within the MOH oversee the health agenda, the existing legal and regulatory frameworks guiding health, and sector accountability mechanisms, both political and technical.

**Outstanding issues**

* Planning and monitoring of health is ill defined in the Ministry of Health and does not cover health activities in all districts. There is a lack of coordination between the different levels of governance and monitoring is not incorporated into the monitoring of health actions.
* Following the restructuring of the MOH, there is a need to review and update the roles of policy makers, regulation and administrative provision, additional work is now required to establish how the effectiveness of these roles is measured and what the outcomes of these processes are.
* There is a need to establish a clear and consistent map of the health sector and its governance.
* The sector health planning process and development monitoring capacity need strengthening. There are still gaps in the coordination between the different levels of governance. There is a need for development planning and monitoring at the inspector and monitor policy implementation at the service level.
* There is a need to improve the capacity of the MOH to conduct a systemic review of the health sector and produce governing texts of quality of quality plans and reports. In addition, some key sector priorities still lack a clear policy or guidance document.
* The health sector legislation is out of date and does not reflect the current health situation. It needs to be repeated and updated and appropriate regulations developed. In addition, there is a need to review the current legislative functions of the MOH.
* There is a need to develop a strategy for the development and maintenance of health infrastructure. Primary health Care Packages, an accountability framework for health public bodies, complaints management and a health workforce strategy are also needed.
* Long term health development is not practised, particularly for capital health expenditure facilities to guide the investment of resources. While there is a general intention to improve health infrastructure, there is no master plan for health infrastructure development nationally. A master plan for baselines should be developed to ensure that all health infrastructure development is aligned with the national health strategy. This would include a comprehensive map of all health infrastructure in the country and a guideline for standardisation across all facilities. Presently, health infrastructure development is ad-hoc and based on budget availability. A master plan would provide a strategic framework. An infrastructure master plan will guide the development of a comprehensive donation policy.
* The sector still lacks a robust framework to guide and measure performance - both for health sector overall and specifically for each agency.
* Capacity of the sector to regulate all its arms is limited - this is particularly the for private sector.
* The sector lacks a dedicated health infrastructure plan to guide its development.
* The health sector has not yet fully integrated its information systems. While there are various systems at institutional level, parallel structures of biomonitors and technicians are involved in decision making leading to inefficiency. However, there is a general awareness that there is a need for a system that provides access to themselves (for all technicians).
This will focus on identifying areas and criteria for collaboration and defining criteria and roles for engaging non-health sector agencies in health promotion. This will be done in partnership with other sectors, such as social organisations, NGOs and international organisations be strengthened in order to ensure that clear terms are set for the nature of the partnership. All stakeholders will be engaged to ensure that the policies are relevant and sustainable. This will include partners in the private sector, such as public employees and private practitioners utilising their public facilities to deliver care to their private patients.

In order to complement government's policy to subsidise more core health services including laboratories and imaging services, the health sector shall explore the possibility of partnering with the private sector to provide these services. This will be done through the development of a framework for public-private partnerships. This will focus on improving current contracting and payment mechanisms for cost-effective services. The priority will be to ensure that there are no disparities in the quality of care provided to patients between the government and partners. This will also include the development of clinical protocols to harmonise and improve continuity of care. Concurrently, the health sector shall explore the possibility of enhancing coordination between the public and private sectors to avoid duplication of patients being under treatment-outcome measures. Watchlists could include enlarging the existing consultant's programme to include more specialists and expanding the use of private facilities for day-care services.

**Regulatory system:** the health sector shall review and ensure a comprehensive and regulatory legal framework exists to guide the implementation of the health agencies by all actors in health services. It shall conduct a review of existing legislation to identify gaps in the regulatory system. This includes the Health Care Package, an acceptable framework for health services contracts, the public complaints management system and the regulatory framework for the private health sector.

**Accessibility system:** The health sector shall put in place appropriate mechanisms to ensure it is accessible to all residents of Seychelles. This includes enhancing the efficiency of the existing referral preventative mechanisms, efficiency monitoring of the implementation health services among other things.

# CHAPTER 5: IMPLEMENTATION FRAMEWORK

## Roles and responsibilities

The NHSP 2022-2026 is a comprehensive, overarching plan which implements the modernisation agenda for the health sector. The **Ministry of Health** is responsible for the implementation of the National Health Strategic Plan. The Ministry of Health will ensure that the NHSP is coordinated with other sectoral plans, and will lead the development of an operational cabinent plan for the sector, whose successful implementation will depend on effective collaboration and coordination with other sectors.

It is recognized that the capacity of the Ministry of Health to lead and oversee the NHSP 2022-2026 is limited. Support from its public bodies to implement programs detailed in the plan, needs to be further developed.

Moreover, based on the operational cabinent plan, the Ministry of Health will manage the implementation, monitoring and evaluating the NHSP 2022-2026. Key among this is the introduction of the **results-based management framework** to improve service delivery and in particular the implementation of Performance Improvement programs- based PPPs.

The Ministry of Health shall also develop a monitoring and evaluation framework and an establishment plan to enable the strategic plan implementation process, longer term arrangements to plan, implement, monitor and evaluate the NHSP 2022-2026, and to ensure that the health sector is able to effectively measure its success.

In this regard, the NHSP process management team will draw up a time-frame of activities, be owned and written by public authorities. Cabinet approval will be sought for the main actions such as:
* Establishing the project management office
* Creating a steering committee
* Other administrative measures

The steering committee will be responsible for the overall coordination of the NHSP, including the monthly online guidance calls and provide guidance and criteria on the creation of energy sectors. The steering committee will also provide guidance to the project management office on whose the "My Health, My Responsibilities", the Seychelles Charter on Health, legislative measures, etc. During the implementation, the Ministry of Health will prioritize the implementation of identified priority policies, and will closely monitor their progress.

It is essential that the Ministry of Health works in close collaboration with other sectors to ensure that formal structures are established and key posts created to be associated with it. Clear management priorities will be identified and agreed upon.

In the long term, a national, multi-stakeholder health Forum, at national as well as local levels, has to be established to provide a platform for consultation and dialogue on health issues, to ensure alignment and mapping linkages with key constituencies. Performance agreements should be instituted in addition to the existing performance agreements with senior medical officers and public health officers, to allow the health policy makers and stakeholders to review progress and modify strategic objectives and activities as necessary.

# Implementation risks and their mitigation

The main areas of anticipated risk are listed below:

1. **Inadequate ownership and management of health services**

The past experience with the NHSP indicates that the reception period should allow for careful analysis of the priorities and logical steps to be taken. This is important to ensure that the priorities are owned by the stakeholders and not just seen as a set of activities to be implemented by the National Care Agency (NCA) and clarifying the extent and limits of authority that will be delegated to the NCA regarding the management of health services. It is therefore critical that the NHSP is developed in the absence presently of an accountability framework to ensure that the public bodies' actions are in line with the government's priorities.

2. **Financial decentralisation and capacity building**

The current financial system is too centralised. In addition, any financial responsibility should be accompanied by systematic capacity building and support, as well as good monitoring of its implementation to avoid risks of misuse of funds.

3. **Extended influence of Ministry of Finance, Department of Public Administration, private organisations, and others**

The MOH and its public bodies are part of the Civil Service and therefore subject to the laws that govern civil servants. Depending on the capacity of the MOH, it may be necessary to seek support from other public bodies to implement the NHSP. However, there is a risk that the MOH may lose control of its strategic direction if the support provided by these bodies is too extensive. Controls and frameworks being to ensure effective management of these organisations. Strategies shall be developed to be addressing this risk, including a clear definition of roles and finding the best way of managing activities while remaining accountable.

4. **Resistance to change from individuals and institutions**

The health sector has primarily been a public sector delivery system. This may lead to resistance in the short term. It will be prudent to incorporate such needs particularly to ensure needed to sustain the organizational change. The MOH will need to conduct a gap analysis to see what resources will be available for implementing the NHSP.

5. **Strategic outlook and sustained implementation**

Sustained implementation may also falter when unplanned initiatives replace or displace strategic projects. The MOH will need to ensure that the NHSP is prioritised and integrated into the annual operational plans of the MOH and its public bodies, and prioritise investments in order to ensure the optimal utilization of resources and deliver the expected outcomes.

# Assumptions for successful implementation of the NHSP

The following is assumed for successful implementation of the NHSP:

1. **Strong political will and ownership:** There must be recognition that successful implementation of the NHSP requires a national effort, driven by supportive leadership at the highest level. This includes support from all the sectors of government whose policies impact significantly on the citizens' health. It is assumed that there will be strong commitment and leadership from the Ministry of Health to coordinate and drive the process.

2. **Inter-sectoral collaboration:** To achieve the NHSP's goals, it is assumed that there will be inter-sectoral collaboration and coherence, synchronised, coordinated and undertaken successfully by all parties.

3. **Sustainable financing:** The NHSP's implementation is dependent on the availability of accountability frameworks and all senior executives in the health sector must account for their contributions to the NHSP.

4. **Institutional capacity:** Proper staffing levels and good capacity of the Ministry of Health will be required to coordinate the NHSP's implementation. It is assumed that the Ministry of Health will have the requisite skills to oversee its oversight role. In addition, it is assumed that the current health workforce is sufficient and that the human resources for health are optimally distributed across the health system.

5. **Clear expectations management:** Politicians and other policy makers will communicate the space for the health sector to operate in. It is assumed that the Government of Seychelles and the highest levels to demonstrate staff and management. The assumption is that people at the highest level will be consistent in their requests.

6. **Buy-in of commitment of health professionals:** All health professionals should feel that implementation of the NHSP is in their best interest.

7. **Adherence to timeline of key actions:** It is assumed that the proposed timelines, target outputs and deliverables of the NHSP, and institutional structures have been a consistent complement to the vision of the NHSP and that delivery of key actions may quality demonstrate staff and reduce commitment.
