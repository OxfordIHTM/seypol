# Executive Summary

Immunization has been one of Seychelles’ greatest public health success stories. However, in order to ensure that all children of Seychelles benefit equitably from this intervention, a strategic, i.e., long-term approach to planning and implementation is essential. This third comprehensive **Multi Year Plan (cMYP)** provides a framework for planning activities to achieve the major objectives of the national immunization programme. It also includes the associated costing and funding plan for the national EPI. An extensive situation analyses showed that they country has made significant progress in some key socio-demographic indices.

Several achievements have been attained including:
* Sustainability of immunization coverage above 99% for all antigens
* Absence of mortality due to vaccine preventable diseases (VPDs)
* 100% procurement of vaccines and injection materials by the Government
* Attainment of polio certification level indicators with acceptance of the country documentation by the Africa Regional Certification Commission (ARCC) in October 2006.

The high level of commitment by the Government and its people, the stable macro-economic environment allowing for availability of free and accessible child health services to all people, active follow up and tracing of Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) target groups to ensure maximum coverage, a high literacy rate, access to the media and information, and the absence of cultural or religious obstacles to immunization, have all contributed to the success of the immunization programme and health sector in general. The delivery of immunization services is integrated with other child survival interventions. It is therefore not surprising that with these achievements and conducive environment, the country has made progress towards Universal health coverage.

However, sustaining high immunization coverage, prevention of the risk of importation of VPDs particularly in view of the numerous tourists visiting the country and high procurement costs are among the challenges and areas of concern of the programme currently and in the future. There is a need to review the Human resources at national level to ensure the required evidence is generated for the sustenance of the programme. In addition, there is a need to address emerging vaccine hesitancy which has just been noted.

The **EPI Comprehensive Multi Year Plan 2018-2023** has been developed through a consultative process and is based on priorities in the National Strategic Framework 2016-2020, as well as the Global Vaccine Action Plan, and the Africa Regional Strategic Plan 2014-2020 and the Global Vaccine Action Plan. Over the next 5 years, the Immunization Programme will aim to:
* Maintain 100% coverage for all antigens
* Introduce Pneumococcal vaccine
* Maintain polio-free status, while exploring possibilities for other new vaccines
* Advocate for negotiated affordable price for procurement of vaccines in a bid to reduce on the expenditure on vaccines, among other priorities.

As a new National Health Strategic planning cycle will commence in 2021 for the period 2021 – 2025, the cMYP will be updated following a review to be conducted in 2021 and projections will then be aligned with the period 2021-2025.

Advocacy at all levels to ensure sustainability of the investments in the programme and of the achievements will continue, Finalization of the EPI Policy will be done to define the
operational framework of the programme and incorporate any advancements planned. The projected cost of the programme in the planning period **(2019 - 2023)** to include health systems shared cost as well as disease surveillance is approximately **$4,881,975**.

The major components of this plan includes:
* The mission
* Strategic goals
* Strategic objectives
* Key activities
* Costing/financing plan

They were developed based on a comprehensive situation analysis taking into consideration the global goals and national priorities.
# 1. INTRODUCTION

## 1.1 Country Profile

The Republic of Seychelles comprises 115 islands in the Indian Ocean. Out of this number, only 22 are inhabited. It is situated between 4 and 9 degrees south of the equator and between 45 and 56 degrees east, covering an exclusive economic zone of more than 1.3 million square kilometres. The largest island is Mahe where 90% of the country population lives and where the capital city, Victoria, is located. Three other islands of major economic importance and have health facilities are Praslin and La Digue and Silhouette.

The socio-economic indicators since achieving independence in 1976 are improving continuously. Sub- Saharan Africa. Nevertheless, Seychelles, as a tiny island country, is not exempted from the socio-economic problems faced by the Small Island Developing States (SIDS). Seychelles is a multiparty democratic small state where national unity, pride and aspirations override ideological and economic differences.

## 1.2 Demographic and Health indicators

According to the end year 2017 population projection the population of Seychelles is 95,821 (NSB,2018). The population is characterised by a slow growth rate, low births, low mortality and external migration. The total fertility rate was 3.6 in 2017 and children under 1year constitute about 1.7% of the total population. 19.7% of the population is aged less than 15 years and 41.4% of the total population are women in the reproductive age (15-44). Good family planning programmes, economic improvement, free primary and early secondary education, free health and increase in family planning participation have contributed to low fertility rate. The crude death rate continued to decrease with slight fluctuation from 8.12 in 2005 to 7.9 in 2017. ### 1.3 Macro economic situation

During the planning period of 2019 – 2023, Seychelles economic performance is expected to continue on the strong trajectory of growth. Further to the macroeconomic performance continuing to be strong in 2016 and 2017 with economic growth reaching 4% (2016) and 4.2% (2017), reflecting increased tourist arrivals, stronger output in the fishing industry, and expanding credit to the private. The strong tourism sector has continued to bring up Seychelles’ economy; the number of visitors rose by 9.8% in 2016 to reach a record high of over 300,000. Up to July 2017, visitor arrivals were up 21.2% over the same period last in 2016. The economic profile is further supported by low commodity prices and a stable exchange rate, inflation (year-on-year) was negative throughout early 2017. The external current account deficit remained largely unchanged, while gross international reserves at end-2016 reached 4 months of prospective imports of goods and services. Supported by lower than budgeted capital outlays and strong tax revenue growth, the 2016 primary fiscal surplus reached 3.4 percent of GDP, exceeding the target by 0.4 percent.

The world bank data for Gross National Income per capita indicate upward trend rising from $10,190 in 2010 to 14,010 in 2015 and $14180 in 2017 (**figure 1**).

There are some risks such as inflation (picked up, to 3.2% year-on-year in July 2017, but this reflects supply-side pressures from increases in administered tobacco prices and tariffs), depreciation of Seychellois rupee slightly against the US Dollar (by 1.2% in 2017) in view of the need for foreign denominated procurement of vaccines and other capital equipment, while gross official reserves which has remained high at $545m as at June 2017 (equivalent to 4.2 months import cover) is expected to ameliorate the risks.

The key, longer-term challenge is to improve educational outcomes and thus strengthen the quantity and quality of skills available to the economy.

## 1.3 National Health System

The Government provides universal access to free primary health care, which results in very good indicators of health outcomes such as infant mortality at 10.9 per 1000 live births, a maternal mortality of zero in most recent years, fully immunised children of 100%, and 99% of birth deliveries assisted by skilled health workers. HIV prevalence is less than 1% and is characterised as a concentrated epidemic among high risk groups.

In recent years, the burden of disease has shifted mainly to non-communicable diseases. Education is fully subsidized up to secondary school level, and partially subsidized at the tertiary level. The gross enrolment in primary and secondary education is close to 100%.

The Government provides free primary health care to assure universal access to health care, universal access to safe drinking water, good sanitation and housing provision, and an advanced social safety net to support the most vulnerable to address Social Determinants of Health. The health emergency policy and contingency plan has catered for emerging important public health priority conditions such as dengue, chikungunya and leptospirosis and considers Ebola, influenza and SARS as potential threats for national health security in the context of IHR and are all included in the national surveillance system.

The State recognizes the right of every citizen to protection of health and to the enjoyment of the highest attainable standards of physical and mental health[^1]. Traditionally, Seychelles has adopted a tier system for its health care delivery. The national health system operates on three main levels of care:

1. Primary/ community care
2. Secondary care at hospital and specialized facilities
3. Tertiary care which includes overseas treatment.

[^1]: Article 29, Constitution of the Republic of Seychelles
Access to primary care is free at the point of use as prescribed by the constitution of the
country. Since the government’s policy on health was formulated in 1976, the Seychelles has
made considerable progress in achieving basic health indicators. Access to and utilization of
health services are among the best in the world.

### 1.3.1 Seychelles Expanded Programme on Immunization

The National Immunization Programme (NIP)) in Seychelles was initiated in 1808 with obligatory small pox vaccination, in 1956 with introduction of DPT and OPV, and was formally established in 1974 to provide the basic antigens against the six childhood killer diseases which are BCG, DPT, OPV and Measles with the support of the World Health Organization. The National EPI expanded to include new antigens over the years such as BCG, OPV, MMR, DT, DPT, ATT, Yellow Fever, Hepatitis B and Haemophilus Influenza Type b, IPV, HPV, Rotavirus Vaccine. Over the years, the immunization schedule has expanded to include new antigens such as MMR in 1989, Yellow Fever and Hepatitis B in 1995, Haemophilus influenzae type b in 2010, HPV vaccine in 2014, IPV in 2015, Rota in 2017 (Fig .2).

EPI is one of the key elements of the Child Health Services offered by the Seychelles Ministry of Health. The Child Health and EPI are closely linked to other programmes and services such as Maternal Health services and the School Health Programmes. The main activities undertaken within the programmes are immunization, growth monitoring, nutrition surveillance, development assessment, post-natal assessment, hearing assessment, home visiting, health education/ promotion, counselling, referral services, follow up care, monitoring and evaluation.

The Child Health/EPI programmes are offered in all the 15 government health facilities on Mahe, Praslin, La Digue and also in 3 satellite clinics on a part-time basis. The programs are staffed by nurses/ midwives with support of other members of the District Heath Team. Immunization services are offered in other Ministry of Health units such as Maternity, Casualty Department, Haemodialysis Unit, Occupational Health Unit and CDCU.

The objectives of immunization programme in Seychelles are to:

* Maintain coverage above 99% for all antigens including 2nd dose of Measles
* Maintain Polio Free certification level status
* Strengthen measles cased- based surveillance and generate evidence for verification of elimination of measles/Congenital Rubella Syndrome and Rubella

* Conduct periodic coverage surveys to establish gaps in parent’s knowledge and to review the service provided and validate coverage performance
* Develop communication plan to support the programme
* Finalize the EPI Policy

## 2.1 Situation analysis of the Immunization programme

### 2.1.1 Service Delivery

High quality immunization services are provided by qualified nurses on scheduled appointment basis in all public health facilities. The system allows tracking defaulters if any, through phone call or home visit until the eligible gets the service.

The Seychelles DTP1, DPT3 and MCV1 coverage shows a high reported coverage above 95% for all antigens that is in conformity with the WHO UNICEF estimate where difference between reported and estimated is below 5% for all antigens. Among the challenges, the migration to other health districts, with limitation of reliable denominator estimate provides lower estimate for some areas and higher for others making it challenging to understand sub national level performance due to the very few populations.

The DPT1-DPT3 drop-out rate has improved from 3% to 1% in the three years. DPT1-MCV1 drop-out rate was 0% in 2016.

As part of strategies to sustain high reported coverage, the programme has embarked on adapting the revised AFRO RED approach that is already drafted for finalisation and will be disseminated to all health districts by end 2018. This will help to standardise health facility microplanning development by catchment areas. The Pneumococcal vaccine introduction will be used to train and update the Immunization health workforce in each health district.

In order to further strengthen service delivery, the EPI program continues to address the recommendations from the comprehensive EPI review (2017), the KAP study findings (2017), the cold chain inventory (2018) to develop rehabilitation plan and the coverage survey findings

### 2.1.2 EPI Coverage performance

The EPI programme in Seychelles has achieved considerable success in preventing and controlling most vaccine-preventable diseases. The programme has sustained high coverage levels of all antigens and has subsequently reduced more than 90% of morbidity and mortality from diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, neonatal tetanus and measles in comparison with the period prior to the implementation of the EPI programme. This has contributed to the continued decline in infant and child mortality.

The reported coverage of all antigens in Seychelles has been high. As per the WHO/UNICEF Joint Reporting Form (JRF) reports, it is noted that in 2014, 20% of the districts reported coverage below 80% for DTP3 coverage but in the period 2005-2015, the estimated DTP3 remained above 95% at national level indicating very good access for immunization services. The Protected at Birth coverage estimated is sustained at 100%, indicating that women of childbearing age are vaccinated against Tetanus. The few targeted clients move to other health centres for services that are vaccinated and are included in the health centre but not in the original leading to artificial low performing district

### 2.1.3 Accelerated Disease Control & VPD surveillance

#### a) Progress towards Polio Eradication

The last, virologically confirmed, case of poliomyelitis in Seychelles was reported in 1966.
Seychelles’ polio National Certification Committee (NCC) was formed in 2001 and the
country documentation for polio free certification has been accepted in 2005 by the Polio
Eradication to the African Regional Certification Commission (ARCC). The country is
conducting active AFP surveillance visits bimonthly. In case an AFP case detected, the stools
specimens will be shipped to the South Africa polio Laboratory at National Institute of
Communicable Diseases (NICD). As expected, the NCC has been submitting the annual polio
progress report documents to the ARCC every year. However, despite active case search
conducted regularly, no AFP case has been notified for over 10 years.

The National coverage for the major antigens including OPV3 has been above 99% for the
past 5 years. The immunization coverage for OPV3 is 100% and as per the WHO UNICEF
estimates (WUENIC) the coverage of OPV3 is above 90% from 2001-2015. Since the
immunization coverage is 100% for children at the age of one year, supplementary
immunization activities are not needed nor done in Seychelles.

In addition, to strengthen the population immunity, IPV was introduced in September 2015.
The switch from tOPV to bOPV was successfully implemented in April 2016. The polio
outbreak response plan is completed which will be called into action in case of need.

#### b) Progress towards Measles/Congenital Rubella Elimination

Seychelles introduced measles vaccine in 1967 followed by change to MMR in 1989, that was
introduced for first and 2nd dose at fifteen months of age that was shifted to 6 years in 2001.
Seychelles has experienced measles outbreaks in 1967, and in 1974 that were responded to
through vaccination campaigns. The outbreak of 1982 to 1983, affected babies who were
too young to have been immunized. The latest outbreak of measles was in 2006 that affected
children below 15 months and young adults up to 18 years of age resulting in 600 cases in
island of Mahé. A vaccination response targeted 6 months to 18 years with no documented measles deaths.

Noting the high reported and estimated coverage of the two doses, the delayed establishment of the fever rash surveillance limited the timely assessment of progress but currently documentation has started for elimination verification since 2018. The coverage of MCV1 (MMR1) & MCV2 (MMR2) has been high and sustained above 95% as per the WHO UNICEF estimates from 2001 to 2015 (Figs 2.1).

The national laboratory has the capacity to test and confirm through serology measles and rubella but is yet to submit the proficiency panel for testing so that the lab can be accredited by WHO for quality assurance since 2015.

### c) Sustaining Maternal Neonatal Tetanus Elimination

Seychelles has sustained elimination of Maternal Neonatal Tetanus (MNT) from early 2000. There is high skilled hospital delivery over 99% and the WHO UNICEF estimate of protected at birth coverage for 2014 and 2015 is 100%. The surveillance system monitors neonatal and general tetanus and there were no reported cases of neonatal Tetanus or general tetanus since 2002 when they were considered as having achieved MNT Elimination Status.

Each pregnant mother is scheduled for an average of 10-13 Antenatal care visits during her pregnancy and will be given the booster doses of TT vaccine during pregnancy. The country needs to update the TT schedule as the country as it shifts to using Td.

### d) Other Key VPDs of importance & other key VPDs of importance under the near horizon

The Rotavirus sentinel surveillance established since 2013, is documenting evidence that guided the introduction in 2017. Although there is no sentinel surveillance for Paediatric bacterial meningitis, the specific pneumonia cases are monitored using the IDSR.
As per the increasing trend of high risk groups for Hepatitis B including mothers, the DSRU generated Hepatitis B infection trend is useful if it can be integrated with the serological result to identify the risk and determine the introduction of Hep B birth dose. The seasonal influenza and respiratory infectious disease are also routinely notified and confirmatory tests are being done. The dengue outbreak has been recurrent and surveillance is monitoring trend for effective control. The DSRU unit supported by the Public Health laboratory will continue generating required evidence to provide guidance for the control, elimination and need for future vaccines by contributing to the local evidence of disease burden.

To monitor the vaccine safety, a national AEFI committee was established in February 2017. AEFI though notifiable under IDSR framework, the current web-based surveillance reporting system does not include AEFI reporting that may have resulted in the notification of reported AEFI that is sub optimal. Notified cases currently are investigated and NIP is tracking the notified cases. Seychelles has not attained the target of AEFI notification yet.

### 2.1.4 Vaccine Supply, Quality and Cold chain

During 2005, the Ministry of Health took on the full responsibility for procurement and management of vaccines and other equipment (previously handled by a private firm). This has contributed to a significant reduction in the cost of vaccines compared to the prices paid through the private provider. To ensure that good quality, safe and effective vaccines are purchased, only WHO pre-qualified vaccines are purchased.

The required vaccines & supply forecasting is done by the NPI manager the on annual basis derived from the annual and cMYP and submits requests to the Finance unit of MOH for procurement, that is 100% funded by the Government for the review period and the allocation of funds for the operation of the National Immunization Programme at all levels. Since 2010, a memorandum of understanding (MOU) is signed for procurement through UNICEF that assures purchase of WHO Prequalified vaccines under which Government pre pays for the procurement. The vaccine management procedures follow WHO recommended standards including new technologies to assure vaccine potency, the use vaccine arrival reports, standardised temperature monitoring, use of VVMs, wastage rate calculation, up to date stock management, and forecasting of needs and distribution, all extensively documented.
There is standard safe disposal for immunisation waste where generated waste is disposed through regular integrated health care waste management system collected from all Health Facilities and incinerated at central level.

The central cold room is powered by Electricity and has backup generator that is automated to switch on in the rare event of a power outage. The Central Vaccine Store (CVS) delivers vaccines bundled to districts on a monthly basis on request from the HFs but transported by the National level to the HFs.

The cold chain space has been expanded recently by additional 500 litres volume and the cold chain replacement plan is followed. There is contingency plan to ensure cold chain in all HF visited and good vaccine management practices were observed. However, there will not be adequate space for store at national level and assessment will guide the Store capacity required.

Currently the monitoring for cold chain in the National Store is done manually twice per day. There is a plan to have the automated Cellular Temperature Monitoring System for the National store that can be expanded to the immunization Health facilities.

The refrigerators in the sub national level have capacity to maintain the temperature up to 14 hours in case of power failure. The refrigerators have regulators so that they withstand temperature fluctuations.

The national regulatory authority currently as its function oversees medicines and drugs also handles vaccine regulation by the chief pharmacist and is yet to recruit full time officer however a National Regulatory Unit is yet to be established.

### 2.1.5 Health information on Routine immunization and Data management

The routine immunization data is more prone to transcription errors as data entry also happens at the national level which compromises efficiency. The verification conducted for immunisation data reports of first quarter of 2017 indicated there are inconsistencies between the different reporting tools within the HF and with the reports sent to Central level.

This is partly due to duplication of documentation tools and lack of computerization that may need simplification. The planned training and assessment on workload indicators for staffing needs will be an opportunity to define the Seychelles specific Human resources needs to achieve the provision of quality of care that as stated in the strategic plan beyond ensuring universal access to health services.

### 2.1.6 Vaccine Preventable Diseases surveillance

Seychelles has adopted the AFRO IDSR strategy framework for disease surveillance and response since 2003. At the national level, the NIP manager is responsible for VPD surveillance while Disease Surveillance and Response Unit (DSRU) head is responsible for the remaining surveillance, and these two units are housed in two different agencies of the MoH the Clinical Care Agency & Public Health Authority respectively.

The VPD surveillance operations are coordinated within the DSRU under the Public Health Authority of the MoH with designated surveillance focal persons at all levels (six at central level and over 100 in all HFs). There is web-based weekly reporting of 47 priority diseases conditions that is robust with web based automated data analysis and outputs that the team found as exemplary and bringing efficiency for timely availability of information for action.

The absence of scheduled technical coordination meeting between the two Departments/units
sometimes results in inefficiencies in data collection, and in review of data for meeting standards set and take action.

## National Public Health Laboratory for Vaccine Preventable Diseases

The Acute Flaccid Paralysis (AFP) surveillance system has reported the latest AFP case in 2005 and sample was processed in South Africa at National Institute of Communicable Diseases. The system is complemented by scheduled annual 6 visits conducted every two months to ensure suspected AFP cases are not missed by the passive reporting system.

The national Public health laboratory that supports measles/rubella surveillance has the capacity to test for measles/rubella serology however is yet to test suspected cases of measles/rubella to effectively support the surveillance system, the latest confirmed outbreak of measles was in 2006. However, it is yet to test the required samples to quality for quality control and join the WHO network. The shift to fever-rash surveillance for elimination mode measles surveillance to verify measles elimination is established in 2018. The samples from Rota sentinel surveillance are tested in the national laboratory.

### 2.1.7 Demand generation, communication, Advocacy

Interpersonal communication is the major communication strategy used to sustain demand for the Immunization services. The Nurses provide information on the vaccine, the procedure, any anticipated effect to be observed after the vaccination, next appointment that is diligently done with professionalism.

There is free airtime allocated by the national media for broadcasting health messages that the immunization programme uses to mobilize the community or raise awareness on vaccines and immunization whenever needed. However, the incident of vaccine hesitancy noted during the HPV introduction, non-consenting care givers emerging is a pointer to potential risk of accumulation of susceptible and needs targeted communication strategy. The revision of public health to include a regulation to make vaccination mandatory is in process.

The communication plan needs to include a risk communication component for anticipated new vaccines to be introduced including addressing severe AEFI event, so that proactive mechanism is in place to mitigate such incidents. There is no focal person for health promotion in the Family health and nutrition. There is a need for all the IEC materials to be consolidated together as guide for the Nurses as job aid for use noting the recent inclusion of antigens and the result of the KAP study that indicated knowledge of care givers was limited to the vaccines that their children are receiving despite good inter personal communication.

# 5. COSTING AND FINANCING OF MULTI YEAR PLAN

Costing the 2019 – 2023 Comprehensive Multi-Year Plan (cMYP) is premised on some parameters and assumptions. The costing of this cMYP for Seychelles is based on the priorities set out in the programmatic section of the cMYP. As this is a follow on cMYP to that of 2013 – 2017, the data for 2017 was used as base line for planning and projections. Standard programme inputs such as vaccines, injection materials and cold chain equipment were costed using the prices that Seychelles pays as a self-procuring country. This is because all the EPI supplies in Seychelles are purchased through Government. Operational costs for routine and the new antigens introduces were based on past expenditure. The staff cost is based on the integrated government salary scale grid with input from staff members themselves. The financing information was obtained from past expenditures of the Government and the only Immunization partner in the country, which is the World Health Organization (WHO).

# 7. CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATION

The process of producing this third cMYP has been beneficial to the management of EPI in highlighting key areas where sustained effort is needed from 2018 and beyond to further improve the EPI programme. The EPI through the Directorate of Family Health and Nutrition will submit this plan to the Senior Management of MoH Seychelles to secure the additional resources needed for the for the smooth functioning of the EPI in general including the required verification surveillance information to provide evidence for elimination of targeted diseases that are due.

We will strive to achieve the goals set. We thank the Senior Management for their past support and we look forward to their continued support in the achievement of the objectives set out in the cMYP of Seychelles 2019-2023.
