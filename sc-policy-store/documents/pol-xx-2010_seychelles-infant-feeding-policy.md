### C. Introduction

Malnutrition has been responsible, directly or indirectly, for 60% of the 10.9 million deaths annually among children under five. 

Breastfeeding is known to be the unequalled way of providing ideal food for the healthy growth and development of infants. As a global public health recommendation, infants should be exclusively breastfed for the first six months of life.

#### i. Aim
To improve, through optimal feeding, the nutritional status, growth, development and health of infants in Seychelles.

#### ii. Objectives
1.  Ensure health advantages of breastfeeding are discussed with all women.
2.  Implement best practice standards (UNICEF/WHO BFHI Ten Steps).
3.  Create a conducive environment for breastfeeding.
4.  Enable health staff to provide accurate information and support.
5.  Foster liaison between health care professionals.
6.  Increase the incidence and duration of exclusive breastfeeding.

***

### D. Rationale

Breast milk is the optimal food for newborn infants. In Seychelles, the 2009 Breastfeeding Evaluation at Victoria Hospital showed that only 62% of babies were exclusively breastfed in the first few days, declining rapidly in subsequent months.

Regarding HIV, the **Seychelles HIV and AIDS Policy (2000)** recommends HIV testing for all pregnant women and providing support for feeding decisions, including free formula for those unable to afford it.

***

### E. Scope of the Policy

*   **Application:** All staff within the Ministry of Health should adhere to this policy.
*   **Target:** Mothers and healthy babies born at term.
*   **Deviations:** Cases involving sick or pre-term babies, or medical contraindications, require documentation by the medical team.

***

### F. In Support of This Policy

The Ministry of Health prohibits:
*   Display of posters/materials promoting breast milk substitutes.
*   Direct/indirect contact between manufacturers/distributors and mothers in facilities.
*   Distribution of samples or gift packs of breast milk substitutes.
*   Acceptance of free gifts, money, or equipment from these manufacturers.

***

### G. Principles

*   Breastfeeding is the healthiest way to feed a baby.
*   Mothers have the right to make fully informed choices and will be supported regardless of their decision (breastfeeding or artificial formula).

***

### The Ten Steps to Successful Breastfeeding

1.  **Written Policy:** Have a written breastfeeding policy communicated to all staff.
2.  **Staff Training:** Train all healthcare staff in necessary implementation skills.
3.  **Inform Mothers:** Inform all pregnant women about benefits and management.
4.  **Skin-to-Skin:** Place babies in skin-to-skin contact immediately after birth for at least an hour.
5.  **Support Lactation:** Show mothers how to breastfeed and maintain lactation.
6.  **Exclusive Breastfeeding:** Give newborn infants no food/drink other than breast milk unless medically indicated.
7.  **Rooming-in:** Allow mothers and infants to remain together 24 hours a day.
8.  **Feeding on Demand:** Encourage breastfeeding on demand.
9.  **No Artificial Teats:** Give no pacifiers/dummies to breastfeeding infants.
10. **Support Groups:** Foster breastfeeding support groups and refer mothers upon discharge.

***

### Detailed Implementation Steps (Summary)

*   **Step 1 (Communication):** Policy available in Delivery Suites, Wards, Clinics, and on the MOH Intranet. Translated into Creole.
*   **Step 2 (Training):** Mandatory training for clinical and non-clinical staff. Integration into NIHSS curriculum.
*   **Step 3 (Information):** One-to-one discussions with midwives by 32 weeks’ gestation.
*   **Step 4 (Skin-to-Skin):** Encouraged for vaginal and C-section births immediately after delivery.
*   **Step 5 (Lactation):** Assistance with second breastfeed within 6 hours; support for expressing milk for separated mother/baby.
*   **Step 6 (Exclusive Feeding):** No water/glucose/formula unless medically indicated. Complementary feeding starts at 6 months.
*   **Step 7 (Rooming-in):** 24-hour bedside care; applied to NICU as condition permits.
*   **Step 8 (On Demand):** No restrictions on frequency/duration; breastfeeding room provided.
*   **Step 9 (No Teats):** Use of cup or syringe feeding as temporary alternatives.
*   **Step 10 (Support):** Provision of helpline and community support group referrals.

***

### I. A Welcome for Breastfeeding Families

Breastfeeding is the normal way to feed. Mothers are supported to feed in all public areas of the health facility.

### J. Supporting Mothers who are Artificially Feeding

Individualised training on safe bottle preparation and sterilisation is provided. No specific brand of formula is promoted.
