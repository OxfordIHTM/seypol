# FOREWORD

Like the rest of the world, our population is ageing. Whilst this unprecedented demographic reality will pose numerous challenges in how we live, work, save, access health care and education as well as take care of each other, it offers many opportunities. Harnessing their skills and experience and encouraging harmonious intergenerational transfers. Our challenge is to provide for a population that will consist of more and more dependents relative to the number of active workers. This means addressing the needs of the elderly, improving labour productivity and competitiveness as well as ensuring the sustainability of the social protection system.

I am pleased to present this first Plan of Action, for the years 2019-2023, to realise and implement the 14 objectives of the National Policy on Population Ageing which was launched in November this year. It is the first because the process we have embarked on is a long-term one and we have to sustain our efforts if we are to manage the transition to an ageing population in a smooth and sustainable manner.

Our society and economy is today radically different from what they were fifty years ago. Through a people-centred approach to development, our Government together with the hard working Seychellois population have transformed our country from an impoverished former colony to a proud and dignified nation. Today, we are ranked by the UNDP amongst countries with high human development and also graduated to high income status in 2015, as gross national income per capita climbed to over US$14,000.

But in just 40 years’ time, we may be living in a country where for the first time in our history, there will be more people above 60 years of age than children under 14 years. This is the direct result of the many years of declining fertility and increasing longevity. This is a worldwide trend and is here to stay.

I am confident that with strong commitment from all partners and stakeholders we shall rise to this new challenge. But I am also optimistic because this is an exciting time full of new opportunities. Growing old is not a disease. It is a natural part of life. This is the time to redefine how we see old age, how we grow old and most importantly how we continue to participate and contribute actively in our own personal development and that of the country.

I would like to thank everyone who has been involved in the development of this Plan of Action and look forward to the same determination in its implementation.

This Plan of Action is being proposed to ensure that as a country, we maintain an adequate standard of living for people as they grow older, while recognizing and

**Mrs Mitcy Larue**
Minister for Family Affairs

# 1.0 Executive Summary

The National Policy on Population Ageing was approved by the Cabinet of Ministers on 9th May 2018. The Minister for Family Affairs, Mrs Mitcy Larue, subsequently delivered a statement to the National Assembly on the 29th May to raise awareness amongst members on the key issues in the Policy. Between the 10th and 11th July 2018, a Conference was organized to sensitize partners and stakeholders on the Policy and present to them the 8 thematic areas of the Policy. In October 2018, a National Steering Committee on Population Ageing was established, chaired by Minister Larue and comprising representatives from all key sectors with the specific mandate of finalising a National Plan of Action on Population Ageing, which was approved by Cabinet in December 2018.

The National Plan of Action on Population Ageing covers a period of 5 years (2019-23). The activities have been derived from the 14 objectives of the Policy on Population Ageing. Six Technical Committees were set up to coordinate and prioritise the activities within their respective sectors and with their relevant partners. The prioritised activities are presented in the Plan in 6 areas: 
* Sustainable Income Maintenance and Service Provision
* Health and Well-Being
* Neglect, Abuse and Violence/Combating Ageism/Intergenerational Solidarity
* Age-Friendly Environment
* Participation in Development
* Labour and Employment

An important characteristic of the Plan is that the activities listed are for the short-term - that is the next 5 years. Population Ageing is already happening but the major impacts will be felt in the long-term. This first Plan of Action is about engaging with the population on the demographic changes that are happening and their impacts. It is about building national consensus around a number of important issues and coming up with sustainable solutions. The Plan is also about assessing the readiness of key sectors to address the challenges that population ageing will bring and making the necessary changes to allow people, communities and key sectors to adapt to the changing demographic realities.

It is hoped that at the end of 2023 when a final assessment of the implementation of the Plan is made, that Seychelles is better prepared to face the challenges of an ageing population. By then, the whole population should know what is happening and likely to continue to happen, what changes we need to make in our personal lives, at sectoral level and national level. This Plan is laying the foundation for a different kind of society and economy. It thus demands a concerted effort from all partners to ensure that we engage with the people, plan consciously and act responsibly.
# 2.0 Goals, Vision and Policy Focus

## Vision
The vision of the National Policy on Ageing is to have a society where the elderly remains valued, are fully empowered to participate and benefit in development and where they are able to live active, healthy and dignified lives.

## Goal
Through the participation of all stakeholders, develop a comprehensive and coherent national response to ageing that is equitable and sustainable.

## Policy Focus

The policy focuses on the following themes:
1. Sustainable Income Maintenance and Service Provision
2. Health and Well-Being
3. Age-Friendly Environments
4. Labour and Employment
5. Participation in Development
6. Intergenerational Solidarity
7. Neglect, Abuse and Violence
8. Combating Ageism

# 9.0 Implementation of the Plan

The Ministry of Family affairs has portfolio responsibility for population issues and has been mandated by Cabinet to coordinate at national level the implementation of this Plan of Action.

It will do this through the National Steering Committee on Population Ageing which has the following Terms of Reference:

* Provide a national platform for discussion and decision-making on population ageing
* Coordinate, Monitor and Report on the implementation of the National Policy on Ageing and its Plan of Action
* Advise Government on key issues and areas of population ageing
* Educate and sensitise the population on ageing
* Promote monitoring and research on population ageing.
* Ensure Seychelles manages the long term transition to an ageing population in a coordinated and sustainable manner

The following ministries and organisations have a key role in coordinating implementation of the prioritised activities at sectoral level:

* Ministry of Family Affairs
* Ministry of Health
* Ministry of Habitat, Infrastructure and Land Transport
* Ministry of Employment, Immigration and Civil Status
* Local Government Department
* Culture Department
* Economic Planning Department
* Youth Department
* Agency for Social Protection
* Seychelles Pension Fund
* National Bureau of Statistics
* National Council for the Elderly

Other organisations will be co-opted as and when necessary.

The implementation of the Plan will be evaluated every 2 and half years and a report submitted to Cabinet. A second Plan of Action will be developed for the period 2024-28.
