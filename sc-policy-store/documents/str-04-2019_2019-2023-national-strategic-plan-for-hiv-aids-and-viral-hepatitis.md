# 2019-2023 National Strategic Plan for HIV, AIDS and Viral Hepatitis

**SEYCHELLES**

## FOREWORD BY THE PRESIDENT

The 2019-2023 National Strategic Plan for HIV, AIDS and Hepatitis is the culmination of many months of preparation by the National AIDS Council working in close collaboration with UNAIDS, other Ministry of Health departments, and local stakeholders from many different sectors. This collaborative approach emphasises the growing awareness among all partners that the challenges of HIV and AIDS can only be successfully addressed by working together. This dedication and commitment were amply demonstrated during the workshop to finalise the Plan and forum to develop the operational plan. Participation by communities and individuals from all sectors and representing a wide range of organisations will help to ensure a truly national action.

The 2019-2023 National Strategic Plan for HIV, AIDS and Hepatitis addresses the realities of Seychelles and the evolving epidemic of HIV, AIDS, other sexually transmitted infections, hepatitis and tuberculosis. The epidemic itself, and its evolving nature, reflect the pattern of behaviour in our society. The complexities of our sexuality, our relationships, our culture, beliefs and attitudes influence the transmission of the HIV and other infections. They also affect our reactions to infection and illness and whether and how we support each other, or stigmatise and discriminate members of our own communities and families. The Strategic Plan is therefore about us, and for us, as a community, as a people and as a nation. In taking into account our realities and specificities and addressing our vulnerable and key populations, our men and women, and our boys and girls, we can better devise the right approaches to tackling the epidemic and its impact.

The Strategic Plan and its Monitoring and Evaluation Framework, which will guide our interventions over the next five years, are expressions of our pledge as a nation and determination to face HIV, AIDS and Viral Hepatitis, not only as medical and health problems, but also to address them as cultural, social and economic issues, affecting all communities of our society and every Seychellois family. Let us now, and in the years ahead, join our efforts and let us ensure that the Plan is translated into concrete, focussed and sustained action to protect our people and care for those infected with or affected by HIV, AIDS and Viral Hepatitis.

**Danny Faure**
President of the Republic of Seychelles
April 2019

## SECTION ONE: INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND

### 1.1 Introduction

The Seychelles is a small island state with a population of 95,000 inhabitants. Life expectancy at birth is 74.8 years (80.8 years for women and 69.5 years for men). The right to health is enshrined in the constitution and all health services are available free of charge at the point of delivery in government facilities. Geographic access to health services is good and the population to doctor ratio was 591:1 in 2016.

The Seychelles’ response to the Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome (AIDS) began 30 years ago. The first Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) infection was detected in 1987 while the first case of AIDS was reported in 1993.

**Epidemiological Summary:**
By December 2017, 676 people were living with HIV or AIDS in the Seychelles. The country has a concentrated HIV epidemic with high levels among certain key populations:
- Youth aged 15-24 years
- People who inject drugs (PWID)
- Men who have sex with men (MSM)
- Sex workers (SW)

**Key Findings from Previous Reviews:**
- HIV prevalence among PWID: estimated 5.8% (2011) rising to 22% (2017).
- HIV prevalence among SWs: estimated 4.6%.
- Hepatitis C (HCV) cases increased from <20 in 2008 to 186 in 2017.

**Review of 2012-2016 NSP Achievements:**
- New reported cases of HIV nearly multiplied by 2.
- AIDS incidence divided by approximately 4.4.
- AIDS Mortality nearly divided by 3.

### 1.2 Global and Regional Commitments

#### 1.2.1 Global Commitments
1. **Lancet Commission (2015):** Urged escalation of AIDS efforts, resource mobilization, accountability, upholding human rights, and investment in research.
2. **Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs):** Commitments to end the epidemic by 2030.
3. **UNAIDS Fast-Track Strategy:** Aim for "Zero new infections, Zero discrimination and Zero AIDS-related deaths" by 2030. Seychelles is committed to the **90-90-90 targets**:
    - 90% of people living with HIV know their status
    - 90% of those who know their status receive treatment
    - 90% of those on treatment have suppressed viral loads
4. **WHO Guidelines (2015):** Recommended that ART should be initiated in everyone living with HIV at any CD4 cell count and promotes the use of PrEP.

#### 1.2.2 Regional Commitments
SADC Regional Strategy 2017-2020 focuses on:
- Addressing legal and policy barriers.
- Ensuring financial commitments.
- Community empowerment.
- Addressing stigma, discrimination and violence.
- Ensuring access to comprehensive health services.

### 1.3 Rationale for the 2019-2023 NSP

The 2019-2023 NSP addresses gaps identified in the 2017 end-of-term review. It adapts to the shift from MDGs to SDGs and adopts the UNAIDS "Fast-tracking to Zero" approach. Critical factors include the need to decentralize services and the emphasis on fiscal efficiency and country ownership.

### 1.4 Process to develop the 2019-2023 NSP

The development process began in 2016, involving Technical Working Groups (TWGs), stakeholder workshops, and extensive consultations with the Ministry of Health, NAC, and civil society organizations.

## SECTION TWO: HIV, AIDS AND VIRAL HEPATITIS SITUATION IN SEYCHELLES

### 2.1 Epidemiological Situation
Seychelles has a concentrated HIV epidemic. By December 2017, the estimated number of PLHIV was 676 (62% males, 38% females). 

- **2017 Data:** 112 new cases diagnosed (77% males, 23% females).
- **Co-infection:** 26% of newly diagnosed cases were co-infected with Hepatitis C (all were PWIDs).
- **Mortality:** 18 AIDS-related deaths reported in 2017.

### 2.2 HIV Testing, Surveillance and Surveys
Testing is provided free by the government. In 2017, 11,359 HIV tests were conducted, with 9.9% testing positive.

### 2.3 Prevention of Mother to Child Transmission (PMTCT)
PMTCT implementation has significantly reduced transmission rates from 35% (pre-2001) to 3.7% (post-2001). Option B+ was introduced in 2012.

### 2.4 Key Populations
- **PWID:** Estimated 2,560 individuals (2017). HIV prevalence rose from 5.8% (2011) to 22% (2017).
- **MSM:** 2011 IBBS found 13.2% HIV prevalence and 41.9% HCV prevalence.
- **Sex Workers:** HIV prevalence 4.6%; HCV prevalence 34.6%.
- **Prisoners:** Prevalence estimated between 4.1% and 9.4% depending on the year/sample.

### 2.5 Co-infections - Hepatitis C
966 cases of Hepatitis C reported (2002-2017). 99% of these cases were confirmed to be PWID.

## SECTION THREE: NATIONAL STRATEGIC PLAN TO MEET THE 90-90-90 TARGETS

### 3.1 NSP at a Glance
Focus is on ending the AIDS epidemic as a public health threat by 2030. Strategic priorities include:
- Identifying and reaching key populations via outreach/in-reach.
- Implementing "Test and Treat" regardless of CD4 count.
- Enhancing social enablers and public-private partnerships.

### 3.2 Guiding Principles
- **Evidence-based interventions:** Focus on highest impact activities.
- **Improved Targeting:** Prioritizing Key Populations (KPs).
- **Quality Improvement (QI):** Adherence to clinical guidelines.
- **Service Integration:** Linking SRH and HIV services.
- **Multi-Sectoral Inclusion:** Broad coordination beyond health.
- **Community Engagement:** Active participation of PLHIV and CSOs.
- **Human Rights:** Zero tolerance for stigma and discrimination.
- **Gender:** Addressing gender inequalities and GBV.

### 3.4 Goal 1: 90% of all PLHIV know their status

#### Strategic Priority 1.1: Scaling up HIV Testing
- **Population Estimates:** Addressing data gaps for PLHIV and KPs.
- **Case Detection:** Shifting toward early detection via decentralization.
- **Outreach:** Using "Test for Triage" and community-led screening.

### 3.5 Goal 2: 90% of all PLHIVs receive ART

#### Strategic Priority 3.1: Increased uptake of ART
- **Accelerate Uptake:** Plugging leaks from testing to treatment.
- **Enabling Environment:** Mentoring of health workers and anti-stigma programs.

### 3.6 Goal 3: 90% of all people on ART will be virally suppressed

#### Strategic Priority 5.1: Retention on ART
- **Facility Based Support:** Uninterrupted drug supplies and skilled providers.
- **Community Based Support:** Shifting non-clinical tasks to lay health workers.

### 3.7 Goal 4: Zero Stigma and Discrimination
Focuses on the "Getting to Zero" vision. Interventions target policy makers, PLHIV, and the general population to ensure rights-based access to services.

### 3.8 Goal 5: Sustainable National Response

#### 3.8.1 Policies and Guidelines
Need for a new National Policy on HIV and AIDS to replace outdated frameworks.

#### 3.8.2 Health Systems (The 6 Building Blocks)
1. Service Delivery
2. Health Workforce
3. Health Information Systems
4. Essential Medicines
5. Financing
6. Leadership/Governance

#### 3.8.3 Community Systems
Prioritizing the capacity of CSOs to lead outreach and adherence support.

#### 3.8.4 Coordination and Management
Based on the **Three Ones’ Principle**:
- One agreed Action Framework
- One National AIDS Coordinating Authority (NAC)
- One agreed M&E System

#### 3.8.5 Monitoring and Evaluation
- **Process:** Inputs $\rightarrow$ Activities $\rightarrow$ Outputs $\rightarrow$ Impact.
- **Review:** Every 6 months with the MoH taking the lead.
- **Mid Term Review:** Scheduled for 2021.
