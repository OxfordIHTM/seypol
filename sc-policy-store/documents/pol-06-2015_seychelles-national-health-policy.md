## 1 Introduction

This National Health Policy builds on and replaces the current national strategic framework (2006-2016), which comes to an end in 2016. The health policy framework positions health at the centre of national development, both as a beneficiary of and a contributor to socio-economic development. It thus promotes the belief that pursuit of health is inseparable from the pursuit of wealth, seeks to ensure the participation of health in the national development planning processes and invites all sectors to ensure that health is considered in all such processes.

The policy document affirms the nation's commitment to health. Its purpose is to guide strategic planning, programme development and implementation, and monitoring and evaluation, not only in the context of health services, but also in the context of the National Midterm Strategic Plan. It also serves as an advocacy tool and a reminder to all actors and stakeholders that the health of the Seychellois nation is a key objective and a measure of economic, social and cultural development.

## 2 SITUATIONAL ANALYSIS

### 2.1 DEMOGRAPHIC AND HEALTH STATUS

The Republic of Seychelles is a small, service-based, island state situated in the Indian Ocean. The country comprises 115 tropical islands spread over an area of approximately 444 square kilometers. It has an exclusive economic zone of almost 1.4 million square kilometers in one of the world’s major tuna fishing grounds. Seychelles with a gross national income per capita of US$ 14,766 (2013) is classified as a high-middle-income country. Tourism and fishing/fish processing are the major pillars of the economy, contributing 30 percent and 8 percent of gross domestic product, respectively. The national unemployment rate is recorded at 4.1 percent for 2014 (4.0 percent for males and 4.2 percent for females). Poverty is relatively low, with less than 2 percent of the population living on less than US$2 per day.

*Note: To inform the preparation of the National Health Policy and the companion National Health Strategic Plan, a comprehensive Health Situation Analysis has been carried out. Figures are primarily sourced from the Seychelles Bureau of Statistics.*

Seychelles mid-year population in 2014 is 91,359 (50.4 percent females, 49.6 percent males). The population is aging, with a median age of 34 years. Overall life expectancy at birth reached 73.2 years in 2014 (78.3 for women and 68.4 for men). 

Seychelles has achieved most of the MDGs. Infant mortality was 10.9 per thousand live births in 2014, and maternal mortality has been zero in most recent years. However, the burden of disease has shifted to non-communicable diseases (NCDs), injuries, and mental health problems. Cardiovascular, respiratory diseases, and cancer account for about 60 percent of deaths.

**Challenges identified:**
- Low contraceptive use (54%).
- High rates of overweight and obesity (60% of adults in 2014).
- High teenage pregnancies and unsafe abortion rates.
- Increasing prevalence of substance abuse.

### 2.2 SOCIAL DETERMINANTS OF HEALTH

Seychelles is classified as a high human development country. The State ensures access to education (100% secondary enrollment), free primary health care, and safe drinking water. 

**Key areas of concern:**
- **Vector-borne diseases:** Dengue, chikungunya, and leptospirosis.
- **Food Security:** Seychelles is a net importer of food. Local production of vegetables and poultry has declined significantly since 1995.

### 2.3 GENDER AND WOMEN EMPOWERMENT INCLUDING GENDER VIOLENCE

While gender parity is strong in education (girls are more likely to be enrolled in tertiary education), disparities exist in job-seeking behaviors and drug dependence (higher in boys). Gender-based violence is increasing; the Ministry of Health is adopting a Risk Indicator Framework for Domestic Violence to improve identification and referral.

### 2.4 ORGANIZATION OF THE PUBLIC HEALTH SECTOR

Since January 2014, the sector has been restructured into a Health Ministry and three public bodies:

- **Health Ministry:** Responsible for policy development, planning, monitoring, and evaluation.
- **Health Care Agency:** Manages the provision of primary, secondary, and tertiary care.
- **Public Health Authority:** Independent entity to regulate the health sector and protect population health.
- **National Institute of Health and Social Services:** Academic arm providing pre-service and in-service education and research.

### 2.5 HEALTH SYSTEM

**Infrastructure:** 14 health centers, 3 cottage hospitals, and specialized tertiary, rehabilitative, and psychiatric hospitals on Mahe.

**Human Resources:** Staffing levels are robust (7 per thousand), exceeding benchmarks. However, there is a shortage of specialists, and attrition is a concern as workers move to the private sector for higher earnings.

**Medicines and Technology:** Sourcing quality products at competitive prices is a priority. There is a recognized need for an equipment management policy to standardize the 90% of equipment that consists of donations.

**Health Information Systems:** Multiple systems exist (laboratory, pharmacy, etc.) but lack integration and a legal framework for data confidentiality.

**Financing:** Health care is free at the point of use, funded primarily by general tax revenue (93% in 2013). Costs are rising due to NCDs and an aging population.

## 3 Vision, Mission, Guiding Principles and Values

### 3.1 VISION

The attainment, by all people in Seychelles of the highest level of physical, social, mental and spiritual health and living in harmony with nature.

### 3.2 MISSION

To relentlessly promote, protect and restore the health and quality of life and dignity of all people in Seychelles, with the active participation of all stakeholders, through the creation of an enabling environment for citizens to make informed decisions about their health.

### 3.3 GUIDING PRINCIPLES AND VALUES

#### 3.3.1 Rights to health

The Government believes in the right to health and human dignity. Article 29 of the Constitution reaffirms the State's commitment to provide free primary health care and promote individual responsibility in health.

#### 3.3.2 Health for All, Health by All and Health in All
- **Health for All:** A principle placing individual and family well-being at the center of development.
- **Health by All:** A philosophy that primary responsibility for health rests with the individual, supported by a shared commitment between government and partners.
- **Health in All:** A conviction that health is inextricably linked with national wealth; all sectors must consider health in their policy decisions.

#### 3.3.6 Values

Health care must be people-centered, respectful of human rights, and based on equity. Access to services will be based on need, with special consideration for the vulnerable.

## 4 POLICY OBJECTIVES

**General Objectives:**
- Improve health status through preventive, promotive, curative, palliative, and rehabilitative strategies.
- Provide high-quality integrated health care and free primary health care.
- Strengthen governance and accountability.
- Achieve self-sufficiency in HR for health and system sustainability.
- Promote research, innovation, and data management.
- Support the private health sector and PPPs.

### 4.1 STRENGTHENING INTEGRATED HEALTH CARE
1. Build a strong health system capable of responding to future challenges.
2. Sustain an optimal national primary health care model.
3. Improve the quality of secondary and tertiary services.
4. Maintain a three-tier national referral health system.
5. Support private health care and public-private collaboration.
6. Support effective complementary health care services.
7. Upgrade Seychelles Hospital to an internationally accredited teaching hospital.

### 4.2 PROMOTING AND PROTECTING HEALTH
1. Increase life expectancy through public health interventions.
2. Address priority communicable and non-communicable diseases via a life-course approach.
3. Prevent and control antimicrobial resistances.
4. Integrate a health dimension into all public policy.

### 4.3 HUMAN RESOURCES FOR HEALTH
1. Ensure adequate, competent, and equitably distributed staffing.
2. Promote careers in health for Seychellois to reduce reliance on international staff.
3. Establish minimum standards for overseas recruitment.
4. Support allied health professionals and health managers.
5. Implement comprehensive HR development plans.

### 4.4 SUSTAINABLE FINANCING FOR HEALTH
1. Maintain free health care in state institutions at the point of use.
2. Prioritize funding for preventive health.
3. Explore partnerships with the private sector and civil society.
4. Promote public awareness of health care costs to encourage rational use.
5. Explore options for sustainable health financing.

### 4.5 RESEARCH AND INNOVATION
1. Integrate research into all health programmes.
2. Adhere to the highest ethical standards for research participants.
3. Ensure partnership research benefits the Seychellois population.

### 4.6 PARTNERSHIP AND PARTICIPATION
1. Promote active participation of all sectors and international agencies.
2. Play a leadership role in health among Small Island Developing States.
3. Promote the participation of individuals and families in health delivery.

## 5 Governance Framework

### 5.1 ORGANISATION OF HEALTHCARE DELIVERY
- Maintain the three-tier system and appropriate referral mechanisms.
- Focus on primary prevention and decentralize specialist services to regional centers.
- Develop mechanisms for joint delivery with community providers.

### 5.2 GOVERNANCE AND ACCOUNTABILITY
- Formalize the separation of oversight, delivery, and regulation functions.
- Ensure equitable resource allocation by the Ministry of Health.
- Encourage integrated working and consultative decision-making between government institutions.
- Strengthen professional councils as regulators.

### 5.3 MONITORING AND EVALUATION
- Develop a robust M&E framework centered on key health indicators.
- Ensure optimal collection and timely dissemination of data.
- Institute regular reporting and public feedback mechanisms.
- Establish a robust health information system (HIS).

## 6 Indicators

### 6.1 HEALTH STATUS
- Life expectancy
- Infant and neonatal mortality rates
- Maternal mortality
- Normal weight proportions (adults/children)
- Smoking rate and physical activity levels
- Prevalence of diabetes, hypertension, and obesity
- HIV infections and AIDS mortality
- Prevalence of Hepatitis C
- Substance use/abuse and related admissions
- Budget allocation to prevention/HP (Target: Increase by 3%)

### 6.2 HEALTH SYSTEM PERFORMANCE
- Potentially preventable hospital admissions
- Waiting times for specialists
- Proportion of Seychellois human resources for health
- Availability of essential medicines and lifesaving commodities
