**Purpose:** AN ACT to provide mental health care and services for persons with mental illness and to protect, promote and fulfill the rights of such persons during delivery of mental health care and services and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.

*ENACTED by the President and the National Assembly.*

## PART I - PRELIMINARY

**1. Short title and commencement**
This Act may be cited as the **Mental Health Care Act, 2020** and shall come into operation on such date as the Minister may, by notice in the Gazette, appoint.

**2. Interpretation**
In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires, —

*   **“advance directive”** means a directive made by a person under section 5;
*   **“Board”** means the Mental Health Care Board established under section 36;
*   **“care-giver”** means a person who resides with the person with mental illness and is responsible for providing care to that person and includes a relative or any other person who performs this function;
*   **“Consultant-in-charge”** means a specialist psychiatrist or physician appointed to be in charge of one or more mental health facilities;
*   **“mental health care or mental health treatment”** includes biological and psychological treatments, social care for mental illness and curative and rehabilitative services, provided either in a health or mental health facility or in the community;
*   **“mental health facility”** means a mental health hospital or a part of a hospital or a psycho-geriatric home for mentally ill elderly persons or a clinic or health centre or other place for in-patient or out-patient treatment of individuals with mental illness;
*   **“mental health professional”** means any health professional trained in mental health care or mental health treatment and registered with the respective regulatory authority or council;
*   **“mental illness”** means a substantial disorder of thinking, mood, perception, orientation or memory that impairs judgment, behaviour, capacity to recognise reality or ability to meet the ordinary demands of life, including mental conditions associated with alcohol and drugs but does not include solely intellectual disability;
*   **“Minister”** means the Minister responsible for health and the term “Ministry” shall be construed accordingly;
*   **“nominated representative”** means a person nominated or appointed under section 12 or 13;
*   **“prescribed”** means prescribed by regulation made under the Act; and
*   **“Tribunal”** means the Mental Health Care Tribunal established under section 43;

## PART II - MENTAL ILLNESS AND CAPACITY TO MAKE MENTAL HEALTH CARE AND TREATMENT DECISIONS

**3. Determination of mental illness**
(1) Mental illness shall be diagnosed in accordance with nationally or internationally accepted medical standards, including the latest edition of the International Classification of Diseases of the World Health Organization, or as may be prescribed.

(2) The mental illness of a person shall not be diagnosed or determined on the basis of —
(a) the political, economic, social status or membership of a cultural, racial or religious group, or any other reason not directly relevant to mental health status of the person; or
(b) nonconformity with moral, social, cultural, work or political values or religious beliefs prevailing in a person's community.

(3) Past treatment or hospitalisation in a mental health facility shall not by itself justify any present or future diagnosis or determination of a person's mental illness.

(4) The mental illness of a person shall be determined by a mental health professional and shall only be diagnosed by a qualified medical practitioner with expertise in mental health.

(5) The determination or diagnosis of a mental illness under this Act shall not be taken to mean that the person is of unsound mind unless he or she has been declared to be of unsound mind by a competent Court.

**4. Capacity to make mental health care or treatment decision**
(1) Every person, including a person with mental illness, is deemed to have capacity to make decisions regarding his or her mental health care or treatment unless the person is unable to —
(a) understand the information that is relevant to make a decision on the person's treatment, admission or personal assistance; or
(b) appreciate any reasonably foreseeable consequence of a decision or lack of decision on the person's treatment, admission or personal assistance; or
(c) communicate the decision under subsection (a) by means of speech, gesture or any other manner.

(2) The information referred to under subsection (1) shall be given to a person using simple language which such person understands or by sign language, visual aids, or any other means to enable the person to understand the information.

(3) Where a person makes a decision regarding his or her mental health care or treatment which is perceived by others as inappropriate or wrong, it shall not automatically follow that the person does not have the capacity to make a mental health care or treatment decision, so long as the person has the capacity to make the mental health care or treatment decision under subsection (1).

## PART III - ADVANCE DIRECTIVE AND NOMINATED REPRESENTATIVE

**5. Advance directive**
(1) Every person, who is not a minor, shall have a right to make an advance directive in such manner as may be prescribed, specifying any or all of the following —
(a) the manner in which the person wishes to be cared for or treated for a future mental illness;
(b) the manner in which the person wishes not to be cared for or treated for a future mental illness;
(c) the individual or individuals, in order of precedence, the person wants to appoint as his or her nominated representative as provided for under section 12.

(2) The person nominated under subsection (1) (c) shall be an adult and shall consent to act as such representative.

(3) An advance directive under subsection (1) may be made by a person irrespective of his or her past mental illness or treatment for mental illness.

(4) An advance directive made under subsection (1) shall be invoked only when such person ceases to have capacity to make mental health care or treatment decisions and shall remain effective until such person regains the capacity to make mental health care or treatment decisions.

(5) Any decisions made by a person while he or she has the capacity to make mental health care decisions shall override any previously written advance directive by such person.

(6) Any advance directive made contrary to any written law shall be ab initio void.

(7) A person making an advance directive shall, until it is proved otherwise, be presumed to have the capacity to do so.

(8) An advance directive made under subsection (1) may be revoked, amended or cancelled at any time subject to section 8 or in such manner as may be prescribed, by the person who made it.

**6. Maintenance of online register**
Any advance directives given under section 5 shall be registered and maintained by the Board and shall be made available in the form and manner as may be prescribed.

**7. Advance directive not to apply to emergency treatment**
An advance directive under section 5 shall not apply to any emergency treatment given under section 29.

**8. Reviewing, altering, modifying or cancelling the advance directive**
(1) Where a mental health professional, a relative, or a care-giver of a person desires not to follow an advance directive whilst a person with mental illness is being treated, such mental health professional, relative or care-giver of the person shall make an application to the Tribunal to review, alter, modify or cancel the advance directive.

(2) Upon receipt of the application under subsection (1), the Tribunal shall... either uphold, modify, alter or cancel the advance directive after taking into consideration:
(a) whether the advance directive was made by the person of his or her own free will and free from force, undue influence or coercion;
(b) whether the person intended the advance directive to apply in the circumstances prompting the application;
(c) whether the person was sufficiently well informed to make the advance directive;
(d) whether the person had capacity to make decisions relating to his or her mental health care or treatment when such advanced directive was made; and
(e) whether the content of the advance directive contravened any written law or any provision of the Constitution.

**9. Access to the advance directive**
The person making the advance directive and his or her nominated representative shall have a duty to ensure that the Consultant-in-charge or a medical practitioner, or a mental health professional, as the case may be, has access to the advance directive when required.

**10. Advance directive for minor**
The parent or legal guardian shall have the right to make an advance directive in writing in respect of a minor and all the provisions relating to advance directive, mutatis mutandis, shall apply to such minor until such time he or she attains the age of majority.

**11. Liability of mental health professional in relation to advance directive**
A medical practitioner or a mental health professional shall not be held liable for any unforeseen consequences —
(a) on following a valid advance directive; or
(b) not following a valid advance directive if the advance directive is not available to the medical practitioner or mental health professional.

**12. Appointment of nominated representative**
(1) Notwithstanding section 5(1) (c) every person who is not a minor, shall have a right to appoint a nominated representative.
(2) The nomination under subsection (1) shall be made in writing with the person's signature or thumb impression or mark and be witnessed by two persons.
(3) The person appointed... shall not be a minor and shall be competent to discharge the duties... and give his or her consent in writing.
(4) Where no nominated representative is appointed... the following persons... in order of precedence shall be deemed to be the nominated representative:
(a) up to a second degree relative;
(b) a care-giver;
(c) a suitable person appointed as such by the Tribunal; or
(d) the Director of Social Services.
(5) A person... may revoke or alter such appointment at any time.
(6) The Tribunal may... revoke an appointment made by it... and appoint a different representative.
(7) The appointment... shall not be construed as the lack of capacity of the person to take decisions.
(8) All persons with mental illness shall have capacity to make mental health care or treatment decisions but may require varying levels of support.

**13. Nominated representative for minor**
(1) Notwithstanding section 12, in case of minors, the parent or person having custody... shall be the minor's nominated representative unless the Family Tribunal orders otherwise.
(2) Where an application is made to the Family Tribunal... and the Tribunal is of the opinion that the parent/guardian is not acting in the best interest... the Family Tribunal may appoint any suitable individual... or the Principal Secretary in the department responsável for children's affairs.

**14. Duty of nominated representative**
A nominated representative shall while fulfilling his or her duties —
(a) consider current and past wishes, life history, values, background and best interests;
(b) give particular credence to the views of the person with mental illness;
(c) provide support in making treatment decisions;
(d) have the right to seek information on diagnosis and treatment;
(e) be involved in discharge planning;
(f) apply for facilitated admission;
(g) apply to the Tribunal for discharge;
(h) apply to the Tribunal against violation of rights.

## PART IV - RIGHTS OF PERSONS WITH MENTAL ILLNESS

**15. Rights and duties with respect to persons with mental illness**
(1) The rights and duties... are in addition to any rights and duties that they may have under any other written law.
(2) Whosoever is performing the duties... shall have regard to the will and preference of the person with mental illness.

**16. Respect human dignity and privacy**
(1) The dignity and privacy of every person with mental illness shall be respected.
(2) Services shall be based on the principle of the **least restrictive alternative**.
(3) Least restrictive alternative means offering an option that meets treatment needs and imposes the least restriction on rights.

**17. Respect of right to live in community**
(1) A person with mental illness has a right to live in, be part of, and not be segregated from the community.
(2) Every person... shall be provided with care... that improve the mental capacity to develop to full potential and facilitate integration.
(3) Admission to a facility shall not amount to segregation.

**18. Non-Discrimination**
(1) A person with mental illness shall not be subject to discrimination on the grounds of his or her mental health status.
(2) Every person... shall receive care... according to standards equivalent to those applicable to any other health care user.

**19. Exploitation and abuse**
Every person/facility... shall ensure that —
(a) the person is protected from exploitation, abuse or degrading treatment;
(b) the person is not subjected to forced labour;
(c) services are not used as punishment.

**20. Duty to inform of individual rights**
(1) Every health care provider shall... inform the person with mental illness in an appropriate manner of his or her rights.
(2) For admissions under section 24 or 29, the nominated representative shall be informed immediately.

## PART V - CARE AND TREATMENT OF PERSONS WITH MENTAL ILLNESS

**21. Admission of persons with mental illness**
(1) A person... may be treated, as far as possible, at their home... without requiring care in a facility.
(2) Where a facility is required, it shall be on a basis of **voluntary admission**, unless facilitated admission applies.
(3) Where a person lacks capacity, he or she may be provided care at a mental health facility.

**22. Voluntary admission**
(1) Admissions shall be voluntary except where conditions for facilitated admission exist.
(2) Any person (not a minor) may request admission to the Consultant-in-charge.
(3) A medical officer or psychiatrist shall examine the person and report to the Consultant-in-charge.
(4) Consultant-in-charge shall admit if satisfied that the person has a severe illness, will benefit, and has understood the purpose of admission of their own free will.
(5) If a person is unable to understand purpose/nature/effects, they shall not be admitted under this section.
(6) A voluntarily admitted person shall not be treated without informed consent.
(7) A voluntarily admitted person has a right to discharge himself/herself, provided the Consultant-in-charge cannot prevent self-discharge for more than 24 hours for assessment if facilitated admission criteria are suspected.

**23. Discharge reports**
The Consultant-in-charge shall issue a discharge report in a prescribed form.

**24. Facilitated admission**
(1) If a person lacks capacity, that person may be admitted as a facilitated admission.
(2) Application shall be made by a nominated representative.
(3) Admission occurs if —
(a) Independent examination by two professionals (one psychiatrist/specialist) concludes the person has severe illness such that they have: (i) threatened/attempted self-harm, (ii) behaved violently toward others, or (iii) cannot care for self to a degree of risk;
(b) Admission is the least restrictive care option;
(c) Person is not eligible for voluntary admission.
(4) Professionals assessing must not be first-degree relatives or in a common law relationship with the patient.

**25. Treatment on facilitated admission and further steps**
(1) Facilitated admission is limited to **14 days**.
(2) Treatment requires consent of nominated representative or advance directive.
(3) At expiry, Consultant-in-charge may discharge or move to voluntary admission (Section 22).
(4) If facilitated admission criteria continue, Consultant-in-charge must apply to the Tribunal for continuance.
(5) The Tribunal shall hold a hearing and either discharge or extend the admission.
(6) Extension is **90 days** for the first time, and subsequent extensions up to **120 days**.
(7) Discharge occurs if criteria are no longer met.

**26. Admission of minor**
(1) Made on application of parent/guardian/custodian.
(2) Facilitated admission procedure (Section 24) applies mutatis mutandis.
(3) Minors shall be accommodated separately from adults.
(4) Parent/guardian/custodian shall stay with the minor.
(5) Treatment requires consent of parent/guardian/custodian.

**27. Person with mental illness at home**
(1) Nominated representative may request home assessment if the person is unwilling to go to a facility.
(2) Health professional shall visit and assess.
(3) If the person is neglecting self or risking safety, the professional may request police assistance to move them to a facility.
(4) Police shall provide necessary assistance.

**28. Person with mental illness wandering on streets**
Police shall convey such persons to the nearest facility for assessment.

**29. Emergency treatment**
(1) Treatment may be provided without consent if immediately necessary to prevent: (a) death or irreversible harm, or (b) serious harm to self or others.
(2) Includes transportation to a facility.
(3) Limited to **24 hours**.

**30. Seclusion and restraints**
(1) No person shall be subjected to seclusion.
(2) Physical restraint only if it is the only means to prevent immediate harm.
(3) Physical restraint requirements:
(a) In accredited facility;
(b) Authorised by psychiatrist/specialist;
(c) Not for punishment/convenience;
(d) Recorded in database.
(4) Maximum period: **three hours**.
(5) Family/nominated representatives must be informed immediately.

**31. Rights of relatives and care givers**
(1) Rights include: (a) visiting, (b) providing feedback/complaints, (c) support from services, (d) social assistance equal to physical illness care-givers.
(2) Inclusion in setting goals and discharge planning.
(3) Involvement requires consent of the patient (if voluntary) or the applicant (if facilitated).

**32. Leave to persons admitted as facilitated admission**
(1) Leave may be granted for up to **seven days**.
(2) Leave may be revoked if necessary for improvement/preventing deterioration.
(3) Refusal to return is handled via Section 33.

**33. Absence without leave of persons admitted as facilitated admission**
(1) Consultant-in-charge shall inform police and the applicant.
(2) Police are responsible for returning the person to the facility.

**34. Admission of person with mental illness under Court Order**
(1) Court orders (CPC 136, 138, 275) are sufficient authority for admission.
(2) Regarded as facilitated admission; Director of Social Services is the nominated representative.
(3) Consultant-in-charge reports condition to the Tribunal at least every six months.
(4) Tribunal holds a hearing on the report.
(5) Tribunal makes recommendations to the Court or President.
(6) Discharge only by order of the Court or President.

**35. Prisoners with mental illness**
(1) Prison officer applies to Consultant-in-charge for assessment.
(2) Psychiatrist may recommend: (a) outpatient treatment, or (b) voluntary/facilitated admission.
(3) Time in facility counts as time in prison.
(4) Following treatment, prisoner returns to prison or is discharged if sentence served.

## PART VI - MENTAL HEALTH CARE BOARD

**36. Establishment**
There is established a Board known as the **Mental Health Care Board**.

**37. Functions**
(a) Oversee planning and management of mental health care in Seychelles;
(b) Promote best practice and efficiency;
(c) Set accreditation standards for facilities;
(d) Set criteria for services and interventions;
(e) Inspect facilities for compliance with Act;
(f) Review use of restraints;
(g) Advise Government on mental health matters.

**38. Membership**
(1) The Board consists of:
* Principal Secretary, Ministry of Health
* Chief Medical Officer, Ministry of Health
* Consultant-in-charge of Mental Health Services
* Director of Social Services, Ministry of Social Affairs
* Chairperson, National Council for the Disabled
* Chief Nursing Officer
* Chief Allied Health Officer
* A user of mental health services
* A family member or care-giver
* Two representatives from civil society

(2)-(4) Minister appoints members and Chairperson; published in Gazette.
(5) Non-ex-officio members serve **three years**, max two consecutive terms.
(6)-(7) Resignation and replacement rules.

**39. Secretary**
Appointed by Board; responsible for meetings, records, and implementation.

**40. Meetings**
(1) At least six times a year.
(2) Time/place determined by Chairperson.
(3) Quorum: **Seven members**.
(4) Presided by Chairperson or elected member.
(5) Simple majority vote.
(6) Casting vote for presiding member.
(7) Interest conflicts: Member must notify and not vote unless authorised.
(8) Annual report submitted to Minister $\rightarrow$ National Assembly.

**41. Decision by circulation of paper**
If meeting is not possible, papers can be circulated for unanimous written approval.

**42. Continuation of members**
Members continue until new members are appointed or for three months.

## PART VII - MENTAL HEALTH CARE TRIBUNAL

**43. Establishment**
There is established the **Mental Health Care Tribunal**.

**44. Membership**
(1) Chairperson: Magistrate nominated by Chief Justice.
(2) Four other members: (a) Consultant psychiatrist/practitioner (10+ years exp), (b) AG Chambers rep, (c) Ministry of Health rep, (d) Civil society rep.
(3) Published in Gazette.
(4) Term: **three years**, one reappointment.
(5) Conflict of interest rule.
(6) Civil/criminal liability protection for good faith actions.

**45. Terms and conditions**
Prescribed by regulations.

**46. Jurisdiction**
(1) Exclusive jurisdiction over mental health matters.
(2) Specifically hears: (a) appeals against facilitated admission, (b) appeals on renewal/extension, (c) rights violation complaints.

**47. Appeals**
Appeals lead to the **Supreme Court**.

**48. Powers**
(a) Summon persons, (b) examine witnesses on oath, (c) require production of documents.

**49. Proceedings**
(1) Adjudicate matters as they arise; evidence laws apply.
(2) Quorum: **Three members** (including Chairperson).
(3) Majority vote; Chairperson has casting vote.
(4) Decisions have same force as Supreme Court judgment.
(5) Parties may be represented by attorney or representative.
(6) Opportunity to be heard and rules of natural justiceApply.
(7) Tribunal chooses most appropriate manner of proceeding.

**50. Reasons for decisions**
Decision must be delivered with reasons.

**51. Secretary**
Appointed by Minister; handles administration, summonses, and enforcement of orders.

## PART VIII - OFFENCES AND PENALTIES

**52. Offences and penalties**
(1) Falsifying advance directive or representative appointment: Up to **5 years** prison or **SCR50,000** fine, or both.
(2) Cruelty, abuse, or willful neglect of patient: Up to **15 years** prison or **SCR50,000** fine, or both.

---

## PART IX - MISCELLANEOUS

**53. Regulations**
(1) Minister may make regulations.
(2) Regulations may cover standards for illness, directives, and discharge reports.
(3) Offences in regulations: up to **SCR50,000** fine and **2 years** prison.

**54. Savings and transitional**
Acts done under the Mental Health Act (Cap 127) continue until amended/withdrawn.

**55. Repeal**
The **Mental Health Act, 2006 (Cap 127)** is repealed.
