## 1.0 Introduction

### 1.1 What is the Policy About?
A National School Nutrition Policy represents efforts to utilize schools as sites for improving the nutritional well-being of all school children. It can provide a framework for implementing nutrition strategies which would ensure that students receive nutrition education messages that are reinforced throughout the whole of the school environment. The school environment is known to powerfully influence students’ attitudes, preferences and behaviours and as such can promote and positively support healthy eating.

The policy will be used to coordinate all aspects of school nutrition including the curriculum, the school tuck shop, provision of school meals, and training of school personnel, establishing linkages with families and school personnel as well as evaluation.

### 1.2 Goals and Objectives
#### 1.2.1 Main Goal
To improve the nutritional well-being of school children in Seychelles through a school environment conducive to healthy eating.

#### 1.2.2 Objectives
*   **a)** To implement nutrition education from crèche through to secondary schools as part of a sequential comprehensive school curriculum;
*   **b)** To harmonize the school’s food provision and environment with nutrition education in the curriculum;
*   **c)** To build the capacity of staff involved in nutrition education and provision in the principles of healthy eating;
*   **d)** To involve the Parent’s Teachers Association and the community in supporting and reinforcing nutrition education;
*   **e)** To provide a tool to ensure the school’s compliance to promoting healthy eating.

#### 1.2.3 Outcomes
*   Integration of nutrition education that is in line with the Seychelles Dietary Guidelines in a sequential and comprehensive school curriculum;
*   Improved school’s food provision and environment that is harmonized with nutrition education in the curriculum;
*   Staff involved in nutrition education and provision trained on the principles of healthy eating;
*   Nutrition education supported and reinforced by the Parent’s Teachers Association and the community;
*   Tool to ensure the school’s compliance to promoting healthy eating;
*   Increase in nutrition-related activities at school level;
*   Reduction in diet-related problems amongst school children (dental caries, obesity…);
*   Advertisement/ promotion of food or drink in accordance with the Seychelles Dietary Guidelines.

## 2.0 RATIONALE FOR A SCHOOL NUTRITION POLICY

### 2.1 Why increase efforts to promote nutrition
Healthy eating pattern in childhood is vital in promoting health; growth, development and helping children attain their full educational potential. Diet is also a key in preventing both numerous short-term and long-term diet related diseases in child and adulthood.

Diet is a known risk factor for the development of **non-communicable diseases (NCDs)** including coronary heart diseases, stroke and cancer. Presently, 75% of all mortality and morbidity in Seychelles are accounted for by NCDs. The two main risk factors include sedentary lifestyles and poor eating habits characterized by diets high in saturated fats and sugars and low in fruits and vegetables.

### 2.2 Why Focus Efforts Through Schools?
Schools provide the most effective way to reach large portions of the population. They reach young people at a critical age of development at which lifestyles, including eating patterns, are developed, tested and adapted.

#### 2.2.1 Nutrition interventions in schools can improve health and well being
Well managed nutrition education programmes can bring about behaviour changes that contribute to improve nutritional well-being.

#### 2.2.2 Schools can provide interventions in ways that are highly cost effective
School health programmes that provide safe and low-cost health service interventions, such as screening and health education, are among the most cost-effective investments a nation can make.

## 3.0 CURRENT SCHOOL NUTRITION ENVIRONMENT

### 3.1 Schools in Seychelles
School is compulsory for children between the age of six and sixteen. There are 25 district primary schools and 13 regional secondary schools.

### 3.2 Nutrition in the Curriculum
Nutrition education is incorporated in the **Personal and Social Education (PSE)** curriculum, a core subject from Crèche to Secondary five. However, challenges exist as PSE is non-examinable and periods are sometimes used for other subjects.

### 3.3 School Meals Programme
Currently, 4000 students benefit from the school meals programme, prepared at five different centres (Victoria, Anse Boileau, Grand Anse Praslin, La Digue, and Silhouette).

### 3.4 Tuck Shops
There are 39 tuck shops leased to private owners. A major gap identified is that food guidelines are not currently included within the lease agreements, making quality control problematic.

### 3.5 Fundraising Activities
Schools often organize food sales for fundraising, but no guidelines or protocols currently exist to control the nutritional value of foods sold or the marketing of these foods.

### 3.6 Packed Snacks and Lunches From Home
Observations indicate that the nutritional quality of some home-packed snacks is poor, though no formal surveys have been conducted.

## 4.0 POLICY FRAMEWORK

### 4.1 Nutrition Education
#### 4.1.1 Curriculum
Content should reflect the principles of healthy eating based on the **Seychelles Dietary Guidelines (SDG)**.
#### 4.1.2 Extra Curricular Activities
Schools should include nutrition-related activities such as school vegetable gardens or cooking classes.
#### 4.1.3 Information, Education and Communication (IEC)
All materials must be in line with the SDG and target both attitude and behaviour change.

### 4.2 Food Provision
#### 4.2.1 Tuck Shops
**Guidelines for Tuck Shops:**
*   **FHNV (High Nutritional Value):** Unlimited number offered daily.
*   **FMNV (Moderate Nutritional Value):** Not more than two offered daily.
*   **FLNV (Low Nutritional Value):** No FLNV should be offered to students on school premises.

##### 4.2.1.2 Foods of High Nutritional Value (FHNV)
*   Fresh fruits and vegetables
*   Baked fruit cakes, vegetable quiches, baked meat/fish balls
*   Wholegrain cereals/bars, plain wholemeal biscuits
*   Brown bread/rolls with healthy fillings (cheese, meat, egg, peanut butter)
*   100% pure fruit juices, water, low-fat milk

##### 4.2.1.3 Foods of Moderate Nutritional Value (FMNV)
*   Local chips (potato, cassava, banana, taro)
*   Fried samoosas, fried chicken, fried fish/meat rolls
*   Popcorn (limited oil/salt), ice-cream

##### 4.2.1.4 Foods of Low Nutritional Value (FLNV)
*   Lollies, candies, chewing gum
*   Corn chips (Doritos), potato chips (Pringles)
*   Doughnuts, swiss rolls, sponge cakes
*   Carbonated sugar-sweetened beverages, high energy drinks

##### 4.2.2.2 Menu Planning by Food Group

**Table 2: Menu planning by food group**

| Food Group | Guidance | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Rice, bread, cereals, pasta, tubers | Every lunch should contain at least one portion. | Provide energy, fibre, vitamins. |
| Potatoes | Oven baked preferred over fried. Limit chips to once a week. | Reduce total fat intake. |
| Fruit and vegetables | Every lunch should contain at least one portion. | Provide vitamins and minerals. |
| Meat, fish and alternatives | Every lunch should contain at least one portion. Limit frying to $\le 2$ times/week. | Major sources of protein and iron. |
| Foods containing fat/sugar | Use should be limited; no active promotion. | Prevent excess fat, sugar, and salt. |
| Drinks | Promote bottled water; no sugary/fizzy drinks. | Protect dental health and reduce sugar. |
| Cooking fats and oils | Use poly/monounsaturated fats. NO palm oil/ghee. | Reduce saturated fat intake. |

## 5.0 COORDINATION AND MONITORING

### 5.1 The National School Nutrition Committee
Multisectoral body including Ministry of Education, Ministry of Health, Seychelles Licensing Authority, NATCOF, and SenPa. 

### 5.2 School Nutrition Action Groups (SNAG)
School-level groups consisting of teachers, students, parents, tuck shop owners, and health professionals to ensure compliance and administer evaluation tools.

---

## 6.0 IMPLEMENTATION

*   **Implementation Date:** January 2009.
*   **School Nutrition Award:** Yearly award for the school with the highest compliance score.
*   **Violations:** SNAG monitors compliance; National Committee advises the Ministry of Education for corrective actions.
