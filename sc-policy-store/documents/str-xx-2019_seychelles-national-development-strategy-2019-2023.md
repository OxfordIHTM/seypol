## MESSAGE FROM THE PRESIDENT

This document presents a strategy for the next 5 years that will help us realise the views, aspirations, dreams and hopes of our citizens. It articulates how those dreams and aspirations will be achieved. This particular document, **NDS 2019-2023**, is the first of three which will guide us through to 2033.

### Key Focus Areas
*   **Service Delivery**: Critical need to improve efficiency and relevance in both public and private spheres.
*   **Education & Health**: Preparing youth for the future and engaging citizens in safeguarding wellbeing.
*   **Climate Change**: Addressing threats and opportunities arising from environmental shifts.
*   **Discipline**: A call for more discipline and responsibility across the student population, parents, and workforce.

### Critical Challenges to Address
*   Maintaining peace and security as a cornerstone for growth.
*   Upgrading infrastructure for sustainable development.
*   Transforming the education system to meet labor market demands.
*   Embracing science, technology, and innovation (STI).
*   Fighting substance abuse and related social ills.
*   Mitigating climate change threats.
*   Realizing potential as a Large Ocean State.
*   Maintaining macroeconomic stability.

**Danny Faure**  
President of the Republic of Seychelles

## MESSAGE FROM THE MINISTER OF FINANCE, TRADE, INVESTMENT AND ECONOMIC PLANNING

(Summary of content) The Ministry is mandated to coordinate the national economic development planning process. Following reforms in 2008-2009, a 15-year Vision exercise (Vision 2033) was adopted, supported by three consecutive five-year National Development Strategies.

### Strategic Priorities
*   **Human Capital**: Building skills to allow the poorest 40% to participate in expanding economic sectors.
*   **Public Finance**: Increasing efficiency in allocation and management of resources.
*   **Blue Economy**: Leveraging the 1.4 million km² EEZ through initiatives like the "Blue Bond" and Maritime Spatial Planning (MSP).
*   **Socioeconomic Needs**: Addressing inflation, cost of living, health standards, substance abuse, and affordable housing.

**Maurice Jean Leonard Loustau-Lalanne**

## CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION

**Vision 2033**: "A resilient, responsible and prosperous nation of healthy, educated and empowered Seychellois living together in harmony with nature and engaged with the wider world."

### The Six Thematic Pillars
1.  **Good Governance**: Focus on transparency, accountability, and rule of law.
2.  **People at the Centre of Development**: Focus on participation, inclusivity, and human capital.
3.  **Social Cohesion**: Promoting national unity, trust, and spiritual wellbeing.
4.  **Innovative Economy**: Utilizing STI and embracing the fourth industrial revolution.
5.  **Economic Transformation**: Shifting towards high-productivity activities and the Blue Economy.
6.  **Environmental Sustainability and Resilience**: Balancing development with nature conservation.

### The "3 Ps" of Development
*   **Productivity**: Boosting efficiency of existing resources.
*   **Participation**: Equipping graduates and the vulnerable to enter the workforce.
*   **Performance**: Creating an agile, efficient public sector that does not crowd out the private sector.

### Scenarios for the Future
*   **Garden of Eden**: The desired outcome; resilient, transformed, and prosperous.
*   **Status Quo**: Non-transformational; vulnerable to economic meltdown.
*   **Time-Bomb**: Transformational but non-resilient; leads to collapse.
*   **Exodus**: The worst-case scenario; failed state with massive emigration.

### 1.1 Seychelles Context and Historical Development
Seychelles is a large ocean state with an EEZ of 1.4 million km². It gained independence in 1976 and transitioned to a pluralist democratic system in 1993. The economy has shifted from plantation-based activities to a high-end tourism and fisheries model.

### 1.2 National Development: Challenges
*   **High-Income Status**: Classified as high-income since 2015, but with significant internal inequality.
*   **Public Sector Efficiency**: Need for better monitoring, evaluation (PM&E), and a robust statistical system.
*   **Health**: Rise in Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs) and an ageing population.
*   **Education**: A gap exists between current educational outcomes and the skills required for a high-productivity economy.

## CHAPTER 2: BROAD STRATEGY FOR DEVELOPMENT

### Pillar 1: Good Governance
**Goal 1**: A transparent, accountable and citizen-centric government.
**Goal 2**: A secure and fair state where the rule of law is upheld.

### Pillar 2: People at the Centre of Development
**Goal 1**: Attainment of the highest level of health and wellbeing.
**Goal 2**: Educated, empowered and responsible citizens.
**Goal 3**: A productive and skilled labour force.

### Pillar 3: Social Cohesion
**Goal 1**: Happy, healthy people living in a harmonious and peaceful community.
**Goal 2**: Ensuring cultural sustainability for the wellbeing of all.

### Pillar 4: Innovative Economy
**Goal 1**: An enabling environment for research and development.
**Goal 2**: A knowledge-based, innovation-oriented economy.

### Pillar 5: Economic Transformation
**Goal 1**: A capable government able to facilitate socioeconomic transformation.
**Goal 2**: Strengthening of existing economic sectors for higher returns.

### Pillar 6: Environmental Sustainability and Resilience
**Goal 1**: A global leader in environmental conservation and climate change resilience.
**Goal 2**: Reduce vulnerability to natural disasters via land use planning.
**Goal 3**: Strengthening of environmental governance and management systems.

## CHAPTER 3: IMPLEMENTATION

Success depends on political will, ownership, and coordinated implementation. Key elements include:
*   **Institutional Arrangements**: Coordination through the National Working Group (NWG).
*   **Financing**: Mobilizing domestic resources and exploring PPPs.
*   **Monitoring**: Using Results-Based Management (RBM) and an e-RBM dashboard.
*   **Public Sector Reform**: Focus on meritocracy and efficiency.

---

## CHAPTER 4: MACROECONOMIC AND FISCAL CONSISTENCY

*   **Growth**: GDP growth expected to average around 3.5%.
*   **Fiscal Position**: Target primary surplus of 2.5% of GDP to reduce public debt to sustainable levels (target 50% by 2021).
*   **Debt Management**: Focus on rolling over short-term treasury bills and extending the maturity of domestic debt.

---

## CHAPTER 5: FINANCING

*   **Resource Envelope**: Estimated at SCR 65 billion for the NDS period (SCR 47bn domestic, SCR 18bn international).
*   **Expenditure**: Projected primary expenditure at approx. SCR 45 billion.
*   **Mobilization**: Focus on tax enforcement, electronic payments, and attracting FDI/venture capital.
