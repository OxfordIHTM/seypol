# NATIONAL FOOD AND NUTRITION SECURITY POLICY (NFNSP)

**June 2013**

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### 1. INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND

#### 1.1 Food and Nutritional Security Status

1. Food and nutritional security remains one of the most important pillars to sustainable development and economic growth in all countries and Seychelles is no exception. Government therefore commits itself to ensure a consistent supply of nutritious, affordable and safe foods for all Seychellois.

2. The Republic of Seychelles is a “Small Island Developing State”, all of which share several unique and common characteristics. All are vulnerable to economic, social and environmental challenges. These arise from the interplay of their smallness, remoteness, geographical dispersion, vulnerability to natural disasters, the fragility of their ecosystems, constraints on transport and communication, isolation from markets, vulnerability to exogenous economic and financial shocks, soaring food prices on international markets, a limited internal market, lack of natural resources, growing populations and changing consumption patterns, limited fresh water supplies, heavy dependence on food imports to meet domestic needs and limited commodities, reliance on only one or two economic pillars, depletion of nonrenewable resources, slow progress in developing and deploying science and technology to address development challenges, limited ability to reap the benefits of economies of scale and migration of experienced and skilled professionals.

3. Seychelles is classified as an upper middle income country (World Bank List of Economies 2012), with a GDP of US$ 1.01 billion and a per capita GDP of US $25 000 (2011), yet 17% of Seychellois live below the national poverty line which in Seychelles is estimated as 13,554 Seychelles rupees per adult equivalent per year.

> According to the FAO definition, food security is understood to “exists when all people, at all times, have physical, social and economic access to sufficient, safe and nutritious food which meets their dietary needs and food preferences for an active and healthy life. Household food security is the application of this concept to the family level, with individuals within households as the focus of concern” (World Food Summit 1996).

> “Nutrition security is when food security is combined with education, a sanitary environment, adequate health services and proper care and feeding practices to ensure a healthy life for all household members”. (UNSCN 6th Report on the World Nutrition Situation).

4. Seychelles has a population of approximately 90 000 people (mid 2011). Food and nutritional security in the Seychelles is a growing concern due to international factors, volatility in food prices, and high dependence on food importation.

5. **Agricultural Production Trends (2011-2012):**
    - **Food Imports (2011):** ~$87.79 million
    - **Food Exports (2011):** ~$40.88 million (91% fish/fish products)
    - **Local Vegetables/Fruits:** Decreased from 65% (1995) to 50% (2012)
    - **Local Poultry:** Fell from 80% (2008) to 10% (2012)

6. The present focus is how to achieve more and to sustain the capacity and the systems to eradicate poverty and to ensure access to nutritious food for all.

7. The universal right to food includes sufficient intake of calories and the right to nutritious food that guarantees health, growth and development at all stages of life.

8. Changing lifestyles and consumption of imported highly processed foods have increased non-communicable diseases. Prevalence of overweight (including obesity) increased from 29% to 52% in men and 50% to 67% in women between 1989 and 2004.

9. In 2004, cardio-vascular complications accounted for 40% of all deaths in Seychelles; diabetes and hypertension rates were 14% and 40% respectively.

10. The country is becoming nutritionally insecure, posing a threat to sustainable social and economic development.

11. Government recognizes the risks of hunger and malnutrition on productivity, children’s learning abilities, and social harmony.

12. Government is mindful of risks including international food price volatility, natural disasters, and piracy. While food imports remain essential, the goal is to revitalize the local agricultural sector.

#### 1.2 Historical and Emerging Issues for Food and Nutrition Security

13. Key issues from the 2008-09 global food crisis:
    - Globalisation of food systems sensitive to external decisions.
    - Increased frequency of adverse weather and climate change.
    - Growing populations and middle class.
    - Intra-regional trade opportunities.
    - Pressure on land, water, and fisheries resources.
    - Debates on national food sovereignty.

14. Government will continually align policies to ensure food and nutritional security.

15. Specific in-country trends:
    - Responsibility cuts across various Ministries and the private sector.
    - Need for efficiency in the use of national resources.
    - **Sustainable Development Strategy Focus Areas:**
        - a) Climate change, renewable energy and water
        - b) Human resource development
        - c) Economic infrastructure (transport and ICT)
        - d) Food security, Trade and Diversification
        - e) Development of National Statistics

16. The economic importance of the agricultural sector has diminished, while fisheries remain a main pillar.

17. Agriculture has been stigmatized. There is a need to remodel the perception of agriculture to attract youth and improve financial support mechanisms.

18. Land tenure is a major constraint. Leases should ideally be for a minimum of 40 years to encourage investment.

#### 1.3 Past and Current Food Security and Nutrition Related Policies

19. The National Sustainable Development Strategy influences efforts to realise food and nutrition security.

20. **Relevant Policies/Documents:**
    - Agricultural Development Strategy (2007-2011)
    - Food Security Strategy 2008-2011
    - Seychelles Biosecurity Policy (draft)
    - National Medium Term priority Framework (FAO support)
    - National School Nutrition Policy
    - Fisheries Policy (2005, being revised)
    - Seychelles Dietary Guidelines 2008
    - Hospital infant Feeding Policy (draft)

21. Designated national and parastatal institutions will be strengthened to execute the new policy.

22. The private sector plays a pivotal role in agri- and fisheries processing.

23. Agricultural land decreased from 600 ha (2007) to 350 ha (2012). New policies aim to reverse this trend.

#### 1.4 Need for a National Food and Nutrition Security Policy

24. The NFNSP is designed to align capacity and ensure resilience against internal and external shocks.

25. 72% of food consumed in 2011 was imported; 60% of Seychellois are overweight (25% obese).

26. **Policy Leadership Goals:**
    - Consistent program and investment interventions.
    - Coordination and coherence in strategies.
    - Strengthening internal resilience to shocks.
    - Strengthening accountability and inclusive decision making.

27. Integral part of the "Health for All" strategy (Government/WHO).

28. Guiding instrument to translate constitutional obligations to the right to food.

29. Complements rather than replaces specific sectoral policies.

30. High-level Ministerial Food Security retreat (Nov 2012) committed to the revitalization of agriculture.

#### 1.5 Context of the NFNSP

31. Multi-sectoral approach considering various Acts, policies, and strategies.

32. The Constitution guarantees the right to food, clothing, shelter, education, health, and a dignified existence.

33. Focus on repositioning agriculture, fisheries, and aquaculture.

34. Commitment to international agreements (World Food Summit 1996, MDGs, CAADP of NEPAD).

35. Consistent with the national vision for sustainable development.

36. Replaces the National Food Security Policy of 2008.

#### 1.6 Overall Goal and Objectives of the NFNSP

37. **Goal:** To guarantee the right to safe, healthy and adequate food at all times and to satisfy the nutritional needs for optimal health for all persons living in Seychelles.

38. **Specific Aims:**
    - Ensure food security via local production and balanced imports.
    - Optimize nutritional status and wellbeing.
    - Strengthen institutional resilience and scientific knowledge base.

39. Other objectives include reducing foreign exchange leakage, enhancing farm income, and creating jobs.

#### 1.7 Scope of the NFNSP

40. Broad scope covering correlations between food/nutrition, economic growth, and poverty eradication.

41. Provides a basis for seeking resources and developing operational strategies.

42. Responsive framework reviewed periodically against globalization and demographic changes.

43. **Key issues addressed:**
    - Agriculture revitalization and trade incentives.
    - Diversification of fisheries.
    - Public awareness of healthy eating.
    - Food safety nets.
    - Biosecurity and food safety.
    - Strategic food reserves.

### 2. SECURING FOOD AVAILABILITY AND ACCESSIBILITY

#### 2.1 Securing National Sovereignty in Food and Nutritional Needs

44. Definition of Food Sovereignty: *“the right of peoples to healthy and culturally appropriate food produced through ecologically sound and sustainable methods and their right to define their own food and agriculture systems”*.

45. **Principles of Food Sovereignty:**
    - **Focuses on Food for People:** Right to healthy food at the center of policy.
    - **Values Food Providers:** Respects rights of farmers and fishers.
    - **Localises Food Systems:** Brings providers and consumers closer.
    - **Puts Control Locally:** Local control over land, water, and seeds.
    - **Builds Knowledge and Skills:** Develops local research and skills.
    - **Works with Nature:** Uses agro-ecological production methods.

#### 2.2 Strengthening Agriculture and Fisheries

46. Agriculture and Fisheries are the cornerstones of food security.

47. Commitment to CAADP Compact for resource allocation across the value chain.

48. Focus on sustainable use of land, water, and forests to prevent resource degradation.

49. Strategy involves modernizing crop/livestock sectors, developing mariculture, and sustainable fisheries management.

##### 2.2.1 Promoting the Agricultural Value Chain

50. **Resolutions to Challenges:**
    - **Trade Policy:** New policy favors local agriculture to reduce import dependence.
    - **Land Allocation:** State Land Bill 2012 to protect and optimize agricultural land.
    - **Water Security:** Ensure accessible and affordable water for producers.
    - **Climate Change:** Mainstream adaptation and "climate smart" practices.
    - **Inputs:** Facilitate access to genetic stock, seed, and fertilizers.
    - **Human Capacity:** Revitalize extension services and attract youth via SAA.
    - **Collaboration:** Seek international research partnerships to intensify production.
    - **Finance:** Develop preferential loan schemes for farmers.
    - **Post-Harvest:** Support private sector initiatives for value-added processing.
    - **Market Info:** Establish early warning systems and market information flow.

51. CAADP implementation process will provide the framework for investment and reform.

52. Identification of strategic commodities for competitive local production.

53. **Local Production Targets:**
    - **Eggs/Poultry:** Aim for 100% local production.
    - **Fresh Pork:** Aim for 100% local production.
    - **Fruits/Vegetables:** Aim for minimum 80% local production.

54. Support for high-tech intensive systems (greenhouses, fertigation).

55. Shift towards intensive agriculture; financial risk sharing with the public sector.

56. Establishing a central wholesale market to improve local product marketing.

##### 2.2.2 Home Gardens and Peri-Urban Farming

57. Support for smallholder farming to contribute to nutrition and income.

58. Revitalize radio/video educational programs on climate smart agriculture via SAA and SBC.

##### 2.2.3 Enhancing the Fisheries Value Chain

59. Fish provides 35-40% of all protein consumed in Seychelles (ca. 65kg per capita/year).

60. Artisanal fisheries provide the bulk of fish consumed locally.

61. **Crisis:** Artisanal catch dropped from 4777 to 2595 tons (2008-2010), an 84% drop. Most stocks are fully or over-exploited.

62. **Resolutions:**
    - **Research:** Support training in marine and mathematical sciences at SFA.
    - **Stock Recovery:** Implement input/output control measures for demersal stocks.
    - **Diversification:** Modernize semi-industrial fishery to target deep-water resources.
    - **Training:** Use Maritime Training Centre to attract young fishers.
    - **Sanitation:** Improve points of sale (concrete tables, drainage, shade).

#### 2.3 Mariculture: Opportunities for a New Industrial Sector

63. Develop high-technology cage culture and ranching of marine species to create jobs and increase local fish supply.

#### 2.4 Food Imports

64. Imports remain a strategy but must align with food sovereignty and risk protection.

65. 72% of food is imported; this high-risk situation is unsustainable.

66. Use of import duties (e.g., 15% on Poultry, Pork, Goats) to mitigate VAT exemptions and protect local farmers.

67. Screening of imported foods for nutritional value and alignment with national health policies.

#### 2.5 Access to Food

##### 2.5.1 Job Creation and Poverty Alleviation

68. Investment in agriculture/aquaculture creates direct employment.

69. Government will monitor basic food prices in relation to the minimum wage to ensure affordability.

70. Linkage to the Medium Term National Development Strategy to reduce poverty.

##### 2.5.2 Access for Vulnerable Sections

71. Vulnerable groups: pregnant women, children under 5, school children, and the elderly.

72. Review of safety nets and health support, including nutrition for those with chronic conditions (HIV/Aids, diabetes, cancer).

### 3. NUTRITION SECURITY FOR THE PEOPLE OF SEYCHELLES

#### 3.1 Understanding and Providing for Nutritional Security

73. 18% of households cannot meet basic caloric requirements, while over half the population is overweight.

74. Focus on optimum health through diet and local production.

75. Hunger/malnutrition is viewed as a barrier to sustainable development.

#### 3.2 Nutrition and Health Care

76. Focus on the link between balanced diets and reduced disease susceptibility.

77. **Lifecycle Approach:** Pre-conception/newborn $\rightarrow$ Early childhood $\rightarrow$ Late childhood $\rightarrow$ Adolescence $\rightarrow$ Adulthood $\rightarrow$ Elderly.

78. **NFNSP Strives to:**
    - Improve women’s nutrition.
    - Support exclusive breastfeeding.
    - Promote nutrition for school children/adolescents.
    - Promote healthy lifestyles for the general population.
    - Improve care for the elderly and sick.

79. **Interventions:**
    - Promote local healthy food businesses.
    - Upgrade food labeling regulations.
    - Monitoring maternal nutritional status.
    - National communication strategy on infant feeding.
    - Implementation of National School Nutrition Policy.

80. Action plans to combat NCDs (obesity, heart disease, diabetes) through healthy eating.

#### 3.3 Education and Public Awareness

##### 3.3.1 Nutritional Education in Schools

81-83. Schools and daycares are primary platforms for influencing eating patterns and behaviors.

##### 3.3.2 Nutrition and Public Awareness

84. Multimedia campaigns to influence behavior and counter advertising of low-nutritional value foods.

85. Integrated holistic approach with data management across sectoral ministries.

##### 3.3.3 Nutrition and Local Cultures

86-88. Promote indigenous fruits/vegetables (bread fruit, starfruit, bananas) and hold local food expos.

### 4. FOOD SAFETY, STANDARDS AND QUALITY CONTROL

#### 4.1 Food Safety and Public Health

89. Goal: Reflect the clean environment of Seychelles in the food produced/consumed.

90. **Legal Framework:** Food Act 1987, Plant Protection Act 1996, Pesticide Control Act 1996, etc.

91. Strengthen the Seychelles Bureau of Standards (SBS) to enhance food quality awareness.

92. Food business operators hold primary responsibility for safety; Government sets minimum standards.

93. Biosecurity Policy addresses contaminants (pesticides, antibiotics, radioactives).

94. Harmonization of laboratory services to enforce national food standards.

#### 4.2 Quality Management and Certification Services

95. SBS/Fish Inspection Unit administer Codex Alimentarius and SADC guidelines. Focus on ISO 9001 certification and HACCP audits.

96. Risk-based inspection systems to counter international dumping.

#### 4.3 Enhanced Food Management and Control Systems

97. Establishment of a **Food Safety Management Committee** to verify standards and liaise with SADC.

98. Current gaps: Lack of sanitary regulations for frozen/chilled poultry imports and limited testing capacity.

99-100. Formulation of new national standards for GMOs, hormonal and anti-biotic residues, utilizing EU regulations as guidelines.

101. Implementation of a full Food Safety Management System including traceability and crisis management.

#### 4.4 Promoting Quality Practices along the Value Chain

102. Addressing post-harvest losses and hygiene to improve local produce's entry into the hospitality sector.

103. **Goals:**
    - Promote safe production practices.
    - Harmonize regulatory frameworks.
    - Improve storage to control disease outbreaks.

104. Adherence to Best Manufacturing Practices (BMPs) for animal feed.

105. Ensuring adequate supply of safe, clean water for registered producers.

106. Application of the **precautionary principle** in cases of scientific uncertainty regarding health risks.

### 5. ALIGNMENT AND HARMONISATION WITH REGIONAL/INTERNATIONAL POLICIES

107-108. Seeking collaboration with international partners for technical assistance and niche market development.

109-110. Re-aligning the Public Health Act, Food Act, and SBS Act with WTO and SADC standards.

111. Policy review scheduled every 5 years.

112. Utilizing RECs (SADC, IOC, COMESA) to identify competitive economic sectors.

113. Adherence to international treaties (CBD, IOTC, FAO Code of Conduct for Responsible Fisheries) to ensure sustainability.

### 6. PREPAREDNESS AND MANAGING RISK AND UNCERTAINTY

#### 6.1 Food and Nutrition Security Information

114-115. Mechanisms for timely data collection to manage integrated programs.

##### 6.1.1 National Data and Information Systems

116. Importance of market information for farmer investment and production planning.

117. **Statistics Department Data Collection:**
    - Production, trade, and consumption statistics.
    - Mapping of food chain operators.
    - Production technologies and practices.
    - Population nutrition surveys.

##### 6.1.2 Research and Analytical Capacity

118-121. Strengthening scientific capacity at SAA, SFA, and SBS to cope with climate change and resource management.

#### 6.2 Strategic Food Reserves and Emergency Management

122-123. Seychelles currently lacks a dedicated strategic food reserve (STC holds some specific items).

124-126. Risk managed under the National Disaster Act and Hyogo Framework for Action 2005-2015.

127-128. Revision of food security management plans regarding reserves and price volatility.

#### 6.3 Insurance and Risk Management

129-130. Proposal to establish joint crop and livestock insurance (based on Rwanda's model) to compensate producers for losses.

#### 6.4 Food Security and Climate Change

131. Coping mechanisms: Early warning weather/drought information and science-led adaptation for livestock.

132. Integration of climate change mitigation into the Agricultural Policy.

#### 6.5 The Role of Gender

133. Recognizing women's key role in breastfeeding and meal preparation to enhance household nutrition security.

### 7. INSTITUTIONAL AND LEGAL FRAMEWORK

#### 7.1 Institutional and Legal Framework for Implementation

134-135. Success depends on partnership between state, producers, importers, and civil society.

136. **New Institutional Structures:**
    - National Food and Nutrition Security Steering Committee (NFNSSC).
    - Joint operations among lead ministries.
    - District committees on Mahe, Praslin, and La Digue.

137-140. Mainstreaming NFNSP into all institutional frameworks; amending laws as gaps appear during implementation.

##### 7.1.1 The National Food Security Steering Committee

141-142. The NFNSSC will coordinate all stakeholders, chaired by the PS in the Ministry of Natural Resources and Industry.

143. **Mandate of the Committee:**
    - Multi-sectoral coordination.
    - Integrate emergency and chronic food insecurity responses.
    - Oversee national programs and projects.
    - Monitor NFNSP implementation (two-way information flow).
    - Advise government on international commitments.
    - Manage a databank on interventions.
    - Prepare quarterly and annual reports for Parliament.

#### 7.2.1 Monitoring, Evaluation and Review

144-145. Establishment of an independent M&E system to ensure transparency and develop measurable targets.

146. Policy review every 5 years.

#### 7.3 Financing

147-148. Financial resources from Government and Development Partners. Emphasis on involving the private sector and seeking assistance for backyard farming.
