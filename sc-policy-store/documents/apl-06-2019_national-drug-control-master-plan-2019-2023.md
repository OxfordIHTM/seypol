# FOREWORD BY THE PRESIDENT

I am pleased to present the **National Drug Control Master Plan for 2019-2023**. This fourth National Strategy on drugs has been developed with the technical support of the Agency for Social Change and the United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC) and the Agency's local strategic partners. This Master Plan is in line with the Government policy of promoting an integrated, holistic approach, taking into account the complex nature of drug-related interventions, supported by evidence-based research and established best practices.

While it is widely accepted that for some drug-dependent persons, long-term residential users can be more effective than puncturements, my government firmly believes that persons can be helped by providing more comprehensive and integrated care. This Master Plan recognises the need for supply and demand reduction strategies to complement each other.

Although progress has been made in lowering rates of substance abuse in Seychelles, the use of illicit drugs continues to be a major challenge, affecting individuals, families and communities. Substance abuse involving drugs, alcohol, or both, is exacerbated by various factors, including:

* Poverty
* Unemployment
* Social exclusion
* Financial problems
* Loss of productivity
* Poor academic performance
* Health issues
* Domestic violence
* Child abuse
* Crime

Moreover, both social attitudes and legal responses to drug use and drug users have evolved over time. Drug abuse remains one of the most complex public health issues facing Seychelles. Indeed, it is sad to say that, with the increasing volume of drugs coming into the country, drug abuse has become an existential threat to our small Island State. To note that our youths are the ones being largely affected by this scourge.
In a nutshell, drug control for Seychelles means creating a balance between **supply reduction activities**, such as seizures and arrests and **demand reduction** bringing preventive education and treatment services to the forefront.

Our strategy is to reduce drug misuse, and substance, such as reducing overdoses, psychiatric problems and the increased risk of HIV and hepatitis. We believe that an integrated approach to treatment, rehabilitation, reintegration, and health allows us to engender as key elements in the strategy to reduce drug demand along with strategies for supply reduction.

This is in accordance to:
* The Ministry of Health's National Strategic Plan
* The Drug Abuse and Rehabilitation Act 2017
* The 2016 United Nations General Session Special Session (UNGSESS) on the World Drug Problem Outcome Document

Substance abuse is multi faceted and requires system-wide reform. It is my belief, that as we work to implement the **National Drug Control Master Plan 2019-2023**, we can end the stigma and discrimination in the facility and drug side community with each sector of our community playing its role.

The Agency for Prevention of Drug Abuse and Rehabilitation (APDAR) and its strategy to implement the Master Plan for Seychelles will require substantial support in the planning and drafting of this National Drug Control Master Plan.

Together, let us strive to realise our vision of a drug safe Seychelles so that more emphasis can be put on improving our quality of life.

# Executive summary

The National Drug Control Master Plan (NDCMP) 2019-2023 is an operational planning tool that provides a coherent framework for comprehensive national drug control in Seychelles. It incorporates the existing national drug control efforts and adds new initiatives based on the legal obligations. Its development has been inspired by the 2016 United Nations General Assembly Special Session (UNGASS) on the World Drug Problem and the UN Convention against Illicit Traffic in Narcotic and Psychotropic Substances. It is also aligned with the existing national policies, including the emending AIDS Policy, Internationalization and the 2030 Agenda for sustainable development and its Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). It is aligned with the relevant national strategies including the Vision 2030 and the National AIDS Strategy.

The overall aim of the NDCMP is to improve the wellbeing and safety of the individuals and population of Seychelles.

Deployed to respond to continuous increase in the number of people who use heroin, these interventions are based on the principle that since HIV and Hepatitis C infections it has a strong emphasis on drug prevention and harm reduction.

Its main goal is to ensure a coordinated and comprehensive approach to national drug control services.

### Strategic Areas

The NDCMP has four strategic areas:

**Strategic area 1: Health and Human Development**
Comprises five themes:
1. **Drug prevention**, with a special emphasis on youth.
2. **Drug treatment**, deployed aimed at increasing the access to evidence-based treatment.
3. **Harm reduction** to reduce the risk of HIV and Hepatitis C infections among users to ensure they can be reintegrated in society and contribute to its economic development.
4. **Overall burden reduction**, aimed at reducing the overall burden of drug use.
5. **Comprehensive package** for HIV/AIDS prevention and preventing deaths by overdose. Additionally, this area relates to psychiatric care, focusing on solutions for medical problems, the scope of extensive treatment, identify bugs, remedies and interventions.

**Strategic area 2: Supply Reduction**
Includes four themes:
1. **Intelligence-led law enforcement**, focusing on the identification of drug trafficking routes and taking appropriate related to drug trafficking.
2. **Inter-agency links**, focusing on links with other organisms concerned to increase the exchange of information between law enforcement agencies and other relevant actors.
3. **Identification of New Psycho Active Substances (NPS)** and pollutants external to the country.
4. **Capacity enhancement** of law enforcement on risks. This also focuses on strengthening collaboration on issues related to drug trafficking at regional and international level.

**Strategic area 3: Cross Cutting Area on Human Rights, Gender, Women, Communities and Perspective of Vulnerable Groups**
Focuses on social justice. Its implementation will ensure that the programmes are in line with national human rights instruments, including the Convention on the Rights of the Child. The objectives are to:
* Ensure a coordinated approach to prevent and treat drug use.
* Ensure people in common access health services, including harm reduction services and drug prevention and treatment.
* Implement policies and strategies to make them more efficient.
* Align other relevant laws and develop more effective means, alternatives to incarceration, and rehabilitation.

**Strategic area 4: Strategic Information and Coordination**
Contains three themes. The first theme aims at strengthening the capacity to collect and use strategic information to allow the
monitoring of trends and developments in drug use, availability as well as health and social consequences. The second theme, **monitoring and evaluation**, is critical to ensure the NDCMP is implemented effectively. This theme concentrates on the development, use and dissemination of, and communication channels between the different actors involved in the response to ensure coherence of the monitoring process and effective information sharing.

The NDCMP 2019-2023 includes a strong monitoring and evaluation framework to allow for proper monitoring. In addition, it makes provision for an in-depth mid-term evaluation and an end of cycle evaluation.

The costed NDCMP is the most critical/essential tool to guide the effective implementation of the plan.

The NDCMP amounts to a total of **SR 314,036,490.70** of which **SR 254,465,558.20** will be from the Seychelles Government consolidated funds and **SR 59,570,932.50** grant.

# 1. Background and rationale of the National Drug Control Master Plan (2019-2023)

To respond effectively to the complex and changing situation of illicit drug production, use, trafficking and related health consequences, it is necessary to have a comprehensive and coordinated approach. Such an approach should include monitoring systems to monitor the situation and detect new trends. It also needs international cooperation, since illicit drug trafficking is a global problem. The Master Plan provides a framework for the national strategy, including where possible joint priorities.

The National Drug Control Master Plan (NDCMP) cannot be separated from the international context, the international drug control system and the international health system. The national strategy should be based on the need to protect public health and development, as well as international framework for drug control. Similarly, the need to protect human health and human rights at the centre of the national and international drug policy has been further stressed in the UNODC's works on the World Drug Problem outcome Documents' adopted in April 2016.

The 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development and its 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) adopted in September 2015 provide a further framework for the national drug control strategy. These goals include health, inequalities, gender, rules of law, partnership and development. More specifically related to drug use, the **SDG 3.5** aims to strengthen the prevention and treatment of substance abuse, including narcotic drug use and strengthen health coverage and strengthening prevention and treatment of substance use. The element of limiting drug demand, including the problem of PWID, were reiterated in the 2016 High Level Meeting on Ending AIDS Policy Declaration.

The African Union (AU) is currently engaged in consultations to develop new criteria on drug control an international drug control system that is more balanced and comprehensive. The African Union (AU) has through the Commission (AU) Specialised Technical Committee on Health, Population, and Protection, coordinated in 2017 the drafting of the AU Draft Strategy on Drug Control. The strategy is based on four strategic pillars:

*   **Pillar one (1):** Security and fight against terrorism and drug trafficking, acknowledging the threat of drug trafficking to security and stability of the AU Member States.
*   **Pillar two (2):** Health, emphasizing the need for a and a plan of action 2017-2019, in line with the 2016 High Level Meeting on the Prohibition declaration.

The strategy identifies the need for a coordinated approach to drug control, including prevention, treatment and rehabilitation, and challenges, finally, the Republic of Seychelles signed in 1996 the Southern African Development Community (SADC) Protocol on Combating Illicit Drugs.

Seychelles has always been conscious of the threats posed by drugs and has developed and implemented several NDCMS:

*   **The first NDCMP 2002-2006:** Focused on drug control preventing production-on the drug activities, fight on the illicit drug trafficking and drug use.
*   **The second NDCMP 2008-2012:** Focused on the Anti-Drug and Marine crime (ADMC) and Guidelines on ESS, on monitoring strategist and longer international links. It targeted the use, abuse, misuse, distribution and trafficking in all forms of illicit drugs, with a focus on the prevention of drug use, treatment and rehabilitation of drug users, and the reduction of use, abuse and trafficking in the country.
*   **The third NDCMP 2013-2018:** Designed to address the health consequences of a dramatic increase in the use of heroin, including its injection, and related increase in HIV and Hepatitis C virus (HCV) infections. It focused on four areas for action: prevention, treatment, law enforcement and interventions.
national coordination. It gave prominence to rehabilitation and social reintegration of people dependent on drugs and recognised the need to develop harm reduction programmes in response to the drug epidemic in the Republic of Seychelles.

The DNCP 2023 - 2025 has been expanded to be developed for the further in hypertensive in the prevalence of drugs, including injecting drug use, and in the number of new HIV and Hepatitis C prevalence.

### 1.1. Aim, goal and main objectives and guiding principles

The DNCP 2023 - 2025 is aligned to the UNGASS 2016 to be developed to strengthen the coordination, cooperation and engagement of all stakeholders for an effective and ambitious health driven response to the drugs problem in Seychelles. It recognizes the need to address the increasing prevalence of drug use, especially injecting drug use, and increased prevalence of HIV and Hepatitis C in the Republic of Seychelles. It also recognizes the need to address the issues of gender, and the cohesion of society including on the prism of inclusion. The DNCP 2023 - 2025 builds on the principles of the 2016 UNGASS Outcome Document and adapts its objectives.

#### 1.1.1. Aim, goal and main objectives

**The over aim of the DNCP is to improve the wellbeing, safety and security of the individual, family and community in Seychelles.**

**Its main goal is to ensure a coordinated and comprehensive approach to national drug control in Seychelles.**

**The overarching objectives of the DNCP are sets:**
* To provide national coordination for identifying and addressing drug related issues.
* To promote participatory processes that facilitate stakeholders engagement and involvement in identifying and addressing drug related issues.
* To foster better understanding between the key national partners to ensure proper coordination in the prevention, management and rehabilitation of drug related issues.
* To develop evidence based policies and strategies.
* To support nationally consistent, drug related legislative and regulatory responses.

#### 1.1.2. Guiding principles

The DNCP 2023 - 2025 is based on four basic principles:
* Effective coordination and communication between all engaged stakeholders in its implementation, monitoring, evaluation and review.
* Evidence based policies and interventions that have been scientifically evaluated to be effective as per international standards and norms.
* Member state ownership of policies that have been developed and implemented in response to the needs based on strategic information.
* Monitoring and evaluation of policies to ensure for human rights and gender equality principles.

The DNCP 2019 - 2023 is aligned to the three international drug control conventions, the UNGASS 2016 outcome document, Agenda 2030 (SDGs) and 2016 HLM on Ending AIDS political declaration including the UNGASS 2016 Outcome Document and the UN Convention against Transnational Organized Crime (UNTOC) (2000) and the UN Convention against Corruption (UNCAC) (2003) and the (2017) and SADC Protocol on Combating Illicit Drugs (1996) as well as national related policies and strategies.
## 1.2 Formulation process

The NDCMP 2019 - 2023 was developed through a multistake-holders consultative process ensuring the contribution of government and non-governmental organisations — organisations including representative of PAWS and existing stakeholders. The process was guided by the National Disaster Management Act (NDMA) to spearhead the first draft of the document. A three-day consultation to review the situation, relevant sectoral policies and strategies, and the current work and gaps in disaster management was conducted. Based on the discussions and identified priorities, a first draft was developed and reviewed in a two-day workshop with the National Disaster Risk Management Committee (NDRMC) and relevant stakeholders (see annex 3). The process was coordinated by APFAR while UNDP provided technical and financial support.

The development of the 2019 - 2023 NDCMP was supported by an extensive literature review and an analysis of the existing disaster management framework in Seychelles. This process was validated by the participants in the first consultation.

The final draft of the NDCMP 2019 - 2023 was disseminated for electronic consultation among the stakeholders.

### 1.2.1 NDCMP 2014-2018 SWOT analysis

The NDCMP 2014-2018 was reviewed by the stakeholders, but participants in the first consultation meeting noted limitations learnt from its implementation (see table in annex 3). The elements of information on lessons learnt are mentioned in the introductory statement of each analysis section.

The detailed analysis of the 2014-2018 NDCMP is presented in the table in annex 3. The strengths of the current recognized element is the establishment of law by a coordination body, the polity and its relevant agencies. The weaknesses are the lack of a coordinated institutional framework, a lack of capacity the regulatory framework and limited funding in all areas of work and the lack of clear policy support. Opportunities were identified in the continued support from the international community. The need to increase engagement of international organisations was recognized as important opportunities to bundle its efforts and resources with the national priorities.

The availability of counselling programmes, aftercare services, increased number of serviced, improved coordination of training, staff qualification, and accessibility of services are also identified as key challenges. The lack of adequate funding for these developments have occurred in treatment, harm reduction and social support.

Among issues related to the reconsideration of the strategy, we have identified the need for NPP and OST, ensuring of stable settings for care and other supportive surroundings, increase coverage of immunizations and increase the availability of medicines for the treatment of HIV/AIDS and Tuberculosis. The review of the legislation is also a need to establish programmes that are responsive for the needs of specific populations including the aged, children, young people and the prisoners within a decent environment.

### Theme 1: Prevention
The overall objective of the intervention use is to avoid or delay the onset of drug use.

Under the theme prevention, three main objectives have been identified.

**The first objective** is to develop and establish prevention programmes to enhance protective factors and reduce risk factors. The overall objective is based on the evidence-based prevention framework and response system (outcome 1). Under this objective it is planned to map existing initiatives and stakeholders and to develop a national prevention strategy. The strategy will be developed based on the latest international norms and standards. Capacity of professionals to deliver evidence-based prevention interventions will be strengthened through a comprehensive training programme.

**The second objective** is to target high-risk groups with tailored prevention programmes using a systematic substance use (outcome 2) through the integration of drug prevention and health promotion programmes strengthening the capacity of health workers to identify and provide early intervention. The objective is based on prevention framework. The following have been identified as key to the success of based prevention interventions:

* The provision of an enabling environment for the implementation of prevention
* The establishment of appropriate support services for the supports and the preventions
* For the promotion a conducive environment for the prevention of drug use

**The third objective** is to promote supportive environment in the communities and in the families. The initiative makes provision for the implementation of environmental modifications to the environments based and layout of the physical environment to reduce risk and the operational setting (outcome 3). It also aims at introducing family-based preventions (outcome 4).
The aim is to ensure that 80% of people leaving treatment facilities are re-integrated in community. It will entail strong advocacy to remove stigmatisation, discrimination and dominant policy requiring the employment of employee in poverty own in capacity as part of CSR.

#### Theme 4: Harm Reduction

A strong harm reduction programme is essential to halt the increase of new HIV and hepatitis infections among PWID. The National Drug Control Strategy (NDCS) incorporates harm reduction treatment and care for PWID are available in the community, including vaccination for the prevention of hepatitis B and C, opioid substitution therapy (OST), and medication-assisted treatment (MAT) were relatively low. To remedy this situation, the NDCMP plans to increase access to harm reduction services (syringe and needle exchange, OST, MAT) for PWID. The NDCMP will also seek to create a supportive support from policy makers and decision makers. Low awareness of elements and knowledge on harm reduction among health professionals and staff within other relevant departments such as police and justice will be addressed through capacity building.

The overall goal of the harm reduction programme is to reduce negative consequences, reduce born related illnesses, and prevent deaths.

It has two objectives under the NDCMP (Strategic Direction 1):
1. To create a conducive environment for the implementation, strengthening and scaling up the harm reduction services in the community.
2. To increase the accessibility of harm reduction services for PWID.

To improve the quality of services (**outcome 1**), the NDCMP will implement address sigma and discrimination through awareness raising and capacity building of health professionals, police, and other key stakeholders in the relevant sectors. It will also establish effective mechanisms for referral of PWID across the health system and ensure that the services are integrated into the general health system. The NDCMP will encourage the community of PWID to engage meaningfully in responding the issue, in including providing (in the POV) delivery of services, key informing a prioritisation process based on a set of agreed criteria.

To increase access to harm reduction services (**outcome 2**), the NDCMP will focus on OST, NVP, testing for HIV and borne hepatitis, and access to ART treatment. The community is also first response in the coordination of effort (where desired) and provide support.

To increase access to harm reduction services (**outcomes 2**) the NDCMP will ensure the decentralization of services to the community level. This will be done through strategic planning based on geographical users and frequency identified taking into consideration the opening hours and days. The target is with promising results in the synchronization with the target on the National AIDS programme with which its strong coordination will be maintained.

Controlled drugs are medicines that are used in the treatment of various illnesses and medical specialties such as palliative care, surgery, intensive care, cytology, gynecology, neuropsychiatry, psychiatry and for opioid substances such as morphine, fentanyl, etc. The NDCMP recognizes the importance of ensuring that the necessary and access to controlled substances for medical treatment are not encountered everywhere. The concentration of these drugs is centrally managed by the Pharmacy Department of the Ministry of Health. quantification of needs, stocking management, store training of health professionals including medical directors and pharmacists, and the distribution of controlled drugs to healthcare facilities throughout the entire country, including rural areas. At the same time, it will enhance the level of diversion and raise awareness of health professionals and patients of risks related to misuse of these medications.

To improve the access to controlled drugs (**outcome 1**) the NDCMP needs to access controlled drugs for medical treatment and deter prescription drug misuse and abuse.

# Strategic Area 2. Supply Reduction

The overall goal of the strategic area 2, is "To enhance law enforcement co-ordination with all partners by maximising collective resources in the identification, detection and disruption of drug trafficking networks."

The consultation had identified the need for better intelligence processes, to improve communication between different agencies and to enhance operational coordination throughout the country. Some improvements in these areas have been made in previous years including the availability of new databases, training of the officers, the use of UNODC asset recovery studies and processed information resources. New legislation and improved guidelines have also been implemented. However, it is essential to continue to work strongly to strengthen supply reduction activities.

This NDRCP aims to strengthen the existing capacity for intelligence collection and using information on increased coordination and information sharing between law enforcement agencies as well as wider partnerships to disrupt drug trafficking networks.

Law enforcement key is the primary reduction activity of drugs that cause harm and to enforce the legislation provisions that seek to prevent crime and reduce activities that adversely affect the society and its economy. Law enforcement is therefore the primary tool for the government to disrupt drug trafficking and its impact factors that constrain the main, law and order and protect citizens.

This strategic area has four themes:

## Theme 1: Intelligence Led Policing and Collaboration

The first theme focuses on intelligence gathering and collaboration: two main objectives (2) to use all resources to pro-actively tackle drug related crime and (2) to focus resources on the reducing illicit trafficking in narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances. The government is committed to enhance and strengthen collaboration between the parties involved in law enforcement and to move resources from reactive to pro-active policing. This will ensure that the resources are used in the most effective way to proactively tackle drug related crimes to generate greater effect. It also forecasts that all parties involved in drug law enforcement should share information on a need to know basis. This will allow the government to relieve/related laws affect its members, their collective ability to identify, detect and disrupt drug trafficking networks. By implementing these objectives, the government will be better placed to disrupt drug trafficking. It also aims to improve efforts in identifying the introduction of new or foreign narcotic drugs and substances or substances into the local drug market, which focusing on the most severe, problematic and harmful narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances that have the greatest impact on the society.

All parties involved in drug law enforcement should be provided with sufficient human and financial resources to implement the objectives of this theme. The government will also prioritize the main objective (1) focus on reducing the growing illicit trafficking in narcotics drugs and psychotropic substances.

## Theme 2: Identify and Exploit Links with Other Forms of Organised Crime

The second theme focuses on identifying and exploiting links between drug crime, focus on the linkages between various criminal activities. Indeed, organised crime in general comes closely intertwined with drug trafficking. This is because the networks used for drug trafficking are often the same ways analysts finding new counts and means to best counter the measures law enforcement have in place. It is using intelligence led policing to identify and exploit links between drug trafficking and other forms of drug trafficking transactions and through with making linkages with other types of crime committed by some organised crime groups or individuals.
### Theme 3: Early Identification of New Psychotropic Substances

The third theme focuses on the early identification of new psychotropic substances. The consultation acknowledged that there are indications that narcotic drugs are being repurposed into new forms to subvert legislation and that there can be a gap in the identification of these to maximise criminal profits without risk. 

The intention is to apply more efforts in identifying the early introduction of new or foreign narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances. It is envisaged that the National Anti-Drug demand reduction sector and requires that all parties involved in drug prevention be empowered to remain alert to emerging trends and substances, thus fostering an environment of vigilance.

# Strategic Area 3: Human rights and Proportionalities Police Stations

The consultation reaffirmed that in the context of Seychelles, citizen rights and freedom need to be protected and promoted. It is important that young people, being the future of the country be protected. Subsequently, the consultation highlighted that the police must be trained in human rights and must appropriate services for their wellbeing and guarantees that all citizen including people in prisons, men and women who use drugs are treated fairly and equally.

The consultation highlighted that the police should adopt adapted treatment related functions for deputy children and vulnerable persons, such as providing accessibility for residential homes in the territory area. Such assistance is particularly important for women, children and elderly. In addition, the consultation bring pregnant women or other vulnerable, in delivery, critical for both themselves and the health of their baby. Using existing situation analysis and identified priorities in the NHSP, this project aims to provide with supervision and health services, including prenatal and postnatal care.

The consultation further noted that the parties of the Misuse of Drugs Act 2008 (MODA) are need to be reviewed to ensure that the law is proportionate and consistent with human rights. This review should existing legislations and ensure it fits the current context of the society.

Finally, the consultation highlighted that the current law focuses on the criminalisation of users as the priority use drugs and focuses on alternative sentencing actions in accordance with emerging evidence worldwide concerning the health and social consequences of drug use. The consultation highlighted the need to treat the PWUD diverted from prison. It was felt that there is a need to assess the effectiveness of the Prevention Police stations.

The overall goal of the strategic area is to ensure that human rights are protected and women are protected at all levels.

The area has two main themes:
1. **Human rights, young people, women and immune persons in prison**
2. **Proportionality of sentencing for PWUD**

The strategic direction for these themes results from the identified gaps pertaining to current police procedures and its junction the police sector.

## Theme 1: Human rights, young people, women and immune persons in prison
This first theme focuses on the alignment with the compliance national and regional, international system and mandates. It acknowledges the need to ensure that the police protect human rights and gender equality. It also addresses implementation of accessible services, responding to the specific needs of young people, women and people in prison without stigma and discrimination.

## Theme 2: Proportionality of sentencing for PWUD
The second theme focuses on two main objectives:
* To review the MODA brings improvements in the implementation to drug related services.
* To decrease use of drugs among minorities.
* To ensure women access services.
* To reduce the incarceration of PWUD.

The goal is to ensure a holistic approach to drug treatment and reduction and prevent systemic issues to lower the incarceration in the community. The objective will specifically look at the introduction of harm reduction services in prison services.

## Theme 3: Proportionality of sentencing for PWUD and Junction the Police Sector
The second objective has two objectives:
1. To ensure legal framework adapted to the context that prevents incorrect incarceration for PWUD.
2. To increase alternative sentencing to prison incorrect incarceration for PWUD.

The first objective plans to review the MODA and related laws, making it more consistent, rational, respecting human rights and aligned with the current context of the society.

The second objective aims to reduce incarceration of PWUD by improving probation services, through increased resources and offering services to alternative implementation.

# Development partners: United Nations

The Strategic Partnership Agreement (SPA) 2018-2022 underscores the United Nations system's areas of collaboration with the Government of Seychelles. This represents a collective, coherent and integrated response by the UN Country Team aligned with the government's national development priorities based on the country's economic situation. In alignment with the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), the SPA translates these into a Common Strategic Results Framework for development partners.

The SPA identifies the following strategic priority areas for the UN and its partners to prioritise their actions in coherent results-based frameworks: The SPA has two overarching strategic priorities:

* **Strategic Priority 1:** Enhancing human development and social protection
* **Development and Key Result Area 4:** Environment and Energy

Promoting the rights of persons with disabilities, including children and the elderly (in line with NOS Key Result Area 3: Social Development and Key Result Area 1: Governance)

## UNICEF Seychelles COUNTRY COOPERATION STRATEGY 2016-2021
Includes, among others, the following:

* **Focus area 1.1:** Strengthen Maritime Law Enforcement capabilities for the prevention and deterrence of all types of illicit trafficking
* **Focus area 1.2:** Improved capacity to investigate, prosecute and judge maritime crime
* **Focus area 1.3:** Improved Maritime Domain Awareness capability through the use of relevant technologies

## WHO: Seychelles COUNTRY COOPERATION STRATEGY 2016-2021
Includes, among others, the following focus:

* **Focus area 2.1:** Improve access to interventions addressing substance use and abuse and rehabilitative services to address drug use and mental health challenges
* **Focus area 2.2:** Strengthen responses to violence against children and women, including root causes and consequences of violence and injuries and the development of prevention strategies.
* **Focus area 2.3:** Improve access to comprehensive quality care through evidence-based interventions for prevention, treatment and care of HIV/AIDS, STIs and Hepatitis.
