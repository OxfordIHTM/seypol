## Foreword

Adolescents are not simply old children or young adults. This deceptively simple observation lies at the heart of **Global Accelerated Action for the Health of Adolescents (AA-HA!): guidance to support country implementation**, which reflects the coming of age of adolescent health within global public health.

For years, the unique health issues associated with adolescence have been little understood or, in some cases, ignored. But that has now changed. Adolescent health and development was made an integral part of the *Global Strategy for Women’s, Children’ and Adolescents’ Health (2016–2030)* (the Global Strategy) because, in the words of the United Nations Secretary-General, "[adolescents are] central to everything we want to achieve, and to the overall success of the 2030 Agenda".

**The Triple Dividend**
Investments in adolescent health bring a triple dividend of benefits:
1. For adolescents **now**.
2. For their **future adult lives**.
3. For the **next generation**.

**Paradigm Shifts in the Guidance:**
* **Asset-based approach:** Addresses adolescence not only through public health lenses of risk but as powerful societal assets.
* **AHiAP Approach:** Promotes "Adolescent Health in All Policies," suggesting key actions in education, social protection, urban planning, and criminal justice.
* **Fragile Settings:** Explicitly includes considerations for humanitarian and fragile settings.
* **Youth Engagement:** Built on the motto "nothing about us, without us," putting young people in the driver's seat.

## Glossary

* **Adolescent:** A person aged 10–19 years. Young adolescent (10–14) and older adolescent (15–19).
* **Burden of disease or injury:** The impact of a health problem in a population, measured by mortality and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs).
* **DALY (Disability-adjusted life year):** A measure combining years of life lost through premature death and years lived with disability.
* **Equity:** The absence of avoidable, unfair or remediable differences among groups of people.
* **Evidence-based intervention:** Interventions found to be effective through rigorous evaluation.

## Executive Summary

### APPLY AA-HA! Together: Seven Overarching Messages
* **A**pproach: A systematic approach for understanding needs and planning.
* **P**revention: Act now on preventable causes (3,000+ deaths daily).
* **P**riority-setting: Tailor actions to specific disease and injury risk profiles.
* **L**eadership: Strong government leadership and institutionalization.
* **Y**ields: Triple dividend across generations.
* **Together**: Work WITH adolescents, FOR adolescents.
* **Whole-of-government**: Adolescent Health in All Policies (AHiAP).

### Key Sections Overview
1. **What is special about adolescents:** Rapid physical, neurological, and psychosocial changes.
2. **Burdens:** High prevalence of road injuries, self-harm, and maternal conditions (for girls).
3. **Interventions:** Focus on positive development and evidence-based health interventions.
4. **Prioritization:** Needs assessment $\rightarrow$ Landscape analysis $\rightarrow$ Setting priorities.
5. **Programming:** Logical framework for translating priorities into a national plan.
6. **Accountability:** Monitoring and evaluation using disaggregated data.

## 1. AA-HA! – A never-before moment for adolescent health

### 1.1 Call for Accelerated Action
Adolescence (10-19 years) is a unique formative stage. The **Global Strategy for Women’s, Children’s and Adolescents’ Health (2016–2030)** identifies adolescents as central to the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development.

### 1.2 Why Invest?
* **Human Rights:** Fundamental rights to the highest achievable standard of health.
* **Triple Dividend:** Immediate benefit to adolescent $\rightarrow$ benefit to future adult life $\rightarrow$ benefit to next generation.
* **Economic Gain:** Potential for demographic dividends and increased productivity.

## 2. Disease and injury burdens, and risk factors

### Global Trends
In 2015, over 1.2 million adolescents died. Two-thirds of deaths occurred in LMICs (Africa and South-East Asia).

**Leading Causes of Death Globally (Example):**
* **Males:** Road injury is the top cause across both age groups.
* **Females:** Lower respiratory infections (younger) $\rightarrow$ Maternal conditions (older).

## 3. Evidence-based interventions

### Core Intervention Areas
* **Positive Development:** Adolescent-friendly health services, health-promoting schools.
* **Unintentional Injury:** Road safety laws, helmet laws, drowning prevention.
* **Violence:** INSPIRE strategies, preventing child maltreatment.
* **SRH & HIV:** Comprehensive sexuality education (CSE), contraception, HIV care.
* **Communicable Diseases:** Vaccinations, TB control, Malaria prevention.
* **NCDs & Nutrition:** Healthy diet promotion, physical activity, iron supplementation.
* **Mental Health:** Psychosocial support, suicide prevention.

## 4. Setting national priorities

### The Three-Step Process
1. **Needs Assessment:** Identify conditions with the greatest impact on health and development.
2. **Landscape Analysis:** Map existing programs, legislation, and resources.
3. **Setting Priorities:** Select interventions based on urgency, feasibility, and evidence.

---

## 5. National programming

### Logical Framework Components
* **Inputs/Processes:** Funding, workforce, governance.
* **Outputs:** Trained providers, available services.
* **Outcomes:** Improved coverage, behavioral changes.
* **Impact:** Reduced mortality and morbidity, improved equity.

### AHiAP (Adolescent Health in All Policies)
Requires coordination across sectors: Education, Social Protection, Transport, Urban Planning, and Criminal Justice.

---

## 6. Monitoring, evaluation and research

### Key Requirements
* **Data Disaggregation:** Essential to split data by age (5-year bands) and sex.
* **Indicators:** Use the 60 indicators from the Global Strategy (e.g., adolescent birth rate, mortality rate).
* **Youth Engagement:** Involve adolescents in the design and interpretation of research.
