# FOREWORD

It is an honour for me to present the National Gender Policy for the Government of Seychelles. It signals Government’s continued commitment to the attainment of Constitutional rights and gender equality as a cornerstone for ending poverty. It further reinforces the objective of achieving long-term sustainable social change by meeting the aspirations of all Seychellois.

This policy is both timely and significant as it finds resonance with the Social Renaissance campaign announced by The President of the Republic of Seychelles, Mr James Alix Michel. The campaign is calling for a change in attitudes and behaviours in response to the increase in social ills.

The SADC Protocol on Gender and Development encompasses commitments made in all sub-regional, continental and global instruments for achieving gender equality, and is seen as the basis for accelerating the implementation of gender equality commitments. Seychelles takes cognisance of the Millennium Development Goals (MDG) and the Sustainable Development Goals (SDG), the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW), as well as the targets of the SADC Protocol on Gender and Development, because we recognise the link between sustainable development in the economic, social and environmental fields.

Gender equality forms part of the broader commitment by the Government of Seychelles to human rights and social justice as set out in the United Nations Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR).

Gender equality encompasses the empowerment of women to claim their equal rights as citizens, as well as changing the attitudes of men and engaging them in the struggle for gender equality and equity. It includes the views, interests and needs of women and men across all key populations at higher risk of discrimination.

The high level of social development and the elimination of blatant discrimination against women lead to the assumption that gender equality has been achieved in Seychelles. However, challenges still exist. These include but are not limited to: Gender Based Violence (GBV), high rates of teenage pregnancy, higher levels of poverty amongst single parent households that are predominantly female, the increasing incidence of sex work and the gender dimensions of illicit drug abuse and HIV and AIDS.

There is also a perception that boys and men are being marginalised in the provision of services. This needs to be rectified through reinforcing the role of men and boys as partners in promoting gender equality. There is a need to strengthen public awareness campaigns on the necessity of targeted gender awareness programmes.

To achieve sustainable development and full empowerment, it is vital that all emerging risks and long-term challenges are analysed with a gender lens. The growing social ills of violent crime, illicit drug and alcohol abuse, theft, prostitution, family disintegration, domestic violence, child abuse, delinquency, poverty, labour opportunities, and the spread of communicable diseases have profound gender dimensions. It is important that women, men, girls and boys sustain the gains achieved over the years, despite mounting risks and challenges and external factors.

My Ministry has identified the development of a National Gender Policy as a priority especially as the SADC Protocol is being aligned to the post-2015 agenda and will thus be extended up to the year 2030. The development of the Policy has been aided by the analysis and sex-disaggregated data gathered for the reporting processes for CEDAW and the SADC Protocol on Gender and Development.

Finally, I wish to commend the goodwill that has contributed to the formulation of this document and call on all stakeholders to continue to give your utmost support and commitment to this National Gender Policy, as we move to the next level: the development of the sectoral and strategic plans.

**Vincent Meriton**
Designated Minister &
Minister of Social Affairs, Community Development and Sports

# 1. BACKGROUND

## 1.1 Policy and legal framework

The Constitution of the Republic of Seychelles (1993) is the country’s supreme law and all other laws and policies are subject to it. Article 5 states that ‘any other law found to be inconsistent with this Constitution is, to the extent of the inconsistency, void.’

The preamble to the Constitution, which reflects the aspirations of the people, proclaims the ‘inherent dignity and the equal and inalienable rights of all members of the human family as the foundation for freedom, justice, welfare, fraternity, peace and unity’. It reaffirms that these rights include the ‘rights to life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness free from all types of discrimination.’

The Seychellois Charter of Fundamental Human Rights and Freedoms (Chapter 3 of the Constitution), which incorporates many of the principles of international human rights instruments, guarantees 25 rights equally applicable to both men and women. Article 48 of the Constitution provides for the fundamental human rights and freedoms in Seychelles to be interpreted in a manner consistent with any international human rights obligations of Seychelles.

The Seychelles Constitution guarantees that “Every person has a right to equal protection of the law including the enjoyment of the rights and freedoms set out in this Charter without discrimination on any ground except as is necessary in a democratic society.”

Article 30 recognises the unique status and natural maternal functions of women in society. It endeavours to take appropriate measures to ensure that all working mothers are afforded special protection with regard to paid leave and working conditions before and after childbirth.

There are no overtly discriminatory laws. The gap lies in the full enforcement and implementation of the law; mainstreaming gender in the legislation drafting process; the harmonisation of laws and domestication of (sub)regional and international treaties and Protocols ratified by Seychelles.

The policy is guided by the gender mainstreaming approaches of the SADC Secretariat, Commonwealth Secretariat and United Nations.

The Policy is principally aligned to the key provisions of the SADC Protocol on Gender and Development, an all-encompassing sub-regional instrument which takes into consideration international and (sub)regional instruments.

# 2. JUSTIFICATION

Seychelles is a leader in the African region and Indian Ocean Community in terms of socio-economic development. The country is guided by people-centred development policies. This has led to consistent progress, resulting in our Small Island Developing State (SIDS) achieving top social indicators comparable to many developed nations. Women are highly visible in the public sphere. The general population is composed of slightly more women (50.4%) than men (49.6%).

# 3. POLICY FRAMEWORK – VISION, GOAL, OBJECTIVES AND GUIDING PRINCIPLES

## 3.1 Vision

A nation of women, men, girls and boys who are equal contributors to and beneficiaries of, national development, able to enjoy equal opportunities and achieve their full potential in both the public and private spheres; and have the freedom to make responsible life choices.

## 3.2 Mission

To achieve gender equality and equity as enshrined in the Constitution, (sub) regional and international human-rights instruments that Seychelles is party to.

## 3.3 Policy Goal

To provide national guidelines for institutionalising and operationalising gender as an integral component of our sustainable development.

## 3.4 Policy Objectives

*   **3.4.1.** Ensure the mainstreaming of gender perspectives into all policies, structures, systems, programmes and activities in order to make them gender responsive and contribute to the effective achievement of sustainable socio-economic and political development.
*   **3.4.2.** Harmonise legislative and administrative frameworks with international and (sub) regional gender-related instruments to achieve set targets on gender equality and equity.
*   **3.4.3.** Provide an overarching framework to guide State and Non State Actors (NSAs) in the attainment of gender equality and equity.
*   **3.4.4.** Promote equal representation and participation of women and men at all levels and sectors of decision-making and ensure that gender equality is championed in all agendas.
*   **3.4.5.** Ensure the generation of sex-disaggregated data

## 3.5 Guiding Principles

*   **Rights based approach:** Integrating international human rights standards and principles in development activities (including women’s human rights and the prohibition of sex discrimination), to enhance accountability by empowering duty bearers to fulfil their obligations and rights holders to claim their rights.
*   **Harmonisation:** Harmonising national legislation, policies, strategies and programmes with relevant (sub) regional and international instruments related to the empowerment of women and girls is key to achieving gender equality.
*   **Empowerment of women:** Gender equality can only be achieved if women and girls have the skills, confidence and agency to claim their social, economic and political rights.
*   **Male involvement:** Men and boys have a key role to play in achieving gender equality.
*   **Gender is a cross cutting issue:** Achieving gender equality requires the recognition that every policy, programme, project and development process affects women and men differently and that the views, interests and needs of women and men be considered when shaping Seychelles’ national development agenda.
*   **Multi-sectoral approach:** All stakeholders including State and NSAs across all sectors need to work together as partners in a co-ordinated way to achieve gender equality and equity.
*   **Poverty eradication:** Gender equality and equity is key to the eradication of poverty.
*   **Household and family relations:** Equitable and responsible decision-making within households by all family members in whatever setting they find themselves, is key to peaceful, happy and thriving family relations.
*   **Identifying and responding to emerging issues:** Gender equality can only be achieved through government and its partners’ ability to identify and respond to emerging issues in an ever changing national and global environment.
*   **Sex-disaggregated data and gender-disaggregated information:** To be able to effectively measure the results of interventions, it is necessary that all government departments, non-governmental agencies and the private sector collect sex-disaggregated data and analyse information with a gender lens.
*   **Strategic and practical gender needs to be taken into account:** Achieving gender equality requires the Seychelles government to be able to respond to both the immediate and longer term gender needs.
*   **Resource allocation:** Implementation of the Policy is a national responsibility, and all sectors will be responsible for providing budgetary and human resources needed for its implementation.

# 4. POLICY PROGRAMME AREAS, OBJECTIVES AND STRATEGIC ACTIONS

## 4.1 Constitutional and legal rights

In line with the SADC Protocol on Gender and Development the Republic of Seychelles endeavours to enshrine gender equality and equity in all laws and practices and where necessary, all discriminatory laws will be reviewed, amended and or repealed.

The Seychelles Constitution guarantees the right to equal protection before the law for “every person without discrimination on any ground”. The Constitution is generally assumed to be gender neutral, making no distinction between the sexes.

**Key issues and challenges**

Seychelles uses a combination of English common law, French civil law and customary law to form the basis of the legal system. While gender equality and non-discrimination against women are not explicit in the Constitution, formal discrimination against women in the public sphere is no longer prevalent in Seychelles because of legislative safeguards.

The gap lies in the alignment of laws especially in line with (sub) regional and international treaties ratified by Seychelles and in the implementation and enforcement of these laws. The patriarchal mind-sets and consequently negative attitudes towards women and gender issues sometimes influences the outcomes of legal matters such as rape and domestic violence cases.

Therefore, there is a need for enhanced gender training of all service providers at all levels including judges, magistrates, tribunal members, lawyers, police officers, politicians and other service providers in the chain to guarantee outcomes that are gender sensitive.

**Policy Commitment:**
The Government shall create an enabling environment that guarantees the enjoyment of human rights, Constitutional and legal protection to every person.

**Policy Objective:**
To promote equal Constitutional and legal protection of women and men at all levels.

To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:
*   **4.1.1** Enshrine gender equality and equity in the Laws of Seychelles and introduce prohibition of all forms of discrimination against women, both direct and indirect.
*   **4.1.2** Ensure that all gender discriminatory legal provisions and gender discriminatory language are corrected in the Laws of Seychelles.
*   **4.1.3** Improve gender-sensitivity of court and Tribunal decisions and procedures.
*   **4.1.4** Build capacity of the justice system to systematically and routinely protect vulnerable witnesses and deliver justice sensitively, fairly and swiftly.
*   **4.1.5** Ensure that crime, justice and security service providers are gender sensitive in the administration of their duties.
*   **4.1.6** Establish the costing and budgeting for implementation of gender related laws and policies and develop a mechanism for tracking funds allocated by government to all programmes.

## 4.2 Governance

Seychelles endeavours to enhance the environment to increase participation of women at decision-making positions within the public and private sectors.

**Key issues and challenges**

Seychelles has made great strides in the area of women representation, and although the country falls short of achieving 50% women in decision-making in the Cabinet, National Assembly, judiciary, police, governing boards, national committees and in business (especially proprietors of big businesses), the level of women participation in these key sectors are remarkable and commendable.

Most political parties’ manifestos are beginning to make explicit reference to “putting women ahead” and put forward a gender agenda, whilst also having a Women’s wing, which forms the backbone of most of the political party support.

It is in the public service sector that women have surpassed the 50% target based on female representation at the level of Permanent Secretaries and senior managers and has reached over 70% at local governance senior decision-making levels.

**Policy Commitment:**
The Government shall promote good governance through gender-balanced leadership in both the public and private sectors.

**Policy Objective:**
To achieve gender-balanced representation and participation of women and men at all levels through capacity development of women at high-level decision-making positions, and promote male participation in community-based and civil society initiatives.

To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:
*   **4.2.1.** Continue to create an enabling environment to continue to sustain representation of women in all senior decision-making positions in public and private sectors, whilst concomitantly sustaining the gains thus far.
*   **4.2.2.** Enhance the leadership capacity and gender awareness of women in parliament, the judiciary and political parties.
*   **4.2.3.** Strengthen the National Assembly’s ownership and commitment towards achieving international and (sub) regional gender equality obligations.
*   **4.2.4.** Strengthen or set up support structures for women in politics and decision-making.
*   **4.2.5.** Strengthen or set up support structures to enhance participation of women in the judiciary.
*   **4.2.6.** Determine the barriers, future trends and conditions required for women’s participation in politics and decision-making.
*   **4.2.7.** Raise awareness and build capacity of men and women decision-makers to become gender champions at all levels of government, agencies, parastatals and private sectors.
*   **4.2.8.** Promote and build capacity of men and boys in civil society to become more active and participate in the gender movement and developing gender programmes and campaigns.
*   **4.2.9.** Strengthen monitoring, evaluation and communication in progress made in representation and participation of women and men towards achieving the SADC 50/50 target.

## 4.3 Education and training

Seychelles will continue to adopt and implement gender sensitive educational policies and programmes addressing gender stereotypes in education, and where necessary, enact laws that promote equal access to and retention in early childhood, primary, secondary, tertiary, vocational and non-formal education.

**Key issues and challenges**

Teachers, curriculum developers and management staff of the Ministry of Education have attended gender-sensitisation training and programmes. Experts have screened locally produced teaching materials for negative gender stereotypes. The majority of teachers are female at primary and secondary level.

There are gender gaps in vocational training but this is gradually changing. Boys lag behind girls in academic performance at primary and secondary level.

**Policy Commitment:**
The Government shall promote the mainstreaming of gender in all education and training programmes, processes and materials to ensure equal representation, participation and performance, with special emphasis on boys and young men, to ensure the complete development of the individual from early childhood and throughout the educational life cycle.

**Policy Objective:**
To increase women, men, girls and boys access to quality education and training programmes to build a skilled and responsible productive work force, free of gender stereotypes, to sustain the economy.

To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:
*   **4.3.1.** Strengthen gender mainstreaming capacity in education and training institutions.
*   **4.3.2.** Consolidate retention in primary and address the issue at secondary, tertiary non-university and university levels.
*   **4.3.3.** Strengthen production, management and sharing of gender statistics in education and training.
*   **4.3.4.** Promote equal access to adult learning opportunities and recognition of existing knowledge and recognition of prior learning and experience.
*   **4.3.5.** Provide early intervention programmes for boys and girls who are falling behind in essential learning areas of the National Curriculum Framework (2013)
*   **4.3.6.** Support initiatives to improve boys’ academic performance within the primary and secondary stages of education and training.
*   **4.3.7.** Mainstream compulsory and validated generic gender modules (incl. GBV) in all education and programmes at the tertiary non-university and university levels.
*   **4.3.8.** Revisit, promote, fund and implement the Plan of Action for Gender Equality in Education.
*   **4.3.9.** Address the issue of inappropriate behaviour amongst male and female students in partnership with teachers, parents, the community and other stakeholders.
*   **4.3.10.** Address the issue of inappropriate teacher-student relationships, in partnership with school management and school council and other relevant bodies.
*   **4.3.11.** Establish mechanisms to encourage more women and girls to study STEM subjects (Science, Technology, Engineering and Mathematics).
*   **4.3.12.** Promote gender responsive inclusive education programmes for children with disabilities in line with the Inclusive Education Policy (2015)
*   **4.3.13.** Promote gender parity in teaching and management staff at all levels and all subjects.
*   **4.3.14.** Build leadership skills of children and youth, to better equip them with necessary life skills that will better promote positive attitudinal and behavioural changes.

## 4.4 Productive resources, employment, and economic empowerment

Seychelles has ratified most of the international and (sub) regional treaties that ensure the equal rights of women in the labour market.

**Key issues and challenges**

There are no restrictions to women regarding the ownership of property and access to credit. However, available data on access to property and credit is limited leading to assumptions that women benefit equally on the whole. Men continue to dominate the running of large businesses, while women operate many of the Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs).

**Policy Commitment:**
The Government shall ensure that economic policies and programmes are gender responsive, address poverty and increase decent work and entrepreneurial opportunities equally for women and men.

**Policy Objective:**
To ensure women and men have full and equal access to, control over and ownership of the benefits of socio-economic growth and development.

To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:
*   **4.4.1** Promote gender parity and gender sensitisation at economic decision-making levels of central government, agencies, and parastatals.
*   **4.4.2** Provide NBS with appropriate resources to conduct time-use studies in order to recognise the contribution of unpaid care work to the national economy.
*   **4.4.3** Promote the collection and utilisation of sex-disaggregated data at all levels of the national economies.
*   **4.4.4** Ensure gender analysis and gender mainstreaming in all national development plans/strategies, economic reform programmes and poverty reduction strategies.
*   **4.4.5** Identify and address the barriers for women to enter, grow and diversify their business opportunities.
*   **4.4.6** Build financial literacy of social welfare recipients, especially young women, single mothers, youth, elderly, and disabled.
*   **4.4.7** Ensure national trade and entrepreneurship policies and programmes support women in business and build the capacity of women and men in the informal sector.
*   **4.4.8** Ensure legal and administrative frameworks to ensure women and men have equal access to decent conditions of service in all sectors of the economy that takes into consideration gender specific needs.
*   **4.4.9** Introduce measures to monitor the impact of economic and loan opportunities on women, including those created through public procurement processes.
*   **4.4.10** Ease the burden of multiple roles played by women.
*   **4.4.11** Promote responsible fatherhood and the concept of positive masculinity to ease the burden of women’s multiple roles and promote functional families.
*   **4.4.12** Ensure and promote that there is no wage gap between women and men, through legal provisions for “equal pay for work of equal value”.
*   **4.4.13** Identify barriers to women in traditionally male dominated fields, including STEM sectors, and increase opportunities for women in these sectors at all levels.
*   **4.4.14** Ensure the safety of all staff, especially women, working unsociable hours, en-route to, during and when they leave work.
*   **4.4.15** Enhance awareness among women employees, especially in the private sector, of legal protection against the termination of employment during pregnancy and take measures to increase reporting of such incidents.
*   **4.4.16** Ensure protection and working conditions for working and lactating mothers, including the implementation of flexi-hours, working conditions and facilities.
*   **4.4.17** Recognise the value of unpaid care work and caregivers and develop appropriate support interventions.

## 4.5 Climate change, sustainable development and disaster risk management

**Key issues**

Men and women experience and respond to climate change effects differently because of their societal roles and responsibilities. In the Seychelles context, women bear a large burden of the responsibility for caring, looking after children, cooking and cleaning.

**Policy commitment**

The government shall recognise the gender perspective in the development of any policy, strategy or programme relating to our country’s sustainable development, climate change, and disaster risk reduction management.

**Policy objective**

To ensure the equal participation of women and men and recognise gender impacts in addressing issues of climate change, sustainable development and disaster risk reduction management.
*   **4.5.1** Ensure that objectives are gender sensitive in national climate change plans by establishing coherence among stakeholders and involving more women in decision-making processes.
*   **4.5.2** Ensure all documents and programmes related to risk and disaster management are gender responsive.
*   **4.5.3** Identify and recognise gender specific impacts and protection measures related to floods, droughts, diseases and other environmental changes.
*   **4.5.4** Build capacity to integrate gender and local population dynamics when designing and implementing mitigation and adaptation programmes.
*   **4.5.5** Take gender responsive measures to address impact of rising sea level to ensure the protection of human settlements.
*   **4.5.6** Draw on and make use of women’s unique knowledge, best practices and coping mechanisms.
*   **4.5.7** Develop, adapt and adopt sustainable technology that is responsive to women and men.
*   **4.5.8** Build capacity in gender responsive monitoring and evaluation of climate change, sustainable development and disaster risk management programmes.
*   **4.5.9** Raise awareness through educational programmes and conduct research
*   **4.5.10** Empower women and men in risks reduction

## 4.6 Food and nutrition security

**Key issues and challenges**

Seychelles is a maritime nation. Food processing and value-addition remain underdeveloped in both the agriculture and fishing industry. Commercial agriculture is a male dominated sector, while many more women are involved in food production at the household level.

**Policy Commitment:**
The Government shall promote access and equal opportunities to facilitate food and nutrition security for all persons in Seychelles.

**Policy Objective:**
To ensure that women and men in Seychelles have access to sufficient quantities of safe, nutritious and culturally acceptable food at all times to live an active and healthy life.

To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:
*   **4.6.1.** Recognise and establish the relationship between gender and food and nutrition security in Seychelles.
*   **4.6.2.** Promote gender responsive programmes and practices that will enhance national food and nutrition security.
*   **4.6.3.** Ensure gender is mainstreamed in all documents relating to food and nutrition security and increase women’s participation at all levels of planning.
*   **4.6.4.** Sensitise and build capacity for all to allow them to contribute to building a nation that is food and nutrition secure
*   **4.6.5.** Recognise that women play a crucial role in ensuring food security at the household level and provide specific funded interventions to support women for better performance in this role.
*   **4.6.6.** Provide the development, generation, and sustenance of activities linked to the Blue Economy.

## 4.7 Gender Based Violence

**Key issues**

Police statistics show that GBV disproportionately affects women and girls. Legislative changes are required to recognise domestic violence as a criminal offence because at the moment the cases are dealt with as simple assault crimes under the Penal Code.

**Policy Commitment:**
The Government shall promote an environment of zero tolerance to all forms of Gender Based Violence at all levels.

**Policy Objective:**
To prevent all forms of Gender Based Violence in the public and private sphere and provide integrated services to survivors and perpetrators.

To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:
*   **4.7.1** Ensure women, girls, and key populations at higher risk of being the target of violence are aware of the criminal nature of GBV.
*   **4.7.2** Strengthen national capacity in GBV research.
*   **4.7.3** End impunity of GBV perpetrators; ensure protection of GBV survivors.
*   **4.7.4** Enhance and realize protection against sexual harassment in the workplace.
*   **4.7.5** Ensure alignment of all other existing laws applicable to GBV accordingly.
*   **4.7.6** Ensure the introduction and implementation of Gender-Based Violence legislation
*   **4.7.7** Ensure survivors of GBV receive standardised and integrated services.
*   **4.7.8** Build skills capacity of service providers to implement standardised and integrated services.
*   **4.7.9** Increase professionalism among GBV service providers.
*   **4.7.10** Ensure comprehensive assistance and care to survivors of GBV.
*   **4.7.11** Support rehabilitation of GBV perpetrators.
*   **4.7.12** Facilitate international, regional and bilateral cooperation to prevent trafficking in persons.
*   **4.7.13** Establish specialised assistance, rehabilitation and reintegration for sex workers.
*   **4.7.14** Address root causes of sex work.
*   **4.7.15** Strengthen capacity in monitoring and evaluation of GBV services.
*   **4.7.16** Ensure safety and security in public spaces for all

## 4.8 Health

**Key issues and challenges**

Health indicators show that women are the major beneficiaries of health programmes. Men are seen to be lagging behind in access to sexual and reproductive health services. Teenage pregnancy remains a concern.

**Policy Commitment:**
The Government shall promote the provision and access of health services for women, men, boys and girls, and the encouragement of male involvement.

**Policy Objective:**
To ensure that all Seychellois adopt healthy lifestyles and have the best possible chance of enjoying safe and satisfying sexual relationships.

To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:
*   **4.8.1.** Provide safe motherhood and encourage responsible fatherhood
*   **4.8.2.** Promote exclusive breast-feeding.
*   **4.8.3.** Sustain access and uptake of family planning services.
*   **4.8.4.** Ensure sustainability of facilities for pregnancy testing.
*   **4.8.5.** Support the prevention and management of infertility.
*   **4.8.6.** Ensure pregnant women and girls have access to quality services for the management abortions.
*   **4.8.7.** Strengthen reproductive health services to reduce cancers of the reproductive system.
*   **4.8.8.** Provide programmes for sexual dysfunction.
*   **4.8.9.** Provide targeted support programmes for MARPs.
*   **4.8.10.** Enhance and promote access of young people to reproductive health services.
*   **4.8.11.** Recognise and support reproductive health rights of people with disabilities.

## 4.9 HIV and AIDS

**Key issues and challenges**

Seychelles is facing a catastrophe regarding drug use, which is intricately linked to HIV and hepatitis C. Anti-Retroviral Therapy (ART) has been made available free of charge since August 2002.

**Policy commitment**

The Government shall ensure that all Seychellois have equal access to HIV continuum of prevention, care and treatment.

**Policy objective**

To create an enabling environment that prevents and supports HIV and AIDS interventions for both men and women.

To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:
*   **4.9.1.** Ensure and encourage HIV testing programmes.
*   **4.9.2.** Sustain coverage of HIV testing and counselling for all pregnant women.
*   **4.9.3.** Determine reasons for ART drop-out among patients.
*   **4.9.4.** Provide targeted treatment, care and support programmes for key populations.
*   **4.9.5.** Encourage support and buy-in by women and men in leadership positions.
*   **4.9.6.** Identify specific vulnerability of girls and women to HIV and AIDS.
*   **4.9.7.** Create opportunities for women living with HIV and AIDS to receive support from support groups.
*   **4.9.8.** Strengthen support to care givers and encourage male participation.
*   **4.9.9.** Facilitate greater leadership in the AIDS response at a strategic level.
*   **4.9.10.** Encourage and promote appropriate education and awareness programmes

## 4.10 Peace building

**Key issues**

Women are largely absent from highest leadership roles in the security sector. Efforts are being made to encourage female recruits in the SPDF and shatter the “glass ceiling” in the Police Department.

**Policy commitment:**
The government shall ensure an enabling environment to foster women representation and participation in defence, police and peacekeeping processes.

**Policy objective:**
To achieve good governance in the peace and security sector, through increased women’s representation and participation.

To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:
*   **4.10.1** Promote women in leadership in the peace and security sector.
*   **4.10.2** Recognise and promote women’s role in conflict resolution, especially in piracy.
*   **4.10.3** Recognise and support men’s and women’s role in promoting peace.
*   **4.10.4** Build national capacity in conflict resolution.
*   **4.10.5** Foster favourable work environments for women in police and defence forces.
*   **4.10.6** Ensure the involvement of women in the implementation of the Reilly report recommendations.

## 4.11 Media, Information and Communication

**Key issues and challenges**

Gender Stereotyping in the media is to be addressed. Capacity building is required to increase gender awareness for improved reporting and advertising.

**Policy commitment:**
The government shall mainstream gender in all information, communication, electronic media, and media policies.

**Policy objective:**
Promote gender equality and equity in and through the media.

To achieve this objective Seychelles will undertake to:
*   **4.11.1** Ensure gender equality is mainstreamed in media/information policies and laws
*   **4.12.1** Ensure equal opportunities for men and women to advance in leadership of publicly funded media.
*   **4.12.2** Ensure all journalists and media practitioners have gender sensitive journalism skills
*   **4.12.3** Promote equal access to views of men and women in the media
*   **4.12.4** Empower the Seychelles Media Commission to ensure gender sensitivity.
