# DRAFT GOVERNMENT POLICY FOR STRATEGIC PLANNING WITHIN THE PUBLIC SECTOR

## 1. Introduction and Rationale
Since the 2008 crisis, the Government has embarked on a series of reforms with the aim of improving the state of the economy and public sector performance. Initially the aim of the public sector reform was to reduce the size of the public sector, but subsequently also to improve service delivery. In 2013, the Government adopted **Results Based Management (RBM)** as it recognised the importance of moving to outcomes orientated approach to drive the Public Sector Reform strategy.

The RBM is a life–cycle approach to management that integrates strategy, people, resources, processes and measurements to improve decision-making, transparency and accountability. The RBM Policy, which was approved by Cabinet in 2013 comprises of four pillars:
1. Strategic Planning (SP)
2. Program Performance Based Budgeting (PPBB)
3. Performance Monitoring and Evaluation (PM&E)
4. Performance Management System (PMS)

The RBM Policy is currently being implemented with PPBB taking the lead and piloting is taking place in a number of MDAs since early 2014. It is expected that by 2019 all MDAs will be PPBB compliant.

The Government recognizes the importance of Strategic Planning as the foundation on which other pillars are developed and is committed to adopt a Strategic Planning Policy to engage all MDAs in the strategic planning process as part of their mandate.

**Strategic planning** is an organizational management activity that is used to set priorities, focus energy and resources, strengthen operations, ensure that employees and other stakeholders are working toward common goals, establish agreement around intended outcomes/results, and assess and adjust the organization's direction in response to a changing environment. It is a disciplined effort that produces fundamental decisions and actions that shape and guide what an organization is, who it serves, what it does, and why it does it, with a focus on the future.

A **strategic plan** is a document used to communicate with the organization the organizations goals, the actions needed to achieve those goals and all of the other critical elements developed during the planning exercise for the medium term (five year period).

The Strategic Plan focuses on strategic outcomes oriented goals for the institution as a whole, and objectives for each of its main service delivery areas aligned to its budget programmes and, where relevant, also to its budget sub-programmes.

The Department of Economic Planning is responsible to oversee the implementation of the Strategic Planning pillar within the RBM Framework hence it is custodian to the Strategic Planning Policy.

## 2. Policy Objectives
Strategic planning is a comprehensive and systematic management tool designed to address the shortcomings where alignment and integration with other pillars are concerned as it helps define the strategy, system, organizational arrangements, methodologies, processes, guidelines, and procedures.

Strategic planning offers at least five compelling reasons for its use:
* Forces a look into the future and therefore provides an opportunity to influence the future, or assume a proactive attitude.
* Provides better awareness of needs and of the facilities related issues and environment.
* Helps define the overall mission of the organization and focuses on the objectives.
* Provides a sense of direction, continuity, and effective staffing and leadership.
* Plugs everyone into the system and provides standards of accountability for people, programs, and allocated resources.

In summary, strategic planning is the key to helping us collectively and cooperatively gain control of the future and the destiny of our organization.

A common template is provided (in Annex 1) for Strategic Planning in MDAs.

## 3. Institutional Arrangement
The success of the Strategic Planning requires the establishment of clear, effective and efficient institutional arrangements. The Government shall create the following institutional arrangement:

* **The Department of Economic Planning** within the Ministry of Finance, Trade and Economic Planning to act as the Coordinating Unit and provide support to the MDA’s;
* **Strategic Planning Focal Persons** in institutions for coordinating Strategic Plans elaboration and implementation in MDAs.

## 4. The Strategic Planning Framework
The implementation of the Strategic Planning Policy shall be guided by the following framework:

1. **A strategic plan shall:**
    * a) Set out the goals and objectives to be met during the period covered by the plan taking into account:
        * i. the strategic direction of the government in the area of the government entity’s mandate as communicated by the responsible minister;
        * ii. the government entity’s mandate as set out in the Act or other instrument creating it;
        * iii. the financial resources of the government entity;
    * b) Identify objective performance measures specific to the goals and objectives set out in the plan;
    * c) Set out the fiscal years covered by the plan.

2. A strategic plan shall be submitted to the responsible minister who shall approve it upon being satisfied the plan is in keeping with the strategic direction, the mandate, and the financial resources of the government entity.

3. A strategic plan shall include a statement that the responsible minister (for departments) or the governing body (for public bodies) is accountable for the preparation of the plan and for achieving the specific goals and objectives.

4. A strategic plan shall be signed by the responsible minister or the chairperson of the public body.

5. A strategic plan shall be submitted to the responsible minister by the date set by the minister.

6. A strategic plan may, subject to the approval of the responsible minister, be amended.

## ANNEX 1: Government of Seychelles Strategic Planning Template

This template proposes a common template for designing strategic plans by sectors in Seychelles. The idea is to keep the strategic plan as brief as possible, **no more than 20 pages total**, to ensure focus.

### Template Structure

**Foreword by the Minister**

**Executive Summary**

#### I. Introduction
This section sets out the rationale and need for a strategic plan and presents the objectives and methodology.
1. **Introduction to the strategic plan and rationale**: Context and usefulness of the SP.
2. **Alignment with broader national context**: Linkage with National Development Plan, SDGs, and Agenda 2063.
3. **Purpose of the strategic plan**: Ensure alignment with current fiscal framework, PPBB, and PM&E.
4. **Methodology**: Conceptual framework, approach, steps, and roles.
5. **Consultation and validation process**: Stakeholder mapping and participatory process.

#### II. Foundational Sector Analysis
Rigorous analysis of the current state, progress, and challenges (e.g., SWOT Analysis).
1. **Overview of the sector**: Core subsectors and global benchmarking.
2. **Strengths**: Key achievements and progress.
3. **Weaknesses**: Service delivery quality, fragmentation, etc.
4. **Opportunities**: Internal and external (e.g., new policies, markets).
5. **Threats**: Internal and external (e.g., climate change, competitors).

#### III. Vision and Mission
1. **Vision**: Long-term aspirational goals.
2. **Mission**: Mandates, thematic focus, and key stakeholders.
3. **Value**: Organizational values and standards (efficiency, transparency, ethics).

#### IV. Sector Objectives
1. **Sector objectives**: Most important medium-term objectives and assumptions.
2. **Impact**: Main impact intended (e.g., educated citizens).
3. **Key Outcomes**: Focused outcomes to support the overarching objective.

#### V. Strategic Choices: Overview of Sector Policies, Programs and Projects
1. **Strategic choices**: Prioritization based on foundational analysis and constraints.
2. **Policies**: Core sector policies (e.g., poverty reduction).
3. **Programs**: Overview of objectives, content, budget, and structure.
4. **Projects**: Overview of objectives, content, budget, and structure.

#### VI. Structure
1. **Sector organization**: Roles, responsibilities, and organigrams.
2. **Implementation arrangements**: Use of RBM tools (PPBB, PM&E, PMS).
3. **Staffing**: Current/projected needs, skills mix, and capacity-strengthening.
4. **Partnerships**: National, international, public, and private.

#### VII. Budget
Alignment with MTEF and PPBB estimates.
1. Current expenditure trends.
2. Alignment with PPBB.
3. Sector public investment plan (PIP).
4. Budget estimates for the duration of the plan.
5. Staffing Budget.

#### VIII. Performance Monitoring and Evaluation
1. **Sectoral performance M&E system**: Instruments, institutional structure, and data dissemination.
2. **Results framework**: Results chain from inputs to impact based on theory of change.

#### IX. Change Management
1. **Stakeholder mapping**: Identifying key stakeholders and their views.
2. **Strategic communications**: Tailor-made messaging and tools.
3. **Risk management**: Risk identification and mitigation strategies.

#### X. Conclusion
Final considerations, roadmap for next steps (cabinet approval), and action plan.

**Accompanied Annexes**
Detailed sector analysis, core activities, budget, staffing, and documentation on consultation.
