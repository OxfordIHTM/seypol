# FOREWORD
# Primary Health Care

Primary health care is essential health care based on practical, scientifically sound and socially acceptable methods and technology made universally accessible to individuals and families in the community through their full participation and at a cost that the community and country can afford to maintain at every stage of their development in the spirit of self-reliance and self-determination.

Primary health care forms an integral part both of the country's health system, of which it is the central function and focus, and of the overall social and economic development of the community.

It is the first level of contact of individuals, the family and community with the national health system bringing health care as close as possible to where people live and work, and constitutes the first element of a continuing health care process.

This **Seychelles Primary Health Care package** serves several purposes. It is a guiding document, a reference document and an operational document at the same time.

It sets out the reason for the existence of the primary health care programmes contained herein and the length and breadth of services that the state will offer free at the point of use in its institutions, as provided for in the Constitution.

The implication is that whatever we have left out of this document (for example optometry services) do not constitute free primary care in the view of the government.

Although this document focuses only on the health aspects of primary health care, we must emphasize that primary health care goes far beyond services and programmes offered by the health sector or within the health sector.

This Primary Health Care Package will evolve with the evolving disease pattern of Seychelles and the Ministry of Health will continually update this document according to the needs of the time.

# 1. INTRODUCTION

The right to the protection of health and to the enjoyment of the highest attainable standard of physical and mental health is enshrined in the Constitution adopted in 1993. Article 29 of the Constitution further provides for the provision of free primary health care (PHC) in State institutions for all citizens and promotes individual responsibility in health matters.

The World Health Organization defines primary health care as a whole-of-society approach to health and well-being centred on the needs and preferences of individuals, families and communities. It addresses the broader determinants of health and focuses on the comprehensive and interrelated aspects of physical, mental and social health and wellbeing. It provides whole-person care for health needs throughout the lifespan, not just for a set of specific diseases. Primary health care ensures people receive comprehensive care - ranging from promotion and prevention to treatment, rehabilitation and palliative care - as close as feasible to people’s everyday environment.[^1]

The Health Care Agency Act, 2013[^2] refers to primary health care as the basic level of promotive, preventive, diagnostic, curative or rehabilitative health services provided by a health care professional at the first point of care.

Seychelles has undergone the epidemiological transition from communicable to non-communicable diseases (NCDs); compared to other countries in the WHO AFRO Region, more disability-adjusted life year (DALYs) are lost from NCDs.[^3] NCDs (Cardiovascular diseases (CVD), neoplasm, diabetes, chronic respiratory diseases) remain an important cause of both morbidity and mortality. CVD and neoplasms cause the majority of deaths.[^4] The 2013 National Survey of NCDs in Seychelles among the adult population aged 25-64 years noted a downward trend for smoking (due to the strict application of the Framework on Tobacco Control)[^5], unchanged or downward trends for high blood pressure and high blood cholesterol, but an upward trend for diabetes and obesity.

Achieving Universal Health Coverage (UHC) – SDG 3.8, including financial risk protection, access to quality essential health care services, and access to safe, effective, quality, and affordable essential medicines and vaccines underpins all other health-related SDG goals and targets. (see Annex 1 for SDG 3 Goals, targets and indicators)

Seychelles has a very strong public health system. With health expenditure at between 4 and 5 % of GDP, many health outcomes are comparable to peer economies with higher investment in the health system. ## 1.1 LEADERSHIP AND GOVERNANCE

The MoH has overall responsibility for the management and development of the health sector. The Ministry, under the executive leadership of the Principal Secretary, ensures that the health system is delivering on the government’s priorities. The Ministry is responsible for policy development; planning; monitoring and evaluation; as well as oversight and coordination of the implementation of health strategies by the three pubic bodies and private sector responsible for health care provision. The Ministry is also responsible for human resource development, health promotion and international cooperation.

The statutory entities under the purview of the MOH have the following mandates:

*   **The HCA** to promote, protect and restore the health of the public
*   **The PHA** to regulate health and provide protection of the population’s health
*   **The National AIDS Council (NAC)**: to provide strategic guidance and coordination of HIV activities on a national level

The Health Care Agency Act 2013₉ puts the responsibility to plan, deliver and evaluate primary health care services to the public on the HCA and places the overall responsibility for the provision of such services on the Director of Community Health Services (CHS).

Additionally, other entities within MoH also contribute to delivery of PHC services. The PHA is responsible for prevention and control of HIV/AIDS and viral hepatitis. The PHA is also responsible for public health programmes at community level. This includes inspection of homes and home environment, inspection

## 1.2 PURPOSE OF THE PHC PACKAGE

The main objective of the PHC package of services is to achieve UHC, ensure equity, quality and efficiency in health, and contribute to the health sector goals. It is based on three principles:

* Meeting people’s health needs through comprehensive promotive, protective, preventive, curative, rehabilitative, and palliative care throughout the life course, strategically prioritizing key health care services aimed at individuals and families as close as possible to where they live, work, play and age
* Systematically addressing the broader determinants of health (including social, economic, environmental, as well as people’s characteristics and behaviours) through evidence-informed public policies and actions across all sectors, and empowering individuals, families, and communities to optimize their health.
* Defining a comprehensive PHC health care package will inform resource requirements: human, physical infrastructure, medicines and medical products; and control the introduction of new services and scaling up of existing services.

## 1.3 PROCESS OF PACKAGE DEVELOPMENT

The PHC package was developed by a technical working group under the leadership of the Principal Secretary. The process was widely consultative, including heads of health centres and units, programme
managers and other frontline staff. The first rounds of consultations enabled the team to put together an inventory of services available at primary health care level. The team then conducted an analysis of the findings against National Health Strategic Plan objectives, WHO recommendations and SDG 3 targets to identify strengths and possible gaps in the services. Writers were tasked to draft optimal services for the different programmatic areas. This was done in consultation with programme managers and frontline staff and led to a robust draft document, which included such services as palliative care, healthy ageing and care and for the elderly, men’s health and domiciliary care. Finally, the team held “Programme Cluster Meetings” bringing together key actors from the different programme clusters from within and beyond MoH to critically review and validate the document. WHO was invited to comment on the final product.

## 1.4 COMMUNITY HEALTH SYSTEM: FACILITIES, PROGRAMME AREAS AND COMPONENTS

The Health Centre is the bedrock of PHC in Seychelles, where PHC services with emphasis on health promotion and disease prevention. These services include communicable and non-communicable disease control, Immunisation, home and food premises inspection, healthy growth and development of children, screening and family health, medical, dental, rehabilitative and palliative services.

There are currently 16 community health centres on the three main islands of Mahé, Praslin and La Digue and one on Silhouette. The Health Centres at Beau Vallon, English River, Anse Aux Pins, Anse Royale and Anse Boileau on Mahé and Baie Ste Anne on Praslin are considered Regional Health Centres.

A typical PHC team includes doctors, dentists, nurses, midwives, physiotherapists, public health officers, health and dental care assistants, pharmaceutical dispensers and health information assistants.

The scope of services offered at a health facility depends on the location of the health facility, the catchment area and the population density. An expanded package of services is available in regional PHC facilities, while twenty-four-hour emergency care services are only available at Anse Royale, Anse Boileau, Baie Ste Anne and Logan Hospital. Not all facilities offer daily doctor’s consultations. The list of health care facilities and scope of services offered at each location shall constitute Annex 2 to this primary health care package.

To address burden of diseases and health needs across age cohorts, PHC includes promotive, preventive, curative, rehabilitative and palliative services. PHC care services are arranged under six broad categories, which group related programmes:

* Family Health
* Disease Prevention and Control
* Oral Health
* Rehabilitation
* Health Promotion
* Integrated Management of Priority Diseases and Other Common Conditions

## 2.1 MATERNAL HEALTH PROGAMME

### WHAT IS THE PROGRAMME?
Maternal health refers to the health of women during pregnancy, childbirth and the postnatal period. The Maternal Health Programme seeks to promote, protect and restore the health of women during pregnancy, childbirth, and the postpartum period and improve maternal and infant survival. It includes the health care dimensions of family planning, preconception, prenatal, and postnatal care.

### WHY IMPLEMENT THE PROGRAMME?
Globally, many women die from pregnancy and childbirth related complications, such as intrauterine infections, severe bleeding, obstructed labour, premature birth and eclampsia.

In Seychelles, maternal and infant mortality rates are relatively low. However, despite remarkable improvements, there are still gaps and weaknesses. The Maternal Health Programme is needed to protect the health and wellbeing of women, infants, children and families. Improving the wellbeing of mothers, infants and children is an important health goal. Their health and wellbeing determine the health of the next generation.

### WHAT ARE THE AIMS OF THE PROGRAMME?
To improve maternal and child survival through the provision of patient–centred care and evidence-based health services and to contribute to the achievement of SDG 3.1, 3.2, 3.8, NHSP targets on maternal and child health and other programme specific objectives/targets

#### Specific objectives

**Antenatal Care**
* To diagnose pregnancy and conduct surveillance of the pregnant woman and her baby
* To detect and promptly manage pregnancy-related complications
* To screen and treat conditions and diseases such as anaemia, asymptomatic bacteriuria, STIs HIV, mental health problems and/or symptoms of stress, gender-based violence/Intimate partner violence; alcohol/drug/substance abuse
* To implement preventive measures, including tetanus (Td) Immunisation, de-worming, iron and folic acid supplementation
* To prevent mother- to -child transmission of HIV, Syphilis, Hepatitis B and HTLV-1
* To support the pregnant woman and her partner prepare emotionally and physically for birth and postnatal care of both baby and mother
* To counsel and support to the pregnant woman and her family to develop healthy home behaviours including nutrition and diet, rest, exercise, etc. and to formulate birth and emergency preparedness plan.

## Intrapartum Care

* To ensure safe and positive birth experience for women
* To provide quality evidence-based care around the time of birth

## Postpartum Care

* Provide essential routine postnatal care for mother and new-born care
* Counselling for mother and family on danger signs for both mother and baby
* Early detection and management of postnatal care complications in both mother and baby
* Promote exclusive breast-feeding for the first six months of the baby’s life
* Promote family planning including birth spacing

## 2.2 EXPANDED PROGRAMME OF IMMUNISATION

### WHAT IS THE PROGRAMME?
The Expanded Programme of Immunisation (EPI) covers the planning, implementation and evaluation of an approved vaccine programme based on WHO recommendations. The EPI programme is designed primarily for all children and susceptible adults. The programme is subject to adaptation based on changing global disease environment.

### WHY IMPLEMENT THE PROGRAMME?
Immunisation is the most cost-effective intervention and safest way to prevent many communicable diseases as well as certain cancers (liver, cervix).

### WHAT ARE THE AIMS OF THE PROGRAMME?
To prevent mortality and morbidity due to vaccine-preventable diseases and contribute to health and wellbeing.

To contribute towards the achievement of SDG 3.2, 3.3, 3.8, 3.d and NHSP targets

#### Specific objectives

* To create demand for Immunisations through communication, education and regulations to mandate vaccinations
* To achieve 100% coverage for all essential vaccines
* To ensure vaccine safety
* To achieve accelerated disease control – measles case-based surveillance, measles elimination by 2020
* To reduce immunity gaps in adults

## 2.4 SCHOOL HEALTH PROGRAMME

### WHAT IS THE PROGRAMME?
The School Health Programme is one of the key programmes offered at schools to promote the health and wellbeing not only of schoolchildren but also of the school staff and families. The School Health Programme provides preventive, promotive and when necessary, curative health care and services.

# 2.8 HEALTHY AGEING AND HEALTH OF THE ELDERLY

## WHAT IS THE PROGRAMME?
The programme provides a framework for integrating and coordinating promotive, preventive, curative, rehabilitative and palliative care needs of older persons. It recognizes the factors and work of different agencies beyond health and social care that have a major effect on the health and well-being of persons in older age.

WHO defines Healthy Ageing “as the process of developing and maintaining the functional ability that enables wellbeing in older age”.

WHO refers to Functional ability as the capabilities that enable all people to be and do what they have reason to value. This includes a person’s ability:

* to meet their basic needs;
* to learn, grow and make decisions;
* to be mobile;
* to build and maintain relationships; and
* to contribute to society.

Functional ability consists of the intrinsic capacity of the individual, relevant environmental characteristics and the interaction between them.

Intrinsic capacity comprises all the mental and physical capacities that a person can draw on and includes their ability to walk, think, see, hear and remember. A number of factors such as the presence of diseases, injuries and age-related changes influence the level of intrinsic capacity.

Environments include the home, community and broader society, and all the factors within them such as the built environment, people and their relationships, attitudes and values, health and social policies, the systems that support them and the services that they implement. Being able to live in environments that support and maintain functional ability is key to healthy ageing.

## WHY IMPLEMENT THE PROGRAMME?
The Sustainable Development Goal 3 focuses on engaging all sectors to ensure healthy lives and promote wellbeing of all at all ages. The proportion of elderly persons is steadily increasing in Seychelles. As of December 2018, 14% of the total resident population were persons aged 65 years and above. The projection is that by 2040, this population will have increased to 18%. As people age, their health needs tend to become more complex with a general trend towards declining capacity and the increased likelihood of having one or more chronic diseases. If people can experience the extra years of life in good health and if they live in a supportive environment, their ability to do the things they value will be different. It is essential that older people receive the highest quality care when they need it to remain as healthy and independent for as long as possible.

## WHAT ARE THE AIMS OF THE PROGRAMME?
* To improve health and wellbeing of the elderly, ensuring they maintain their functional ability within age friendly environments
* To contribute towards the achievement of national and global health targets.

## Objectives

* To provide comprehensive accessible quality medical, mental and social care for all elderly
* To provide tailored health promotion and disease prevention interventions
* To promote patient centred care
* To postpone and/or restore functional limitations
* To make PHC facilities more “elderly-friendly”
* To promote a multisectoral approach to ensure age friendly environments and services
* Strengthen data systems to collect, disaggregate, report and utilize data on older persons

## 2.10 PALLIATIVE CARE PROGRAMME

### WHAT IS THE PROGRAMME?

Palliative care is an approach that improves the quality of life of patients and their families facing the problem associated with life-threatening illness, through the prevention and relief of suffering by means of early identification and impeccable assessment and treatment of pain and other problems, physical, psychosocial and spiritual.

Palliative care affirms life and regards dying as a normal process, does not seek to hasten nor postpone death, provides relief from pain and other distressing symptoms; integrates the psychological and spiritual aspects of care; and offers a support system to help patients live as actively as possible until death.

### WHY IMPLEMENT THE PALLIATIVE CARE PROGRAMME?

Universal health coverage includes the full spectrum of services needed throughout life – from health promotion to prevention, treatment, rehabilitation, and palliative care. It follows, therefore, that UHC cannot be achieved without an integrated palliative care programme.

Palliative care relieves the suffering of patients with life limiting communicable and non-communicable conditions such as cancer, dementia, COPD, cardiovascular disease and HIV, and supports their families. NCDs remain a key cause of morbidity and mortality. The Health Adjusted Life Expectancy (HALE) at birth for Seychelles was 65.7 years in 2016, which represents a five-year gap between life expectancy and HALE. This effectively means that citizens will live on average five years in less than perfect health. A significant proportion of this population cohort will require some form of palliative care. To ensure equitable access to palliative care services, care cannot just be provided by specialist services, but should be integrated into primary health care.

### WHAT ARE THE AIMS OF THE PROGRAMME

To provide evidence-based, cost-effective and equitable palliative care services in the continuum of care, across all levels, with emphasis on primary care, community and home-based care.

#### Specific objectives

* To provide comprehensive and integrated palliative care in home and community-based settings
* To support civil society organisations including patient support groups in the provision of services for patients requiring palliative care;
* To provide bereavement counselling

This document addresses only the primary health care component of the National Diabetes Programme.

### WHY IMPLEMENT A DIABETES PREVENTION PROGRAMME?

Health Care Interventions to prevent and control CVD included in this package, including SeyPEN interventions, are delivered at the different primary health care facilities.

* To contribute towards the achievement of SDG 3.4, NHSP targets

### Specific objectives

* 25% reduction of NCD
* 10% reduction in alcohol use
* 10% reduction in prevalence of insufficient physical activity
* 30% reduction in the prevalence of tobacco use
* 25% reduction in the prevalence of raised blood pressure
* 0% increase in obesity and diabetes
* At least 50% of eligible people receiving drug therapy and counselling to prevent heart attack and stroke

# 4 ORAL HEALTH

## WHAT IS PROGRAMME?

The oral health services division provides preventive, promotive, interceptive, curative and restorative oral health services to promote, protect and restore good oral health throughout the life course.

## WHY HAVE THE PROGRAMME?

Good oral health is essential to general health and quality of life.

# 5. REHABILITATION PROGRAMME

## WHAT IS THE PROGRAMME?
Rehabilitation is a fundamental health service for people with a wide range of health conditions, throughout the life-course, and during all phases of acute, sub-acute and long-term care. It is a set of interventions designed to optimize functioning and reduce the experience of disability and/or limitations.

Rehabilitation puts strong emphasis on educating and empowering people to manage their health conditions, adapt to their situation and remain as independent and active as possible.

The Rehabilitation services in the Seychelles include the following fields:

* Physiotherapy
* Occupational Therapy
* Speech pathology
* Audiology
* Orthotics and Prosthetics
* Acupuncture
* Podiatry

## WHY IMPLEMENT THE PROGRAMME?
Rehabilitation services are important for the following reasons:

* An ageing population
* A rising prevalence of NCDs
* An increase in injuries and their consequences
* An increase in developmental difficulties in the population

Anybody may need rehabilitation at some point in their lives whether they have experienced an injury, disease, illness or detriments in functioning associated with ageing.

## WHAT ARE THE AIMS OF THE PROGRAMME?
To assist persons experiencing or are likely to experience limitations in everyday functioning due to ageing or a health condition including chronic conditions or disorders, injuries or trauma to achieve and maintain optimal functioning in interactions with their environments.

To contribute towards overall health and well-being.

### Objectives

* To improve mobility
* To promote functional independence
* To promote social participation
* To facilitate children's learning and education.
* To help persons with disabilities to work and become productive members of society.

Rehabilitation is offered for different health conditions (including acute and chronic diseases), injuries or trauma having an impact on the functions and capacities of an individual. The interventions are offered according to specific needs of the patients and consist of:

* **Self-care interventions** that help the person to carry out daily living activities independently, e.g. eating, dressing, toileting, bathing, taking care of the body, etc.
* **Interventions that improve mobility** and help the person to move around the home and in the community so that he/she can play, go to school or work, etc.
* **Interventions that improve functional independence** to help the person to develop desired real-life skills which are required on a day-to-day basis. * **Interventions that help persons with disabilities** to work and be productive members of society.

# 6. HEALTH PROMOTION

Health promotion is the ‘process of enabling people to increase control over and to improve their health’. Health promotion not only embraces actions directed at strengthening the skills and capabilities of individuals, but also actions directed towards changing social, environmental and economic conditions so as to alleviate their negative impacts on public and individual health.

The key role for health promotion actions are embedded clearly in the vision of the health sector. The vision is the ‘attainment by all people, of the highest level of physical, social, mental and spiritual health, whilst living in harmony with nature. The health sector hopes to attain this vision by relentlessly promoting, protecting and restoring the health and quality of life and dignity of all people in Seychelles, with the active participation of all stakeholders, through the creation of an enabling environment for citizens to make informed decisions about their health’.

A healthy, productive population forms the pillar of the country’s sustained growth and prosperity. Seychelles faces threats to the health and wellbeing of the people. The double, and increasing burden, of rates of infections (like HIV, Viral Hepatitis and pneumonia), as well as chronic non-communicable diseases (like diabetes, hypertension, cancer and cardiovascular disease), combined with social and mental health problems (like injection drug use, psychological disorders), is creating an unprecedented challenge to the sustainability of livelihoods and economic growth.

Most of these key disease conditions have an antecedent chain of risk factors and/or risk behaviours – trends of such risks as obesity, smoking, alcohol abuse, inadequate physical activity and poor nutrition are highly prevalent in Seychelles, with some, such as obesity, showing alarming increases across all age groups, including children, in recent decades.

Many of these common risk factors, as well as certain common disease processes are influenced by the circumstances in which people live. These factors are referred to as **social determinants of health**, and include:
* Housing
* Employment, income and wealth
* Security
* Water and sanitation
* Food
* A stable eco-system
* Sustainable resources
* Social justice and equity

Interventions through health promotion aim to prevent and/or control these common risk factors, preventing and mitigating disease and suffering. Improving social determinants of health through various cross-sectoral advocacy and actions also has the potential to improve health. Effective health promotion provides cost-saving benefits of improving productivity through a healthier population and reducing the need for expensive curative treatment.

## WHAT ARE THE INTERVENTIONS?
Health Promotion interventions involve advocacy, facilitation and mediation to:

* **Build healthy public policy**: by putting health on the agenda of policy makers in all sectors, raising awareness of the health consequences of decisions, and ensuring these responsibilities are accepted. This will be achieved through appropriate legislation, fiscal measures, taxation and organizational change, with good coordination across sectors.
* **Create Supportive Environments**: by generating living and working conditions that are safe, stimulating and satisfying. Systematic assessment of health impacts of a rapidly changing environment – particularly in areas of technology, work, energy and urbanization – will help inform actions to create supportive environments.
* **Strengthen Community Actions**: empowering communities to take ownership and control of actions at community level to set priorities, make decisions, and plan and implement strategies to improve health, including mobilizing community resources, improved self-help, social support and public participation in health matters.
* **Develop Personal Skills**: support personal and social development through providing information, education for health, and enhancing life skills. This allows people to exercise more control over their own health and environments and to make choices more conducive to health. Enabling people to learn, throughout life, to prepare themselves for all of its stages and to cope with chronic illness and injuries is essential. This has to be facilitated in school, home, work and community settings.
* **Reorient Health Services**: the role of the health sector, beyond providing clinical and curative services, must move increasingly towards health promotion, with linkages between the health sector and broader social, political, economic and physical environmental components. Reorienting health services also requires health research as well as changes in professional education and training. This must reflect as a change of attitude and organization of health services focusing on a more holistic approach to care.

## INTERSECTORAL COORDINATION
Health promotion is by its very nature decentralised, and involves the entire population, prompting for a ‘whole-of-society’ approach to improving the Nation’s health and wellbeing, through the empowerment of people and the engagement of, and cooperation with, stakeholders across society.

## HEALTH SECTOR FRAMEWORK FOR HEALTH PROMOTION
Health promotion within the health sector can be seen as a cross-cutting programme, with actors being all health professionals and all patients, and indirectly, the whole population.

Promotive activities in Seychelles are decentralised, and are carried out as part of routine responsibilities of health professionals across all programmes within health, as well as other sectors.

Health Promotion activities are coordinated at the National level by the Health Promotion Unit of the Ministry of Health Secretariat. They are typically targeted at priority diseases and risks – NCDs, reproductive health, including HIV/AIDS, and substance abuse.

The Health Promotion Programme:

* Assists in planning and conducting a wide range of health promotion programmes to address prioritised health issues;
* Offers graphics support in the production of Behavioural Communication and Change materials to programmes to address prioritised health issues;
* Liaises with the local media for all health-related issues and production of health promotion programmes;
* Provides audio visual support to various units of the Ministry of Health through its equipment and media facilities.
* Assists in the dissemination of various educational materials;
* Collects, preserves and makes its resources available both in print and in electronic formats to serve the informational and educational needs of all units;
* Develops and strengthens linkages with other sectors and organizations that have major influences on health and social issues.

# 9. REFERRAL AND LINKAGES

One of the investment priorities of the NHSP is strengthened integrated health care with the overall objective of ensuring clients receive seamless quality services for holistic well-being in line with their expectations.

To meet the health needs of patients/clients, health services are available in a number of facilities at the community, secondary and tertiary levels and are delivered by different health care providers. There is a need for proper referral mechanisms to ensure that patients transition smoothly and safely through the different levels of care and health providers.

> An effective referral system ensures a close relationship between all levels of the health system and helps to ensure people receive the best possible care closest to home. It also assists in making cost-effective use of hospitals and primary health care services (WHO).

A referral can be defined as a process in which a health provider at a one level of the health system, having insufficient resources (drugs, equipment, skills) to manage a clinical condition, seeks the assistance of a better or differently resourced facility at the same or higher level to assist in, or take over the management of, the client’s case. Key reasons for deciding to refer either an emergency or routine case include:

* To seek expert opinion regarding the client
* To seek additional or different services for the client
* To seek admission and management of the client
* To seek use of diagnostic and therapeutic tools
* To meet the client’s expectation to be seen by specialist

A good referral system can help to ensure:

* Clients receive optimal care at the appropriate level
* Hospital facilities are used optimally and cost-effectively
* Clients who most need specialist services can accessing them in a timely manner
* PHC services are well utilized and their role in both prevention and curative aspects is enhanced
* Secondary and tertiary facilities and services are well utilized

## I0. IMPLEMENTATION CONSIDERATIONS

Seychelles is working towards the achievement of Universal Health Coverage (SDG 3.8). UHC means that all people receive the health services they need, including services designed to promote better health, prevent illness and to provide treatment, rehabilitation and palliative care of sufficient quality, while at the same time ensuring that the use of these services does not expose the user to financial hardship.
UHC, like the PHC Package is a social contract defining health services for people along the life-course and addressing burden of disease. The WHO calls on countries to accelerate implementation of UHC and to adopt new and innovative approaches to address their health needs. The report on global progress on UHC underlines the following key points:

* **Primary Health Care is the programmatic engine to for UHC**
    * PHC offers a cost-effective way to address comprehensive health needs close to where people live.
    * PHC offers a platform for integrating programmes across the age cohorts with NCD control programmes
    * PHC leads to community empowerment, social responsibility, and multisectoral links to education, nutrition and sanitation.

* **There is a need for increased spending on PHC**
    * WHO urges countries to spend an additional 1% of their GDP on PHC.

* **Countries need to work on efficiency and quality of services**
    * High and upper middle-income countries, with high service coverage and low financial hardship face the major challenges of sustained efficiency, quality and equity gains.

* **Critical to attaining UHC is a formal monitoring mechanism to assess progress**
    * Stronger country data systems are needed to determine not only the percentage of people using a service but also the need and quality of those services

* **Use a health system approach to implement UHC**
    * Moving towards UHC means that priority actions and investments are needed along each building block of the health system; this requires trade-offs. These trade-offs will be influenced by imperatives that change over time as choices of citizens evolve, the economy develops, the population ages, or the disease burden shifts.

# IMPLEMENTATION OF THE PHC PACKAGE

The Government has committed to the PHC Package; the HCA now has the responsibility of planning, organizing, delivering and monitoring the package of services in the PHC Package. Some key considerations for implementation include:

* Define inputs needed
* The content of the PHC Package will inform the funding, human resource requirements, the health infrastructure and commodities needed
* Many investments will be cross-cutting across different programmes and interventions

### Develop service standards for the provision of key services

* Standards of care are statements that define the key functions, activities, processes and structures and systems required for organisations to be in a position to provide quality services.
* Standards inform clients what to expect
* Standards help ensure equity

### Develop clinical guidelines/protocols

* Clinical guidelines relate to the diagnosis, management and rehabilitative aspects of care for key health conditions/diseases in the PHC Package
* Authorities should monitor adherence to guidelines to ensure patients/clients receive the care they need

### Develop service delivery models adapted to different population groups

* Use flexible, multi-skilled and team-oriented workforce for delivery of care, explore opportunities for task sharing and task shifting
* Develop delivery approaches to reach vulnerable and hard to reach populations
* Build staff capacity with skills in supporting patient empowerment and self-care
* Build quality into the services
* Add value through partnerships with actors in other sectors (education, social affairs etc.)

### Use the Chronic care model

The Chronic Care Model (CCM) is an organizational approach to caring for people with chronic disease in a primary care setting. The system is population-based and creates practical, supportive, evidence-based interactions between an informed, activated patient and a prepared, proactive practice team. CCM is designed to help practices improve patient health outcomes by changing the routine delivery of ambulatory care through six interrelated system changes meant to make patient-centred, evidence-based care easier to accomplish:

* **Delivery system (re)design:** Chronic illness care generally requires multidisciplinary care teams across different programmes. The health care workforce needs a core set of competencies to achieve optimal outcomes for patients with chronic diseases
* **Patient empowerment:** Patients are encouraged to set goals, identify barriers and challenges, and monitor their own conditions
- **Self-management support**: patients are informed, motivated, and sufficiently skilled to carry out their treatment plan on a day-to-day basis.
- **Decision support**
- **Clinical information systems**: Health professionals can use patient monitoring systems to identify patients’ needs, plan care over time, monitor responses to treatment, and assess health outcomes.
- **Community resources**: Communities can provide services that complement and support the care provided in health care organizations. Community organizations can bridge the service gap between the organised clinical care and the real world of patients and their families.

### Monitor and evaluate implementation of programmes

- Define reporting requirements (national and international); determine minimum data sets data sources and reporting pathway for each programme.
- Collect and analyse data, use data for accountability, to inform decisions, for prioritization
- Collect and analyse data to measure core functions of PHC:
    - **Access** - Do patients have affordable, timely access to primary health care
    - **Availability of quality primary health care services** - Are primary health care facilities functioning, with workers who are motivated, competent, and equipped to provide quality services?
    - **People-centred care** - Does the system offer the well-established key functions of primary health care including, first contact accessibility (from the user perspective), coordination, comprehensiveness, continuity, and safety?
    - **Organization and Management** - Does the system appropriately organize and manage important elements of primary health care delivery, including team-based care, supportive supervision, population health management, and use of information systems that aid in monitoring services and continually improve quality?
