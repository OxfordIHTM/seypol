## Executive Summary

The National School Health Policy 2023, developed by the Ministry of Health and Ministry of Education, has a vision that:

> *“All Seychellois children achieve their full development potential in life”.*

And a mission to:

> *“Work with school-going children, their parents/guardians, school staff and other stakeholders to create and maintain a conducive learning environment that supports healthy living, quality education and optimal child development”.*

**The specific objectives are to:**

1. Create and foster sustainable and conducive school environments that protect and promote the health, safety, security and the well-being of children and support learning and optimal child development.
2. Improve availability and access to health education, health skills building, and promotive, preventive and rehabilitative health services to the children.
3. Engage all stakeholders especially school staff and parents to uphold a culture of healthy living, safety, and risk reduction in teaching and learning in the school environment.
4. Integrate any emergency and/or disaster prevention, preparedness and response into relevant sector/school emergency and disaster response plans.
5. Invest to support optimal child development to secure our future, the next generation.

The development of the policy was undertaken in consultation and collaboration with key stakeholders, including the Ministry of Employment and Social Affairs (MoESA) and the Ministry of Youth, Sports and Family (MoYSF), parents, teachers, students, school health nurses and the community.

Successful implementation of the policy will involve joint-actions across stakeholder MDA’s, NGO’s and the general society, teachers, schoolchildren and their parents/guardians.

To liaise with the Ministry responsible for the Youth and Adolescent Health Policy that is being drafted for implementation of Health, Safety and Wellbeing.

# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 The Link between Health and Education

Schools are essential for young people to acquire knowledge, socio-emotional skills including self-regulation and resilience, and critical thinking skills that provide the foundation for a healthy future. Access to education, educational attainment and safe and supportive school environments have been linked to better health outcomes across the life course. In turn, good health is linked to reduced drop-out rates and greater educational attainment, educational performance, employment and productivity.

WHO has long recognized the link between health and education and the potential for schools to play a central role in safeguarding student health and well-being. In 1995, WHO launched the Global School Health Initiative, which aimed to strengthen approaches to health promotion in schools. Among those approaches, pairing children with health services occupies an important place.

**School health services (SHS)**, as defined in this guideline, are services provided by a health worker or other trained staff, to students enrolled in primary or secondary education, either within school premises or beyond.

In all WHO regions, school-age children and adolescents (those aged 5–19 years) experience a range of largely preventable health problems, including unintentional injury, interpersonal violence, sexual and reproductive health issues, communicable diseases, non-communicable diseases and mental health issues. In addition, school-age children and adolescents have positive physical, sexual, psychosocial and neurocognitive health, and developmental needs as they progress from childhood to adulthood. Schools offer a unique opportunity to implement effective health services at scale for children and adolescents.

Social determinants of health define the environment in which children live, grow, and can affect their lives in both positive and negative ways. Access to education, safe and supportive school environments and safe and supportive families have been linked to better health outcomes.

Additionally, it is well known that the risk factors for non-communicable diseases, communicable diseases, and injuries among youth, are internalised during childhood and adolescence. These are caused by behaviours that contribute to unintended injuries and violence; sexual behaviours that result in unintended pregnancy and sexually transmitted infections, including HIV infection; alcohol and other drug use; tobacco use, unhealthy dietary behaviours; and inadequate physical activity.

The school represents an ideal setting to reach a large group of the population, which include children, school staff and families. School is also the ideal environment to promote not just learning, but also to cultivate personal values for growth and development, healthy living habits and teach about safe or unsafe practices.

## 1.2 The Seychelles Context

The Government of Seychelles values child health and well-being, optimal child development, and children achieving their full potential in life. Since independence, the country has provided universal access to free health and education for its citizens. This commitment is further reflected in the Government spending on health and education, which routinely rank amongst the top three amongst annual budgets for all sectors.

Education is compulsory for the first 11 years, up to Secondary 5, and provided free of charge across a network of state schools. Tertiary education is also provided free of charge, but on a merit-based system.

Health care services are also free at the point of use for all citizens, spanning preventive, promotive and curative services. There is a robust school health programme, encompassing nutrition, vaccinations, regular medical and health examinations and a dental health service. There are district health centres, which tend to be close to schools, providing easier access and availability of medical and health services.

## 1.3 Policy Context

The government of the republic of Seychelles remains committed to the advancing of health and education to optimise child growth and development. The **Constitution** stipulates the responsibility of the State to protect, provide and promote the rights of every citizen. **Article 29** outlines the right for every citizen to enjoy the highest attainable standard of health, and **Article 33**, the right to free education of its citizens.

The **National Development Strategy 2019-2023**, which sets forward a promising path for Seychelles, echoing the common desire to see the country develop further, with the Seychellois people owning and actively participating in the development process.

The **National Health Policy (2016)** to further ensure the effective realization of the health rights, with the principle of *“Health for all, Health by all and Health in all”*. This is reinforced through the six strategic directions (SD) in the **National Health Strategic Plan (NHSP) 2022-2026**. 
* **SD2 Protect and Improve Universal Health Coverage** (UHC) supports the strengthening of primary health care.
* **SD3 Improve Health Security** ensuring public health standards and services in educational settings and the community.
* **SD4 Promote healthy populations**, which attempts to address the social determinants of health.

Primary health care is an integral part of the country’s health system. The **Primary Health Care (PHC) Package** sets out the services that the State offers, free at the point of use in state health institutions. The programmes in the PHC Package are organised along a life course approach (maternal, child, school and adolescent, sexual and reproductive health, men’s health and healthy ageing), and are further supplemented by disease-specific programmes for key health conditions (diabetes, cancer, HIV/AIDS, mental health, cardiovascular diseases, and nutrition).

The **Education Act 2004**, and amendments in 2005 & 2017, make provision for the establishment of a comprehensive system of education and training. The Act and amendments reflect universal and national values, which promote the complete development of the person and equip the person to participate fully in social and economic development. MoE is further guided by the **Education Sector Medium Term Strategic Plan 2018-2022 and Beyond**, **Strategic Plan 2021-2024 (raising aspirations and ensuring worthwhile learning outcomes)** which are the main drivers to institutionalize a culture of achievement and excellence for all at all levels of the education and human resource development system.

Additionally, the National School Health Policy 2023 is in line with the **Health Promoting School Guidelines** published by the MoE in 2019. The guidelines are informed by the WHO Ottawa Charter, which proposes five key action areas for health promotion:

1. Build healthy public policy
2. Create supportive environments
3. Strengthen community actions
4. Develop personal skills
5. Reorient health services

Built on the **WHO’s Health Promoting Schools Framework**, the guidelines serve as a strategy to help school communities manage health and social issues, enhance student learning and improve school effectiveness.

A number of other policies internal to the education sector exist:

* **School Nutrition Policy 2008**: improve nutritional well-being; utilizes School Nutrition Action Group (SNAG).
* **Inclusive Education Policy (2015)**: integrate special education needs and disability.
* **National Anti-Bullying Policy and Strategy (2018)**: prevent and tackle institution-based bullying.
* **Whole School Behaviour Management Policy (WSBMP) 2021**: promote discipline and behavior management.
* **Code of Conduct**: adherence expected in all schools.
* **Other Guidelines**: Educational Institutions Emergency Planning Guide 2017, Tuck Shop Breakfast Guidelines 2007, Coronavirus Guidelines 2020, Road Safety Guideline, Fire Safety in School 2005, Teenage Pregnancy Policy 2005, Guidelines for School Lunches 2008.

Seychelles is also committed to the **Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)**, specifically SDG3 (Good Health and Wellbeing) and SDG4 (Quality Education), and the **African Union Continental Education Strategy for Africa 2016-2025** (Agenda 2063).

## 1.4 Policy Development Process

The development of this policy was initiated in December 2016, attraverso the school health programme manager and the World Health Organisation country office (WHO-CO). The process was divided into two phases:

1. **Initial Period (2017-2020)**: First draft developed jointly by the Family Health and Nutrition Directorate of the Health Care Agency (HCA) and Ministry of Education with external technical assistance.
2. **Resumption (2022-2023)**: Following a halt during the COVID-19 pandemic, a new team from the Family Health directorate and the MoH policy unit reviewed the process and resumed stakeholder engagement and finalisation.

# 2. Situation Analysis

## 2.1 Health of the Seychelles Population

### General health

The mid-year population estimate for 2021 is 99,258. About 14% of the population are of school-going age (5-14y).

#### Births and Deaths
* **Births**: 1,665 live births in 2021; crude birth rate 16.8 per 1,000.
* **Deaths**: 925 deaths in 2021; an increase over 2019 and 2020.

#### Life Expectancy
There was an increase in overall life expectancy (LE) in 2020 (77.3y), followed by a significant drop in 2021 (73.5y). A wide gap persists between males and females (8 to 11 years).

#### Fertility
Total fertility rate (TFR) was 2.29 in 2020. Registered live births to girls aged 10-19 years was 193 in 2020 (12% of total live births).

### Child health and health risk behaviours

#### Substance Abuse, including Alcohol
Substance abuse typically starts in late primary to early secondary school. Referrals of minors frequently come from schools, with males accessing services more than females.

| **Boys (<18 y)** | 1 | 44 | 50 | 305 | 59 |

#### Excess Weight in schoolchildren
Childhood obesity is a major public health issue. Trends in excess weight (overweight and obesity) continue to increase in boys and plateau at high levels in girls.

#### Diet and Exercise
According to the GSHS 2015:
* Only 45% of students were physically active (>=60 mins/day, 4 days/week).
* 39% reported eating five portions of fruits/vegetables per day.
* High consumption of sugar-sweetened drinks.

#### Sexual behaviours
40% of students had had sexual intercourse (GSHS 2015), but only 50% used a condom at their last intercourse. 46% reported being bullied in the past 30 days.

#### Oral health of schoolchildren
Dental caries is the predominant condition. In 2010, 87% of children showed visible debris on teeth due to poor oral hygiene.

#### Accidents and injuries
Key causes of death: Road traffic accidents (15-19y) and drownings (5-9y).

#### Mental health
Behavioural disorders are the most common reason for children seeking mental health care, followed by substance abuse-related disorders.

#### Mortality amongst schoolchildren
Average 1.2% of all deaths occur in the 5-19y age group; 72% are boys.

### Socio-economic context

Poverty levels and inequalities were reducing until 2019. COVID-19 has had significant implications on social, economic, and environmental contexts.

## 2.2 Education in Seychelles

Focus is on wellbeing, safety, and academic attainment. There are nine learning areas. Special Education Needs (SEN) teachers support children with learning difficulties.

**Key Curriculum Components for Health:**
* **PSCE (Personal Social and Civic Education)**: Focuses on holistic development, healthy living, sex education, and emotion management.
* **Physical Education (PE)**: Focuses on physical development and healthy lifestyles (60 mins/week).
* **Home Economics**: Focuses on safe food preparation and hygiene.

## 2.3 Health system

Delivered through 17 primary health care centres and one tertiary hospital. 

| Programme | target age-group | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Maternal Health** | Antenatal to Postnatal | Improve maternal and child survival. |
| **Child Health** | Birth to 3-4 years | Immunization, growth monitoring, and developmental assessments. |
| **School Health** | 4 through 17 years | Comprehensive, preventive and promotive health services. |
| **Adolescent/Youth** | 12 through 30 years | Under development; includes students in schools. |

### The school health programme

Implemented by 15 school health nurses. Core activities include:
* **Health screening**: Anthropometric measurements, vision/hearing, and head-to-toe exams (Crèche, P4, S1, S4).
* **Immunisation**: P1 (MMR, DTP, Polio), P6 (HPV), S3 (Tetanus, Polio).
* **Health Education**: Integrated into PSCE curriculum.
* **Staff Wellness**: Screening and stress management.
* **Nutrition**: Nutritionists provide education and assessments.

## 2.4 Education system

Consists of 25 primary, 11 secondary, and 9 private schools, plus special needs schools and tertiary centres (TECs).

### Other Stakeholders

Collaboration includes government organizations, NGOs, private organizations, and parents.

## 2.5 SWOT-analysis of school health programme

| Strengths | Opportunities |
| --- | --- |
| Well-established health/education systems; Fully financed by Gov; Strong collaboration between sectors; Comprehensive school health programme; Subsidized school meals; Use of risk indicator framework. | Support from WHO/UNESCO; Integration of school health into professional training (NIHSS, SITE); SDG 2030 goals; International agreements; Partnership with National Sports Council; Global Standards for Health Promoting Schools. |
| **Weaknesses** | **Threats** |
| Shortage of dedicated nurses/teachers; Reluctance to teach "taboo" topics; Need for better on-site screening infrastructure; Sub-standard sanitation in some schools; Low dedicated PE time (80 mins/week). | Social ills/poverty spilling into schools; High rates of substance abuse and obesity; Teacher attrition; Vaccine hesitancy; Religious contradictions; Unhealthy food marketing; Pandemic-related learning loss. |

## 2.6 Priority issues identified for school health

* Staff diversion to clinic work/ad-hoc activities.
* Social degradation (poverty, substance abuse, violence).
* Obesogenic environment.
* Budgetary constraints following COVID-19.
* Accidental deaths (road, drowning).
* Capacity constraints for teachers and nurses.
* Inadequate parental engagement.
* Suboptimal school infrastructure/hygiene.

# 3. Policy Directions

## 3.1 Vision

All Seychellois children achieve their full development potential in life.

## 3.2 Mission

Work with school-going children, their parents/guardians, school staff and other stakeholders to create and maintain a conducive learning environment that supports healthy living, quality education and optimal child development.

## 3.3 Objectives

1. Create and foster sustainable and conducive school environments that protect and promote health and well-being.
2. Improve availability and access to health education and services.
3. Engage all stakeholders to uphold a culture of healthy living.
4. Integrate health emergencies prevention and response into disaster plans.
5. Invest in optimal child health and development.

## 3.4 Policy values

1. Caring | 2. Development | 3. Excellence | 4. Partnership | 5. People | 6. Productivity | 7. Professionalism | 8. Reward and Teamwork | 9. Service

## 3.5 Principles

1. Holistic government approach to school health
2. A whole-school approach
3. Targeted and timely interventions
4. Meaningful engagement of parents, students, teachers, and community
5. Leave no one behind

## 3.6 Policy statements

### Objective One: Health-supportive school environment
* Maintain cleanliness, safety, and security in all schools.
* Improve nutrition facilities (dining halls, tuck shops).
* Emphasize high-quality, inclusive physical education.
* Phase out non-sanctioned takeaways.
* Enforce minimum distances between schools and health-detrimental product outlets.
* Ensure proper ventilation, potable water, and sanitation.
* Respect privacy and dignity of students and staff.

### Objective Two: Improve access to quality school health services
* Deliver comprehensive services: Promotion, Screening, Immunisation, and Public Health.
* Foster linkages for: Malnutrition, Substance Abuse, Tobacco, Sexual Health, Mental Health, Child Abuse, and Physical Disabilities.
* Apply an equity lens for vulnerable children.
* Adopt Health-Promoting Schools guidelines (2019).

### Objective Three: Engage stakeholders to support school health
* Leverage parents and staff for implementation.
* Bolster community leader support.
* Include health-related topics in curricula (PSCE, PE) and make them examinable.
* Formalize training for PE teachers.

### Objective Four: Education System Security, Safety and Resilience
* Integrate health emergency response into wider disaster plans.
* Periodic risk assessments for fire, flooding, and other hazards.
* Provide skills and equipment for emergency response.

### Objective Five: Invest in Children
* Explore introduction of health promotion officers in schools.
* Ensure adequate numbers of school health nurses and continuous professional development (CPD).
* Ensure adequate funding for staffing, equipment, and infrastructure.
