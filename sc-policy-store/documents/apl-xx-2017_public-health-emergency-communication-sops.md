# Ministry of Health – Seychelles

## Public Health Emergency Communication SOPs

### 1. Purpose

1.1 These crisis communication Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) serve as an additional component of the Risk Communications Plan. The SOPs define key protocols and responsibilities in the event of a public health emergency, but are to be utilized flexibly with specific emergency decision-making procedures as outlined in the general response plan and according to identified needs in the event of any actual or potential public health crisis.

### 2. Overview

2.1 During public health emergencies, early, credible and frequent exchange of information and advice between health officials and the public is fundamental to the success of the response, and should employ a diverse range of techniques and tools ranging from media and social media communications to interpersonal dialogue and community engagement. Rumours, misreporting and other communication risks must also be closely monitored and managed in line with communication best practices. Risk communications is ultimately the responsibility of all responders, partners and management, and all must be familiarized with their respective roles and responsibilities in relation to Crisis Communications, as outlined in the Ministry of Health’s Risk Communications Plan.

### 3. Activation

3.1 The Lead for Risk Communications will sit on the EOC and will work with the Public Health Commissioner in a swift and thorough assessment of the situation to determine whether a communications response is warranted, and if so, the type of communications and scope of commitment needed.

**Action:** The Risk Communications Lead will be responsible for activating the Emergency Communications Committee as appropriate to the situation at hand.

### 4. Guiding Principles

4.1 Five essential practices and guiding principles must inform all communications during a public health emergency: build trust, announce early, be transparent, respect public concerns, and plan in advance.

*   **Build trust:** As the foundation for effective outbreak communication, the most critical objective is to build, maintain, or restore public trust in those responsible for managing the outbreak and issuing information about it. This means establishing effective systems for timely two-way communications with those facing public health risks, including through community engagement and dialogue.
*   **Announce early:** Early announcement contributes to early containment in a situation where every day counts. Equally important, early announcement wins public confidence that authorities are openly reporting what they know when they know it, setting expectations that information will not be concealed. This means being ready to communicate even in uncertainty: aim to communicate fast and communicate first.
*   **Be transparent:** Transparency characterizes the relationship between the outbreak management and preparedness teams and the public. Transparency can be defined as communication that is clear, candid, easily understood, complete, and accurate.
*   **Respect public concerns:** The public is entitled to information that affects their health and the health of their families. Public concerns should be treated as legitimate, explored, and respected as a force that will influence an outbreak’s impact. Today, effective risk communication is viewed as a dialogue between technical experts and the public and rumour management (listening and responding) is fundamental to effective emergency preparedness and response.
*   **Plan in advance:** Outbreak communications must be a part of outbreak management planning from the outset. This means being prepared: having protocols in place as well as coordination mechanisms, with plans, materials and messages ready for dissemination for identified public health risks.

### 5. Emergency Communications Committee (Core Team)

5.1 The Emergency Communications Committee is to be mobilized immediately in the event of an actual or potential public health emergency and should consist of stakeholders with defined core skillsets spanning social mobilization, media relations, health promotion, clinical (for technical guidance), and coordination. Membership should include at a minimum:

 i. Health Promotion Director
 ii. Communications/Public Relations Officer
 iii. Red Cross Representative (Social Mobilization)
 iv. Representative from the Ministry of Tourism
 v. Representative from the Department of Risk and Disaster Management (DRDM) (coordination)
 vi. A clinician (MoH)

Other representatives from the Ministry of Health, other Ministries, DRDM and partners can be asked to serve on the Communications Committee on a case-by-case basis as determined appropriate.

### 6. Role of the Communication Committee

6.1 Once activated, the Emergency Communications Committee must ensure the following actions are taken in the event of any potential or confirmed public health emergency:

* Collaborate and communicate across all appropriate channels to ensure effective ongoing communications to targeted audiences.
* Ensure spokespeople are mobilized, briefed and prepared to communicate with key stakeholders and the general public.
* Develop, test and disseminate concise, clear public health messages and materials.
* Undertake timely communication with the mass media.
* Engage and orient community stakeholders and external partners to support public awareness on the response and preventive/protective public health actions.

6.2 Respective organizational units will be responsible for providing input on their area of expertise and specifically:

#### Committee Lead (DoH Communications Lead)
* Coordinates Communications Response
* Feeds information to management and other internal stakeholders (through IMS, EOC)
* Organizes Communications Committee meetings and assigns tasks
* Knows incident-specific policy, science, and situation
* Reviews plan and mobilizes resources, including human and financial capacity and supplies
* Provides first-level approval for communications content
* Ensures consistency of messages and information
* Obtains required clearance of materials for release to the media on political or sensitive topic- related information
* Determines the operational hours and days and meeting schedules
* Secures working space in the EOC
* Develops media plan
* Communicates media protocols to responders
* Leads on media relations (organizing daily press briefings, schedules interviews, answering queries)
* Produces media content in consultation with the Committee (talking points, media releases, FAQs, info packs)
* Drafts talking points and briefs spokespeople
* Disseminates online content (website and social media)
* Oversees and disseminates media monitoring and reports
* Oversees operations of 141 hotline
* Oversees and disseminates call log monitoring and reports

*Above tasks are performed with the assistance of other members of the Public Relations/Communications Unit staff as appropriate.*

#### Health Promotion Director
* Shares updates, messages and materials with partners
* Engages Communication Focal Persons in the districts
* Engages community partners
* Organizes orientation of community partners
* Supports testing of materials and messages within the community
* Provides feedback from the community to the Committee
* Produces health promotion content in consultation with the Committee (posters, leaflets, guidelines for talks, informatory videos)
* Assists with the dissemination of online content (website and social media)
* Gathers b-roll footage
* Established contact with content producers.
* Identifies relevant areas for reinforced communications and health promotion
* Assist relevant units with production of health promotion materials

#### Clinician
* Works with subject matter experts to create and review situation-specific fact sheets, Q&A documents and supportive materials for health workers / responders
* Provides technical review of communications content and messages
* Liaises with Case Management and other response pillars.
* Provides additional support for technical response on social media.
* Assist with review of health promotion messages
* Assist with production of health promotion materials

#### DRDM Representative
* Establishes communication and coordination protocols with identified partners and stakeholders
* Ensures access to DRDM systems and resources (e.g. SMS)
* Helps organize and facilitate meetings and briefings to provide information and receive input from partners or stakeholders
* Oversees contact lists for key decision makers such as officials, legislators, and special interest groups
* Calls into effect MoUs for priority broadcasts, SMS and other legal mechanisms as needed

#### Tourism Representative
* Provides linkages and liaison with PoE, immigration officials and structures, and supports messaging to international audiences

#### Ministry of Education Representative
* Creates the link to every education institution in the country and looks into the vulnerable population with its connection to all parents and thus touching more than one sector when passing information and taking necessary actions for promoting and protecting lives.

**Other critical staff members to be kept informed:**

6.3 Representatives of the Committee will also meet annually with representatives of other Committees within the Incident Management System to review and test the Emergency Communication SOPs.

### 7. Resumption of normal operations

7.1 The Committee will continue to meet on a regular basis until such time the incident/crisis has been resolved or as the decided by the Incident Manager.

### 8. Media protocols

8.1 The Communications Unit, along with the DRDM, has primary responsibility for managing ongoing communications with media and external audiences throughout an emergency or crisis situation.

8.2.1 Following an emergency event, every effort should be made to facilitate media access to the emergency area.

* Where entry to the emergency area is unavoidably restricted, one representative each from the print, voice and visual media, should be allowed access on the basis that materials should subsequently made available to all accredited media.
* When a no fly zone has been imposed, the media must be made aware. However, every effort will be made to negotiate at least a temporary exception for media access.
* In a major disaster there will be considerable interest from overseas media, who would likely arrive in Seychelles within hours of the emergency occurring. International media should be afforded the same courtesies as local media in terms of media releases and access to media conferences and disaster sites.

8.2.2 During a disaster, accreditation of all media personnel is essential. Photo ID and proof of media employment will be required prior to the issue of a media security pass.

8.3 During a public health emergency, the only people at the DoH authorized to speak to the media are the Public Health Commissioner and his/her assigned deputy, unless expressly authorized by the Commissioner.

8.4 Any media requests should be forwarded immediately to the Communications/Public Relations Unit.

8.5 Any written statements to the press must be approved by the Public Health Commissioner or Incident Manager and sent via the Communications Unit.

8.6 Streamlined clearance processes must be in place: press products should aim to be cleared within an hour of being sent to the first-line reviewer.

**Clearance Workflow:**
`Communications Committee Creates` $\rightarrow$ `HED Director Reviews` $\rightarrow$ `PHC Approves` $\rightarrow$ `Govt / Districts / Media / Partners`

8.7 Committee members must monitor the media. Media monitoring reports should be produced by the Committee Lead and fed back into the IMS at least daily.

### 9. Social media and online

9.1 The Ministry of Health website and social media accounts are managed by the Communications/Public Relations Unit.

9.2 Web and social media messages must be approved by the Communications Committee Lead, under the guidance of the PHC and IMS.

9.3 Ministry of Health staff must conduct themselves with discretion on social media and never share confidential or sensitive information on personal accounts.

9.4 Individual staff should not use Ministry or government logos in their personal social media profiles.

### 10. Communication with PHEOC and NEOC

**a. Risk communication to NEOC when it is activated and operating**

* **Methods of Communication with NEOC:** Includes phone connection, email, and other means to ensure efficient information flow.
* **Information flows:** reports/communications should be as brief as possible. The Communications Lead and PHEOC must decide if a development necessitates an immediate situational report or a later, fuller report.
* **Progress Reporting:** NEOC will provide daily reports to the Communications Lead including:
    * Numbers of Casualties (Total, new, and discharged).
    * Numbers of individuals at Quarantine facility (Total, new arrivals, and home returns).
    * Resources Remaining (Totals and exhaustion forecasts).

### 11. Communication Response Timeline

#### First 12 hours
* Decision taken to activate the Committee.
* Initial alert shared; meeting called to discuss press release, talking points, and key messages.
* Media monitoring begins.
* Nominate and brief spokesperson (initial holding statement).
* Support dissemination of advice to health workers.

#### First 24 Hours
1. **Activate Internal Pathways:** Notify communication partners, engage leadership, share contact lists, and activate public health helpline.
2. **Establish Messages:** Be First, Right, Credible, Empathetic, Promote Action, and Show Respect. Convey empathy, establish facts, commit to response, and tell public what to do.
3. **Utilize Spokespersons:** Use pre-trained individuals.
4. **Share Public Messages Quickly:** Public notices, press conferences, press releases, radio, SMS, social media, and updated web presence.

#### First 48 Hours
1. **Internal Engagement:** National and district level in-person meetings.
2. **Public Information:** Daily briefings in EOC and IMS; continue SMS/Social Media.
3. **Targeted Communication:** Activate channels to community influencers (District Councils, religious leaders, etc.).
4. **Refine Messages:** Ensure messages are short and clear. Examples: "We know how to stop the spread of XXXX."
5. **Data Evaluation:** Revise messages based on new credible data.
6. **Rumour Management:** Log and report rumours via feedback mechanisms.
7. **Media Follow-up:** Press conferences with PHC/Incident Manager followed by media house verification.

#### First 72 hours
* Develop materials highlighting reactivity and action.
* Contact international media for interviews/visits.

#### One Week and Beyond
1. **Continued internal engagement:** Assess effectiveness of district plans.
2. **Community Influencers:** Door-to-door campaigns, town criers.
3. **Actionable Messages:** Reinforce key behaviors through multiple channels.
4. **Layered Communication:** Use Radio, Print, Posters, Helpline, SMS, Dramas, and Social Media.
5. **Village-level reach:** Person-to-person conversations via District Communication Focal Persons.
6. **Two-way dialogue:** Soliciting feedback to change messaging strategies.
7. **Share Victories:** Share stories of survival and local innovation.
8. **Monitor Impact:** Use KAP surveys, RBA surveys, Google trends, and social media analysis.

#### Post-Crisis
1. Evaluate problems and review lessons learned.
2. Revisit Risk Communication Strategic Plan for improvements.
3. Return to pre-emergency planning.

### Appendix 2: Media Guidelines

**Role of the media:** Vital for reaching large numbers quickly. Television and radio are priority for rapid crises.

**What reporters want:** Timely answers, access to experts, and visuals.

**Messaging Guidelines:**
* Remain accessible and respect deadlines.
* Be honest about gathering information (use: "The situation is developing...").
* Do not speculate or release unconfirmed info.
* No "off the record" comments.
* Acknowledge fears and provide actionable steps for the public.

**Interview Do’s and Don'ts:**
* **Before:** Research topic, prepare a 1-minute summary, and identify key positive messages.
* **During:** Be concise, avoid jargon, reframe loaded questions, and don't repeat negative prompts.
* **General:** Never lie, avoid humor during emergencies, and avoid saying "No comment."

**Useful Phrases:**
* "Before we tackle that, I need to add..."
* "I don't have this information on hand but let me get back to you..."
* "While you raise an interesting point, I think the more important issue is..."

### Appendix 3: Hotline Protocols

1. **Number:** 141
2. **Manning:** Established in MoH/EOC; manned by retired health workers/volunteers.
3. **Agreement:** Volunteers must standby for mobilization.
4. **Normal Hours:** 24/7.
5. **Rotation:** Weekly (Monday 9:00 a.m. handover).
6. **Emergency Hours:** Teams of 2-3 rotating every 4 or 8 hours.
7. **Staffing:** 1 logger, 2 answering/forwarding.
8. **Backup:** Backup officer available when lines are busy.
9. **Responsibilities:** Answer calls, record incidents, contact responders, and provide end-of-duty reports.

**Referral Criteria:** Refer calls if the person is unsatisfied, if there are immediate health/safety risks, or in case of emergencies.

**Hotline Form Elements:**
* Date/Time
* Name/Location/Phone
* Query Nature
* Action Taken
* Personnel Name
