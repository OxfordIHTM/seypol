# 1 PREFACE

The WHO Third Generation Country Cooperation Strategy (CCS) crystallizes the major reform agenda adopted by the World Health Assembly with a view to strengthen WHO capacity and make its deliverables more responsive to country needs. It reflects the WHO Twelfth General Programme of Work at country level, and aims at achieving greater relevance of WHO's technical cooperation with Member States by focusing on identification of priorities and efficiency measures in the implementation of the WHO Programme Budget.

This Third Generation CCS draws on lessons from the implementation of the first and second generation CCS, the country focus strategy and the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals Partnership Framework. The CCS is also in line with the global health context and the move towards Universal Health Coverage, integrating the principles of country ownership, a focus on results, inclusive partnerships and transparency and mutual accountability.

**Dr Matshidiso Moeti**
WHO Regional Director for Africa

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Over the past four decades, Seychelles has made remarkable social and economic progress. Its population enjoys free primary health care, which is guaranteed under the country's Constitution. The country is in the high human development category and is classified as a high-income country.

Despite its success, Seychelles faces a number of challenges. Mortality is primarily driven by noncommunicable conditions, particularly cardiovascular and respiratory diseases and cancer that account for 60-70% of all deaths.

The CCS is built around the following five strategic priorities:
1. Communicable and noncommunicable diseases
2. Service quality
3. Organization and management
4. Human resources for health
5. Health for all at all ages

# CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION

## 1.1 Overview of the Country Cooperation Strategy

The Country Cooperation Strategy (CCS) is WHO's medium-term strategic vision to guide its work in and with Seychelles in support of the country's health agenda as defined in the National Health Policy (NHP) and the National Health Strategic Plan 2016-2020 (NHSP).

## 1.2 WHO Corporate Policy Framework: Global and Regional Directions

WHO's global vision for health is defined in the 12th General Programme of Work 2014-2020 (GPW). It identifies six leadership priorities:
- Advancing universal health coverage
- Accelerating the achievement of the health-related MDGs
- Addressing the challenge of noncommunicable diseases
- Implementing the provisions of the IHR (2005)
- Increasing access to medical products and other health technologies
- Addressing the social, economic and environmental determinants of health

# CHAPTER 2: HEALTH AND DEVELOPMENT SITUATION

## 2.1 Political, social and macroeconomic context

The Republic of Seychelles is a small, service-based, island State. The estimated mid-year population in 2015 was 93,419. Seychelles is classified as a high-income country based on the World Bank classification.

## 2.2 Health status (burden of disease)

The overall life expectancy at birth reached 74.2 years in 2015. The major causes of death are diseases of the circulatory system (30.7%), neoplasms (21.5%), and diseases of the respiratory system (12.5%).

### 2.2.2 Communicable diseases

HIV prevalence among the general population is less than 1%. Vector-borne diseases such as dengue, chikungunya and leptospirosis have assumed public health importance.

## 2.3 Health System response

The National Health Policy drafted in 2015 reaffirms the right to health for all citizens. 

### 2.3.1 Governance and management

Since 2014, the public health sector has adopted a new structure to separate functions of entities within the sector.

### 2.3.2 Service delivery systems

There are 17 health centres, 3 cottage hospitals, 1 tertiary hospital, 1 rehabilitative hospital, and 1 psychiatric hospital.

### 2.3.3 Human Resources for Health

Seychelles has a robust staffing situation but is heavily dependent on expatriate professionals.
