# INQUEST INTO DEATH ACT, 2021
**(Act 47 of 2021)**

**I assent**

**Wavel Ramkalawan**
President
8th November, 2021

**AN ACT TO PROVIDE FOR THE CIRCUMSTANCES UNDER WHICH AN INQUEST INTO DEATH MAY BE HELD; TO SET OUT THE PURPOSES OF AN INQUEST; TO IDENTIFY THE CIRCUMSTANCES UNDER WHICH A POST-MORTEM EXAMINATION MAY BE CONDUCTED AND TO PROVIDE FOR POWERS OF FORENSIC PATHOLOGISTS; AND TO PROVIDE FOR MATTERS CONNECTED THEREWITH OR INCIDENTAL THERETO.**

ENACTED by the President and the National Assembly.

## PART 1: PRELIMINARY

### Short title and commencement
**1.** (1) This Act may be cited as the **Inquest into Death Act, 2021**.
(2) This Act comes into operation on such date as the Minister may, by notice published in the Gazette, appoint.

### Interpretation
**2.** (1) In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires —
*   **“body”** means a dead person, and includes any part of a person, whether or not the identity of the person concerned is known when the body part is discovered or is later determined, —
    *   (a) without which no person can live; or
    *   (b) discovered in such circumstances or such state that it is probable that the person is dead,
    but does not include a stillborn child within the meaning of the Civil Status Act, Cap 34;
*   **“cause of death”** includes not only the apparent cause of death as ascertainable by inspection or examination of the body, but all matters necessary to enable an opinion to be formed as to the manner in which the deceased person came by that person's death;
*   **“Chief Officer”** means the person appointed as Chief Officer of Civil Status under section 5 of the Civil Status Act, Cap. 34, and, when the context permits, includes a civil status officer;
*   **“death”** means the permanent disappearance of all evidence of life in a person at any time after the person's birth;
*   **“death qualifying for an inquest”** means any death the circumstances of which are set out in Schedule 1;
*   **“forensic pathologist”** means a pathologist appointed under section 4 as a forensic pathologist;
*   **“inquest”** means a hearing held under Part 6 of this Act by a person specified in section 3;
*   **“investigation”** or a variation thereof means an investigation into a death conducted by a police officer, forensic pathologist, Magistrate or any person empowered to hold an inquest, and includes any post-mortem examination;
*   **“Magistrate”** means a Senior Magistrate or Magistrate and shall be construed as including a person appointed under section 3(2) or 3(4) to hold an inquest into a person's death;
*   **“medical practitioner”** means a person registered as a medical practitioner under the Medical Practitioners and Dentists Act, Cap. 126;
*   **“medical treatment or care”** includes any operation or any surgical, diagnostic or therapeutic procedure;
*   **“Minister”** means Minister responsible for legal affairs;
*   **“pathologist”** means a medical practitioner who has specialised training, experience or academic qualifications in the field of pathology;
*   **“place of custody”** means any place in which any person may lawfully be held, confined, detained, imprisoned or committed;
*   **“Public Health Commissioner”** means Public Health Commissioner appointed under the Public Health Authority Act, 2013;
*   **“video link”** means a technological arrangement whereby a witness... is able to see and hear and be seen and be heard by the Magistrate.

(2) For the purposes of this Act, a person is in **official custody** while the person is —
*   (a) held, confined, detained, imprisoned or committed under any written law in any place of custody;
*   (b) proceeding to or from any such place of custody under the supervision of a police officer or any public authority charged with the person's custody; or
*   (c) being taken into or escaping from such custody.

### Authority to conduct an inquest
**3.** (1) Any Magistrate shall be empowered to hold an inquest into a person's death.
(2) The President may, in consultation with the Chief Justice, appoint a person specified in Schedule 3 to hold an inquest...
(3) A person appointed under subsection (2) shall... take and subscribe to the judicial oath set out in the Official Oaths Act, Cap. 153.
(4) Where the Chief Justice deems it necessary, a Judge may hold an inquest into a person's death instead of a Magistrate.

### Appointment of forensic pathologist
**4.** (1) The Minister responsible for health may appoint a pathologist or suitably qualified medical practitioner as a forensic pathologist.
(2) All appointments made under subsection (1) shall be published in the Gazette.

## PART 2: REPORTING OF DEATHS

### Obligation to report death
**5.** (1) Any person who becomes aware of a death which is, or appears to be, a death qualifying for an inquest shall... report the death to the police.
(2) Any person who, without reasonable excuse, contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence...
(3) Nothing in this section or section 6 shall derogate from any other obligation to report a death under any other written law.

### Reporting of death occurring in official custody
**6.** If a person die —
*   (a) in the course of being arrested or detained...;
*   (b) while the person is in official custody,

it shall be the duty of the person exercising the power of arrest, the person in charge of the place of custody, or the person in whose official custody the deceased was, to submit a report to the Commissioner of Police within 24 hours.

### Body not to be removed
**7.** (1) In the case of a death qualifying for an inquest, no person shall remove, or in any manner alter the position of, a body unless... authorized by a police officer, pathologist, forensic pathologist or medical practitioner, or necessary to prevent destruction/damage.
(2) Any person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an offence...

## PART 3: INVESTIGATIONS INTO DEATH

### Police investigation into deaths
**8.** A police officer who receives information about any death qualifying for an inquest shall —
*   (a) proceed immediately to the location...;
*   (b) cause the body to be examined...;
*   (c) use best endeavours to investigate the death.

### Police officer to provide report to the Attorney General and Magistrate
**9.** (1) The police officer shall submit a copy of the police report and medical report to the Attorney General and a Magistrate.
(2) The police officer shall provide further information and comply with directions from the Magistrate.

### Attorney General or Magistrate may direct forensic pathologist to conduct an investigation
**10.** (1) The Attorney General or Magistrate may direct a forensic pathologist to investigate the cause of death and request relevant medical/healthcare records.
(2) The forensic pathologist shall regularly inform the police, Attorney General and Magistrate of progress.

### Powers of forensic pathologist
**11.** (1) A forensic pathologist may —
*   (a) view the body at the location or a convenient place;
*   (b) require medical practitioners to provide detailed reports and records;
*   (c) request information from public officers or body corporate;
*   (d) request Magistrate to direct police assistance.
(2) Compliance is required notwithstanding any restriction on disclosure imposed by written law.

## PART 4: POST-MORTEM EXAMINATION AND POWERS OF MAGISTRATE TO ORDER BURIAL, CREMATION OR EXHUMATION

### When post-mortem examination is necessary
**12.** (1) A Magistrate may order a post-mortem examination after consulting a pathologist if necessary to establish manner and cause of death.
(2) A Magistrate *shall* order one if the Attorney General makes an application for an inquest.

### Conduct of post-mortem examination
**13.** (1) A post-mortem examination shall be performed by a pathologist, forensic pathologist, or approved medical practitioner.
(2) The practitioner may perform necessary operations, retain body parts, or appoint experts for special analysis.

### Post-mortem examination report and special examination report
**14.** (1) The practitioner shall draw up a report of findings, certify the cause of death, and send it to the Magistrate.
(2) Special examination reports shall also be sent to the Magistrate.
(3) Reports shall be admissible as prima facie evidence at any inquest.
(4) Practitioners may be summoned as expert witnesses.

### Power of Magistrate to order exhumation
**15.** A Magistrate may, after consulting the Public Health Commissioner or via AG application, order the exhumation of a body.

### Power of Magistrate to order release of body for burial or cremation
**16.** (1) The Magistrate has control of the body until an order of release is issued.
(2) The body shall be examined by a medical practitioner/pathologist.
(3) Release occurs after post-mortem conclusion (if ordered).
(4) Order of release may be for burial, cremation, transport out of Seychelles, or lawful retention.
(5) Release is mandatory if the Public Health Commissioner determines it is in the interest of public safety.
(6) Recipients of the certificate must apply to the Public Health Commissioner.

## PART 5: CIRCUMSTANCES UNDER WHICH AN INQUEST MAY BE HELD

### Jurisdiction of Magistrate to hold an inquest
**17.** (1) Jurisdiction exists where —
*   (a) body found in Seychelles;
*   (b) death occurred in Seychelles;
*   (c) cause of death occurred in Seychelles;
*   (d) death occurred on a Seychelles-registered aircraft/vessel;
*   (e) death occurred outside Seychelles on/from aircraft/vessel with AG authorization.
(2) Jurisdiction exists notwithstanding absence of a body.

### Duty of Magistrate to hold an inquest
**18.** (1) A Magistrate *shall* hold an inquest if —
*   (a) person dies in official custody;
*   (b) AG makes an application;
*   (c) death falls under Schedule 2 (unless death was natural).
(2) A Magistrate *may* decide not to hold an inquest if death was natural or not in public interest/national security.
(3) Factors to consider: unnatural/violent nature, public safety, family desire, foreign investigations.
(4) Decisions not to hold an inquest must be reported to the Attorney General.

### Attorney General may make an application for the Magistrate to hold an inquest
**19.** (1) The AG may apply for an inquest if necessary/desirable.
(2) The AG may apply for exhumation with Public Health Commissioner approval.
(3) The AG may apply to re-open a concluded inquest for further investigation.

## PART 6: INQUEST INTO DEATH

### Purpose of inquest
**20.** (1) To inquire into cause and circumstances: identity, how/when/where death occurred, cause, and circumstances.
(2) Findings shall not determine criminal/civil/disciplinary liability.
(3) Magistrate shall record findings at the conclusion.

### Law of evidence not applicable
**21.** A Magistrate is not bound by the law of evidence and may conduct the inquest as they think fit.

### Questioning of witnesses
**22.** (1) Interested persons or those with permission may examine witnesses.
(2) Maker of a report (sec 14) may be summoned as a witness.
(3) Witness may give evidence via video link.

### Inquests to be made public
**23.** Proceedings are open to the public unless justice/security requires exclusion.

### Adjournment of inquest when criminal proceedings are commenced
**24.** (1) Inquest is adjourned if a person is charged with offences (e.g., Penal Code or Road Transport Act) relating to the death.
(2) The President may amend the list of qualifying offences.
(3) Resumed inquest findings must not be inconsistent with criminal proceedings results.
(4) If Magistrate decides not to resume, they must sign the record and notify the AG and Health authorities.
(5) AG must inform Magistrate of the result of criminal proceedings.
(6) "Criminal proceedings" includes preliminary inquiry, trial, and appeal.

### Adjournment of inquest when Commission is appointed
**25.** (1) Inquest is adjourned if a Commission of Inquiry or similar body is appointed.
(2) Resumed inquest may regard findings of the Commission.
(3) If no resumption is needed, the Magistrate signs the certificate and notifies authorities.

### Magistrate's certificate
**26.** (1) Magistrate shall sign a certificate setting out the cause of death.
(2) Certificate must be forwarded within 48 hours to Chief Officer, AG, Ministry of Health, and Public Health Commissioner.
(3) If no inquest was held under 18(2), a certificate based on evidence must still be issued.
(4) Certificates may be issued in the absence of a body.

### Admissibility of evidence in subsequent judicial proceedings
**27.** Evidence from an inquest is NOT admissible in subsequent judicial/disciplinary proceedings, except for perjury charges.

## PART 7: MISCELLANEOUS

### Change of Magistrate
**28.** Another Magistrate may hold, continue, or re-open an inquest if the original Magistrate is unable to do so.

### Exemption
**29.** The President may exempt any person or class of persons from being subject to an inquest.

### Power to make Regulations
**30.** (1) The Minister and Chief Justice may make regulations for the purposes of this Act.
(2) Including procedure/practice for conduct and prescribing required items.

### Consequential amendments and transitional provisions
**31.** (1) Repeals sections of Criminal Procedure Code, Cap. 54.
(2) Repeals sections of Peace Officers Act, Cap. 157.
(3) Inquests started prior to this Act continue under previous laws.

## SCHEDULE 1: DEATHS QUALIFYING FOR INQUEST
1. Identity not known.
2. Unnatural or violent.
3. Resulted from an accident.
4. Resulted from medical treatment or care.
5. Death in official custody related to that custody.
6. Consequence of law enforcement operation.
7. Involving public/commercial transport.
8. Person in possession of immigration permit.
9. On board Seychelles-registered vessel/aircraft.
10. Outside Seychelles on aircraft/vessel (with AG authority).
11. Caused by unlawful act or omission.
12. Manner or cause unknown.
13. Suspicious circumstances.

## SCHEDULE 2: DEATHS FOR WHICH INQUESTS SHALL BE HELD
1. Identity not known.
2. Official custody (related to custody).
3. Consequence of law enforcement operation.
4. Caused by unlawful act or omission.
5. Manner or cause unknown.
6. Suspicious circumstances.

## SCHEDULE 3: PERSONS WHO MAY BE APPOINTED TO HOLD AN INQUEST
1. An Attorney-at-Law with at least 3 years standing.
2. An expert who conducts inquests in another country.
