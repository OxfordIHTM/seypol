## 1. Executive Summary

### 1.1. A brief description summarizing the nature of the problem addressed by the Industrial Policy (IP)
Seychelles Economy has seen a positive growth post the global Covid-19 pandemic. However, the vulnerabilities that we were exposed to during the Covid-19 pandemic, and including the climate change issues continue to hover around the country’s economic stability. 

To that effect the government via its National Development Strategy has prioritized a **Transformative Economic Agenda** with the key theme being economic resilience, diversification and environmental sustainability, and climate change resilience. This Industrial policy (IP) will look to directly to support these two objectives as whilst promoting a healthier nation. This IP goes even a step further, especially taking into account Seychelles SDG commitments and would take the form of a **“Green Industrial Policy”** keeping sustainability as one of the core themes of the IP.

### 1.2. A list of the main objectives of the Industrial Policy
Based on extensive data analysis and consultation with the support of the EQuIP Methodology the following IP objectives were developed:

*   **Objective 1: Increase higher value-added productive activities:** Create an ecosystem that reduces reliance on service-based activities.
*   **Objective 2: Diversify exports to higher value-added products to increase resilience:** Diversify the number of high value-products exported.
*   **Objective 3: Reduce import dependency by promoting local made products on the domestic market:** Empower population to venture into businesses using existing local demand (e.g., cinnamon powder).
*   **Objective 4: Increase the quality of manufacturing jobs:** Address the lack of attractiveness of the sector for local workforce and youth.
*   **Objective 5: Promote the emergence of Green Industry/Circular Industry:** Ensure manufacturing growth is sustainable and protects the environment.

### 1.3. Introduction to the Intervention Logic
Intervention logic spells out exactly how industrial policy objectives, intervention areas and individual instruments relate to one another. The **EQUIP methodology** leads from the understanding of the Macro objectives of government to ensure the IP satisfies broader national development priorities.

### 1.4. A brief summary of agreed / recommended actions to address the problem
1. Integrated funding scheme for industrial development with highly selective selection criteria
2. Public investment for waste to energy facility/company
3. Secure International Funding for the transformation of new industries
4. MIEI Business Incubator for product development and innovation support platform
5. R&D and Innovation Fund
6. Local content program for tourism to create growth markets for local producers
7. Agro-industry linkage program
8. Reform the ITZ programs to maximise the benefits to the local economy
9. Re-skilling program focused on new industries
10. Introduction of technological & innovative vocational trainings
11. Feasibility study and Action Plan for sustainable construction
12. Acceleration program for Renewable energy & energy efficiency
13. Recycled products and new green products market intelligence program

### 1.5. Implementation period
Five years (**2023-2028**), with an initial action plan for the first 2 years followed by a review.

## 2. Introduction

### 2.1. Brief overview of the previous IP
Developed for 2015-2020, focusing on the cost of doing business, access to land, STI, quality of production, export competitiveness, and intellectual property protection.

### 2.2. How does the IP relate to the Government's priorities?
Aligned with the **National Development Strategy (NDS)**, specifically:
*   **Transformative Economic Agenda (TEA):** Promoting high-value activities for diversification and inclusive growth.
*   **Environmental Sustainability and Climate Change Resilience:** Promoting activities that do not harm the environment.
*   **A Healthy Nation:** Promoting locally produced goods and safe working conditions.

**IP Vision:** *“This new Industrial Policy aims at promoting a sustainable economic transformation by diversifying into higher value added & green industries that offer attractive employment for Seychellois.”*

### 2.3. Methodology applied
Guided by the **“Enhancing the Quality of Industrial Policies” (EQuIP)** methodology, a collaboration between UNIDO and the German Development Cooperation (GDC). It follows a systematic step-by-step approach:
`NDS` $\rightarrow$ `IP Objectives` $\rightarrow$ `Intervention Areas` $\rightarrow$ `Policy Instruments`.

### 2.4. Process for the elaboration of this policy
A participatory approach involving 4 national stakeholder consultation events between October 2022 and April 2023.

## 3. IP in the context of the National Development of the Seychelles

### 3.1. Overview of the National Development Priorities
1. **The Transformative Economic Agenda (TEA):** Boosting sustainable growth through diversification (Tourism, Fisheries, Blue Economy, Agriculture, Digital Economy).
2. **A Healthy Nation:** Improving wellbeing, lifestyles, and health systems.
3. **Environmental Sustainability and Climate Change Resilience:** Balancing economic growth with conservation as a SIDS.

### 3.2. The contribution of the Industrial Policy to National Development
*   **TEA:** Stimulating diversification into new productive activities.
*   **Environmental Sustainability:** Focusing on niche high-value products over resource-intensive industries and promoting green goods.
*   **Healthy Nation:** Supporting production of healthy local products and ensuring safe work environments.

#### 3.2.1. Transformative Economic Agenda
Focuses on green diversification and inclusive growth. 

**3.2.1.1. IP contribution to Diversification:** 
Reducing heavy reliance on the service sector (which grew from 76% to 84% of GDP between 2006-2020) and reversing the decline of the manufacturing sector.

**3.2.1.2. IP contribution to Inclusive growth:** 
Targeting redistribution of income toward the lowest 20% by creating high-quality jobs that attract youth and women.

#### 3.2.2. Contributions to environmental sustainability and climate change resilience
Adopting a **“Green Industrial Policy”**. Key targets include:
*   **Greening Construction:** Shifting toward sustainable building materials to reduce non-metallic mineral extraction.
*   **Greening Fisheries:** Shifting toward higher value-added products to reduce pressure on fish stocks.
*   **Circular Economy:** Converting industrial by-products into waste-based products.

#### 3.2.3. Contribution to A Healthy Nation
Promoting local organic food products and reducing industrial pollution.

### 3.3. Harmonization with other relevant national strategies
Harmonized with the National Development Strategy, Tourism Policy, and Seychelles Fisheries Sector Policy and Strategy.

## 4. Industrial Policy Objectives

| Objective | Goal |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Increase higher value-added activities** | Increase overall benefits generated by the manufacturing sector. |
| **2. Diversify exports** | Shift dependency from few resource-based activities to high value-added products. |
| **3. Reduce import dependency** | Empower local manufacturing to satisfy existing domestic demand. |
| **4. Increase quality of jobs** | Provide high-quality roles with better salaries and conditions to attract locals. |
| **5. Promote Green/Circular Industry** | Reduce environmental footprint and actively contribute to sustainability. |

### 4.1. Objective 1: Increase Higher Value-added productive activities
**Diagnosis:** MVA share in GDP has remained stagnant between 5% and 8%. Benchmarks (Mauritius, Sri Lanka) suggest potential to reach 11-16%.
**Priority Sectors:** High value food, cosmetics, marine biotechnology, furniture, waste-based products, and sustainable construction materials.

### 4.2. Objective 2: Diversify exports to higher value-added products
**Diagnosis:** Export basket is highly concentrated (Top 3 products = >90% of exports). Canned tuna exports show negative growth (-7%).
**Priority Sectors:** High value food products and marine biotechnology.

### 4.3. Objective 3: Reduce Import dependency
**Diagnosis:** Manufacturing trade deficit exceeded 1 billion USD in 2020. Manufactured imports make up 98% of total trade in goods.
**Priority Sectors:** High value food, cosmetics, furniture, and sustainable construction materials.

### 4.4. Objective 4: Increase the quality of manufacturing jobs
**Diagnosis:** Industrial jobs are the lowest in share (8-10%) and average salaries are lower than in tourism.
**Focus:** Creating roles with higher skill requirements to attract youth and promote women's economic empowerment.

### 4.5. Objective 5: Promote the emergence of Green Industry / Circular Industry
**Diagnosis:** High domestic material extraction (80.4% non-metallic minerals for construction). 
**Priority Sectors:** High-value fish by-products, sustainable construction materials, and waste-based products.

### 4.6. Synergies and trade-offs
*   **Synergy:** High value-added activities $\rightarrow$ Higher margins $\rightarrow$ Better wages (Obj 4) $\rightarrow$ Export diversification (Obj 2).
*   **Trade-off 1:**- Increased productivity vs. Environmental impact (addressed by the "Green" focus).
*   **Trade-off 2:**- Export diversification vs. Import dependency (addressed by using local raw materials).
*   **Trade-off 3:**- Value addition vs. Job quality (addressed by focusing on complex, high-skill roles).

## 5. Intervention Areas & Policy Instruments

### 5.1. Intervention Area 1: Financing for New Industries Available
**Problem:** Lack of financing for new manufacturing investments. Loans are heavily biased toward Tourism and Construction.
**Instruments:**
1. **Integrated funding scheme:** Consolidated, highly selective funding for high-value activities.
2. **International Funding:** Tapping into green financing mechanisms.

### 5.2. Intervention Area 2: Technology, Innovation and R&D support
**Problem:** Low GERD (0.4%) and lack of a National Innovation System.
**Instruments:**
1. **Enhanced MIEI Business Incubator:** Shared production facilities, testing, and certification.
2. **R&D and Innovation Fund:** Supporting start-ups and STEM expertise.

### 5.3. Intervention area 3: National Value Chains Developed
**Problem:** Strong dependency on foreign suppliers and lack of collaboration between local farmers and hotels.
**Instruments:**
1. **Local Content Program for Tourism:** Mandatory local content requirements.
2. **Agro-industry linkage program:** Linking raw material producers with processors.
3. **Reform ITZ programs:** Attracting companies that create strong national linkages.

### 5.4. Intervention Area 4: Skills & Practical Training
**Problem:** Lack of specialized technical expertise for modern industrial production.
**Instruments:**
1. **Extended Re-skilling program:** Aligned with priority high-value sectors.
2. **Innovative Vocational Trainings:** Updating curricula at technical schools.

### 5.5. Intervention Area 5: Production and Use of Green Goods Enhanced
**Problem:** High reliance on extractive materials and fossil fuels.
**Instruments:**
1. **Sustainable Construction Action Plan:** Feasibility study for alternative materials.
2. **Renewable Energy Acceleration:** Preferential tariffs/solar self-use for new industries.
3. **Recycled Products Market Intelligence:** Developing markets for waste-based goods.

## ANNEX 3: Additional Potential Policy Instruments
*   **Financing:** Subsidised interest, VAT/Tax exemptions, Angel Investors.
*   **Tech/Innovation:** National R&D policy, mapping National System of Innovation.
*   **Value Chains:** Energy price reduction, incentives for local packaging.
*   **Skills:** Lifting minimum wage to attract workers, awareness campaigns.
*   **Green Goods:** Levy on concrete usage, preferential land for green industry.

## ANNEX 4: CRITERIAS FOR SECTOR SUPPORT
1. High value added (input-output).
2. Use of local raw materials.
3. Not manual labor dependent.
4. Eco-friendly/Waste-derived.
5. Supports resource efficiency (e.g., solar panels).
6. High-grade value for raw products.
7. High value food/agro products.
8. Supports greening of other sectors.
9. Usable by local tourism.
10. Marine biotechnology.
11. Furniture/Interior decoration.
12. Sustainable construction materials.
