# 1. INTRODUCTION

## 1.1 International Perspective

In March 2010, the United Nations General Assembly resolution 64/2551 proclaimed 2011-2020 the Decade of Action for road safety with the goal to stabilise and reduce the forecast level of road fatalities around the world by increasing activities at national, regional and global level.

On the launch for the Decade of Action for Road Safety on 11th May 2011, the UN Secretary-General Ban Ki-Moon stressed that:

> "The Decade of Action for Road Safety can help all countries drive along the path to a more secure future... Today, partners around the world are releasing national or citywide plans for the Decade, hosting policy discussions and enabling people affected by road crashes to share their stories widely. Now we need to move this campaign into high gear and steer our world to safer roads ahead. Together, we can save millions of lives."
The global plan for the Decade has 5 major pillars that all countries need to address:

1. **Road safety management**
This pillar focussed on the strengthening of institutional capacity to further national road safety efforts. It is also to create multi-sectorial partnership from government, private sector and the non-governmental organisations. It encouraged national government to develop national road safety strategic plans, conduct research, monitor and evaluate current situations as well as create regional road safety instruments.

2. **Safer roads and mobility**
Pillar two highlights the need to improve safety of road networks to benefit all road users, especially vulnerable road users. It raises the inherent safety and protective qualities of road networks through improved safety-conscious planning, design and construction. It encourages land use planning to respond to the safe mobility needs as well as encourages research and development for safer roads and mobility.

3. **Safer vehicles**
The third pillar addressed the need to improve vehicle safety and encourage harmonisation of standards, consumer information schemes and incentives to accelerate the uptake of new technologies such as car seat belts, information on safety performance of vehicles as well as crash avoidance techniques.

4. **Safer road users**
Pillar four highlighted the need to develop comprehensive programmes to improve road user behaviour including the development of public awareness programmes and better laws to ensure the use of seat belt and helmet wearing, reduce drink-driving, speed control and child restraints.

WHO statistics (2015) in *Global Status Report on Road Safety* shows that road safety laws improve road user behaviour and reduce traffic crashes, injuries and deaths related to the concerns above. These are:
# Reducing Drink-Driving

* Enforcement of drink-driving law around the world could reduce alcohol-related crashes by 20%. Setting and enforcing legislation on blood alcohol concentration (BAC) limits of 0.05 g/dl can significantly reduce alcohol-related crashes. For young and novice drivers this should be less than 0.02 g/dl as they are at a much increased risk of road traffic crashes when under the influence of alcohol as compared to older, more experienced drivers. In Seychelles the law states that 0.035 g/100ml or 35 mg/100ml of breath which is within the limit set.

*Road safety week banner sponsored by the Rotary Club of Victoria*

# Reducing Speed

* Setting national speed limit is an important step in reducing speed. An adult pedestrian has less than 20% chance of dying if struck by a car at less than 50km/h but a 60% risk of dying if hit at 80km/h. For every 1km/h reduction in average speed, there is a 2% reduction in the number of crashes.

*Road safety week banner sponsored by the Rotary Club of Victoria*
## Motorcycle Helmet Use and Quality

* Motorcyclists are more exposed to injuries during an accident, but wearing a helmet can reduce the risk of death by almost 40% and the risk of severe injuries by approximately 70%.

*Road safety week banner sponsored by the Rotary Club of Victoria*

## Seat-Belt Use

* Seat belt use reduces the risk of fatality among drivers and front-seat passengers by 45-50% and the risk of minor serious injuries by 20-45%. For rear-seat passengers, seat-belts can reduce fatal and serious injuries by 25% and minor injuries by up to 75%.

## Child Restraint Use

* Mandatory use of child restraints can reduce fatalities as a result of a crash by approximately 90% amongst infants and between 54% and 80% among young children.
* Children are safer seated in the rear of a vehicle than in the front.

## 5. Post care response

The fifth pillar promotes the improvement of emergency treatment and longer term rehabilitation for crash victims. These include pre-hospital systems, hospital trauma care and rehabilitation, single nationwide telephone number and support to injured and bereaved persons.
## 1.2 National Perspective

The number of road accidents on the Seychelles roads continues to be a great concern. In 2015 the country recorded a total of 2,039 accidents with 12 fatalities. In 2016 the total number of road accident increased to 2,199 and the country recorded the highest number of fatalities (14) for the past three years. However, in 2017, the country recorded a significant decline in the total number of road crashes which was at 1,936, thus a decline by 263 crashes as compared to 2016. The year 2017 also saw a significant decline of in the number of road fatalities to 8. The country has also seen an increase in the number of motorcycle and scooter accidents.

The Road Transport Act has been revised to include regulations on drink driving, seat belt law for both front and rear seat occupants, child restraints and mobile phone use while driving and motorcycle helmet laws to be in line with best practices and the Global Plan’s 5 pillars under the UN Decade for Road Safety.

According to the WHO Global Status Report on Road Safety (2015), the BAC limit for the general population in Seychelles is 0.08g/dl which is 0.03g higher than the recommended limit by WHO and there are no set limit for young and novice drivers. It stated that 60% of road traffic deaths in Seychelles were under the influence of alcohol. The rate for wearing helmet by motorcyclists is 90% for both drivers and passengers and the rate for wearing seat belts is 40% in the front seats and 2% rear seats. All statistics in the Global Status Report is 2013 statistics provided by the Department of Land Transport, Seychelles Land Transport Agency, Ministry of Health and the Police.
Further, for the past few years the national **‘Arrive Alive Campaign’** has been established to urge the general public to travel safely from their homes and back, with tips on how to be safe on the road at all times. The Seychelles Police have been equipped with Alco meters (Breathalysers) to test alcohol in drivers and also with portable laser speed guns to continue with the enforcement of laws on the road and ensure that offenders are fined appropriately.

With the aim of improving road safety in Seychelles, the government has seen the importance of developing a more comprehensive road safety policy document than the 1997 Road Safety Policy Statement for Seychelles. In 2003 a Road Safety study was conducted by the Transport Research Laboratory from United Kingdom. This document was used as the main document to improve Road Safety. However, based on the study, no policy statements, National Strategy or Targets were prepared to lead road safety awareness in Seychelles. With no policy or strategic direction, it has been difficult to monitor Road Safety issues and assess effectiveness of any measures being taken to improve safety.

Nonetheless there are several partners in road safety which are working in close collaboration to make sure that road safety measures are upheld in Seychelles.

## 2. SITUATIONAL ANALYSIS

### 2.1 Current Situation

#### Engineering Measures
There are several measures which have been implemented, e.g. white lining, traffic signs, crash barriers, pedestrian railings, road maintenance, road widening, construction of footpaths, etc…

However, there is a necessity to re-assess the monitoring and evaluation measures to fully understand who are involved, why crashes are occurring and where it is taking place.

**New Two Lane Road in Victoria (Sea Front), including Pavement Construction**

#### Education
For several years public education has been extremely limited to TV spots during festive seasons and national events. Road users are reminded to be safe on the road and to take precautionary measures to avoid accidents. Road safety education is being undertaken in state schools through
the PSE school curriculum, however it is felt that there is still not enough road safety education in school by concerned agencies.

## Enforcement
The level of seat belt enforcement is still low with 40% for front seats and 2% for rear seats (Police data, 2013). Speed enforcement is also low although the Police are equipped with Laser Speed Detectors and Alco meters to test alcohol in drivers. Over the past two years the Police Traffic Section has intensified spot checks on vehicles and drivers to determine if vehicles are road worthy, by increasing road patrol with road side random alcohol testing. However, it must be noted that it is a legal requirement for all drivers involved in an accident to be tested for alcohol.

## Health Care (Injury Management)
Tremendous improvement has been achieved in the health care system especially in the post-crash response and service. More ambulances have properly trained paramedics. The emergency accident centre has been upgraded with state of the art equipment, trained staff in the Trauma Unit and proper contingency emergency plan to attend to any accidents.

## 2.2 Way Forward

Seychelles is a signatory to the **Decade of Action for Road Safety 2011 to 2020**, therefore it is important that Seychelles honour its commitment and move forward to improving road safety in the country. The preparation of the Road Safety Policy will bring more cohesion amongst partners to find collective solutions to address road safety issues in Seychelles.

The Road Safety Advisory Committee was revived in January 2016 and is comprised of partners from government, private sector and NGOs, with the aim of creating an inter-governmental platform to collectively undertake policy development, projects and programmes on road safety in Seychelles.
It is important that the enforcement of the laws and regulations are reviewed to ensure that legislations are enforced. Enforcement officers must also be retrained and empowered to fully participate in the process and be aware of the benefits of proper and effective enforcement.

Necessary financial resources must be made available specifically for Road Safety activities, education, engineering measures and improvement in pre-cash sensitization.

The health sector must maintain the level of post-crash services and continue to improve in this area.

Research, data management and analysis is extremely important if we are to fully understand the causes of road accidents in Seychelles, therefore it is necessary to introduce a new computerized system to record and analyse all road traffic accidents. Such system must take into consideration all the stakeholders within the government, private sector and civil society.

# 4. POLICY STATEMENT AND STRATEGIES

## 4.1 SAFER ROADS
### 4.1.1 Land Use Planning

**Current Status**

Seychelles is known for its mountainous road networks on Mahé, Praslin and parts of La Digue. Many of the road networks lack sufficient pedestrian walkways. The lack of designated walkways for pedestrians, forces many pedestrian’ activities to occur by the side of the road carriageway. The lack of street lights in certain areas further places potential pedestrian and cyclist at risks, or other vehicles parked along the road side.

The use of public buses is very high in Seychelles. Bus stops are found on all road networks on Mahé and Praslin. Although in recent years there are many bus shelters which have been built, the majority of bus stops are painted on the road with limited or no waiting space for passengers, which potentially leads to safety issues for passengers, particularly in rural areas where roads maybe narrower.

The Seychelles Strategic Land Use and Development Plan (2015) and the Victoria Master Plan (2015) have made provisions for land use planning in Seychelles and the Capital Victoria respectively. Land use planning is vital for the role it plays in allocation of land for development of land transport infrastructures such as parking, road networks, walking, bus stop, buses and cycle lanes.

**Policy Statement**

The Government shall include land transportation infrastructure in all land use planning in Seychelles.

**Strategies to Implement Policy**

* Develop a legal and planning framework to obtain additional land for road developments.
* Strengthen the mechanism for the acquisition of land for road widening and bus shelters for the benefit of the society.

### 4.1.2 Road Safety Design and Safety Engineering

#### Current Status
SLTA is the leading organization in regards to road design and safety. The Agency has adopted several safety measures such as white lining, traffic signs, crash barriers, street lights, cat’s eyes, raised speed bumps, road maintenance, road widening and construction of footpath etc. However there are no proper assessments of such measures or proper analysis of accidents to determine their effectiveness, i.e. a before and after assessment of the current situation.

The majority of road networks in Seychelles are very old dated to the colonial period, thus require major renovations to modernise it and ensure that new safety engineering measures are included. Poor road conditions in certain areas which comprises of potholes, poor surfacing and insufficient road marking is at times common hazard to motorists and pedestrians. Sufficient road signs on secondary road are non-existent. Further, motorists are creating safety hazards on the roads which include speeding and illegal parking posing a risk to themselves, other motorists and pedestrians.

**Re-development of Anse Aux Pins Road**
# Policy Statement 1
The Seychelles Land Transport Agency shall continue to promote road safety engineering as a compulsory mechanism for designing and implementing road design or traffic management improvements that will cost-effectively reduce road crashes with the best interests of motorists, pedestrians, particularly the most vulnerable road users.

# Policy Statement 2
The Department of Land Transport shall establish standards of best practices in the construction of roads and its amenities to ensure safety of all road users.

## Strategies to Implement Policy

* Monitor the safety of all road networks - public and private roads, identify locations with high incidence of crashes and carry out corrective measures.
* Review the capacity requirements of all road networks and undertake upgrading as and when necessary, and keep in mind the Seychelles Strategic Plan.
* Design, construct and maintain all road networks nationally.
* Ensure that proper standard, guidelines and specifications for asphalt surfacing of roads are respected and markings are done simultaneously.
* Utility companies shall ensure that overhead lines along road networks are set at the correct height and are properly monitored to not pose a danger to motorist.
* Ensure that all new road constructions are approved by the Planning Authority and the Seychelles Land Transport Agency and Department of Transport closely monitors their construction.
* Ensure that the design and construction of all road networks take into account the needs of the vulnerable and physically disadvantaged persons, cyclists and pedestrians.
* Ensure that the highest priority is given to developing projects which aim at implementing measures to reduce inappropriate speed and reduce the risk for vulnerable road users.
* Remain committed to the development and implementation of safety conscious standards and guidelines for the use of roads, intersections, and road furniture and traffic management schemes.
* Implement engineering measures to increase road safety.
* Ensure that large vehicles are only used where it is safe to do so, or upgrading sections of roads as required prior to introducing the service.
* Ensure safety audit are carried out on all new roads to be constructed and also when undertaking upgrading of existing roads.
* Undertake safety measures for drop off and collection points at all schools

## 4.2 SAFER VEHICLES
### 4.2.1 Vehicle Safety, Inspection and Roadworthiness

#### Current Status
All vehicles are imported in Seychelles. All imported vehicles and second hand vehicles to be used in Seychelles are licensed by Seychelles Licensing Authority upon completion of a roadworthiness test by the Vehicle Testing Station (VTS). After which the test is repeated yearly to determine the vehicle roadworthiness and safety. Upon completion of the roadworthiness test all vehicles must have at least a third party insurance cover.

#### Policy Statement
The Government shall continue to have appropriate mechanism in place to ensure that acceptable vehicle design, standards and specifications are in conformity with the needs of various population groups and to deter the use of unsafe vehicles on public roads through the enforcement of proper vehicle standards and inspection procedures.
# Testing of Vehicles at VTS

## Strategies to Implement Policy

* Adopt national standard for importation of new vehicles based on recognised international standards such as European Union (EU), British, Japanese, Australian, American standards and South African Bureau of Standards (SABS).
* Develop standards for new vehicles that enter into the country especially in regards to safety features through the Department of Land Transport, Road Transport Commission and VTS.
* Amend policies which regulate and control the importation of used and second hand vehicles and spare parts.
* Ensure that second hand vehicles are roadworthy through yearly testing before being granted a road license and insurance.
* Intensify vehicles inspections through the VTS and spot checks by the Seychelles Police.
* Modernise the computerisation of all vehicle registration to encourage up to date data on road transport fleet, vehicle types for comparison with safety violation, evasion of road transport permit and insurance.
* Implement the Demerit Point System to ensure compliance on the roads.
* Ensure that buses specifications are in line with the road networks. This will ensure that large buses are only used where it is safe to do so, or upgrading sections of roads as required prior to introducing such services.
* Emphasise and encourage all new vehicle to have airbag and side protection.
* Introduce reflective number plates for better enforcement by cameras.
* Upgrade the VTS and decentralise its services to other islands.
* Ensure that penalties collected from offences should return to the road transport agencies to upgrade, maintain equipment, and implement road safety projects and measures.
* Restrict and regulate the use of pickup trucks to carry passengers at the rear.
* Encourage construction companies that use pickup trucks to carry staff to invest in staff buses.
* Ensure that only specialised vehicles are allowed to transport plants e.g. excavators.
* Written-off vehicles should not be used on the road and should be de-registered by the Seychelles Licensing Authority.
* Develop guidelines for repairable written-off vehicles before it can be registered and licence.
* Ensure that vehicles involved in road accidents should be properly assessed by the VTS and pass all safety tests before being allowed back on the road.
* Intensify checks at the point of entry to ensure that vehicles meet the standards set and that they comply with regulations and policies in force.
* Encourage Insurance companies to consider cheaper premium for vehicles which have been well maintained, in good conditions and have low emission.
### 4.2.1 Vehicle Weight Control

#### Current Status
It is recognized that vehicles react differently when it is overload, thus making it less stable, difficult to steer and takes longer to stop. Vehicle overloading can also cause strain on vehicle tyres which can cause tyres to overheat and blow. Vehicle overloading causes excessive wear and damage to roads, bridges and pavements if not properly monitored.

The importation of used oversize commercial vehicles designed for the transportation of goods and construction by registered businesses can be done under strict guidelines. Requests are submitted to the Road Transport Commission prior to importation.

The commercial motor vehicle policy are as follows:-

1. Pickup truck and van less than 1500 Kg must be new and right hand drive.
2. Pickup truck and van of 1500 Kg and less than 2999Kg must be right hand drive and must not be older than 3 years.
3. Pickup Truck and van 3000 Kg and above: must be right hand drive and must not be older than 5 years old.
4. Plant Vehicle and machinery (as per Annex 1, classify under HSCODE 84.26/84.29/84.30) must not be older than 10 years old; those that have passenger seats in front must be right hand drive.
5. Used motor Vehicles imported by business for commercial use may, in exceptional circumstances, be transferred to an individual prior to the expiration of the 2 year stopper. The importer is not permitted to import other second hand vehicle of similar tonnage for a period of 2 years.
6. In line with Trades Tax (Amendment of schedule 3) Regulations, 2009, this policy defines special purpose motor vehicle (under chapter 87.05) as one manufactured to carry out specific tasks and are other than those principally designed for the transportation of persons or goods, (for example breakdown lorries, crane lorries, fire-fighting vehicles, concrete-mixer lorries, road sweeper lorries, praying lorries, mobile work).

## 4.3 SAFER DRIVERS & PASSENGERS
### 4.3.1 Vehicle Licensing and Registration

#### Current Status
To be able to drive a motor vehicle on the road a motorist must be granted a permit by way of a license issued by the SLA. A driving test must be undertaken by the Police Traffic Section upon completion of a period of driving lessons provided by an approved driving instructor. A driving test is designed to ensure that at least a minimum standard of competence is achieved by a driver before being allowed unrestricted access to the public roads, subject to other criteria in respect of the vehicles or classes of vehicles. A motor vehicle can be driven by a person of 18 years who has a driving license and a motorcycle by a person of 16 years.
There is perception by the general public and authorities that drivers are reckless, arrogant and show lack of consideration for others. Subsequently statistics shows that the main causes for road traffic crashes are related to driver’s reckless behaviour, the abuse of alcohol, and speeding.

In accordance with the **Road Transport Act, 52 (1), revised edition 1991**, ‘licences to drive motor vehicles shall be issued separately in respect of private vehicles, motor cycles, public vehicles, (other than taxis), commercial vehicles, omnibuses, tractors, rollers and mobile cranes: Provided that a licence to drive a private vehicle, shall entitle the holder thereof to drive a commercial vehicle the carrying capacity of which it does not exceed 2000 kilograms.’ The Seychelles Licensing Authority is the organisation which issues driving licenses in Seychelles.

Driving licences are categorized into 7 classes, and these are:

* **Class 1**: Motor Cycle
* **Class 2**: Private Vehicles including Commercial Vehicles up to 2000 kilograms unladen weight
* **Class 3**: Taxi
* **Class 4**: Commercial Vehicles up to 5000 kilograms unladen weight
* **Class 5**: Private Omnibus
* **Class 6**: Public Omnibus
* **Class 7**: Any other Vehicles

Driving licences are now being issued for a period of ten years irrespective of the class of licence.
# Policy Statement

## 4.3.5 Child Passengers

### Current Status
The Road Transport Regulations have been amended to include compulsory use of car seat for children in Seychelles. The Road Transport Act, also make provision for children of less than 10 years to not be driven on motor-cycles. Further, the Road Transport (Public Service Vehicle) Regulations (Part II Construction and Equipment) under the Road Transport Act states that:

(a) A child under five years of age and not occupying a seat shall not be counted as a passenger; and
(b) Three children over five years of age and under ten years of age shall be counted as two passengers.

This definition is to decide if a vehicle is overloaded with passengers or not.

## 4.4 EMERGENCY RESPONSE AND VICTIM SUPPORT

### 4.4.1 Emergency Medical Response and Service to Accident Victims

#### Current Status
Emergency medical response is defined as a network of services coordinated at national or regional level to provide aid and medical assistance from primary response to care. It involves personnel trained in rescue, stabilization, transportation and advanced treatment of traumatic or medical emergencies. The chance of a person surviving a road accident may be greatly enhanced if the person is attended to quickly on the scene by a first aider. The quality of post-crash treatment is equally important in considering whether or not a crash victim lives or dies. To achieve quick response time and good quality medical care, requires a higher degree of co-ordination between
the many agencies including Health, Fire and Rescue Service, Police, Red Cross and many others, involving the public, private and NGOs.

Although there has been tremendous improvement in the health sector in regards to response time and emergency services, there are many more services that needs to be improved, for example, ambulance services, attitudes of ambulance drivers, training for emergency personnel and coordination amongst sectors to name a few.

Presently, paramedics are trained for a period of 3 years, during which a combination of American and European curriculum is used. The nurses at the trauma centre are well trained, however, often some senior trauma nurses are pulled out of the trauma centre to rotate in other sections thus creating inconveniences as junior staff are often unprepared to deal with emergencies.

Further there is a need to train ambulance drivers in defensive driving and stress on the correct attitude for drivers. There is a need to increase training for all emergency responders, training of crowd control at the scene of an accident and introduce better and modernised equipment used by responders to be used in emergency cases. Sensitisations amongst the general public particularly the motorists needs to be enhanced as often they are not courteous towards emergency service vehicles on the road.

### 4.9.1 Transportation of Dangerous Goods

#### Current Status
Hazardous products that are transported by road in Seychelles include bulk petroleum products, bulk hazardous substances, waste and containers in transit. There is a lack of classification and labelling of dangerous substances transiting on the roads in Seychelles. The Traffic Police and Highway Patrol Unit monitor movements of vehicles transporting hazardous materials hence prevent insecure loading of such materials.
At the moment there is no contingency plan, policy, guideline in regards to road accident involving vehicle carrying hazardous materials in order to prevent intoxication or other damages to humans or the environment. With the increasing volumes and types of hazardous materials being distributed and transported and with the increasing level of road traffic and population densities, it is essential to work on a policy guideline/legislation. **Hazardous materials** is any item or agent either biological, chemical, physical which has the potential to cause harm to human, animals, and the environment.

### The United National Classification of dangerous goods

* **Class 1:** Explosives
* **Class 2:** Gases
* **Class 3:** Flammable liquids
* **Class 4:** Flammable solids; substances which on contact with water emit flammable gases
* **Class 5:** Oxidizing substances and organic peroxides
* **Class 6:** Toxic and infectious substance
* **Class 7:** Radioactive material
* **Class 8:** Corrosive substances
* **Class 9:** Miscellaneous dangerous substances and articles

#### Policy Statement 1
The Government shall ensure the development of policy guidelines to ensure the safe transportation of dangerous goods on all roads.

#### Policy Statement 2
The Government shall ensure the development of policies for characteristics, specifications and equipping of motor vehicles and trailers to be used in the transportation of hazardous materials.

#### Policy Statement 3
The Government shall ensure the safe transit of hazardous materials.
# Seychelles Petroleum Company Head Quarters

## Strategies to Implement Policy

* Create a National Emergency System for Transport of Hazardous Materials (NESTHM) for the setting of standards and certification for transporting hazardous substances.
* Undertake capacity assessment for the reaction of accidents with all emergency partners and develop appropriate legislations to make it compulsory to comply with new rules and regulations by all sectors concerns.
* Develop appropriate legislations to make it compulsory to adopt labelling, classification and markings for packing and packaging of hazardous substances.
