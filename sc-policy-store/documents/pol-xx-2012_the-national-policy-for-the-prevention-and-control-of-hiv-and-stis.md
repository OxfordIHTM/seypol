# National Policy on HIV and AIDS and Other STIs
**Republic of Seychelles**

## 1. Acknowledgements
The National AIDS Council expresses its sincere gratitude to all those who have participated in the formulation of the National Policy on HIV and AIDS and Other STIs of the Republic of Seychelles. Much has changed since then in terms of social, cultural, political and economic climate both in Seychelles and in the world. The present policy takes into consideration these very issues to ensure that its formulation remains relevant and adjusted to present needs and priorities.

The update of the Policy has been a true collaborative effort between the National AIDS Council, health sector and its multiple partners at both technical and the highest levels. The work has indeed been hard work, but refreshing, stimulating and inspiring.

Firstly, special thanks go to all members of the National Steering Committee who have made contributions and sacrifices and devoted much time to the update of the Policy, in spite of their busy schedules.

Secondly, special thanks and gratitude are also bestowed to all members of the thematic Technical Working Groups for their invaluable contributions and especially, for working so diligently on the various parts of the document to ensure clarity, cohesion, coherence and complete comprehension.

Thirdly, the National AIDS Council is grateful to the UNDP, the UNAIDS and WHO and other international partners who have supported the whole process through technical and financial assistance.

Fourthly, the National AIDS Council also thanks all these people and organisations who took time to provide their inputs, in the process of finalisation of the policy. The dedicated personnel at the National AIDS Council Secretariat and the National AIDS Control Programme provided much needed support in the preparation of meetings, providing documents and they engaged themselves in duties beyond their job description requirements to ensure success of the process.

Last but not least, we would like to thank all those, who have not been mentioned above, who have had a role through the various stages in the development and publication of the policy. All of your contributions were worthwhile and have been truly appreciated.

## 2. Forward
Seychelles has made significant progress in its response to HIV and AIDS since the “United Nations Declaration of Commitment on HIV/AIDS” in 2001 and the “United Nations declaration on Universal Access" in 2006. There have been various frameworks to ensure that the country gives the epidemic the importance it deserves. In 2000, the first National Policy on HIV and AIDS and Other STIs was published. Later on, National Strategic Plan on HIV and AIDS 2005 – 2009 provided guidance to the national response.

Since then, much water has flowed under the bridge. Given the many developments in the global HIV landscape and in the country modes of transmission trends, the need of reviewing the National Policy was strongly felt and a review exercise was recently conducted with our stakeholders and partners in 2011. I take this opportunity to express my sincere gratitude to all those who have worked on both documents in these past months.

The Seychelles now finds itself in a critical situation and at a crossroad in relation to HIV and AIDS. It has become clearer that the epidemic is definitely concentrated and driven by unsafe sexual practices and injecting drug use. Time has come for momentous decisions to be taken. The right balance must be found between human rights of the individual and public health issues and priorities, between human compassion and the harsh realities of inadequate and limited funding and between HIV and AIDS programmatic actions and competing national priorities.

The new National Policy will thus be the roadmap to guide our national response to HIV and AIDS and other STIs in this country. I am confident that each and everyone involved will rise up to the challenges and contribute to the creation of a Seychelles where we are realistic, pragmatic, compassionate, caring, proactive, open-minded and dedicated in the ways we choose to address this issue. I am also confident that we, as a nation, will honour our international commitments, as stipulated in various instruments, such as the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, the International Covenant of Economic, Social and Cultural Rights, the Universal Access Declaration, the Millennium Development Goals and others.

The new National Policy will be the corner stone and the main thrust for the renewed vision, vigor and efforts to combat the scourge of HIV and AIDS. It is an opportunity for Seychelles to build on the successes of the past and to move forward with a view to deploy new strategies to ensure the attainment of national development goals and objectives such as those set out in Vision 2017 and the National Medium-Term Strategic Plan. Alignment of the Policy to other national development plans and strategic documents is vital to ensure coherence, cohesiveness and coordination.

The ultimate goal is the elimination of HIV and AIDS in our communities. I urge all of us to work with commitment and compassion towards that goal.

***

**President of the Republic of Seychelles**
**The Chairperson**
**National AIDS Council**

## 4. Background
1. The Republic of Seychelles consists of a group of 115 granitic and coralline islands off the East Coast of Africa in the Indian Ocean. The land mass is about 455 km². However, the Exclusive Economic Zone is well over 1 million km². The three principal islands are Mahé, Praslin and La Digue. 76% of the population lives on the main island, Mahé. 11% live on the two other main islands.

2. According to the UNDP Human Development Index Report 2011, the HDI is 52 out of 187. A high of 36 was achieved in 2003. However, Seychelles income per capita has grown steadily since independence in 1976, e.g., from USD8,000 in 2002 to USD9,028 in 2009 and now to USD10,486 in 2010.

3. The population in 2011 stood at 90,945 (Population and Housing Census – 2010). It mostly consists of the descendants of African, including Malagasy slaves, their French masters, other Europeans, Asians (Indian and Chinese), and Arab trader. Around 20% of the population is less than 15 years of age. 9% are aged 65 and over. 68% of the population is in the 15 to 63 year bracket. The official languages are English, French and Creole, with the latter being the native language of most Seychellois and is French-based.

4. The population of Seychelles is greatly bolstered by two main factors: tourists and migrant workers. In 2010, there were some 150,000 visitors and about 8,000 migrants. Both populations are transient in nature. However, tourists are present for the short term whilst migrants, amongst which are traders, construction workers, teachers, management cadres, diplomats, are living in the country for the longer term.

5. The first case of Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) infection in Seychelles was diagnosed in 1987, and the first case of Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome (AIDS) was reported in 1992. As at September 2011, there were 419 cumulative HIV cases and 227 AIDS patients.

6. Seychelles does collect surveillance data. The data currently available have been compiled from the records of antenatal clinics, the blood bank and the CDCU. Testing of specimens is regionalized: Seychelles Referral Hospital, Baie Ste Anne Hospital (Praslin) and Logan Hospital (La Digue).

### A History of the National Response - 1980s and 1990s

7. The government of Seychelles has made a concerted effort at preventing HIV and controlling AIDS and other Sexually Transmitted Infections since the late eighties. A Short-Term Plan (1987-1988) and a Medium-Term Plan (1989-1993) were implemented.

8. Several projects have been undertaken specifically to strengthen national capacity. Main strategies adopted include:
- Information, Education and Communication (IEC)
- Condom promotion and distribution
- HIV surveillance activities
- Management of sexually transmitted diseases
- Ensuring safe blood transfusion

9. **Accomplishments included:**
- Higher level of awareness and knowledge of HIV/AIDS and STIs
- Safer blood transfusion
- Increase in condoms distributed
- Better integrated outreach programs
- Increased involvement of media
- Increased VCT sites
- Improved management of STIs
- Improved political commitment

10. **Issues requiring attention:**
- Persistent risky behaviour despite awareness
- Inadequacy of prevention programmes targeted at youth
- Inadequate openness and denial of the problem
- Inadequate support systems due to confidentiality
- Persistent stigma surrounding HIV and AIDS
- Inconsistent surveillance activities
- Insufficient evidence-based data
- Inadequate monitoring and evaluation

11. Discrimination and stigmatisation led many people to avoid knowing their HIV status or seeking care.

12. To address these, the first **National Policy (2001)** was proposed.

13. The Policy emphasised respect for human rights and dignity, and took into consideration social, cultural and religious norms.

14. The Policy recognized that HIV and AIDS was a major development crisis affecting all sectors, particularly productive segments (ages 20-49).

15. Other effects include lowering of life expectancy, increasing the dependency ratio, and reducing GDP growth.

### A History of the National Response - The First Decade of the New Millennia

16. Since 2000, Seychelles has made tremendous strides in developing a robust health response.

17. One MDG target was to achieve universal access to treatment by 2010. This was achieved as antiretroviral therapy has been free since August 2002.

18. However, there is a high dropout rate due to stigma, lack of decentralized services, and perceptions of inadequate confidentiality.

19. The target to halve the spread of HIV/AIDS by 2015 is not being achieved; annual data shows a continual increase in infections.

20. National campaigns were rarely targeted to specific populations (SWs, MSMs, IDUs), leaving them virtually hidden and unprotected.

21. KAP study (2003) showed persistent misconceptions (e.g., AIDS caused by mosquitoes). Additionally, Hepatitis C cases have increased exponentially.

### Review of the National Policy and Recommendations

22. In early 2011, a review identified issues such as vague references to human rights and the need for clear positions on controversial issues (e.g., VCT for teenagers 15-17 without parental consent).

23. Recommendations included making vulnerable populations and international human rights obligations explicit.

24. Lack of an explicit M&E framework was also noted as a key gap.

25. The new **National Policy for the Prevention and Control of HIV and AIDS and STIs 2011** was drafted to address these findings.

## 5. HIV and AIDS Situation

### 5.1 The Current Situation
26. As at September 2011, 491 persons (285 males, 206 females) tested positive. 315 persons are living with HIV, with 151 on ARV. Men are more frequently affected (58%) than women (42%).

#### New Cases
27. From January to September 2011, 31 new cases were reported (65% male, 35% female).
28. 17 new AIDS cases were reported; 65% were late-stage diagnoses.
29. 4 new HIV positive pregnancies were reported.
30. Regarding Hepatitis C: 145 cumulative cases (2002-2011). All new cases are IDUs.

#### Drivers of the Epidemic
31. Factors contributing to the spread include non-regular partnerships, unprotected sex, and high tourist/migrant flux.
32. Increase in injecting drug use (Heroin specifically) and sex work since 2007.
33. Studies on MSM and IDUs indicate high risk.
34. Health-seeking behaviour in men remains low.
35. Financial burden is estimated at SR5 million annually for ART alone.
36. HIV/AIDS directly impacts national goals for human resource development and productivity.

#### Justification for the new National Policy
37. The 2001 policy no longer reflects current realities. A new policy is needed to provide a foundation for the National Strategic Framework.

## 6. Overall goals of the policy

### 6.1 International Obligations and Alignment with Human Rights instruments
1. The policy is guided by major human rights instruments:
2. **ICCPR:** Rights to self-determination, equality, freedom of expression, and non-discrimination.
3. **ICESCR:** Rights to work, education, and decent health standards.
4. **CRC:** Special rights for children, protection from abuse.

### 6.2 Other major instruments:
- Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination
- CEDAW (Discrimination Against Women)
- International Convention on the Protection of the Rights of All Migrant Workers
- African Charter on Human and Peoples’ Rights
- African Charter on the Rights and Welfare of the Child
- The MDG Declaration

### 6.3 Specific HIV and AIDS related commitments and principles

#### 6.3.1 HIV and AIDS related commitments
- UNGASS Declaration (2001)
- UN MDGs declaration
- Abudja declaration (2001)
- Maseru Declaration (2003)
- Brazzaville Commitment (2006)
- UN Universal Access Declaration (2006)
- Windhoek progress report (2011)
- UNGASS Political Declaration (2011)

#### 6.3.2 HIV and AIDS guiding principles
38. **Three Ones:** One coordinating authority, one action framework, one M&E framework.
39. **GIPA:** Greater involvement of people living with AIDS at all levels.
40. **Gender issues:** Consideration of roles of boys, girls, men, and women.
41. **UNAIDS 2011-2015 Strategy:** Advance human rights and gender equality to reach "Zero".

#### 6.3.3 Alignment with National Policies and Priorities
42. Guided by the 1993 Constitution (Charter of Fundamental Rights and Freedoms).
43. Aligned with Draft Medium-Term National Development Strategy 2011-2013.
44. Integrated with Ministry of Health vision: achieving highest possible physical, social, mental and spiritual well-being.

#### 6.3.4 Alignment with Contemporary Scientific Evidence
54. Derived from international best practices and a rigorous methodology for strategic planning.

55. **Policy should be:**
- **Strategic:** Target primary transmission ways.
- **Evidence-based:** Use scientific evidence to identify drivers.
- **Prioritised:** Agreed criteria for resource allocation.
- **Results-based:** Focus on clearly stated results (RBM).
- **Aligned:** Coherent with national budgets and planning.

**Core Values of the National Policy:**
- Respect for, Protection and Fulfillment of Human Rights
- Integration & Mainstreaming
- Pragmatism and Realism

57. **Main Goal:** To prevent and control the spread of HIV/AIDS and STIs, and to care for those infected and affected.

### 6.4 Specific Objectives of the Policy
59. **Preventing new infections:**
- Create conducive environments for behaviour change.
- Provide harm reduction measures.
- Educate and raise awareness.
- Promote HIV testing.
- Early identification and treatment as prevention.

**Controlling the pandemic:**
- Safeguard human rights of PLWHA.
- Improve coordination of activities.
- Increase access to services via multi-sectoral approach.
- Sustain commitment.
- Promote and use research/surveillance.

## 7. Policy and position statements
60. Establish clear guidelines on controversial issues:

### Human Rights of the Individual
61. Rights set out in ICCPR, ICESCR, and the Seychellois Charter shall be respected.
62. Seropositive persons shall have full access to health and social services without restriction of movement.
63. Gender equity ensured for all.
64. Rights encompass responsibilities; criminal behaviour will not be tolerated.
65. Legal frameworks should ensure sexual and reproductive rights are upheld. Consensual sex and sex work shall not be impediments to care.
66. Calls for the end of laws making consensual sex between adult men and sex work illegal.

### The Role of Beneficiaries
68. Principle of **“Nothing for Us Without Us”**; beneficiaries must be involved in design and implementation.
69. Critical for Key Populations (SWs, IDUs, MSM) and MARPs (youth, inmates, migrants).

### Access for 15 to 17 year olds
71. 15-17 year olds can access contraceptives and HCT without parental consent, guided by “Fraser guidelines”.

### Confidentiality
74. Informed consent required for testing and disclosure for all persons.
75. Shared confidentiality promoted for clinical management, provided informed consent is obtained.

### Science and Research
77. Seychelles will adopt evidence-based measures confirmed by science.

### Improving Access to Health and Social Services
78. Mobilisation of govt and non-govt sectors to provide decentralized care.
79. Universal access to ARV without discrimination.
80. Care includes prophylaxis, treatment of opportunistic infections, and PMTCT.
82. Negotiating price reductions for drugs to ensure affordability.
84. Strengthening support for orphans and vulnerable children (OVCs).
86. Improving health service access for prison inmates, including harm reduction (condoms).

## 8. Dedicated HIV and AIDS Programmes
88. **Key Programmes:**
- **Prevention & Behaviour Change:** IEC materials, primary prevention, targeted communications, mental health support, VCT outreach, blood safety, harm reduction, condom distribution, STI control, and PMTCT.
- **Care & Treatment:** Provision of affordable medicines and quality management.
- **Impact Mitigation:** Psychosocial and financial support; ensuring non-discrimination in employment/education.
- **Resource Mobilisation:** Sustainability of programmes.
- **Coordination Mechanisms:** Ensuring coherence and effective resource use.

## 9. National Coordination
**National Coordination and Response:**
- **National AIDS Council (NAC):** Overall program oversight and coordination. Supported by a Secretariat (NAS) based in the Office of the President or Vice-President.
- **Coordinating Mechanism (CM):** Facilitates networking and collaborative implementation.
- **NAC Terms of Reference:** Lead implementation, coordinate regional/international participation, facilitate mainstreaming, support NGOs, monitor trends, promote research, and manage reports.
- **National AIDS Council Secretariat (NAS):** Provides guidance on prevention, treatment, coordination, and M&E.
- **AIDS Control Programme (Ministry of Health):** Provides technical expertise and leadership in the health sector.

**Organizational Structure:**
`NAC Board of Directors` $\rightarrow$ `NACS` $\rightarrow$ `Intersectoral Working Groups` $\rightarrow$ `Service Providers/Community Initiatives`

## 10. Monitoring and Evaluation Framework
98. Acknowledges existing weaknesses in data sharing across sectors.
99. Data is key for advocacy and prioritisation.
100. NAC developed an M&E Framework using **Results-Based Management (RBM)**.
101. All agencies shall share data with the Surveillance and Data Collection Sub-Committee.
102. **MEF Components:**
- **Sentinel sites:** (CDCU, antenatal clinics, blood bank).
- **Surveys:** Evaluation of products/services (condoms, peer education).
- **Cross-cutting measures:** Economic and social indicators.
- **Academia:** Research from NIHSS and University of Seychelles.

## 11. Conclusion
103. The Government recognizes that HIV/AIDS is dynamic. This policy serves as a reference for international commitments and will be under regular review to update trends and maintain a Human Rights approach.
