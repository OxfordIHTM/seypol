## Foreword
### Creating Decent Work for all

It is an honour for me to present the new **National Employment Policy for the Republic of Seychelles**, which reflects the Government’s strategies for achieving its employment and human resource development objectives. The previous National Employment Policy was adopted in 1999 and its revision reaffirms the importance that the Government attaches to work with a view to continuously improving the quality of life of the people of Seychelles.

I am delighted to note that this is the first policy document adopted under the **Seychelles Decent Work Country Programme (2011–2015)**, which is being implemented with the strong collaboration of the **International Labour Organization (ILO)**. In this context, I wish to express my sincere appreciation to the ILO for its technical assistance throughout the review process.

The new employment policy reflects a time when our country is calling upon us to strive for greater accomplishments, whilst appreciating the sound moral values which are crucial for individual, organisational and national productivity. It is our attitude towards work that will direct the Seychelles economy to a sustainable future.

Furthermore, this important document is timely in addressing the socio-economic impact of external shocks on the labour market. Since the past economic reforms, coupled with the world financial crisis of 2008, we have witnessed a shift of labour whereby the private sector is today leading the economy and generating the majority of employment opportunities and small businesses. At the same time, other challenges have emerged, notably our limited human resources and a skills mismatch in areas of demand. The increase in demand for labour has also led to greater mobility in the labour market. Amidst these challenges, the Government has continuously been engaged in ensuring that its economic and social policies keep abreast with the changing dynamics of the labour market.

Accordingly this new policy renews our efforts to ensure that employment is at the heart of our national development strategies, so that decent jobs are continually being created with our labour force becoming more productive and competitive. My Ministry will unceasingly promote human resource development, appropriate working conditions, stable industrial relations and employment research as these will further contribute to the growth of our economy.

We have ensured that all key stakeholders have been consulted in the formulation of this new policy. I therefore urge the employers’ and workers’ representatives and the private sector to collaborate closely with the Government in the implementation of its new strategies. The new National Employment Policy reflects the hard work of all those who have contributed significantly to its development; its successful implementation, however, will depend on the commitment of all of us. This is our National Employment Policy: let us take ownership of it and let it be our guiding tool to guarantee that we attain our vision of creating decent work for all.

Finally, I wish to reiterate my gratitude to all our national and international partners for their support towards this new employment policy. Rest assured that my Ministry is fully committed to seeing its successful realisation.

**Idith Alexander**
*Minister for Labour and Human Resource Development*

## Executive Summary

The new National Employment Policy (NEP) for Seychelles is in keeping with the changing socio-economic environment and future development of the country. Since the previous employment policy was adopted in 1999, Seychellois society has undergone socio-economic changes. Prior to the late 1990s, most economic activities were carried out by government departments and parastatals. As a result, the economy was largely dominated by the public sector, employing over 65 per cent of the total labour force. Presently the public sector employs around 28 per cent of the labour force.

Although economic development is generally regarded as the principal driving force for sustainable development, it is nonetheless insufficient to achieve overall socio-economic objectives. The Seychelles Government has long recognised and further acknowledged the fact that human development and well-being cannot stand on their own either. Sustainable and durable development and progress (including human resources development) need a solid base in the form of good infrastructure and facilities, as well as policies and an institutional framework that can create an environment conducive to both domestic and foreign private investment. Such investments should aim at longer-term growth that would generate local employment. Without considerable investment in human and capital resources, economic and social progress would take longer to achieve. The Government's commitment to human development, as shown by the huge investment in health, education and training, is further proof that it is creating an enabling environment for sustainable growth and decent and productive employment for all. In spite of this, we acknowledge that the country still has some key employment issues and challenges to address such as the emigration of skilled labour at all levels, a growing number of migrant workers, low productivity levels and attitudes to work, skills shortages and mismatch of supply and demand for labour and a growing informal sector.

“Decent work for all” as advocated by the International Labour Organization (ILO) has been placed at the heart of the NEP. Presently, Seychelles has ratified 37 international labour Conventions out of which 29 Conventions are in force and 8 have been denounced. Furthermore, Seychelles is also benefiting from the Decent Work Country Programme (DWCP) of the ILO. Seychelles' DWCP priorities are:
1. Review of labour legislation and strengthening the dispute resolution framework;
2. Promotion of quality employment and reduction of unemployment, particularly for women and youth;
3. Promotion of effective tripartism and social dialogue.

All these priorities are addressed by the NEP.

The NEP places great emphasis on skills development, acquisition, and retention. Integrating employment objectives as cross-cutting issues within the fiscal, monetary, trade and other sectoral policies is one key objective of the NEP. The spirit of consultation has been the cornerstone in drafting the NEP and already the sense of ownership is strong among the stakeholders. The NEP was conceived with a view to consolidating the linkages with other sectors, and thus the role of each stakeholder in ensuring the successful implementation of the NEP is crucial. The NEP will provide an all-embracing support framework to several existing national and sectoral policies related to employment and human resources development and it will further consolidate government commitment to “Decent work for all”.

## Acknowledgements

The new National Employment Policy for Seychelles was developed between 2012 and 2013 with the technical assistance of the International Labour Organization (ILO) and through the services of a local consultant. The identification of challenges and formulation of strategies have been guided by the findings of technical research, which was undertaken at the preliminary stage of the review in order to analyse the employment situation of the country. More than 20 organisations from various economic sectors were consulted and a draft of the policy was validated at a national tripartite workshop. Subsequent meetings were held with the Ministry and other key stakeholders and the policy framework was submitted to the ILO for their final comments, before receiving the endorsement of the Government.

## I. VISION

The long‐term vision of the National Employment Policy is to provide productive, non‐discriminatory, exploitation‐free, decent, safe and healthy work opportunities for citizens of working age. This will be achieved through building an investment‐friendly environment, in addition to managing a labour market which contributes to the national economy so that it can compete at the global level. The employment policy also ensures sustainable economic development by promoting full, productive and freely chosen employment for the country’s workforce; developing high–quality, multi‐skilled human resources that are in line with the changing needs of the industry and international labour standards.

## II. SETTING POLICY PRIORITIES

Employment challenges at the national and sectoral levels have been exacerbated in particular by issues relating to the quality of training, availability of appropriate skills, productivity, emigration of skilled professionals (“brain drain”), and synergy between the roles of social (employment and training) institutions. More importantly, the academic orientation of education and training is a major challenge to employment creation as it results in a lack of functional skills.

## III. THE POLICY FRAMEWORK

### III.A. Policy objectives

In contributing to the country's changing socio‐economic environment and future development, the employment policy endeavours to achieve the following objectives:
1. Promote full, productive and freely chosen employment without discrimination;
2. Meet national human resource requirements in line with labour market demands;
3. Maintain the right competitive edge to perform better and to stimulate economic growth and development;
4. Promote an information‐technology‐literate and competent labour force;
5. Equip Seychellois with the knowledge, competencies and entrepreneurial mindset required to compete successfully in a global, knowledge‐based economy;
6. Promote a healthy and productive labour force through continuous investment in health, education and training;
7. Promote fair and good working conditions to ensure sound industrial relations and protect the health and safety of workers.

### III.B. Guiding principles

The proposed employment policy framework is set out within the context of prudent economic management, sound macroeconomic policies and a stable political environment. The following will be promoted:
1. Fostering a shared, partnership approach that will create smart partnerships at all levels;
2. Improving the quality of employment, and not only the quantity, by promoting decent, productive and freely chosen employment;
3. Ensuring that employment remains at the heart of socio‐economic policies;
4. Nurturing a stronger job‐creation and entrepreneurial culture;
5. Establishing well‐targeted policies and programmes based on an efficient and effective Labour Market Information System (LMIS);
6. Encouraging an employment culture that enhances productivity and fair and equitable performance‐based reward.

### III.C. Policy

1. The National Employment Policy has been designed to ensure the country has an educated, multi‐skilled, disciplined, motivated, healthy and highly productive workforce. The Government is committed to the provision of 13 to 17 years of free education to its youth.

**Government shall:**
2. Remain committed to creating the necessary enabling environment for private‐sector‐led participation in socio‐economic development and growth.
3. Mainstream labour productivity in the national development strategies.
4. Make greater use of timely, accurate and reliable data from the Labour Market Information System (LMIS).
5. Continually monitor the dynamics of the labour market.
6. Promote a merit‐based performance appraisal system throughout government and parastatals.
7. Ensure proper management of migrant workers accompanied by progressive localisation.
8. Maintain tripartite social dialogue and bipartite collective bargaining.
9. Safeguard the right and duty of every citizen to work and enjoy favourable conditions without discrimination.
10. Ensure that the minimum wage regulation is being enforced.
11. Ensure that its social and employment policy objectives support and protect the social welfare of its citizens.
12. Strengthen integration of Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET).
13. Ensure that effective career guidance and career counselling services are provided.

**Government and social partners shall:**
14. Promote the importance of good balance between work and social life.
15. Promote a performance reward management system.
16. Promote equal remuneration for Seychellois and non‐Seychellois for work of equal value.
17. Encourage a remuneration system that reflects changes in the cost of living.
18. Strengthen the mechanism to protect employment affected by natural disasters including climate change.
19. Take a leading role in sensitising and educating workers and employers on rights and responsibilities.
20. Formulate sensitisation programmes on the benefits of formal employment.

## IV. POLICY IMPLEMENTATION AND INSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORKS

### IV.A. Policy implementation

Mainstreaming employment in the national development strategies will guarantee that employment objectives are integrated as cross‐cutting priorities within fiscal, monetary, trade, and other sectoral policies.

### IV.B. Institutional frameworks

#### 1. Ministry of Labour and Human Resource Development (MLHRD)
The MLHRD is the authority responsible for labour and employment issues in Seychelles. Its mandate includes:
- Improved economic efficiency and productivity
- Skills development and employment growth
- Tripartite social dialogue
- Occupational health and safety
- Protection of workers’ fundamental rights
- Sensitisation on rights and responsibilities

#### 2. Employment Tribunal
Strategies for the Tribunal include:
- Functioning as an independent body/authority under the hierarchy of the court
- Managing its own budget and administration

#### 3. National Consultative Committee on Employment (NCCE)
Set up to provide expertise and guidance on pertinent employment issues, consisting of representatives from the MLHRD, Association of Seychelles Employers, Seychelles Federation of Workers’ Union, SCCI, and the Attorney General’s Chambers.

#### 4. Ministry of Education
Provides education from primary to post‐secondary level. Key challenges include improving learning outcomes and adapting curricula of professional centres to meet industry demands.

## V. RESOURCE MOBILISATION

Requires high level of collaboration and financial resources. While generally funded through the national budget, support from development partners such as the ILO, IMF, and World Bank is also sought. Public-private partnerships (PPP) are emphasized, particularly in the tourism sector.

## VI. MONITORING AND EVALUATION

### VI.A. Monitoring and evaluation
Evaluations should take place at regular intervals (biannual during the first two years, then annually). The LMIS is a critical tool for this process.

### VI.B. Review of Policy
The policy will be reviewed every five years.

### VI.C. Achievement indicators
- Number of newly registered SMEs
- Wage differential by age, gender, occupation, sector, and nationality
- Employment and job placement data
- Measures of inclusiveness (Gini coefficient, poverty levels)
- Macroeconomic stability measures
- Graduate placement rates from PSTIs and university
- GOP applications and approvals
- Migration of skilled Seychellois
- HIV/AIDS prevalence rates
- Employment rates of disadvantaged groups
- Sectoral and national labour productivity levels

## Appendix I: Strategies of the National Employment Policy

### A. Problem Identification (Issues and Challenges)

1. **Macroeconomic management:** Exchange rate instability, high production costs, limited economic diversity.
2. **Human resources and skills:** Skills mismatch, poor responsiveness of training institutions, weak performance management.
3. **Labour market policies:** Lack of quality data, unclear structure for employment services.
4. **Sectoral/SME development:** Need for updated legislation, lack of entrepreneurial skills, growing informal sector.
5. **Migration and localisation:** Small population, "brain drain", high number of migrant workers.
6. **Youth employment:** Unemployment and school dropouts, substance abuse.
7. **Vulnerable groups:** Productivity issues, chronic diseases (diabetes, hypertension).
8. **Non-discrimination:** Need for equality for women, elderly, disabled, and HIV/AIDS patients.
9. **Social protection:** Sustainability of current schemes.
10. **Social dialogue:** Weak worker education, need for stronger PPPs.

### B. Strategies

#### 1. Prudent economic management
- Promote employment in support of growth
- Place HR development at the heart of fiscal and monetary policies

#### 2. Human resources and skills development
- **Primary/Secondary Education:** Improve performance and reduce dropout rates (target 90% completion).
- **TVET:** Create an integrated policy framework and allocate appropriate budgets.
- **Tertiary Education (PSTIs):** Involve industry in curricula, establish Governing Boards, and strengthen links with the University of Seychelles.
- **University of Seychelles:** Align courses with labour market needs and focus on research.
- **Sustaining Skills:** Implement retention schemes and a reward system to counter emigration.

#### 3. Active labour market policies
- **Jobseekers:** Incorporate interview skills in curricula and revamp career guidance.
- **Employment Services:** Enhance coordination between MLHRD and private agencies.
- **Retraining:** Provide budgetary provisions for retraining jobseekers.
- **LMIS:** Establish a fully functional system by the end of 2014 with standardised data formats.

#### 4. Sectoral and SME development
- Formalise the informal sector
- Develop a national productivity management framework
- Promote entrepreneurial culture and financial support for SMEs

#### 5. Migration and localisation
- Review minimum wage regulations
- Implement strict localisation plans for organisations employing non-Seychellois
- Reserve specific occupations for Seychellois only

#### 6. Youth employment
- Improve data on youth unemployment
- Strengthen career guidance and internship placements
- Stimulate youth entrepreneurship and provide support for young mothers

#### 7. Vulnerable groups
- Offer subsidised training/retraining
- Provide flexible working conditions and fiscal incentives for inclusive hiring

#### 8. Non-discrimination
- **Gender Equality:** Promote parity in leadership (target 40% women on boards), support breastfeeding, and tackle sexual harassment.
- **Disability:** Ensure removable accommodation in workplaces and equal remuneration.
- **Elderly:** Protect against age discrimination and promote active ageing.
- **HIV/AIDS:** Enforce the Policy on HIV/AIDS in the Workplace and prevent forced testing.

#### 9. Social protection
- Shift to a holistic approach for disability assessment
- Link MLHRD and the Agency for Social Protection to encourage productive employment

#### 10. Labour market institutions
- Conduct periodic reviews of the Employment Act and Industrial Relations Act
- Establish "labour clinics" for high-issue organisations

## Appendix II: Ministry of Labour and Human Resource Development’s core functions

1. Formulate and implement employment and labour policies.
2. Enforce and review employment legislation (Employment Act, etc.).
3. Adhere to ratified ILO Conventions.
4. Provide employment services and promote full employment.
5. Promote social dialogue and collective bargaining.
6. Ensure a safe and healthy working environment (OSH Decree).
7. Promote Decent Work and productivity.
8. Act as the government focal point for international labour relations.
9. Collate and disseminate statistics via LMIS.
10. Coordinate national human resources planning.
11. Promote lifelong learning.
12. Design and implement HR development research.
13. Manage the Government Scholarships Scheme.
14. Promote training initiatives to meet national needs.
