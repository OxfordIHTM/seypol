# SEYCHELLES
# NATIONAL POLICY AND STRATEGIC FRAMEWORK ON DISABILITY
**JANUARY 2014**

## Preface

Seychelles’ commitment to the development of its people has always been paramount. The development of this National Policy and Strategic Framework on Disability is evidence of this commitment. It is in itself a great achievement because it is a collaborative and participatory effort. Through its development process it has helped to open the eyes of many on the issues that relate to disability and in some ways also helped to change mindsets. For these reason those who have collaborated should be proud.

This policy and strategic framework is modern and forward looking setting important parameters for the implementation of Seychelles’ commitments towards persons with disabilities and to human rights in general. The challenge is to translate the optimism embodied in the document into realism. A lot is already being done. It is hoped that this comprehensive document will allow for efforts to be better coordinated and increase participation where necessary.

## Acknowledgement

This policy document would not have been possible without the invaluable contribution of all the stakeholders including government officials, NGO’s, persons with disabilities and their care givers.

We rely on the same support to keep it as a ‘living’ document.

## 1. BACKGROUND

### 1.1 Introduction

Seychelles became a party to the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) in 2009. This has helped to push disability issues high on the national agenda with the convention guiding the way.

This first National Policy on Disability of the Government of Seychelles is aimed at addressing the barriers that have been preventing persons with disabilities from reaching their full potential. It recognises that whilst a lot has been done to give persons with disabilities opportunities a lot still remains to be done to meet the aspirations of the Seychellois disabled population and attain full compliance to the CRPD.

The policy aims to integrate an awareness of disability issues into policies, practices and procedures in all areas of service provision and programme development within the scope of the Millennium Development Goals (MDG) and the CRPD. It also gives some guidance as to how this might be achieved and links to other polices and strategies where appropriate. This policy supports a social model of disability where disability is defined as the loss or limitation of opportunity to take part in society on an equal level due to barriers in society and/or the environment.

For this policy to be successful all sectors will need to pledge their commitment to the mainstreaming of disability within policy agendas. It is hoped that the adoption of this policy will facilitate the empowerment of persons with disabilities to claim their rights as citizens and change public mindset about disability.

This policy document has been compiled through wide consultations with all relevant stakeholders to ensure its ownership from the start.

It is structured in the following manner:
1. The programme area
2. The articles of the CRPD which are relevant to the programme area
3. Key Issues (identified in consultation)
4. Policy commitment
5. Policy objective
6. Strategic actions

### 1.2 Policy and legal framework

The Constitution of the Republic of Seychelles (1993) is the country’s supreme law and all other laws and policies are subject to it. Article 5 states that ‘any other law found to be inconsistent with this Constitution is, to the extent of the inconsistency, void.’

The Constitution guarantees the right to equal protection before the law for “*every person without discrimination on any ground*”. The Seychellois Charter of Fundamental Human Rights and Freedoms (Chapter 3 of the Constitution), which incorporates many of the principles of international human rights instruments, guarantees 25 rights. Article 36 of this chapter recognises the rights of persons with disabilities in the following way;

*The State recognises the right of the aged and the disabled to special protection and with a view to ensuring the effective exercise of this right undertakes*

* To make reasonable provision for improving the quality of life of and for the welfare and maintenance of the aged and disabled
* To promote programmes specifically aimed at achieving the greatest possible development of the disabled

The Policy is aligned to the principles enshrined in the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities and its optional protocol. The Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) and its Optional Protocol provide the normative framework for its members to address the rights of persons with disabilities and ensure that they are included in all development efforts. The Convention has an explicit, socio-economic development dimension that recognizes the importance of development to the full realization of the rights in the CRPD.

## 2. JUSTIFICATION

The 2010 NSB census recorded a population of approximately 2169 persons with disabilities representing 2.5 % of the population of the Seychelles. They frequently face barriers to participate effectively in all aspect of society. The barriers are spread across sectors and include discrimination in work, accessing quality education, transportation etc.

Given that persons with disabilities are more likely to live in poverty than their non-disabled peers, their inclusion in all development activities is essential, if internationally agreed development goals, including the Millennium Development Goals (MDGs) are to be achieved in an equitable manner.

The Seychelles has shown its commitment in addressing the barriers that people with disabilities face through the ratification of the Convention (CRPD). Its principles and provisions now need to be translated into the everyday planning and implementation processes of the country and this policy is developed to accomplish just that.

The policy responds to the request of people with disabilities themselves, who have been calling for a new approach to disability, grounded on the conviction that all persons should be equally valued: an approach focusing on the need to adapt society so as to enable (rather than disable) people with disabilities.

## 3. POLICY FRAMEWORK – VISION, GOAL, OBJECTIVES

### 3.1 Vision
A nation where persons with disabilities enjoy full and equal participation in all aspects of life

### 3.2 Mission
To promote an inclusive society for persons with disabilities

### 3.3 Policy Goal
To provide guidelines for the inclusion of persons with disabilities in all national programmes

### 3.4 Specific Policy Objectives

* Enact appropriate legislations to domesticate the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities.
* Promote the participation of women and men with disabilities in decision-making.
* Ensure the mainstreaming of disability rights into all policies, structures, systems, programmes and activities in order for them to contribute effectively to national development.
* Improve service delivery for persons with disabilities

## 4. GUIDING PRINCIPLES

In order to address the challenges that persons with disabilities face there are a number of interlinked principles that must always be at the centre of all attempts to respond to these challenges:

* ***Human rights based approach:*** All measures to advance the rights of persons with disabilities shall not affect the human rights and dignity of persons especially persons with disabilities.
* ***Harmonisation:*** Harmonising national legislation, policies, strategies and programmes with the Convention (CRPD) or any other relevant human rights instrument
* ***Empowerment:*** A human rights-based approach to disability aims to empower persons with disabilities to make their own choices, advocate for themselves, and exercise control over their lives.
* ***Inclusion and participation:*** Particular attention must be paid to the empowerment of persons with disabilities so that they can claim their own rights.
* ***Sustainable development:*** Mainstreaming disability is an integral part of relevant strategies of sustainable development
* ***Multi-sector approach:*** All stakeholders including State and NSAs across all sectors need to work together as partners in a co-ordinated way to achieve equality for persons with disabilities.
* ***Indivisibility:*** The promotion of one right may not justify the violation of another right. Consequently, there is no hierarchy among rights.
* ***Inter-dependence and Inter-relatedness:*** The promotion of specific human rights must be part of a comprehensive effort to realize human rights in a holistic way.
* ***Poverty eradication:*** The goal of halving poverty (MDG 1) cannot be met if the exclusion of persons with disabilities is not tackled
* ***Equalisation of opportunities:*** Equitable progress in the agreed global development agenda cannot be achieved without the inclusion of persons with disabilities. The state should facilitate an environment where persons with disabilities have equal opportunities to the non disabled

## 5. POLICY PROGRAMME AREAS, OBJECTIVES AND STRATEGIC ACTIONS

### 5.1 Disability and legal rights

**Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Article 4, 5**

*States shall:*
* Endeavour to adopt all appropriate legislative measures for the implementation of the rights recognised in the CRPD
* Review, amend and or repeal all legislation that discriminate against persons with disabilities

**Key issues**

The Seychelles Constitution guarantees the right to equal protection before the law for “*every person without discrimination on any ground*”. There are no laws that overtly discriminate against persons with disabilities. However, the lack of certain specific provisions and gaps in mainstreaming rights during the drafting process make it such that certain rights are not upheld. Laws that refer to persons with disabilities, such as the Employment Act, fall short of CRPD requirements. There is a need to explicitly prohibit humiliation and discrimination, and to provide economic and social security.

**Policy Commitment:**
The Government shall ensure that all persons are equal before and under the law

**Policy Objective:**
To ensure a legal environment that offers persons with disabilities the same opportunities as the non disabled

**Strategic Actions:**
* Assess the need for a specific legislative framework.
* Review, amend and or repeal all discriminatory laws as necessary.
* Amend the National Council for Disabled Act 1994.
* Build capacity of the justice system to protect vulnerable witnesses.
* Ensure courts are accessible to persons with disabilities.
* Review terms used to refer to persons with disabilities in laws and regulations.
* Align terms, definitions and classification to international norms.

### 5.2 Women with disabilities

**Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Article 6**

*States shall:*
* Take measures to ensure the full and equal enjoyment by women with disabilities of all human rights and fundamental freedoms
* Take all appropriate measures to ensure the full development, advancement and empowerment of women with disabilities

**Key issues**

Women and girls with disabilities experience double discrimination due to gender and disability (MDG 3). Areas of concern include reproductive health, access to sexual and reproductive health information, and a higher risk of gender-based violence and abuse.

**Policy Commitment:**
The Government shall promote an equitable society through gender-balanced programmes which integrate persons with disabilities.

**Policy Objective:**
To ensure that no one gender is left behind in efforts to ensure that persons with disabilities fully enjoy their rights

**Strategic Actions:**
* Create an enabling environment to facilitate increased participation of women with disabilities in decision-making.
* Promote and build capacity of women and girls in civil society.
* Organise training related to leadership development.
* Ensure that national gender policies extend to persons with disabilities.

### 5.3 Children with disabilities

**Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Article 7**

*States shall:*
* Ensure the full enjoyment by children with disabilities of all human rights and fundamental freedoms on an equal basis with other children
* Ensure that the best interest of the child is the primary consideration
* Ensure that children with disabilities have the right to express their views freely

**Key issues**

Children with disabilities face barriers to basic human rights and inclusion. Mainstream schools and day care services are often not equipped to cater to their needs. Although the National Framework for Early Childhood Care and Education exists, it lacks specific strategies for children with disabilities.

**Policy Commitment:**
The Government shall take necessary steps to promote the inclusion of children with disability within society

**Policy Objective:**
To ensure that children with disabilities enjoy the same opportunities as non disabled children

**Strategic Actions:**
* Ensure national policies for children take into account issues faced by children with disabilities.
* Provide an effective remedy mechanism for rights violations.
* Allocate necessary resources for children with disabilities and their families.
* Conduct awareness-raising campaigns to prevent discrimination.
* Integrate disability needs into ECCE strategies.
* Maintain the principle of the best interest of the child.
* Uphold the right to live with family as a primary option.

### 5.4 Disability and Accessibility

**Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Article 9, Article 21**

*States shall:*
* Ensure that persons with disabilities access on an equal basis with others, to the physical environment, to transportation, to information and communications technologies and systems

**Key issues**

Accessibility is one of the biggest barriers in Seychelles. Public transport and many public buildings are inaccessible. There is currently no law mandating public building accessibility. Access to information (e.g., sign language on TV) is also poor.

**Policy Commitment:**
The Government shall remove administrative, procedural and physical barriers that prevent equal access to people with disabilities

**Policy Objective:**
To ensure that persons with disabilities have access to public services and information

**Strategic Actions:**
* Put legal provisions in place to guarantee public building accessibility.
* Ensure planning authorities consult with persons with disabilities on public projects.
* Invest in communication technologies for education.
* Invest in suitable public transport.
* Provide training for stakeholders on accessibility.
* Provide signage in Braille and easy-to-read formats.
* Develop tax/customs waivers for necessary technology imports.
* Make public information available in different formats.
* Provide sign language and captioning on national television.
* Ensure service providers make "reasonable adjustments".

### 5.5 Disability and situations of risk and humanitarian emergencies

**Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Article 11**

*States shall:*
* Take all necessary measures to ensure the protection and safety of persons with disabilities in situations of risk, including armed conflict and natural disasters

**Key issues**

Persons with disabilities are at higher risk during crises (e.g., floods, tsunamis). Current disaster response from the DRDM lacks specific policies or representation from disability organisations.

**Policy commitment**
The government shall recognise the specific needs of persons with disabilities in disaster risk reduction and management.

**Policy objective**
To ensure that the necessary framework to ensure the protection of persons with disabilities during times of risk and humanitarian emergencies are in place

**Strategic Actions:**
* Review risk and disaster management programmes to mainstream disability.
* Build capacity to address disability needs in disaster management.
* Ensure participation of DPOs in developing emergency plans.

### 5.6 Disability and participation

**Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Article 29, 30**

*States shall:*
* Ensure full participation in political and public life on an equal basis
* Promote a non-discriminatory environment for public affairs participation
* Ensure participation in cultural life and sporting activities

**Key issues**

Participation is often ad-hoc rather than systemic. While some District Support Groups exist, they need strengthening. There is a growing interest in Paralympic sports, but continuity is needed.

**Policy Commitment:**
The Government shall provide appropriate support to ensure the participation of persons with disabilities in national development processes, sports, leisure and cultural life

**Policy Objective:**
To ensure the participation of persons with disabilities and their representative organisation in all aspects of mainstream society

**Strategic Actions:**
* Facilitate participation of marginalized groups (women, children, those with intellectual disabilities).
* Provide accommodation and capacity building for decision-making processes.
* Ensure resources are available for sports.
* Choose accessible venues for consultations.
* Strengthen DPO capacity to lobby for participation.

### 5.7 Disability and Security of person

**Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Articles 14-16**

*States shall:*
* Ensure the right to liberty and security on an equal basis, including freedom from torture and exploitation

**Key issues**

Persons with disabilities are more vulnerable to crimes, drug/alcohol abuse issues, and violence due to their inability to defend themselves. The “social renaissance” campaign aims to address these vulnerabilities.

**Policy Commitment:**
The Government is committed to make the right of persons with disabilities to safety and security an everyday reality

**Policy Objective:**
To prevent all forms of situations where persons with disabilities feel unsafe

**Strategic Actions:**
* Provide better protection against sexual harassment.
* Ensure safety and security in public spaces.
* Ensure victim services cater to persons with disabilities.
* Integrate disability into the Social Renaissance Action Plan.

### 5.8 Disability and Health

**Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Article 23, Article 25**

*States shall:*
* Ensure access to gender-sensitive health services and rehabilitation
* Ensure the right to decide freely and responsibly on reproductive health

**Key issues**

Despite “health for all” policies, barriers remain in physical access and negative attitudes. Early intervention is crucial but awareness is low. Reproductive health information is often not provided in disabled-friendly formats (Braille/Sign language).

**Policy Commitment:**
The Government shall ensure that all Seychellois including persons with disabilities enjoy a healthy life

**Policy Objective:**
To ensure access to health services and information by persons with disabilities

**Strategic Actions:**
* Ensure safe motherhood.
* Make available targeted health information in various formats.
* Raise awareness on the prevention of disability.
* Ensure there is no forced sterilization of women with disabilities.
* Educate persons with disabilities on Reproductive Health rights.

### 5.9 Disability, Skills Training and Employment

**Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities; Article 27**

*States shall:*
* Prohibit discrimination in employment
* Protect the right to decent work and equal remuneration
* Ensure access to technical and vocational guidance

**Key issues**

Employment is linked to dignity, but disability is often seen as a lack of ability. There is a gap in vocational training quality. Furthermore, the termination of Invalidity Benefits upon employment acts as a disincentive.

**Policy commitment:**
The government shall ensure that the relevant structures, systems and provisions are in place to facilitate the entry of persons with disabilities into the world of work.

**Policy objective:**
To create an enabling environment for the employment of persons with disabilities

**Strategic Actions:**
* Employ more persons with disabilities in the public sector.
* Promote self-employment opportunities.
* Reorganise the Vocational Unit.
* Review the Social Security Act regarding Invalidity Benefits.
* Provide job placement and maintenance assistance.
* Build gender-sensitive statistics on PWD employment.
* Raise awareness on the right to work among employers.
* Introduce temporary special measures and incentives for employers.
* Ensure reasonable accommodation in the workplace.
* Lobby for accessible workplaces.

### 5.10 Disability and Education

**Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities; Article 24**

*States shall:*
* Ensure an inclusive education system at all levels and lifelong learning

**Key issues**

Steps have been taken (e.g., Baie Ste Anne Praslin Primary), but equality is not yet guaranteed. Teacher education and curricula often fail to meet special needs. The Ministry of Education is developing a policy on inclusive education.

**Policy commitment:** The Government in collaboration with stakeholders shall provide a conducive environment for inclusive education that takes care of the special needs of children with disabilities

**Policy objective:** To ensure that persons with disabilities have access to quality education

**Strategic Actions:**
* Finalise and implement a National Policy on Inclusive Education.
* Develop an action plan for implementation.
* Make reasonable individual accommodations within the general system.
* Invest in teacher training on special needs education.
* Develop special educational needs modules at university level.
* Invest in resource centres and personnel (psychologists, therapists).
* Ensure expatriate teachers are trained in Special Needs Education.

### 5.11 Disability and Technical Aids

**Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities; Article 20**

*States shall:*
* Take effective measures to ensure personal mobility with the greatest possible independence

**Key issues**

Technical aids (white canes, hearing aids, wheelchairs) are essential for participation but are often unaffordable or difficult to import.

**Policy commitment:** The government shall facilitate access by persons with disabilities to technical aids

**Policy objective:**
To ensure that persons with disabilities have the relevant support to participate effectively in society

**Strategic Actions:**
* Provide information on assistive aids development.
* Provide aids to those without financial means.
* Waive import fees for technical aids.
* Provide mobility skills training.
* Establish mechanisms for low-cost local production of aids.

### 5.12 Disability and rights to a home and to live independently

**Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities; Article 19**

*States shall:*
* Take effective measures to allow persons with disabilities to live independently and be included in the community

**Key issues**

Persons with disabilities are often wrongly viewed as unable to live independently. Independent living is a precondition for enjoying other rights.

**Policy commitment:** The government shall facilitate the enjoyment by persons with disabilities of a healthier and more satisfying life on their terms

**Policy objective:**
To ensure that persons with disabilities who are able and willing have the opportunity to have a home and live independently

**Strategic Actions:**
* Make general services more accessible.
* Promote the concept of living independently.
* Use role models to advocate for this right.

### 5.13 Disability and awareness raising

**Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities; Article 8**

*States shall:*
* Take immediate, effective and appropriate measures to raise awareness throughout society

**Key issues**

Negative perceptions are a major barrier. DPOs and NGOs need more empowerment to lead lobbying and awareness efforts.

**Policy commitment:** The government shall support initiative to promote the rights of persons with disabilities

**Policy objective:**
To raise awareness on disability and get more partners involved

**Strategic Actions:**
* Maintain campaigns promoting PWD capabilities.
* Promote the Creole version of the CRPD.
* Develop a child-friendly version of the CRPD.
* Implement school awareness campaigns.
* Encourage media to portray PWDs consistently with CRPD principles.
* Establish training programmes in educational institutions.
* Use specific international days for promotion.
* Seek donor funding for specific awareness campaigns.

### 5.14 Disability and Rehabilitation

**Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Art 26**

*States shall:*
* Enable persons with disabilities to attain maximum independence and full inclusion

**Key issues**

Community-Based Rehabilitation (CBR) is preferred over institutional care. While strides have been made, funding for community skills training is often short-term and inconsistent.

**Policy commitment:** The government shall take measures to ensure that community based rehabilitation (CBR) is adopted as a strategy

**Policy objective:**
To ensure access to comprehensive and multi disciplinary rehabilitation services

**Strategic Actions:**
* Create awareness on CBR benefits.
* Establish clear guidelines for agency roles in CBR.
* Establish multi-disciplinary assessment mechanisms.
* Provide training for caregivers and professionals.
* Seek funding for community based skill training.
* Maintain private sector and NGO participation in CBR.

### 5.15 Disability, adequate standard of living and social protection

**Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Article 28**

*States shall:*
* Safeguard the right to an adequate standard of living including food, clothing and housing

**Key issues**

PWDs are more prone to poverty. Social protection should empower independence rather than encourage state dependency. Medical assessment of disability needs to move toward a holistic approach.

**Policy commitment:** The government shall ensure a sustainable social protection system that caters for persons with disabilities

**Policy objective:**
To ensure that adequate social support are in place for persons with disabilities

**Strategic Actions:**
* Ensure effective access to poverty reduction programmes.
* Provide financial assistance, counseling, and respite care to those in poverty.
* Encourage work to reduce dependency.
* Review Social Security Act regarding the termination of Invalidity Benefits.
* Shift from a purely medical to a holistic disability assessment approach.
* Ensure access to adapted public housing and clean water.
* Ensure equal access to retirement benefits.
* Consult DPOs in the development of social protection policies.

### 5.16 Disability, information, statistics and research

**Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Article 31**

*States shall:*
* Collect appropriate information, including statistical and research data

**Key issues**

There is a serious lack of reliable data, leading to overlooked needs. The 2010 Census was a start, but more specific data is needed for evidence-based planning.

**Policy commitment:** The government shall sensitize agencies and NGOs on the importance of collecting data on persons with disabilities

**Policy objective:**
To ensure the availability of data on persons with disabilities for evidence based planning

**Strategic Actions:**
* Make data collection tools available.
* Develop a database on people with disabilities.
* Ensure PWD data is included in national surveys/census.
* Disseminate available statistics and research.
* Encourage specific research in disability areas.
* Ensure timely reporting to the UNCRPD committee.

## 6. IMPLEMENTATION FRAMEWORK

### 6.1 Institutional framework

The Social Affairs Department (Ministry of Social Affairs, Community Development and Sports) oversees disability issues. A multi-sectoral committee will be set up to coordinate implementation.

The National Council for Disabled (NCFD) serves as the key advisory agency for the government.

#### 6.2 The Social Affairs Department’s Core Function

* Sensitize individuals and families on personal/social responsibility.
* Provide support to restore social functioning.
* Secure protection of vulnerable groups.
* Rehabilitate and re-integrate marginalised groups.
* Carry out research and social impact assessments.
* Strengthen co-operation with stakeholders.
* Ensure availability of resources for the Department.

#### 6.3 The National Council for Disabled Core Function

* Coordinate activities of organisations engaged in the welfare of disabled persons.
* provide care and assistance.
* Promote rehabilitation and employment services.
* Secure employment for disabled persons.
* Advise Government on education, sports, and training programmes.
* Assist in providing necessary facilities for disabled persons.

#### 6.4 The Multi Stakeholder Committee on the implementation of the National Policy on Disability

* Ensure involvement of all relevant stakeholders.
* Hold stakeholders accountable for specific actions.
* Ensure the policy remains a ‘living’ document.

#### 6.5 Stakeholder responsibilities

**Government/Ministries**
* Create an enabling environment via legislation.
* Provide administrative structures and funding.
* Mainstream disability in all policies and services.
* Strengthen NCFD's monitoring capacity.

**National Council for Disabled**
* Monitor and evaluate policy implementation.
* Assist stakeholders in implementing actions.
* Organize sensitization sessions.
* Advise on policy revisions.

**NGOs/ Civil Society**
* Ensure the participation of PWDs and their families.
* Sensitize families on rights and policy.
* Lobby government for legal/administrative frameworks.

**Families/ communities**
* Facilitate participation in mainstream activities.
* Help PWDs advocate for their own rights.

## 7. RESOURCE MOBILISATION

Implementation requires human capacity, technical aids, and financial backing. While the government is the primary funder via the national budget, private sector and NGO support, along with development partners, are essential to fill knowledge and experience gaps.

## 8. POLICY REVIEW

This policy shall be reviewed every 5 years or earlier upon the decision of the Multi stakeholder Committee.
