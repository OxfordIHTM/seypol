# DRAFT Government Social Media Policy

**FOR REVIEW - CONFIDENTIAL LAURIE-MAE LEPATHY- ANSAH**

## Context and Overview

### 1. Introduction

Globally, social media platforms are vastly changing the way we communicate. They are generally the first ports of calls for keeping people in touch, getting the latest news, coordinating events and staging live streaming. They enable great collaboration and engagement between families, friends, colleagues, organisations and members of the public. They help to positively promote advocacy and strengthen profiles as well as facilitate quicker networking, in real time.

In the Seychelles, among the various social networking websites available, the most commonly used are Facebook, YouTube, Instagram and Twitter. In Government, the majority of employees have access to such social media platforms either at work or outside of work. For example, Facebook is the one place people go to for entertainment or the latest news.

## Rationale

It’s important to note that with the rapid rise of social media comes the need for responsibility, compliance and reputation management. This is particularly vital given the recent increase in the misuse of social media. In the case of the Seychelles, we are seeing a worrying pattern of online abuse, attempts at defamation, security breaches, mismanagement of personal data and an increase in the posting of false, unverified statements that lead to confusion, create unnecessary panic or general discontent.

In order to remove such negative practices – specifically within Government offices – and promote the safe and effective use of social media, certain key guidelines need to be provided and rules set, which must be adhered to by all employees. This will help highlight the real dangers posed by social media, help protect Government’s reputation by highlighting core responsibilities of users, educate and provide guidance on who holds the official voice for Government in any Ministry, Department or Agency.

## Scope

This policy applies to **all Government employees including contractors and temporary workers across all Ministries, Departments and Agencies (MDAs)** who use social media for both personal use (accessing from a device outside of work – including personal ones like smartphones) and work purposes (accessing from a device at the Ministry, Department or Agency or what we can also refer to as ‘the work place’). It also covers the use of social media either when employees are in the Seychelles or abroad - whether on personal visits or work missions.

Social media sites and services covered include (but are not limited to):

* **Social networks** – Facebook, Twitter, YouTube, WhatsApp
* **Sharing and discussion forums** – Delicious, Reddit, Drop Box and Huddle
* **Photographic networks** – Flickr, Google Photos, Instagram, Snapchat
* **Question and answer platforms** – Quora and Yahoo Answers
* **Professional networks** – LinkedIn

This policy should be used alongside The Government of Seychelles’ internet use policy

## Responsibilities of Employees

### 4.1 Personal use of social media

Use of social media personal accounts should be restricted to outside normal working hours. The only exception is if you are using it for work purposes. Below are some core best practice:

* **Know the social network** - spend time becoming familiar with the social network before contributing. It’s important to read any FAQs and understand what is and is not acceptable on a social network site before posting messages or updates
* **If unsure, don’t post it** - err on the side of caution when posting to social networks. If you feel an update or message might cause complaints or offence - or be otherwise unsuitable - then do not post it
* **Be thoughtful and polite** - many social media users have got into trouble simply by failing to observe basic good manners online. Adopt the same level of courtesy used when communicating via email

* **Don’t escalate things** - It’s easy to post a quick response to a contentious status update and then regret it. Always take the time to think before responding, and hold back if you are in any doubt at all

# Key rules to be adhered to by ALL employees:

* Personal social media accounts cannot be used to share opinions on behalf of any Ministry, Department or Agency
    * Before you **personally engage, reply to posts/comments or participate in any conversations or discussions anywhere on social media platforms** make sure that it’s not in relation to information posted on colleagues at work, Managers, Heads of Departments, CEOs, Ministers, Government Ministries, Departments or Agencies
    * Making any commitments or promises on behalf of your Ministry, Agency or Department on your personal account is not allowed. Direct enquiries should go to your Communications or PR officers at your work place
    * Make sure that information you are sharing is not **defamatory or could incur liability** for Government

* If the information you are sharing could **damage the image or reputation of the Government of Seychelles, do not post**
    * **Offensive or harassing material** that negatively impact others is forbidden – even if this is done through the creation of fake accounts
    * Beware of your messages, some status updates or links to material or **content can be classed as inappropriate**

**Inappropriate content includes:** pornography, racial or religious slurs, gender-specific comments, information encouraging criminal skills or terrorism, or materials relating to cults, gambling and illegal drugs.

This definition of inappropriate content or material also covers any text, images or other media that could reasonably offend someone on the basis of race, age, sex, religious or political beliefs, national origin, disability or any other characteristic protected by law.

* Social media should not be used for any **illegal or criminal activities**
    * **Unsolicited views** on social, political, religious or other non-business related matters should not be broadcast on social platforms
    * Be mindful of when uploading, forwarding links and try to identify **spams, junk emails or chain emails and messages**

# Social media management in all MDAs

* Social media platforms’ management in Government offices will be the duty of the Communications or PR officer, where relevant, in consultation with the Information Officer. In cases where a person performs both roles, this will be handled by the Communications and PR officer.
* The Communications or PR officer should monitor the social media platforms regularly and respond appropriately to comments

* **Before posting** any latest news or other public-facing information, reply to comments or provide updates, the Communications or PR officer should run the content by their Principal Secretary or Minister and where appropriate (for example in the publication of statistics or reports), get appropriate vetting, collective agreement and sign off or even clearance **prior to** publication. This is to ensure that the right information is being disseminated in a timely manner and that all parties have been consulted and agree on the content.
* In the event that the Communications or PR officer is absent, then a deputy should be pre-appointed and trained on what they should do, when and how, sticking to the same guidelines followed by the Communications or PR officer
* Employees are free to comment, reply to posts or share opinions on official Government social media sites but **not to generate new content on behalf of any Ministry, Agency or Department**.
* Employees can also help the Communications or PR officers to roll out communication ideas like new videos or info graphics for official media sites. This can be for example suggestion of items that support their respective Ministry’s, Department’s or Agency’s vision or objectives
* Please note that the **key rules mentioned in section 4.2** are also applicable in the work place and must be adhered to at all times by all Government employees including Communications, PR and Information officer.
* MDAs need to ensure that adherence to the social media policy is a contractual condition in the contracts of their service providers (inclusive of contractors and consultants) and agreed to by their temporary employees.

# Communications and PR officers’ key roles

In managing a Ministry, Agency or Department’s social media platform(s) the Communications, PR or Information officer should:

* Carefully consider the social media channel(s) being used, suit(s) the Ministry’s, Agency’s or Department’s communications objective. Ongoing measurement is key. This can be done through the use of metrics or analytics (for example via the Insights feature on Facebook and Instagram). Monitoring number of likes, comments and shares is advisable
* Respond to enquiries and requests for information in a timely manner
* Monitor activities and keep the social media platforms fresh by providing regular updates, adding latest news and photos
* Share insightful articles, videos, media and other content in line with the Ministry’s Agency or Department’s objectives
* Take care to read comments from participants carefully before responding or sharing
* Comment on posts from colleagues, stakeholders or the public so that they are encouraged to remain active on the site(s)
* Be open to receiving ideas from colleagues and others in the work place
* Support announcements and other initiatives/projects being carried out or launched by their Ministry, Agency or Department by either commenting, sharing or liking the posts and inviting colleagues and others to do the same
* Create a social media crisis management model as part of their overall communications strategy. This will include when and how to react to contentious issues posted on social network platform(s) which can come up at any time. They should discuss next course of actions with their PS or Minister.

# Social media accounts creation

The Communications or PR officer will be responsible for:

* Creating a new/or deleting an existing or redundant social media page within a Government Ministry, Agency or Department. They will need to obtain agreement from their respective PS or Minister before actioning
* Submitting requests to the Department of Information Communications Technology (DICT) for either giving individuals access to YouTube/removing access, once the PS or Minister has given the go-ahead

# Intellectual property

The Government respects and operates within Intellectual property laws applicable in Seychelles (eg. Copyright Act)

# Users cannot use social media to:

* Publish or share any copyrighted software, media or materials owned by third parties, unless permitted by that third party
* Share links to illegal copies of music, films, games or other software
* Share or use images/photos without the agreement of people featured in them

If employees wish to share content published on another website, they are free to do so if that website has obvious sharing buttons or functions on it.

# Security, confidentiality and data protection

All employees should be aware of the need for confidentiality, security and data protection issues that can arise from using social networks.

**CONSIDER A CLAUSE TO PROTECT WHISTLEBLOWERS?**

Users must not:

* Share or link to any content or information owned by the Government of Seychelles that could be considered confidential or sensitive. Please consult with the Principal Secretary or Minister if you are in doubt.
* Share or link to any content or information owned by another Ministry, Agency or Department that could be considered confidential or commercially sensitive. For example, if another Ministry’s document was leaked online, this should not be mentioned by anyone on social media unless a response has been formulated through an official channel.
* Share or link to data in any way that could breach any prevailing data protection legislation in force.

# Protecting social accounts

* All Government social media accounts should be protected by strong passwords that are changed regularly and shared only with authorised users. These passwords need approving by the Minister or PS
* Wherever possible, two-factor authentication (often called mobile phone verification) passwords should be used as security
* When necessary DICT will carry out relevant system updates or upgrades to make sure Government remains in line with local and international norms and security requirements.

# Avoiding social scams

* All Government employees should be on alert and look out for phishing attempts, or any other attempts, where scammers may try to use deception to obtain information relating to either a Ministry, Agency, Department or the Government through social media platforms. If such attempts are detected the impacted/targeted MDA and DICT should be immediately alerted.
* Employees should avoid clicking links in posts, updates and direct messages that look suspicious. In particular, users should look out for URLs contained in generic or vague- sounding direct messages.
# Policy enforcement

## Monitoring social media use

* Government IT and internet resources — including computers, smart phones and internet connections — are provided to carry out legitimate Government business. They are not for personal use. Employees therefore should always be careful when using them to access social media keeping in mind the key rules as per 4.2.
* All data relating to social networks written, sent or received through the Government’s computer systems is considered part of official Government of Seychelles records.
* Government reserves the right to seek the deactivation of any social media accounts that are not officially endorsed by the Government and impersonating Government officials or MDAs.

# Potential sanctions

* Knowingly breaching this social media policy is a serious matter. **Employees who do not abide by the set of guidelines or rules set out in this policy** will be subject to disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment.
* Contractors or non-permanent employees working for MDAs will be considered to be in breach of their contracts or work agreements for non-adherence to this policy.
