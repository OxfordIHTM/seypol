Since the initiation of the Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) in Seychelles, it has known many successes. The Ministry of Health recognizes the crucial role that immunization plays in reducing child morbidity and mortality and it affirms its responsibility to ensure that the people of Seychelles are protected from vaccine preventable diseases.

This policy document provides strategic guidance for the implementation of the immunization programme in Seychelles. It has been developed to ensure an effective and coordinated response to improve the quality of life of the population through the provision of optimal immunization services against vaccine preventable diseases.

## Introduction / Background

The national immunization policy of Seychelles was first developed 2012 It was updated in 2017 to include new antigens and technologies that were introduced in the program. The changes in the expanding scope of the Immunization program over the past decade have prompted the Ministry of Health to revise the policy in 2017.

The national EPI policy will assist in translating global policies into local action and improve the availability, quality and safety of immunization. It will also provide a framework for monitoring and evaluation.

### Purpose

This policy is issued to standardise the principles that immunization providers need to adhere ensuring standard practices are followed to facilitate the attainment of national targets to drive expected public health outcomes such as control, elimination and eradication of targeted diseases by vaccines for gaining population health, productive individuals that contribute to the economic development of the country.

### Policy Development Context

The EPI policy has been largely informed by the international and national laws and policies. The include the Sustainable Development Goals, the convention of the riths of the chil, the UN convention on the Rights of People with Disabilities, the international conference on population and Development, the paris decleration on harmonization and Alignemnt and the International Health Regulation.

In 2015, Seychelles has updated the National Health policy which builds on the revised National Health Strategic plan that replaces the national strategic framework that ended in 2016 (2006-2016). The health policy framework positions health at the centre of national development, both as a beneficiary of and a contributor to socioeconomic development. The policy affirms the nation's commitment to health. Its purpose is to guide within the medium Strategic plan for strategic planning, program implementation, monitoring and evaluation.

The vision of the National Health Strategy, is the attainment, by all people in Seychelles of the highest level of physical, social, mental and spiritual health and living in harmony with nature. The mission is to improve the health status of the Seychellois population through policy actions in all sectors and by expanding preventive, promotive, curative, palliative and rehabilitative health programmes including Immunisation program. Hence the need for the National Immunization Programme, to align and update the policy.

### Guiding Principles

**Equity**

This policy will ensure equitable service delivery to all people in Seychelles despite their social, gender, economic, demographic or geographical definition and allocation of resources in accordance with needs.

Access to health care is a fundamental human right spelt out in the Universal access to health services and the Ministry of health is and has been providing free immunisation services as part of the free health care provided to all the communities.

The Seychelles Expanded Programme on Immunisation (EPI) policy direction is based on WHO/ UNICEF guidelines and other national and international standards.

Despite our significant successes in maintaining the high immunisation coverage amongst our population, the EPI is presently competing with other priorities the Ministry of Health. The current health sector reforms and the drive for cost-effectiveness call for re-orientation in the co-ordination, planning, management, monitoring and evaluation of immunisation services.

The increasing complexity of the working environment for staff involved in immunisation and the high turnover rate of health personnel, also dictate a strong need for updated policy direction in the EPI. More than 60% of our medical practitioners are non-Seychellois and they may not be acquainted with the local situation and requirements of EPI programme in Seychelles. They therefore need to be made acquainted with this EPI policy that aims towards achieving our national goals.

* **Safety and potency:** Safety and potency of vaccine including prcrurment storge, transportation and administration will be ensured at all times
* **Universal accessibility:** Immunization Services shall be accessible to all eligible people in Seychelles along the life course at the nearest health center
* **Acceptability:** The vaccines delivered through EPI program should be acceptable to all targeted groups, care takers and community
* **Ownership and sustainability:** the policy shall provide a freamework to support the Sustainable delivery of Immunization services in a cost effective and an uninterrupted manner. Alternative and sustainable options for immunization financing and organization and financing shall be explored guided by the comprehensive costed multiyear plan for Immunization. Stakeholders will be actively involved in planning , implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the immunization services at all levels
* **Evidence based programming /interventions:** Decision making shall be supported by scientific and proven findings. There will be room for introduction of new vaccines and innovative new technologies onto the national scheduele as deemed necessary based on scientific evidence and as resources allow.
* **Accountability:** All immunization stakeholders , service providers and partners will take responsibility for their actions and value for money will be considered for cost effectiveness

### Applicability and Scope

This policy will be effected from the endorsement date and will be applied by both public and private health care services who are or will be providing immunization services for the population in Seychelles.

Free Primary Health Care at the point of use is a Constitutional Right of all Seychellois and the Immunisation Programme is one of the key components of primary health care. Purchase of all vaccines, injection supplies and all other vaccine spending used for the routine immunisation programme are financed entirely by the Government of Seychelles.

The Ministry of Health regards communities as being the main stakeholder and partner in the immunisation programme and actively promote and support all initiatives geared towards awareness creation, demand generation, attitude change and community participation. All possible avenues and community-based structures are involve in the EPI programme activities and follow up and tracing of clients is one of the priority activities of the Child Health/EPI nurse.

The frame work for this policy is derived from the constitution of Seychelles and the Public Heath Act of xxxx . It is effected from xx/xx/2018 until it is reviewed or updated. This immunization policy for Seychelles provides a review of present Immunization policy recommended by WHO and adopted by the EPI program and will serve as the basis to develop appropriate guidelines, work plans and strategies for health workers at all levels. It has taken into consideration the AFRO Regional Immunization strategic plan strategies that is aligned with the Global Vaccine Action plan (2012-2018); that have both defined goals that Seychelles EPI has adapted and articulated where the aim is achieving universal immunization coverage by 2020. ## 8. Policy statement by System component

### 8.1 Service Delivery and programme management

The government of Seychelles shall remain fully committed towards the National Immunization Programme.

* All relevant health facilities in Seychelles shall provide quality and timely immunization services as part of their routine programmes;
* All health workers shall abide to the recommended National Immunization Schedule,
* Service providers shall follow standard procedures for administration of vaccines in accordance with set guidelines,
* Relevant health facilities shall organize their own immunization sessions depending on their target groups,
* Outreach services shall be organized in order to ensure that all of the target groups are reached and immunized.
* There shall be guidelines to address immunization needs for high risk and vulnerable groups
* EPI services shall be fully integrated with other related programmes or services
* New vaccines and immunization technologies shall be introduced based on the need of the country

### 8.2 Vaccine supply, quality and logistics

* A qualified logistician shall be responsible to manage all aspects of vaccine supply, EPI logistics and report directly to the EPI manager
* The cold chain system shall be carefully monitored in all health facilities to ensure that good quality of vaccines is maintained.
* All vaccines procured for the national EPI shall be pre-qualified by WHO and shall include Vaccine Vial Monitor (VVM)
* There shall be vaccine stock of six months’ supply and two months buffer stock at national level at all times
* There shall be vaccine stock of one month supply at health facilities at all times
* The contingency plan shall be adhered to in the event of a breakdown in the cold chain system. Back-up generators shall be made available and operational at central and peripheral levels
* All cold chain equipment used in EPI shall be at WHO standard and maintained accordingly
* A cold chain inventory monitoring system shall be in place to ensure adequate cold storage facilities at central and peripheral levels
* Auto-Disabled syringes shall be available for use in administration of all vaccines
* A Cold chain replacement plan shall be in place for standardized cold chain equipment with regular updating of inventory

### 8.3 Advocacy and Communication

* A Social Mobilization Communication Officer shall be responsible to manage all aspects of EPI advocacy and communication and shall report directly to the EPI manager
* EPI shall make available appropriate information, education and communication materials which shall be disseminated through appropriate media and mechanisms to all stakeholders,
* All stakeholders shall have access to appropriate information, education and communication on national EPI
* Advocacy, communication and social mobilization strategies shall be developed to address vaccination refusal and defaulters that will be derived from the 2016 Immunization Communication strategic plan inclusive of risk communication strategy to address AEFI and vaccine hesitancy for future use

### 8.4 Vaccine preventable disease surveillance

* Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) strategy shall be applied by all stakeholders using a common methodology in recording, reporting and case/outbreak investigations and response.
* The Epidemiology and statistics Division shall continue to exchange information with local, regional or international bodies through publication of the weekly, monthly and annual Epidemiological Bulletin.
* The DSRU will support the monitoring and tracking of surveillance performance indicators for use to verify eradication, elimination of targeted VPDs aligned with national and global goals and targets
* Sentinel sites shall be established and operated supported by the DSRU to support evidence generation for disease burden of newly to be introduced for documentation of magnitude of disease burden and post introduction to monitor impact of introduced vaccines

### 8.5 Programme management

* All health facilities shall submit reports according to the reporting system in place
* An Inter-Agency Coordinating Committee shall be established and functional with the terms of reference determined by the Ministry for Health; in the interim as alternative the Senior MOH management committee will oversee the issues.
* Regular Supervision shall be conducted by the EPI Manager to all immunization sites.

### 8.6 Human resources capacity

* All new EPI staff shall be oriented to the EPI policy within six months of taking their post
* EPI curriculum shall be reviewed periodically to take into consideration new development

### 8.7 Sustainable financing

## II. VACCINE PREVENTABLE DISEASES IN SCHEDUELE OF SEYCHELLES PROGRAM (Optional)

### 2.1 Tuberculosis
Tuberculosis is a bacterial infection that is caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* from humans and *Mycobacterium bovis* from cattle. The transmission is by droplet infection. The disease is characterised by persistent cough for over two weeks, loss of appetite, loss of weight, fever and night sweats. In young children it is difficult to diagnose but might present as failure to respond to antibiotic treatment for acute respiratory infection. In some cases, it may present with generalised painful lymphadenopathy, signs suggesting meningitis or central nervous system diseases in children.

### 2.2 Diphtheria
Diphtheria is caused by the gram positive bacterium *Corynebacterium diphtheriae*. The only natural host is man and transmission occurs via respiratory contact. Incubation period is less than two weeks. Like any other respiratory infections transmission is increased in overcrowded and poor socio-economic conditions. The disease presents as swelling of the neck (bull neck), pseudo greyish membrane on the naso pharynx obstructing the airway and the membrane bleeds easily to touch.

### 2.3 Tetanus
Tetanus is caused by a gram positive anaerobic spore forming bacterium called *Clostridium tetani*. It is not a communicable disease but is acquired through environmental exposure. The main natural habitat for clostridium tetani spores is soil or animal and human feaces. In neonates it presents with history of normal suck and cry for the first two days of life and history of onset of illness between 3 – 28 days of age followed by stiff neck, inability to suck, lockjaw and body spasms.

In adults, there is a history of a wound within the past month followed by lockjaw, stiff neck, convulsions and photophobia. Neonatal tetanus is an important cause of infant mortality. Maternal tetanus can occur by post partum contamination of the uterus. In addition to vaccination, improving hygiene practices during delivery, proper cord proper and wound care are important interventions to reduce tetanus.

### 2.4 Pertussis (Whooping Cough)
Is a highly contagious disease caused by infection of the respiratory tract by the gram-negative *Bordetella-pertussis*. The transmission is via close respiratory contact with a symptomatic individual during the early stage of infection. Pertussis causes a severe cough of 4 - 8 weeks duration, with a characteristic whoop often with cyanosis and vomiting. In young infants the cough may be absent and the disease may manifest itself as spells of apneoa.

### 2.5 Poliomyelitis
Poliomyelitis is an acute viral infection which spreads via the faeco-oral route, thus transmission is higher in areas of poor sanitation. Where sanitation is good, pharyngeal spread becomes more important. The incubation period is between 6 and 20 days. The disease is characterised by sudden onset of fever followed by acute flaccid paralysis. The risk of paralysis is approximately 1 in 200 infections among infants below 1 year and 1 in 100 infections among children aged 1 - 14 years. The majority of cases with wild polio virus are asymptomatic.

### 2.6 Measles
Measles is an acute viral infection that is transmitted by close respiratory contact characterised by fever, generalised maculo-papular rash, and any of the following (coryza, conjunctivitis and cough). The incubation period is 7-10 days. Most deaths occur through secondary infections of the respiratory or gastrointestinal tract.

### 2.7 Mumps
Mumps is due to an infection by the mumps virus. It can be transmitted by respiratory secretions (e.g. saliva) from a person already affected with the condition. When contracting mumps, the virus travels from the respiratory tract to the salivary glands and reproduces, causing the glands to swell.

Individuals infected with the mumps virus are contagious for approximately 15 days (6 days before the symptoms start to show, and up to 9 days after they start). The mumps virus is part of the paramyxovirus family, a common cause of infection, especially in children.

### 2.8 Rubella
Rubella is an infectious viral disease characterized by a mild cutaneous maculopapular rash. The rubella rash occurs only in 50%-75% cases and is sometimes misdiagnosed as measles or scarlet fever. Rubella is a benign disease and children usually develop few or no constitutional symptoms. Post auricular and posterior cervical lymphadenopathy is characteristic and precedes the rash by 5-10 days. Arthralgia or arthritis may occur in up to 70% of adult women with rubella. Rare complications include thrombocytopenic purpura and encephalitis.

Rubella is transmitted through direct or droplet contact from nasopharyngeal secretions and has an average incubation period of 17 days. Persons with rubella are most infectious when rash is erupting, but they can shed virus from 7 days before to 7 days after rash onset. There is no specific treatment for rubella but the disease is preventable by vaccination

### 2.9 Hepatitis B
The causative agent is the Hepatitis B virus (HBV), a DNA virus whose natural host is man. The virus predominantly infects hepatocytes, in which it replicates. A component of the virus coat, Hepatitis B Surface Antigen (HBsAg) is present in large amounts in the bloodstream of infected individuals. Hepatitis B Surface Antigen is the first marker of infection to appear in the serum of persons exposed to the virus.

**Transmission routes:**
1. **Vertical:** from mother to child in utero and during birth
2. **Horizontal:** between siblings/family members by unspecified mechanism. Most likely by unrecognised transfer of saliva or blood from skin wounds.
3. **Parenteral or pecutaneous:** via infected blood, blood products, saliva and other body fluids. Routes of transmission include transfusion, sharing of contaminated needles, accidental needle prick-injuries, tattooing, ear piercing, oral surgery, splashes onto mucous membrane or penetration of skin by an infected object.
4. **Sexually:** via semen, vaginal secretions and blood.

Hepatitis B infection has a long incubation period (90 days on average) during which the individual is highly infectious. Individuals with chronic HBV infection are at a greatly increased risk of developing cirrhosis of the liver or primary hepatocellular carcinoma.

### 2.10 Haemophilus influenza type b diseases
Haemophilus influenza type B (Hib) is one of the six related types of bacterium. In 2000 H. Influenzae type B (Hib) was estimated to have caused two to three million cases of diseases , notable some types of pneumonia and meningitis,and 450 000 deaths in young children worldwide. The Hib bacterium is commonly present in the nose and throat .Bacteria are transmitted from person to person in droplets through sneezing and coughing.Infected children may carry Hib bacteria without showing any signs and symptoms of illness,but they can still infect others.The risk of disease is highest for children between six months and two years of age.Hib disease should be suspected in the case of any child with signs and symptoms of meningitis or pneumonia.

### 2.11 Human Papilloma Virus
Genital HPV infections are primarily transmitted by sexual contact, predominantly but not exclusively through penetrative intercourse. HPVs are highly transmissible, and most sexually active men and women will acquire an HPV infection at some time in their lives. Whereas most HPV infections are transient and benign, persistent genital infection with certain viral genotypes can lead to the development of anogenital precancers and cancers (WHO, 2009).

Diseases caused by HPV include cancers of the cervix, vagina, vulva, penis and anus; a subset of head and neck cancers; anogenital warts; and recurrent respiratory papillomatosis. In 2005, there were about 500 000 cases of cervical cancer and 260 000 related deaths worldwide. Cervical cancer incidence rates vary from 1–50 per 100 000 females; rates are highest in Latin America and the Caribbean, sub-Saharan Africa, Melanesia, and south-central and South-East Asia. Most cases of cervical cancer are diagnosed in women aged >40 years (WHO, 2007).

## 3. AVAILABLE VACCINE PREPARATIONS in use in the program

### 3.1 Bacille Calmette Guerin (BCG)
Bacille Calmette Guerin contains live attenuated *Mycrobacterium bovis*. It is a freeze-dried vaccine. The vaccine requires reconstitution with appropriate diluent before administration. BCG is very sensitive to light and usually manufacturers pack it in dark containers/vials. The clinical efficacy in preventing pulmonary tuberculosis has ranged from 0 – 80%. Efficacy does not depend on BCG strain or manufacturer. BCG vaccine protects against TB meningitis, miliary tuberculosis as well as leprosy. Hence the vaccine protects against blood borne dissemination of the bacteria but does not limit the growth of localised foci that occurs in pulmonary tuberculosis. Therefore BCG immunization at birth will reduce the morbidity and mortality from tuberculosis in infants.

### 3.2 Tetanus Toxoid
Tetanus toxoid (TT) is a formaldehyde inactivated preparation of tetanus toxin adsorbed onto aluminium salts to increase its antigenicity. The vaccine is stable and can withstand exposure to room temperature without a significant loss of potency. It induces the formation of specific antitoxin, which neutralises the toxin and is a highly effective vaccine. In most cases, the efficacy of two doses of TT during pregnancy in preventing NNT has ranged from 80 - 100%.

### 3.3 Pertussis Vaccine
The pertussis vaccine used in Seychelles is whole cell pertussis that is whole cell vaccine that contains whole pertussis bacteria killed by chemicals or heat. The vaccine is effective in preventing serious illness but does not protect completely against infection from the organism.

### 3.4 Diphtheria Toxoid
It is a formaldehyde inactivated preparation of diphtheria toxin, adsorbed onto aluminium salts to increase its antigenicity. This toxoid protects against the action of the toxin. Immunised persons can be infected by toxin - producing strains of diphtheria, but the systemic manifestations of diphtheria do not occur. Diphtheria toxoid is almost always administered together with tetanus toxoid and pertussis vaccine as part of DTP vaccine and is also available as a component of DT.

### 3.5 Poliomyelitis Vaccine
There are two types of vaccine against poliomyelitis: oral and injectable. The current Oral polio (OPV) vaccine is composed of two sero types of attenuated polio viruses (1, and 3). Because of its ease of administration, superiority in conferring intestinal immunity, Seychelels EPI adopts OPV as the vaccine of choice for Polio eradication. However it has included 1 dose of IPV at 4 months since 2015.

**Inactivated Polio Vaccine (IPV):**
IPV is produced from wild-type poliovirus strains of each serotype that have been inactivated (killed) with formalin. IPV provides serum immunity to all three types of poliovirus, resulting in protection against paralytic poliomyelitis. Generally three spaced doses are administered to generate adequate levels of seroconversion, Most studies indicate that the degree of mucosal immunity in the intestine is significantly less than that provided by OPV, although this difference may be less pronounced in the pharyngeal mucosal lining.

### 3.6 Measles Mumps Rubella Vaccine
Measles vaccine is a live attenuated viral preparation derived from various measles virus strains. It is very sensitive to light and most manufacturers pack it in dark coloured containers/vials. The vaccine efficacy has been measured at 85% if given at 9 months of age and 95% at 12 months.

### 3.7 Hepatitis B Vaccine (HB)
There are two types of Hep B vaccine containing HbsAg, which are Plasma derived vaccine and DNA recombinant vaccine. Both vaccines are safe and immunogenic and highly efficacious with over 90% of susceptible children developing a protective antibody response after 3 doses.

### 3.8 Haemophilus influenza B Vaccine
Hib vaccine is one of the new generations of vaccines known as conjugate vaccines. Conjugation is a process whereby part of the Hib bacterium is connected (conjugated) to certain protein derived from toxoids of tetanus or diphtheria toxoid – like proteins, or a mix of proteins from another bacterium. With three doses, 90 - 99% of children vaccinated develop adequate protection against Hib infections.

## 4. REGULATORY ISSUES RELATING TO VACCINE USE

Seychelles currently imports all vaccines for its immunization programme.

## 5. COLD CHAIN MAINTENANCE

Cold chain is a system used for keeping and distributing vaccines in a potent state which consists of a series of storage and transport links from manufacturer to recipient. The elements of an effective cold chain system are knowledgeable health personnel, adequate functional equipment, vaccine supplies and transport. All vaccines are sensitive to heat, light and/or cold.

**Table 1: Recommended Temperatures for Vaccine Storage**

| LEVEL | DURATION | TEMPERATURE |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Central** | 3-6 months | Polio -15°C to -25°C; Measles and BCG -15°C to -25°C or +2°C to +8°C; Toxoids +2°C to +8°C |
| **Health Centre** | 1 month | +2°C to +8°C |
| **Transit** | Not more than 24 hours | All vaccines +2°C to +8°C |

* Polio is the vaccine that is most sensitive to heat and therefore should be kept on the coldest part of the refrigerator.
* MMR and BCG are very sensitive to light and should not be exposed to direct light.
* All toxoids are affected by freezing. If frozen, they will lose their potency. These vaccines should be kept on the part of the refrigerator where temperatures are likely to stay between +2 to + 8 degrees centigrade.
* All vaccine refrigerators or freezers or cold rooms should have temperatures checked twice daily.
* A shake test should be done to determine if toxoids were frozen.
* Vaccines should be transported under cold chain using the fastest possible means.
* Fully conditioned icepacks should be used to transport toxoids and Hepatitis B at temperatures between +2 and + 8 degrees centigrade.
* Polio, Measles and BCG may be transported at temperatures below 0°C.
* EPI refrigerators should be used for storage of EPI vaccines **only**.
* Do not store vaccines in the door shelves of the refrigerator.
* Health workers should ensure that vaccines are not frozen in the vaccine carrier by using fully conditioned ice packs.
* All levels should keep reserve stock: Central/Provincial (25%), District/Periphery (100%).
* **Cold chain failure procedure:**
    * Pack vaccines and diluents in a cold box or vaccine carrier.
    * A cold box can keep temperatures for up to five days.
    * A vaccine carrier (Flask) can keep temperatures for 48 hrs.
    * Transport vaccines to the nearest health facility with functional cold chain if power is not restored within 48 hours.
    * Have a contingency plan for cold chain failure.

**Conditioning frozen coolant packs**

Allow coolant packs to sit at room temperature until ice begins to melt and water starts to form. You should check to see if a coolant pack has been conditioned by shaking it. If ice starts to move, then the icepack is conditioned. This will prevent freeze-sensitive vaccines from freezing.

## 7. VACCINE ADMINISTRATION

* Vaccines should be administered by qualified licensed nurses and doctors.
* The injection site should not be cleaned. Alcohol or anti septic lotion or spirit should **NEVER** be used to clean the injection site.
* Give information on possible side effects to the mother or guardian.
* Vaccines should always be kept in a vaccine carrier with a functional thermometer, freeze tag and fully conditioned coolant packs.
* Use auto-disable syringes.
* Record date and batch number on the Child Health Card and EPI register.
* Use provided diluents from the same manufacturer. **DO NOT USE WATER FOR INJECTION OR NORMAL SALINE FOR RECONSTITUTING BCG AND MEASLES VACCINES.**
* Different vaccines should never be mixed in one syringe for injection.

## 8. INJECTION SAFETY

Safe injection is defined as one which does not harm the patient, health worker, public, or environment. The goal is to eliminate all risks of infections attributed to unsafe injection practices.

* Auto Disable (AD) syringes should be used at all health facilities.
* No disposable syringe or needle should be reused.
* Used needles/syringes should be disposed of immediately into a safety/sharps box without recapping.
* Destruction of used injection materials should be via high-temperature incineration.

## 9. MONITORING COVERAGE AND DROP OUTS

* All health facilities should monitor coverage and drop out rates on a monthly basis and reflect this on the immunization monitor chart.

## 10. MONITORING VACCINE WASTAGE

* Every health facility should monitor vaccine wastage monthly using the recommended vaccine wastage monitoring form and stock card.

## 11. MULTIDOSE VIAL POLICY

**Use of opened vials in subsequent sessions:**

Applies only to: Pentavalent, DTP, TT, DT and OPV, provided they are not expired, contaminated, or submerged in water, and VVM is acceptable.

* **Does NOT apply to:** BCG and Measles (reconstituted vaccines). These must be discarded at the end of the session.
* Opened vials of Pentavalent, DTP, Hep B, TT and DT returned to the refrigerator should be labeled with the date opened and discarded after seven days.
* Vials taken for outreach/national days are discarded at the end of each day.
* Discard immediately if sterile procedures were not observed or contamination is suspected.

## 12. INTERVAL BETWEEN MULTIDOSES

* Minimum interval between doses is **four weeks**.
* If a dose is given at less than the recommended 4 weeks interval, it should not be counted as part of the primary series.

## 13. HOSPITAL ADMISSION POLICY ON IMMUNIZATION

* Measles, Mumps and Rubella vaccine should be given on admission to children 15 months to 14 years if not previously vaccinated.
* Health workers should ascertain the vaccination status for all admitted children.

## 14. RESPONSE TO VACCINES

* Maternal antibodies can interfere with response. For example, measles immunization is typically given at 9 months.
* In Seychelles, Hepatitis B first dose is given at 3 months due to lower perinatal infection rates.
* Preterm infants should follow the same schedule as term infants.

## 15. CONTRAINDICATIONS TO VACCINATIONS

Fever, diarrhoea, mild respiratory infection and malnutrition are **NOT** contraindications.

* **BCG:** Should not be given to a child with symptomatic HIV infection.
* **DPT/Pentavalent:** If a child severely reacted to a previous dose (anaphylaxis, collapse, convulsions), DT should be given instead.
* **Neurological disease:** Children with uncontrolled epilepsy or progressive encephalopathy should not be given DPT; give DT instead.

## 16. MISSED OPPORTUNITIES

A missed opportunity occurs when an eligible person visits a health facility but does not receive their due vaccines. Facilities should use the "supermarket approach," offering immunization daily regardless of the visit's primary reason.

## 17. THE EXPECTED EFFECTS OF IMMUNIZATION ON DISEASE EPIDEMIOLOGY

* **Herd Immunity:** Relevant for live viral vaccines (Measles, Polio) which reduce transmission rates.
* **Toxoids:** (TT, Diphtheria) protect against the toxin but not necessarily the infection itself.
* Immunization changes the relative age distribution of cases, shifting them toward older ages.

## 18. OUTBREAKS

Outbreaks can occur in pockets of low coverage or among older children. 

* **Polio:** Suspected outbreak is a cluster of 2+ compatible AFP cases in neighboring districts within two months. Investigation must be rapid (within 48 hours).
* **Neonatal Tetanus:** Investigate cases and immunize all non-immunized WCBA in the area.
* **Measles:** Response includes mass vaccination of children 6 months to 14 years in the affected area.
* **Diphtheria:** Rapid widespread immunization for age groups 3-59 months.
* **Pertussis:** Prevention involves immunization with Pentavalent vaccine.
* **Pneumococcal/Diarrheal:** Highlighted as significant causes of child mortality globally.

## 19. HIV INFECTION AND IMMUNIZATION

* Children with known or asymptomatic HIV should receive all EPI vaccines.
* HIV-infected children should receive early measles vaccine at 6 months and repeat at 9 months.
* BCG should **not** be given to children with clinical symptoms of HIV/AIDS.

## 20. ADVERSE EVENTS FOLLOWING IMMUNIZATION (AEFI)

* Each event must be investigated. An emergency kit for anaphylactic shock must be available at all levels.
* 5 copies of AEFI forms must be completed for each incident.

Focuses on social mobilisation and interpersonal communication. The National Immunization Awareness Week shall be commemorated annually.
