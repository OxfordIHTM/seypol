## Executive Summary

Immunization has been one of Seychelles’ greatest public health success stories. However, in order to ensure that all children of Seychelles benefit equitably from this intervention, a strategic, i.e., long-term approach to planning and implementation is essential. This third comprehensive Multi Year Plan (cMYP) provides a framework for planning activities to achieve the major objectives of the national immunization programme.

Several achievements have been attained including sustainability of immunization coverage above 99% for all antigens, absence of mortality due to vaccine preventable diseases (VPDs), 100% procurement of vaccines and injection materials by the Government and attainment of polio certification level indicators. 

However, sustaining high immunization coverage, prevention of the risk of importation of VPDs particularly in view of the numerous tourists visiting the country and high procurement costs are among the challenges. 

The projected cost of the programme in the planning period (2019 - 2023) to include health systems shared cost as well as disease surveillance is approximately **$4,881,975**.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

### 1.1 Country Profile

The Republic of Seychelles comprises 115 islands in the Indian Ocean. Out of this number, only 22 are inhabited. The largest island is Mahe where 90% of the country population lives and where the capital city, Victoria, is located.

### 1.3 Macro economic situation

During the planning period of 2019 – 2023, Seychelles economic performance is expected to continue on the strong trajectory of growth. The world bank data for Gross National Income per capita indicate upward trend rising from $10,190 in 2010 to 14,010 in 2015 and $14,180 in 2017.

### 1.3 National Health System

The Government provides universal access to free primary health care. The national health system operates on three main levels of care:
1.  Primary/ community care
2.  Secondary care at hospital and specialized facilities
3.  Tertiary care which includes overseas treatment.

## HEALTH SECTOR FINANCING

Domestic health expenditure for health as a percentage of current health expenditure has been on the increase in Seychelles rising from 90.48% in 2011 to 99.54% in 2015.

### 1.3.1 Seychelles Expanded Programme on Immunization

The National Immunization Programme (NIP) in Seychelles was initiated in 1808 with obligatory small pox vaccination. Over the years, the immunization schedule has expanded to include new antigens such as MMR, Yellow Fever, Hepatitis B, Haemophilus influenzae type b, HPV, IPV, and Rotavirus Vaccine.

### 1.3.2 Organisational & functional arrangement of the programme

The National Immunization Programme falls under the direction of Health Care Agency of the Ministry of Health, overseen by the Director of Family Health and Nutrition.

## 2. SITUATION ANALYSIS

### 2.1 Situation analysis of the Immunization programme

#### 2.1.1 Service Delivery
High quality immunization services are provided by qualified nurses. The DPT1, DPT3 and MCV1 coverage shows a high reported coverage above 95%.

#### 2.1.2 EPI Coverage performance
Reported coverage of all antigens has been high. DTP3 national level estimates remained above 95% between 2005-2015.

#### 2.1.3 Accelerated Disease Control & VPD surveillance
*   **Polio Eradication:** Last confirmed case in 1966. Polio-free certification accepted in 2005.
*   **Measles/ CRS Elimination:** Latest outbreak in 2006. MMR coverage sustained above 95%.
*   **MNT Elimination:** Sustained elimination since early 2000.

#### 2.1.4 Vaccine Supply, Quality and Cold chain
Since 2005, the Ministry of Health handles full procurement. Only WHO pre-qualified vaccines are purchased. Procurement is managed via UNICEF MOU.

## 3. The Strength, weakness, Opportunities and Threats

#### Table 4. Analysis of Strength, Weakness by Immunization System Components 2013-June 2018

| Component | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Service Delivery** | All births registered; tracking system for defaulters; a high coverage sustained; integrated services. | School dropouts; emerging vaccine hesitant groups. |
| **Vaccine Supply & Cold Chain** | Forecasts consistently done; WHO pre-qualified vaccines; good cold chain monitoring. | Stock out of HPV 2017; Store capacity not optimum; over-dependence on one focal person. |
| **Advocacy & Communication** | KAP study conducted; strong one-to-one communication; free media airtime. | Sub-optimal awareness of new vaccines (IPV, Rota, HPV); limited budget; emerging hesitancy. |
| **VPD Surveillance** | Electronic data system functions well; high timeliness; National Polio Certification Committee active. | Need for refresher training; private sector fails to notify; Central lab awaiting WHO accreditation. |
| **Management & Leadership** | Updated EPI policy; AEFI committee established; quarterly reporting system. | NITAG not established; no regular inter-programme meetings; weak supervision at HF level. |
| **Financing** | 100% government financing for vaccines and materials. | Some reliance on WHO for cold chain equipment. |
| **Data Management** | Accurate targets using Post Natal records; unique National ID for tracking. | Inconsistencies between daily and monthly records; no backup at HF level. |
