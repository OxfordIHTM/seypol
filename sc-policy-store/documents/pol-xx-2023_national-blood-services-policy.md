## FOREWORD

Blood saves life and the gift of blood is a philanthropic offering of the highest order.

Our country and our Ministry of Health take absolute pride in the fact that we have always sought to consistently provide safe blood to all recipients and to a large extent, we have succeeded in doing so with flying colours. Kudos goes, first and foremost, to our generous voluntary blood donors.

Where we have not entirely succeeded thus far, is in permanently having adequate supplies of all blood groups and blood products in the blood bank. Thankfully, every flash appeal for blood donations, that we have made at critical times, have always fallen on willing ears and our fellow citizens have come forward, often in droves, to offer this profound gift of life.

Our modus operandi, as caregivers, is to be proactive rather than reactive, to be ahead of the curve rather than behind it, to be ready rather than apologetic. This new policy aims at ensuring that our country has adequate supplies of blood and blood products at all times, night and day, in good times and bad times, so that we can continue to provide safe blood on demand, to all residents and visitors in this country, whenever and wherever they need it.

The government is closely watching the rapid evolution of the national health system, with the increasing number of health service providers, some of which are now venturing into post-basic or even advanced health care. This National Blood Services Policy provides for a new front to be rapidly opened in the area of legislation, regulation, and robust standard setting, all in the interest of patient safety and higher quality of care.

As I talk of patient safety and higher quality of health care, I cannot omit to steer the conversation also in the direction of prevention. A road traffic accident prevented means less blood is needed to address its consequences. Health and safety at work and play mean fewer misfortunes and less blood and blood products needed. Many diseases that need blood transfusion can also be prevented by sustained preventive actions at the level of the individual, the family and the community. If we continue to work on prevention, early diagnosis and health and safety, less blood will need to be transfused and whatever supplies we have will reach much further forward.

I thank all the voluntary blood donors who are ever ready to help our fellow citizens. I thank all the numerous organizations in the public, private and non-profit sectors who, for many years, have been faithful with strong advocacy and with actions that resonate even louder than their words through their repeated donations of blood. Each drop of blood offered infused new life in not just an individual but also a family and a community and for that the whole nation is grateful.

In fine, I thank the team of health professionals and health service users who have laboured for long hours to put together this important policy document. Let’s work, now, on the crucial operationalization.

**Peggy Vidot**
*Minister for Health*

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Blood and blood products is an important component of modern health care. Blood transfusion may decrease mortality, and prolong and improve the quality of life of many people with different pathologies. Timely access to safe blood products, and the optimal use of such products, are essential for effective health service delivery and the achievement of universal health coverage.

The Clinical Laboratory at the Seychelles Hospital has ran a blood transfusion services unit for many years to respond to an increasing demand for blood and blood products.

In 2001, the WHO Regional Office for Africa adopted a strategy for blood safety with the aim of improving both the quality and adequacy of blood supply through sustainable implementation of blood transfusion policies in all countries in the region.

In line with WHO recommendations, Seychelles has developed this first National Blood Services policy to guide the organisation of blood services, and address many of the challenges and gaps in the existing service.

The ultimate aim is to ensure universal access to, and rational use of safe blood and blood products, for the people of Seychelles. The policy statements are set out under the following priority directions:

1. Establish an appropriately structured, well-coordinated and sustainably resourced national blood system.
2. Establish appropriate national framework of regulatory controls, standards and quality assessment programmes.
3. Ensure well-functioning and efficiently managed blood services.
4. Implement effective patient blood management to optimize clinical practice of transfusion.
5. Implement effective surveillance, haemovigilance and pharmacovigilance, supported by comprehensive and accurate data collection systems.
6. Build active partnerships to jointly address existing challenges and emerging global threats.

Required implementation arrangements are outlined for effective service delivery and defines key indicators to support effective monitoring and evaluation of the service.

The policy also provides recommendations for the sustainability and better institutionalisation of this crucial service situation through the provision of relevant infrastructure, governance mechanisms, human resources, funding and monitoring and evaluation.

Ministry of Health will ensure all the recommendations of this policy are implemented and will develop a strategic framework to strengthen the national blood transfusion services to ensure continuous, sustainable and quality blood and blood products.

## GLOSSARY

* **Autologous Blood Transfusion:** Blood or blood components collected from one individual that are intended solely for later transfusion or any human applications for the same individual.
* **Blood Components:** A blood constituent that can include plasma, red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets.
* **Blood Donor:** A person who gives blood for transfusion.
* **Blood Products:** Components that can be derived from donated blood and transfused individually. This includes red cells, platelets and plasma.
* **Blood Transfusion:** A potentially life-saving medical procedure in which compatible donated blood is provided to a receiver in-need, through an intravenous infusion.
* **Cryoprecipitate:** The part of plasma that contains a number of clotting proteins that help control bleeding made by thawing fresh frozen plasma between 1 and 6˚C and then collecting the insoluble proteins.
* **Fresh Frozen Plasma:** A blood product most commonly made by separating plasma from whole blood collection and placing it in freezing conditions (-18°C or less).
* **Family Replacement Donor:** A donor who gives blood when a member of the donor’s family or community requires it.
* **Haemovigilance:** A set of surveillance procedures covering the entire transfusion chain, from the donation and processing of blood and its components, to their provision and transfusion to patients and their follow-up.
* **Packed Red Cell concentrate:** Made from a unit of whole blood by centrifugation and removal of most of the plasma, leaving a unit with a haematocrit of about 60%.
* **Plasma Derived Medicinal Products:** Prepared industrially from human plasma by pharmaceutical companies (e.g., albumin, coagulation factors and immunoglobulins).
* **Platelets Concentrates:** Small, colourless cell fragments in our blood that form clots and stop or prevent bleeding.
* **Quality Management System:** The overall process of ensuring that the National Blood Service operates in a way that ensures maximum quality in all aspects of its operations.
* **Regular donor:** Donor who gives blood on a regular basis (at least twice per year).
* **Transfusion Transmitted Infections (TTIs):** Infections resulting from the introduction of a pathogen into a person through blood transfusion.
* **Voluntary Non-Remunerated Blood Donors (VNRBDs):** A donor giving blood, plasma or cellular components on his/her own free will and receives no payment for it.
* **Whole Blood:** Made up of red and white blood cells, and platelets, all of which are suspended in plasma.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Blood and blood products is an important component of modern health care. Blood transfusion may decrease mortality, and prolong and improve the quality of life of many people with different pathologies.

### 1.1 Background

Seychelles provides comprehensive health services, including blood transfusion services, to all citizens, free at point-of-use. The National Universal Health Coverage (UHC) service coverage index is estimated at 71.

## 2. SITUATION ANALYSIS

### 2.1 Organisation of Current Blood Transfusion Services

The Blood Transfusion and Serology Unit of the Clinical Laboratory delivers Blood Transfusion Services (BTS) in Seychelles.

**Infrastructure:** One blood donation centre at Seychelles Hospital and a mobile vehicle. Infrastructure is currently out-dated with limited space.

**Human Resources:** Staffing has remained relatively constant despite increasing demand.

**Table 2: Staffing of blood transfusion service at end of 2022**

| Cadre | Number | Key roles and responsibilities |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Laboratory technicians | 8 (part-time) | Blood drives, components production, quality control, TTI testing, inventory. |
| Laboratory technologists | 2 (part-time) | Same as technicians + supervisory and leadership roles. |
| Senior Programme Officer | 1 (full-time) | Donor recruitment and retention, organizing blood drives. |
| Health care assistant | 1 (full-time) | Bleeding of donors, pre-donation screening, post-donation care. |
| Laboratory assistant | 2 (part-time) | Providing post-donation care. |
| Medical Officers | Ad-hoc | Redeployed from ward and emergency services as needed. |

**Finance:** No independent budget; functions under the Clinical Laboratory budget line.

### 2.2 Blood Donation

Average 1500-1900 units collected annually (1849 in 2021).

| Donor classification | Number (%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Voluntary Non-Remunerated Blood Donors | 960 (52%) |
| Family Replacement donors | 888 (48%) |
| Autologous blood donors | 1 (<1%) |

### 2.3 - 2.7 Technical Summary

* **Quality Management:** Draft SOPs exist; need for a comprehensive QMS.
* **Supply & Transport:** Capacity for 500 whole blood units. Cold chain maintained for inner islands.
* **Processing:** Screening for HIV, HBV, HCV, HTLV-1 and syphilis. Components (FFP, Packed Red Cells, Platelets) produced since 2011/2014.
* **Use:** Demand increasing due to clinical specialty expansion (especially ICU).
* **Haemovigilance:** No formal reporting mechanism for transfusion reactions currently in place.

**Table 4: SWOT analysis of blood services in Seychelles**

| :--- | :--- |

| Increase regular donor base; External partners (Red Cross); Modernisation; Hospital Masterplan; WHO support. | Increasing prevalence of blood-borne pathogens; Small donor base; Increasing demand from private sector; Rising cost of consumables. |

## 4. POLICY FRAMEWORK

* **Vision:** A sustainable and quality NaBS that complies with international standards.
* **Mission:** To provide adequate and timely supplies of safe blood and blood products.
* **Main Objectives:** Strengthen leadership, develop modern structure, enable sustainable financing, ensure safety/quality, and ensure ethical use.
* **Guiding Principles:** No acceptable alternative to human blood; blood is a scarce resource; safety of donor and recipient is paramount; preference for VNRBDs.

---

## 5. POLICY STATEMENTS

**PD 1: Appropriately structured, well-coordinated and sustainably resourced system**
* Establish clear structure (Infrastructure, HR, Budget).
* Set up a multi-disciplinary Hospital Transfusion Committee (HTC).
* Government funding via HCA budget.

**PD 2: National framework of regulatory controls and standards**
* Develop regulatory requirements via Public Health Authority (PHA).
* Mandatory screening for TTIs (HIV, Hep B/C, HTLV-1, syphilis).
* Implement external quality assessments.

**PD 3: Well-functioning and efficiently managed services**
* Ensure provision of infrastructure via Hospital Master Plan.
* Shift recruitment strategy toward 100% VNRBDs.
* Maximize utility of donated blood to reduce waste.

**PD 4: Effective patient blood management**
* Implement a technical manual for clinical guidelines (indications, administration, monitoring).
* Modernize labelling and identification systems.
* Ensure informed patient consent.

**PD 5: Effective surveillance, haemovigilance and pharmacovigilance**
* Integrate data with (e)HIS.
* Establish a stepwise haemovigilance system for reporting reactions.
* Regular review of transfusion practices by the HTC.

**PD 6: Build active partnerships**
* Collaboration with international standards bodies and NGOs.
* Maintain MOUs with private sector and civil society.

## 7. MONITORING AND EVALUATION

### 7.1 Data Collection and Reporting
Core indicators include: % of VNRBDs, donor deferral rates, TTI reactivity rates, wastage reasons, and number of transfusion reactions.

### 7.2 Regulatory Inspection
Entrusted to the regulatory units of the Public Health Authority (PHA).

### 7.3 Policy Review
Suggested review every 5 years.

---

## 8. CONCLUSION

This policy framework aims to institutionalize the NaBS, improve recruitment, and ensure the rational and safe clinical utilization of blood to maximize patient benefits while minimizing risk.
