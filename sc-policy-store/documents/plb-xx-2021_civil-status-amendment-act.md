# HEALTH IMPLICATIONS: THE CIVIL STATUS AMENDMENT ACT, 2021

*Gazetted on 8th November, 2021*

## Why was the law amended?
To reform the laws on the registration of births and deaths in Seychelles so as to generate more reliable vital statistics. The vital statistics help to inform social and economic planning across both public and private sectors in various areas of national development such as health, education, labour, industry and trade, and population growth.

The health sector has a particularly strong need for reliable vital statistics when it comes to tracking the progress of national strategies to reduce the levels of child, maternal and disease-specific mortality. Furthermore, the statistics have a linkage to electoral registers and social security benefits.

## Implications for Clinical Practice
The Civil Status (Amendment) Act, 2021 has implications for clinical practice and as such relevant health professionals should get acquainted with the following inferences:

| Section | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **#1: Notification of Births and stillbirths** | A medical practitioner, nurse or midwife who attends a birth shall give a notification of birth to the mother of the child or an authorized person. A notification of birth shall also be given to the Chief Officer of the Civil Status within 3 days after the birth of a child, indicating whether the child was born alive or was stillborn. If a pregnancy resulted in the birth of more than one child, a separate notification shall be given for each child and each statement shall state the number of children born and the birth order of the child. |
| **#2: Registration of Deaths** | The body of a deceased person or the remains of a body shall be examined by a medical practitioner, pathologist or forensic pathologist. The medical practitioner shall give a notification of death to the Chief Officer of the Civil Status, the Principal Secretary of Health and designated family member within 24 hours of a death. Furthermore, the medical practitioner shall submit a Medical Certificate of Cause of Death to the Chief Officer of Civil Status and the Commissioner of Public Health. |
| **#3: Prohibition of Issuing a Certificate of Death** | A medical practitioner shall not submit a Medical Certificate of Death to the Chief Officer of Civil Status and the Commissioner of Public Health where the death qualifies for an inquest or when the medical practitioner is of the opinion that the death is of suspicious circumstances and must be reported to the Commissioner of Police. |
| **#4: Particulars of Notification of Births and Stillbirths** | A notification of live birth shall contain the time, day, month and year of the birth of the child; the sex of the child; the name, date of birth, telephone number, email address and profession of the mother of the child; the nationality of the mother of the child; the marital status of the mother of the child; the national identity number or passport number of the mother of the child. In addition, notification for stillbirth shall contain the weight and gestational age of the foetus as well as the name of the certifying medical practitioner or midwife. |

## Key Definitions

*   **“birth”**: means the complete expulsion or extraction of a foetus from a female, irrespective of the duration of the pregnancy, where after such expulsion or extraction there is breathing, beating of the heart, pulsation of the umbilical cord or unmistakable movement of voluntary muscle, whether or not the umbilical cord has been cut or the placenta is attached.
*   **“stillbirth”**: shall have the definition assigned by the Minister for Health by notice published in the Gazette.
*   **“death”**: means the permanent disappearance of all evidence of life in a person at any time after the person’s birth.
*   **“death qualifying for an inquest”**: means any death the circumstances of which are set of in Schedule 1 of the Inquest into Death Act, 2021.
*   **“Medical Certificate of Cause of Death”**: means the Medical Certificate of Death as may be prescribed.
