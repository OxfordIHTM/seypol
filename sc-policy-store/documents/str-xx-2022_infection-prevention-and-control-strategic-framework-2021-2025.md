# PREFACE

The Government of Seychelles remains committed to attaining the health-related sustainable development goals through achieving universal health coverage. SDG target 3.8 calls on countries to achieve UHC, including financial risk protection alongside access to quality essential health care services. Over the years, we have made considerable progress in making health care available and accessible to all. The time has now come to focus on the quality of health care services.

Safety is a critical component of quality care. Patients should not be harmed by care that is intended to help them. Effective infection prevention and control practices protect patients from harm from healthcare-associated infections and safeguard healthcare workers, the Ministry of Health’s most valuable asset. The COVID-19 pandemic has taught us just how vital infection prevention and control is. Hand washing has taken on a new meaning; face masks have moved out of the clinical setting into our everyday lives, and visiting our loved ones in hospital is no longer a given.

It, therefore, gives me great pleasure to present the **Infection Prevention and Control Strategic Framework 2021-2025**, which provides a roadmap to improving infection prevention and control practices within the health sector and beyond. The Ministry of Health acknowledges that attaining success in Infection Prevention and Control will require effective public health programmes and multisectoral collaboration and cooperation. This framework provides the foundations for such partnerships.

I thank the IPC Team, which has been at the forefront of the fight to improve IPC in Seychelles. I thank all those who have worked tirelessly to bring this task to fruition. Last but not least, I thank each and every health care worker who has striven assiduously during these past two years to minimise the impact of COVID-19 on the health of our nation.

**Mrs. Peggy Vidot**
Minister for Health
Seychelles

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Nationally, healthcare associated infection (HAIs) remain a major cause of avoidable patient harm and pose a serious risk to patients, clients, health care workers in health care facilities and the community. These infections are a leading cause of death and unnecessary expense to individuals and the government.

Systems for effective Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) encompass evidence–based practical solutions, including:
* Existence of leadership and georeferenced arrangements.
* Surveillance of HAIs and AMR.
* Utilization of evidence-based and context specific practices.
* Effective handling of patient care equipment.
* Administrative controls for enabling environment.
* Environment and infrastructural considerations.

This national strategic framework set out for the period **2021 to 2025** builds on the findings of the 2014–2018 IPC strategic plan and recent 2021 assessments.

# 4. CURRENT SITUATION

## 4.1 Governance, leadership and organisational arrangements for IPC
Strong leadership is the *sine qua non* of a successful IPC programme. Seychelles is yet to put in place a standalone IPC programme. Currently, the IPC unit is under the jurisdiction of the Health Care Agency. 

### 4.1.1 Application of multimodal strategy for IPC interventions
Multimodal strategy (MMS) is considered the best approach for behavior change. However, evidence indicates the concept is not yet well understood or applied in the country.

## 4.2 IPC Guidance documents
Guidelines were developed in 2014. While widely available in public facilities, availability is lower in private facilities, and staff adherence remains inadequate.

## 4.3 Human resources for IPC
Training has centered mostly on COVID-19 since 2020. There is a noted theory-practice gap in pre-service training. A systematic induction programme for new and foreign HCWs is needed.

## 4.4 Healthcare associated infections surveillance
Systems are fragmented and ad hoc. The 2019 evaluation score was 38%. AMR stewardship mechanisms are also generally fragmented.

### 4.4.1 Outbreaks and public emergency preparedness
While a readiness plan exists, many HCWs are unaware of its contents. The IDSR system is functioning, and ATP is used to verify cleaning in high-risk areas.

## 4.5 Built environment, materials and equipment
73% of facilities conformed to physical standards in 2019. Challenges include triage/isolation area design and frequent stockouts of basic supplies at the user level.

## 4.6 IPC supportive supervision, monitoring and evaluation
National performance in 2019 was 21%. Monitoring is often ad hoc and paper-based due to lack of technical proficiency and internet access.

## 4.7 Major IPC challenges
1. Inadequate top commitment.
2. Inadequate dissemination of guidance documents.
3. Insufficient human resources.
4. Theory-practice gap in training.
5. Ineffective surveillance systems.
6. Limited data for evidence-based decisions.
7. Staff motivation and engagement issues.
8. Equipment and supply shortages.
9. Limited IPC leadership in infrastructure decisions.

# 5. STRATEGY DEVELOPMENT PROCESS

The process involved an evaluation of the 2014-2018 plan, literature reviews, and workshops with the IPCC and TWG, supported by WHO consultants.

# 6. GOALS

To position the prevention and control of infectious diseases as an important tactical area of focus in the mainstreaming of health care quality and service improvement.

# 7. OBJECTIVES OF THE STRATEGIC FRAMEWORK

1. Institutionalize IPC programmes at national and facility levels.
2. Promote an enabling environment with top leadership commitment.
3. Ensure availability of evidence-based national IPC guidelines.
4. Ensure all HCWs are knowledgeable on IPC related to their roles.
5. Harmonize system for IPC information management.
6. Share lessons to promote best practice.
7. Improve HAI and AMR surveillance.
8. Optimize the built environment and availability of essential infrastructure.

# 8. EXPECTED KEY ACHIEVEMENTS

* Fully established national IPC programme.
* 100% of facilities have a functioning IPC focal person.
* 100% availability of national IPC guidelines in all facilities.
* 90% of HCWs trained in standard and transmission-based precautions.
* 90% of facilities have recommended hand hygiene resources.
* 60% adherence to hand hygiene guidelines.
* 100% availability of tracer PPEs.
* 100% of surgical hospitals reporting SSI surveillance.
* At least half-yearly national support supervision.

# 9. GUIDING PRINCIPLES AND CORE VALUES

1. Safety and zero tolerance of avoidable infections.
2. Client-centered care.
3. Effectiveness.
4. Efficiency.
5. Teamwork.
6. Standardization.
7. Applicability in the local context.
8. Excellence.
9. Sustainability.
10. Partnership.

## 10.1 Institutionalize structural changes

**Key Activities:**
* Inaugurate IPCC every two years.
* Appoint national IPC coordinator and assistants.
* Develop annual plans endorsed by MoH.
* Establish links to Patient Safety and Quality Assurance programmes.

## 10.2 Promote multimodal strategies (MMS)

**Key Activities:**
* Develop and disseminate MMS guidelines for precautions.
* Train focal persons and HCWs on MMS usage.

## 10.3 Develop and disseminate guidance documents

**Key Activities:**
* Finalize and train on national IPC guidelines and COVID-19 SOPs.
* Incorporate IPC into professional regulatory guidelines.

## 10.4 Educate and train HCWs, patients and clients

**Key Activities:**
* Provide initial/refresher training for IPC trainers.
* Review health training curricula with NIHSS.
* Develop visual aids (posters/leaflets) for hand hygiene and PPE.
* Implement national communication strategy for public awareness.

## 10.5 Strengthen surveillance of HAIs, outbreaks and AMR

**Key Activities:**
* Adopt international case definitions for SSI, VAP, etc.
* Integrate laboratory activities with HAI surveillance.
* Implement the AMR action plan in collaboration with the Pharmacy Unit.

## 10.6 Strengthen supportive supervision, monitoring and evaluation

**Key Activities:**
* Develop and disseminate supervision guidelines.
* Conduct annual national IPC performance audits.
* Establish a baseline for key performance indicators (KPIs).

## 10.7 Enhance built environment (WASH)

**Key Activities:**
* Collaborate with PIU and CIC to incorporate IPC in building fixtures.
* Advocate for domestic and sluice rooms for housekeeping.

## 10.8 Standardize availability of materials and equipment

**Key Activities:**
* Develop standardized list of IPC supplies with specifications.
* Institutionalize an IPC logistics tracking system.

# 11. MONITORING, REVIEW AND EVALUATION

Mandate belongs to the IPCC. Annual reviews will occur, with a formal end-term evaluation in 2026.

# 12. IMPLEMENTING THE STRATEGIC FRAMEWORK

Primary responsibility lies with the IPCC and IPC unit staff. Agency and facility managers must incorporate these actions into their local plans.

# 13. ASSUMPTIONS AND RISKS

**Assumptions:** Continued government commitment and political will for health emergency preparedness.

**Risks:**
* Unforeseen pandemics causing shifts in focus.
* MoH restructuring/management changes.
* Economic instability.
* Population unrest.
