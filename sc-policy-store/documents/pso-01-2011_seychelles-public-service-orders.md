## CHAPTER I: INTRODUCTION AND ORGANISATION OF GOVERNMENT

### I. INTRODUCTION

#### 1. GENERAL

**1.1 Authority**

(a) The Public Service Orders (hereinafter referred to as "Orders") are Orders of the President, issued under his authority, by the Minister who has been assigned by the President, under Article 70 of the Constitution, with the responsibility of the organisation, development and management of the Public Service.

(b) The terms and conditions of service of all employees shall, subject to the provisions of these Orders and to the direction of the President, be determined by the Chief Executive Officer of that organisation.

(c) This, the 3rd revised edition of these Orders, contains the general conditions of service for the public service of the Republic of Seychelles and replaces all editions of the previous Orders as amended from time to time. Many of the changes that have been introduced in recent years and that have all been incorporated into these Orders have been published in circulars or promulgated under the relevant legislation. The provisions of the Public Service Orders are consistent with the laws of Seychelles.

(d) These Orders are to be strictly adhered to in order to maintain equity and impartiality in handling personnel matters. However:

- (i) where no provision exists, it should be assumed that there is no authority and therefore a request must be submitted to the Authority responsible for Public Administration for approval;
- (ii) where special circumstance indicate that the provisions of the relevant Orders are inappropriate or would cause hardship, the Chief Executive Officer in this particular instance should seek for a variation of the provision/s concerned from the Authority responsible for Public Administration.

(e) The powers of constituting and abolishing offices and dissolving State Enterprises in the Public Service, of making appointments to offices, so constituted, and of promotion, termination of appointments, disciplinary control including dismissal of persons appointed to such offices are vested in the President in accordance with Article 66 of the Constitution.

(f) The office of a Judge, Justice of Appeal, Attorney General, Auditor General, Chief Justice, Ombudsman, Public Service Appeal Board and Electoral Commissioner shall not, without consent, be abolished during continuance of office, as provided for under Articles 132, 76, 158, 144, and 115 of the Constitution.

(g) It is emphasized that although these Orders have no legislative force, they are published by authority of the President. Where reference to an Act is made in these Orders, such reference shall be taken to include reference to any subsidiary legislation passed under or additional to the Act. If the Orders are in any way at a variance with the terms of any legislation, the terms of the legislation shall naturally prevail.

**1.2 Use and Availability**

(a) It is the responsibility of each Chief Executive Officer to ensure that these Orders and all government notifications and instructions are brought to the attention of employees and made readily available.

(b) It is equally the duty of all employees to be acquainted with these Orders and any amendments. All copies remain the property of Government.

(c) Non-compliance with, abuse or disobedience of a Public Service Order shall form the basis of a disciplinary charge.

(d) A public service employee shall not be allowed either to benefit or lose by any error or omission which may have occurred in the compilation of these Orders.

(e) Where any expenditure has been incurred by the organisation as a result of any action done by an employee without authority, it shall be recovered from the person who benefited.

**1.3 Compliance**

These Orders and any regulations of Government or instructions established by the Authority responsible for Public Administration are applicable to all employees within government organisations, except where contrary provisions appear.

**1.4 Applicability and Non-applicability**

(a) The term "employee" applies to all persons employed in the Public Service (Ministry, Department, Authority, Board or Public commercial enterprise organisation).

(b) These orders shall not apply to the following offices in so far as they are inconsistent with the Constitution (though they apply to their support staff):
- The President, Vice President or Minister.
- The President of the Court of Appeal.
- Chief Justice, Justices of Appeal or Judges.
- The Attorney General.
- The Auditor General.
- The Ombudsman.
- The Electoral Commissioner.

(c) Unless specifically excluded, these Orders apply to members of the disciplined services.

#### 2. INTERPRETATION

**2.1 Dispute regarding the Interpretation of the Orders**

The Authority responsible for Public Administration shall be the deciding authority in the first instance.

**2.2 Appeals against the Interpretation of the Orders**

An appeal lies with the Minister responsible for Public Administration.

**2.3 Appeals to the Relevant Institutions**

Employees may appeal to the Public Service Appeals Board, Ombudsman, and the Authority responsible for Public Administration. Judicial review is available from the decision of any of those Institutions.

**2.4 Judicial Review**

An employee may apply for judicial review at his/her own expense within three (3) months from the date of the Order or decision.

### II. THE ORGANISATION OF GOVERNMENT

**6. GOVERNMENT VISION FOR THE PUBLIC SERVICE**

The public service must be made up of men and women who possess a high degree of professionalism, integrity and ability to assist the country’s leadership to achieve good governance.

**7. THE STRUCTURE OF THE PUBLIC SERVICE**

The Public Service is organised into two main entities:
(a) **The Civil Service**: Comprises Ministries and Departments. Headed by the Principal Secretary.
(b) **State Agencies**: Consists of Companies, Corporations, Councils Agencies, Boards, Authorities and Foundations. Headed by the Chief Executive Officer.

**8. THE EXECUTIVE AUTHORITY**

Vested in the President and exercised in accordance with the Constitution.

**9. FUNCTIONS OF PUBLIC SERVICE APPEALS BOARD**

Role is to hear complaints regarding:
(a) Appointments to an office;
(b) Promotions;
(c) Disciplinary proceedings;
(d) Termination of appointment;
(e) Qualification decisions.

**10. FUNCTIONS OF THE AUDITOR GENERAL**

Audits accounts of the Cabinet office, National Assembly, Government departments, courts, and statutory corporations.

**11. BOARDS OF PUBLIC SECTOR ORGANISATIONS AND AGENCIES**

Board members monitor performance. Requirements include:
- Regular meetings.
- Keeping the parent Ministry informed.
- Attendance is mandatory (persistent failure leads to revocation).
- CEOs do not draw allowances for board attendance.

**12. FUNCTIONS OF MINISTERS**

(a) Political responsibility for Ministries and Departments.
(b) The President may temporarily exercise a Minister's function.
(c) Ministers provide policy guidance for enterprises/agencies; CEOs manage day-to-day operations.

**13. CLASSIFICATION AND STANDARDISATION OF PUBLIC BODIES**

1. **Public bodies providing essential Government services**: Core tasks, financed by state budget, supervised by parent Ministry.
2. **Regulatory Bodies**: Principal authority for licensing/standards, budget dependent on Ministry of Finance.
3. **Public Bodies providing non-Government Services**: Not-for-profit, socio-economic development, partially funded by sponsorships/donations.

**14. VALUES TO BE UPHELD BY CHIEF EXECUTIVE OFFICERS**

CEOs must promote compliance, provide honest advice, maintain ethical standards, strive for efficiency, avoid favouritism, and support employee development.

**15. INTEGRITY IN PUBLIC OFFICE**

Requires a strong leadership culture. All employees are subject to the Public Service Orders and regulations.

**16. POLICE CLEARANCE**

Prospective candidates (including expatriates) shall be subject to police clearance to ensure integrity and good conduct.

**17. OFFICIAL SECRETS DECLARATION**

All employees must subscribe to the oath of secrecy (State Security Act) and the Code of Conduct. This restriction applies post-employment.

**18. TENURE OF OFFICE NOT AS OF RIGHT**

Offices may be abolished by the President without assigning reasons, except for specific high offices (e.g., Chief Justice, Attorney General).

**19. DELEGATION OF AUTHORITY**

Administrative authority is granted to CEOs by the Minister responsible for Public Administration to provide clarity of roles.

**20. MANAGEMENT AUDITING**

Reviews application of procedures and compliance with policies to identify waste, fraud, or abuse.

## CHAPTER II: ENTRY AND PROGRESSION IN THE PUBLIC SERVICE

**26. EMPLOYMENT LEGISLATION**

The Employment Act in Seychelles applies to all public service employment contracts.

**27. EMPLOYMENT POLICIES**

Focuses on placing the right people in the right jobs via training and counselling. Emphasis on productivity, results, and a safer working environment.

**28. APPOINTMENT PRINCIPLES**

Appointments of citizens and locally recruited non-citizens shall be on local terms and conditions.

**29. THE ESTABLISHMENT REGISTER**

All positions must feature on the approved establishment register. Appointments only to vacant positions.

**30. VARIATIONS TO THE ESTABLISHMENT REGISTER**

Variations for Ministries/Departments must be approved by the Authority responsible for Public Administration.

**31. APPOINTMENTS LIMITED BY THE ESTABLISHMENT REGISTER**

Personnel expenditure must not exceed the approved establishment unless funds are available.

**32. CATEGORIES OF APPOINTMENTS**

- Continuous employment
- Fixed term contract
- Multilateral/Bilateral agreement
- Casual basis
- Part-time employment
- Consultancy basis

**33. CITIZENS NOT TO BE APPOINTED ON EXPATRIATE TYPE OF CONTRACT**

Citizens may not be appointed on expatriate terms. PSC contracts are for Seychellois citizens only.

**34. RECRUITMENT AND SELECTION PRINCIPLES**

Recruitment is based on merit (qualifications, training, skills, leadership, motivation). 

**35. MEDICAL EXAMINATIONS BEFORE APPOINTMENT**

Medical fitness certificates are required for drivers, expatriates, and food handlers.

**36. HEALTH SERVICES FOR EXPATRIATE EMPLOYEES**

Government meets primary health care costs for expatriates and dependants via special identification cards.

**37. APPOINTMENTS ON CONTRACT**

Contracts must be in accordance with Seychelles law. Forms may be varied by the Principal Secretary to meet technical cooperation needs.

**38. CASUAL EMPLOYMENT**

Should be avoided; maximum duration is three months. Rates follow Employment Act 1995.

**39. EMPLOYEES ENGAGED ON PSC CONTRACTS**

Applies to SG 9 and above. Renewable fixed term of two years. Requires submission of plans and targets.

**40. APPOINTMENT OF LOCUM TENENS**

Principal Secretary of Health may appoint locum tenens Medical Officers.

**41. ACTING APPOINTMENTS**

- For temporary full duties of a higher post.
- Responsibility allowance paid if exceeding one month.
- Maximum 12 consecutive months.

**42. NON-DISCRIMINATION**

Gender equality in employment and pay for equivalent qualifications/experience.

**43. ISSUANCE OF LETTER OF APPOINTMENT**

Effective from date of assumption of duty. Includes job description and specification.

**44. INSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK FOR EXPATRIATE EMPLOYMENT**

- **Localisation Policy**: Aim to minimize dependence on expatriates.
- **Limits**: Max 3 consecutive contracts of 2 years (total 6 years).
- **Roles**: NHRDC estimates demand; Authority responsible for Employment authorises posts; Authority responsible for Public Administration approves appointments.

**45. PUBLIC SERVICE CADRES: SCHEMES OF SERVICE**

- **Closed Cadre**: Unique to one organisation.
- **Common Cadre**: Common to several organisations.

**46. STAFF MOVEMENTS**

Covers appointments, transfers, promotions, secondments, and resignations.

**47. POSTINGS AND TRANSFERS**

- **Postings**: Temporary movement within the same organisation to a different station.
- **Transfers**: Movement to posts of same level; mogą be at employee request or organisation interest.
- **Secondment**: Attachment to approved institutions.
- **Promotion**: Based on performance/qualifications. No backdating.

**48. VARIATIONS OF TERMS AND CONDITIONS**

Must be in writing and discussed with the employee. Agreement is necessary.

**49. CONSULTANCIES AND CONSULTANTS**

External consultants require approval. Employees may undertake consultancies if not in conflict with official duties and with CEO/Authority approval.

## CHAPTER III: SALARIES AND ALLOWANCES

### I. SALARIES

**55. GOVERNMENT’S POLICY ON THE SALARY STRUCTURE**

Remuneration linked to performance and productivity to ensure a fair quality of life.

**56. SALARY STRUCTURE AND POSTS**

- 12 grade structure (G1–12).
- Principle: "Equal pay for work of equal value."
- Progression via "steps" (15 steps per band) based on performance.
- Base pay include consolidated allowances (graduate supplementation, milk, etc.).

**57. SALARIES**

- **Payment**: In arrears, monthly, in Seychelles rupees.
- **Progression**: Non-automatic; based on meritorious performance and annual appraisal.
- **Enhancement**: Awarded upon obtaining higher relevant qualifications.

**58. DEDUCTIONS FROM SALARY**

Limited to Income Tax, Pension Fund, overpayments, and debts due to government unless written authority is given.

**59. SUB-ASSIGNMENTS OF SALARY**

Employer not obliged to accept unless conditions are met. Employee is responsible for the agreement upon exiting.

**60. PAYMENT OF SALARY IN ADVANCE**

Only for approved overseas training, official duty, or treatment.

**61. SUSPENSION OF SALARY**

Employee suspended without pay ceases to be entitled to salary/allowances.

**62. SALARY WHILST ON TRAINING**

- $\leq 3$ months: Full salary.
- $> 3$ months overseas: Two-thirds salary.
- Local full time: Full basic salary.

**63. REFUND OF TRAINING EXPENSES**

Refundable at the discretion of the CEO of the Authority responsible for Public Administration if directly related to work.

**64. WAGES/ALLOWANCES FOR STUDENTS**

- Post secondary: SR500/week
- Secondary: SR300/week

### II. ALLOWANCES

**68. ALLOWANCES**

Temporary monetary amounts to compensate for specific purposes. Subject to limits approved by the Authority responsible for Public Administration.

**69. DUTY JOURNEYS WITHIN THE REPUBLIC**

- First 7 nights: Hotel/Guest house up to SR650; others up to SR435.
- Over 7 nights: Max SR500.

**70. SUBSISTENCE ALLOWANCE**

Covers accommodation, meals, and transport. Reduced per diem paid if trip is fully funded.

**71. REFUND OF MEAL CHARGES**

Refund up to SR200 for meals when returning home at night, or a flat SR75 allowance without receipts.

**72. GOVERNMENT HOSPITALITY**

Funds allocated to CEOs for official entertainment to cultivate goodwill; must be simple and not pretentious.

**73. REIMBURSEMENT OF ENTERTAINMENT COSTS**

- Cocktail parties: SR65/head
- Lunches: SR150/head
- Dinners: SR185/head

**74. OUTFIT ALLOWANCE**

For overseas travel:
- Once a year: SR3,000
- 4-6 times a year: SR4,500
- $> 6$ times a year: SR6,500

**75. CLOTHING ALLOWANCE**

SR1500 per annum for those on overseas training.

**76. HOUSING ALLOWANCE FOR RETURNING GRADUATES**

SR2000/month for 36 months for citizens returning with recognized degrees.

**77. OVERTIME ALLOWANCE**

Justified only by exceptional circumstances. Can be paid as a commuted allowance or time off in lieu.

**78. TRANSPORT ALLOWANCE**

- Motor car: SR 4.15/km (first 100km), then SR3.50/km.
- Motor cycle: SR3.55/km (first 100km), then SR2.98/km.

**79. BICYCLE ALLOWANCE**

Commuted rate of SR120 per month.

**80. DRIVING ALLOWANCE**

SR300 per month for employees not employed as drivers but required to drive.

**81. BAGGAGE ALLOWANCE**

Expatriates entitled to excess baggage (25-45kg depending on contract duration).

**82. DISTURBANCE ALLOWANCE**

- Within Mahe/Praslin: SR800
- Between inner islands: SR1150
- Outer islands: SR1450

**83. RESPONSIBILITY ALLOWANCE**

Difference between employee’s post and higher post functions performed for over one month.

**84. DUTY ALLOWANCE**

For assuming duties of other staff (same level), paid for 1-6 months.

**85. ON CALL ALLOWANCE**

Compensation for being available beyond established hours.

**86. STAND BY ALLOWANCE**

Paid when required to remain at work or available for emergencies.

**87. SHIFT ALLOWANCE**

Compensates for irregular working hours.

## CHAPTER IV: LEAVE

**93. ANNUAL LEAVE**

- Entitlement: 21 working days per annum.
- Cycle starts 1st January. No leave for first 6 months of service.
- Leave is compulsory for recuperation.

**94. CONVERSION OF ANNUAL LEAVE INTO CASH**

Not automatic; requires strong justification and CEO approval.

**95. RECALL FROM OR DEFERMENT OF ANNUAL LEAVE**

CEO may recall employee in interest of service or defer leave based on exigencies.

**96. ACCESSIBILITY WHILE ON LEAVE ABROAD**

Critical personnel must provide address and remain accessible.

**97. FORFEITURE OF ANNUAL LEAVE**

Occurs during lawful custody, suspension, or resignation without notice.

**98. UNPAID LEAVE**

- Max 48 days per cycle (CEO approval).
- Up to 2 years (Authority responsible for Public Administration approval).

**99. SABBATICAL LEAVE**

Max 2 years without pay for professional development.

**100. LEAVE ENTITLEMENT OF EXPATRIATE EMPLOYEES**

Earned leave taken during contract; does not extend contract duration.

**101. MATERNITY LEAVE**

14 weeks paid leave (min 12 weeks after confinement). Prohibits night work/overtime from 6th month of pregnancy.

**102. SICK LEAVE**

- Max 30 days paid sick leave in 12 months.
- Further 30 days paid if hospitalised.
- Max 60 days unpaid sick leave following paid exhaustion.

**103. SPECIAL LEAVE OF ABSENCE**

- **Sporting Events**: 3 weeks full pay/year.
- **Conferences**: Full pay for official selection.
- **Compassionate**: Max 4 working days full pay.
- **Civic Duties**: Max 14 days paid per 12 months.

**104. LEAVE RECORDS**

Maintained in employee's personal file.

**105. HANDING OVER**

Mandatory handover of files, projects, and assets to supervisor/deputy before leave.

## CHAPTER V: CODE OF CONDUCT AND DISCIPLINE

### I. RULES OF CONDUCT

**111. BEHAVIOURAL EXPECTATIONS**

Employees must be courteous, polite, fair, objective, and beyond corruption.

**112. DISCREDITING THE PUBLIC SERVICE**

Avoid reckless actions (heavy gambling, excessive drinking) that discredit the image of the service.

**113. RESTRICTION ON EXTERNAL INTERESTS**

No commercial/voluntary undertakings that conflict with government interests or impair usefulness. Prior permission is required.

**114. FEES PAID BY QUASI-PUBLIC BODIES**

Ex-officio members may retain honorariums for Chairmanship/Directorship of public sector agencies.

**115. RESTRICTIONS ON DISCLOSURE OF INFORMATION**

Unauthorized disclosure of government documents is a serious offence. Restriction applies after employment ends.

**116. CONTRIBUTIONS TO THE MEDIA**

No acting as editor or making policy communications without authority from Minister/CEO.

**117. PUBLICATION OF BOOKS**

Approval from Authority responsible for Public Administration required for works connected to official duties.

**118. USE OF GOVERNMENT PROPERTY**

Forbidden for private tasks unless hired out under approved agreement.

**119. PRESENTS AND GIFTS**

Forbidden to solicit or receive valuable presents. Exceptions: insignificant value or collective gifts for occasions.

**120. PECUNIARY EMBARRASSMENTS**

Serious debt/insolvency must be reported. Persons in such states cannot handle public funds.

**121. PARTICIPATION IN POLITICAL ACTIVITIES**

Permitted outside working hours. Absolute political impartiality required during discharge of duties.

**122. DISPOSAL OF PERSONAL PROPERTY**

Sale of personal property to Government requires sanction from CEO of Finance.

**123. PARTICIPATION IN TRADE UNIONS**

Encouraged and shall not prejudice official career.

**124. LEGAL PROCEEDINGS AGAINST EMPLOYEES**

Government may provide legal defence if act was committed in good faith during official duties.

**125. PROCEEDINGS FOR DEFAMATION**

Employees may institute proceedings for defamation but must inform CEO.

**126. RELATED DUTIES**

Employees must perform assigned post duties and any other related duties called upon in public interest.

**127. ABSENCE FROM DUTY**

Must communicate absence within one day. Absence $> 2$ days without leave is a disciplinary offence.

**128. OFFICE HOURS**

Mon-Fri: 8:00–12:00 and 13:00–16:00. Punctuality is mandatory.

**129. DRESS CODE**

Professionalism and decency required. Uniforms/Protective clothing provided for specific categories (Police, Nurses, etc.).

### II. CODE OF DISCIPLINE

**133. DISCIPLINARY AUTHORITIES**

Powers vested in President, delegated to CEOs, Commissioner of Police, and Prisons Superintendent.

**134. DISCIPLINARY OFFENCE**

Any act, omission, or misconduct that warrants proceedings. Minor offences become serious if repeated (2+ times).

**135. CATEGORIES OF OFFENCES**

Minor and Serious.

**136. DISCIPLINARY PROCEDURES**

Includes investigation, opportunity to explain, and right to representation (colleague or Trade Union).

**137. SUSPENSION FROM DUTY**

Suspension without pay is possible. Max 40 days except in formal proceedings.

**138. TERMINATION IN THE PUBLIC INTEREST**

The President may remove an employee if considered in the public interest.

**139. MONITORING OF DELEGATED POWERS**

All disciplinary actions by CEOs must be reported in writing to the Authority responsible for Public Administration.

**141. APPEALS**

Review request within 14 days. Further appeal to the Public Service Appeal Board.

**142. CRIMINAL CHARGES NOT CONNECTED WITH EMPLOYMENT**

Suspension without pay if detained. Termination possible if found guilty of a serious offence.

## CHAPTER VI: HUMAN RESOURCES DEVELOPMENT AND TRAINING

**147. NATIONAL HUMAN RESOURCES DEVELOPMENT POLICY**

Focus on lifelong learning, capacity building, and generic skills (communication, teamwork, etc.).

**148. RESPONSIBILITY FOR TRAINING**

Management is responsible for organizational goals. Authority responsible for Public Administration assists in training implementation.

**149. IN-SERVICE TRAINING**

Priority given to local training. Overseas training considered if local resources are exhausted.

**150. BONDING POLICY**

Scholarship candidates must sign bonding agreements to serve the country for a specific period.

**151. TRAINING REPORTS**

Reports required during course and a final report within three weeks of return.

**152. SALARY WHILST ON OVERSEAS TRAINING**

- $\leq 3$ months: Full salary.
- $> 3$ months: Two-thirds salary.

**154. LEAVE ENTITLEMENT**

Full time courses $> 1$ academic year are not leave-earning.

**155. RETURNING GRADUATES**

Authority responsible for Public Administration handles first appointment of returning employees.

**156. AIRFARES**

Return air passages provided for the candidate only.

## CHAPTER VII: EXIT FROM THE PUBLIC SERVICE

**162. RETIREMENT**

- Mandatory: 63 years.
- Voluntary: 60 years.
- Special provisions for those prior to 1979 (start at 55).

**163. RESIGNATION**

- Continuous employment: 1 month notice (or pay in lieu).
- Contract/Agreement: 3 months notice (or pay 2 months in lieu).

**164. TERMINATION OTHERWISE THAN BY MISCONDUCT**

Possible on medical grounds, deterioration in standards, public interest, redundancy, or localisation.

**166. DEATH GRATUITY**

Paid to legal heirs: 3 months basic salary + pro-rata gratuity and earned leave.

**167. RECOVERIES**

Amounts owed to Government must be collected before exit.

**168. PENALTIES FOR EXCEPTIONAL EXIT**

Employees resigning before bonding period expires must refund training expenses proportionally.

**169. CERTIFICATE OF EMPLOYMENT**

Issued within two weeks of exit; legal requirement under Employment Act.

### II. COMPENSATION AND GRATUITY

**175. PAYMENT OF COMPENSATION**

Accrued benefit for continuous employment from 1st Jan 1979. Rate: 5/6 of one day’s wage for each completed month of service.

**176. PAYMENT OF GRATUITY**

Paid every 5 years. Rates vary by duration (e.g., first 5 years = SR6,666.67).

**177. CONTINUITY OF SERVICE**

Specified breaks (e.g., approved unpaid leave $\leq 24$ months) do not break continuity.

**178. PENSION AND SOCIAL SECURITY**

Governed by Pensions Act (Cap 159) and Social Security Act (Cap 225).

## CHAPTER VIII: TRANSPORT, TRAVELLING AND ACCOMMODATION

**185. TRANSPORT**

Approved methods include air, road, rail, or sea.

**186. ECONOMICAL MANNER**

Journeys must be in the most economical manner.

**188. AIR TRANSPORT**

Economy class required. Business class considered for CEOs and above.

**190. USE OF GOVERNMENT VEHICLES**

- Private use prohibited unless authorised (charged at SR5.70/km for first 100km, then SR5.30/km).

**191. OFFICIAL PASSPORT**

Issued to senior officers (Head of Division, etc.) for official duties.

**192. MISSION REPORTS**

Required within two weeks of return from overseas duty.

**199. HOUSING POLICY**

Focus is on general population housing, but provided to specific public service categories.

**201. CRITERIA FOR GOVERNMENT ACCOMMODATION**

Provided for: technical cooperation agreements, expatriates, and exceptional Senior Officer cases (max 12 months).

**202. HOUSING ALLOWANCE FOR EXPATRIATES**

If no government housing available, allowance up to SR4,500/month.

**203. RENTAL ASPECTS**

Rental contributions deducted from salary based on grade: Super Exec (SR1,500), Grade I (SR1,200), etc.

**204. SUB-LETTING**

Strictly prohibited.

**206. HOTEL EXPENSES UPON ARRIVAL**

Expatriates contribute to hotel costs (e.g., unaccompanied SR100/night).

**207. HANDING OVER**

Inventory sign-off required upon allocation and vacating.

## CHAPTER IX: CORRESPONDENCE AND MISCELLANEOUS ISSUES

**303. FORMS OF CORRESPONDENCE**

Conciseness and courtesy are required. Use of coloured ink restricted (President: Red, Auditor General: Green, Finance Audit: Purple).

**304. FOREIGN GOVERNMENTS**

Conducted through the Authority responsible for Foreign Affairs.

**307. ACKNOWLEDGEMENT**

All letters from public must be acknowledged immediately.

**316. COMPLAINTS**

Must be in writing, signed, and through normal channels. Anonymous complaints are disregarded.

**318. LEGAL ADVICE**

Sought in writing from the Attorney General. Requests usually from Head of Division level.

**319. LEGISLATION**

Drafted/scrutinised by the Office of the Attorney General. Process involves Cabinet Memorandum $\rightarrow$ Drafting Instructions $\rightarrow$ Draft Bill $\rightarrow$ Publication $\rightarrow$ Enactment.

**320. TELEPHONE CALLS**

Use local calls to save correspondence. Equipment use for private business must be minimal.

**321. STORAGE OF RECORDS**

Records transferred to National Archives. Disposal periods specified in Appendix E.

**323. INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY**

Managed by the Authority responsible for Information Technology.

**330. OCCUPATIONAL SAFETY**

Employers must ensure health/safety per Occupational Safety and Health Decree (Cap 151).

**333. TIME-BAR ON CLAIMS**

Claims made more than one year after becoming due are time-barred.
