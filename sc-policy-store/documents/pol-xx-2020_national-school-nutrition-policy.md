## 1. Introduction

The National School Nutrition Policy (NSNP) 2020, has a series of objectives that aim to create and promote an enabling environment for good nutrition and healthy eating in schools. The expected end result is, an optimally nourished generation who is knowledgeable about healthy eating and who has access to nutritious foods on the school premises. The NSNP 2020 replaces the NSNP version of 2008, which included the food guidelines that guided the tuck shops and school meals.

Over the past decade, there have been numerous development in the nutrition field and it is imperative that the NSNP 2020 reflects these changes now that revision is due. Compared to the previous version, this document will outline strategies that need to be taken in order to increase nutrition knowledge amongst school going children. The policy will act as a bridge for every school child and staff to become an ambassador for nutrition in their homes and community.

The NSNP 2020 contains broad objectives laid down to achieve the missions and visions of nutrition programs in schools. A national nutrition action plan for school nutrition will be developed to accompany the implementation of the NSNP over the 10 years.

## 2. Background

### Nutritional situation in the Seychelles
Healthy eating pattern in childhood is vital in promoting health; growth, development and helping children attain their full educational potential. Diet is also a key in preventing both numerous short-term and long-term diet related diseases in child and adulthood.

Seychelles has experienced significant changes in dietary and lifestyle patterns which have been linked to a rise in non-communicable diseases like diabetes and heart disease all of which have their origins in early life and childhood. Sedentary lifestyles and poor eating habits characterized by diets high in saturated fats and sugars and low in fruits and vegetables have become more common leading to a significant increase in the prevalence of overweight and obesity.

Of particular concern now is the increasing prevalence of childhood fatness and obesity. The NCD surveillance data 2018 amongst school children confirms that overweight and obesity have increased over the past decades. Presently, 32.2% of girls and 25% of boys are classified as overweight or obese. In terms of obesity, this represents a doubling in prevalence over the last 18 years. As in adults, girls tend to be more overweight than boys. Many research now indicates that risk factors found in childhood are potentially indicative of adult NCDs, indicating the importance of targeting prevention efforts in children. Overweight children are twice as likely as normal weight children to be obese as adults and childhood obesity increases the risks of early diabetes, elevated blood pressure and cholesterol levels; all which can cause NCDs in early adulthood. Surveillance data indicate that Seychellois children who are obese also have elevated blood pressure which means that they will enter adulthood already as hypertensive.

Over nutrition is now recognized as a major problem in the Seychelles. According to the National Health Accounts 2016 & 2017, the DoH spent around SCR412 to SCR450 million in 2016 and 2017 respectively to treat conditions related to NCDs including obesity, overweight, and other illnesses caused by unhealthy diet. This figure is likely to increase as the rate of overweight and obesity has increased compared to 20 years ago, from 28% to 57% in men and from 51% to 72% in women according to the latest heart study results in 2013. The rate of obesity has also increased dramatically in children, from a total of 10.6 % to 28.6% in both boys and girls over the last decade.

Community surveys have consistently shown that the general Seychellois diet is rich in protein (mostly fish), carbohydrates (which includes consumption of carbonated sweetened drinks) and fat, and poor in fiber with low consumption of vegetables. Risk factors for NCDs in the Seychelles population have more than doubled in the last ten years in Seychelles. According to the latest Seychelles Heart Study 2013; 64.5% of adults are overweight, 40% are hypertensive and 22.7% are categorized as diabetic. Since adult cardiovascular diseases have their beginnings in childhood and adolescence, both in terms of the adoption of unhealthy attitudes and practices and in pathophysiological changes, the promotion of healthy nutritional habits and the prevention of obesity must start as early as possible.

### Right to Health
The constitution of the Republic of Seychelles which is the most fundamental and supreme law of the Seychellois nation, recognizes the right to health of every citizen, in chapter 3, 29 (c) to take steps to reduce infant mortality and promote healthy development of the child. This declaration stipulates that access to free nutrition education and affordable nutritious food is the fundamental rights of all citizens of the republic of Seychelles regardless of background, race, socio-economic status or gender.

### The Rights of the Child
The Convention of the Rights of the child for Seychelles in Article 24, looks at rights of children and young people in relation to some aspects of health, and this includes provision to adequate nutritious foods (article 24.2 (c)), and children’s eating habits. The convention makes mention of the importance and superiority of breast feeding of infants and supports breastfeeding within the first weeks of life despite having figures that 50% of newborns are also offered bottle feeding within the immediate post-partum period before discharge from maternity. Breastfeeding is known to be the unequalled way of providing ideal food for the healthy growth and development of infants. As a global public health recommendation, infants should be exclusively breastfed for the first six months of life to achieve optimal growth, development and health. Thereafter, infants should receive nutritionally adequate and safe complementary foods to meet their growing nutritional requirements while breastfeeding continues for up to two years of age or beyond. Decades of epidemiological research have established that breast feeding is associated with a significant reduction in risk of later overweight and obesity. According to analysis of recent data of 2017, exclusive rates of breast feeding at one-month old was only 42%, and at 5 months old it was only 12%. This is much lower than the global rate which was 40% in 2016. It is not surprising therefore to observe children being overweight or obese at a very age; as young as 4 years old, given the feeding practices that are being employed.

### Alignment with the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)
Seychelles as other member states of the United Nations, adopted Sustainable Development Goals (SDG), by General Assembly resolution in September 2015, with the aim of achieving the 17 goals by 2030. By these declarations, the DoH acknowledges and support every effort to minimize the risks of non-communicable diseases amongst the population and alleviate infirmities amongst infants, children and adolescents and ensure promotions of healthy lifestyles that will contribute positively towards growth and development.

School Nutrition programmes that create synergies between the food available in schools (including tuck shops and school meals), food environment, food and nutrition education, and local food system, have the potential to contribute to multiple SDGs. These include SDG1(No Poverty), 2 (Zero Hunger), 3 (Good health and well-being), 4 (Quality Education), 10 (Reduced Inequalities) and 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production). The school nutrition policy 2020 seeks to foster these synergies to advance sustainable development more efficiently through the school setting. In addition, the approach is well aligned with other national and global commitments including the NCD strategic plan 2016-2025, the Zero Hunger Challenge and Global Nutrition targets for 2025 by UNICEF respectively.

It is imperative therefore, that optimal nutrition is promoted across the lifecycle. In particular, it recommends harnessing the potential of schools to reach the poor and nutritionally vulnerable populations in order to improve diets and address malnutrition.

### The Link Between Nutrition and Academic Performance
Healthy nutrition improves child well-being and learning ability, leading to better academic performance. Evidence shows positive links between children who are well nourished and improved learning attendance, behavior and consequently good teacher and child relationships. Good nutrition is also known to foster mental, social and physical well-being, leading to increased self-esteem and positive body image.

Eating a balanced diet reduces chances of falling ill with cancer, heart disease, CVA or diabetes-whatever the weight. A balanced and nutritious diet feeds the body as well as the mind. According to literature, there is a wide range of evidence showing the link between good dietary habits and academic performance. Many studies have shown positive outcomes for better diet quality in terms of behavior, cognitive, and school performance. Hunger due to insufficient food intake is associated with lower grades, higher rates of absenteeism, repeating a grade, and an inability to focus among students.

Lack of adequate consumption of specific foods, such as fruits, vegetables, or dairy products, is associated with lower grades among students. Deficits of specific nutrients (i.e., vitamins A, B6, B12, C, folate, iron, zinc, and calcium) are associated with lower grades and higher rates of absenteeism and lateness among students. It is clear that diet plays a major role in the education of a child hence the reason for doubling the effort to support school children attain maximum of their academic potential.

Adequate nutrition is also necessary for children to become fit and productive adults, capable of fulfilling their responsibilities in life. Although Seychelles do not have statistics confirming the rate of under-nutrition in children, a few cases of both micro- and macronutrient deficiencies have been detected. These have been shown to affect school aptitudes, time for school enrolment, concentration and attentiveness in early childhood. Children with a history of severe undernourishment perform less well on tests of IQ and specific factual knowledge than children in matched comparison groups. Undernourishment also impairs the ability to concentrate, learn and attend school regularly. A child who is malnourished and subsequently suffering from poor health cannot adequately take advantage of instructional and learning materials, irrespective of individual ability and willingness to learn.

Thus, good nutrition is needed to strengthen the learning potential of children, to enable them to learn effectively and maximize investments in education. Good health and nutrition are needed for concentration, regular school attendance and optimum class performance. Studies in different countries show that the academic performance and mental ability of pupils with good nutritional status are significantly higher than those of pupils with poor nutritional status.

### Rationale
A NSNP represents efforts to utilize schools as sites for improving the nutritional well-being of all school children and staff. It can provide a framework for implementing nutrition strategies which would ensure that students receive nutrition education that are reinforced throughout the whole of the school environment.

The policy will have a very wide reach, targeting not only students but also teachers, school personnel, families and other community members, all of which are directly or indirectly part of the school community.

In this modern society, nutrition information is overwhelming, and it is imperative that school children learn the basic facts on nutrition so as to be able to make correct dietary decisions. The school environment is known to powerfully influence students’ attitudes, preferences and behaviors and as such can promote and positively support healthy eating.

The NSNP will provide a robust framework for the nutrition programme in school, and also foster strong partnership with the whole school community as well other relevant stakeholders, thereby creating the enabling environment for school children to discover and learn nutrition to the fullest.

The policy will be used to coordinate all aspects of school nutrition. School children will not only learn the theory of nutrition (school curriculum) but will also have the chance to observe through structures that are in place to support such learning such as the tuck shop/canteens and school meal facilities. Most children learn better through observations, and when nutrition messages that are taught in class are then transferred into actual practices, this will definitely enhance learning and gradually build good dietary habits.

## 3. Mission, Vision, Goal and Objectives

**VISION:** School children are optimally nourished and physically, socially, mentally and spiritually healthy.

**MISSION:** To promote healthy eating by engaging profoundly with school administration, teachers, parents, students and everyone else that matters to create and enabling environment for optimal nutrition.

**GOAL:** All students and staff (public and private schools) in Seychelles to adopt healthy eating behaviors in line with this policy.

### OBJECTIVES
In order to attain the overall goal, the following strategic objectives have been developed to deliver the NSNP:
1. To promote and sustain optimal nutrition status of school children and staff
2. To provide nutrition education at all levels of the education system
3. To increase awareness and intake of locally available nutritious foods among school children, staff and their communities
4. To promote and sustain a healthy learning environment through improved nutrition
5. To improve the academic performance of school children by promoting consumptions of nutritious foods
6. To build capacity of school staff and students to become ambassadors for nutrition in their schools and communities
7. To provide a framework for the development and implementation of a sustainable national school nutrition programme
8. To promote partnership and multi-sectoral collaboration in the implementation of the school nutrition programme
9. To initiate and continuously strengthen extended school community-based nutrition activities
10. To attain gradual decrease or zero increase in the prevalence of overweight and obesity in school going children in Seychelles

## 4. Policy Scope

The policy is expected to be implemented in all state and private schools over a period of 10 years. School settings present the ideal place for implementation of such policy due to the existence of a captive audience in a well-structured environment. Nutrition education and programmes can be incorporated in the existing structure such as in the curriculum, school meals, school tuck shops, extra-curricular activities, nutrition clubs etc.

### 4.1 Focusing on school setting for change
Schools provide the most effective way to reach large portions of the population, including young people, school personnel, families and community members. It is a perfect setting where students can be reached at influential stages in their lives, during childhood and adolescence when lifelong nutritional patterns are formed.

Schools offer more effective, efficient and equal opportunities than any other setting to promote health and healthy eating. They reach young people at a critical age of development at which lifestyles, including eating patterns, are developed, tested and adapted in schools.

Through social interactions between students, teachers, parents and others, schools provide excellent opportunities because eating habits are formed early in life. Schools also provide a setting to introduce nutrition information and technologies to the community and can lead the community in advocating policies and services that promote good nutrition. No other setting than schools offer these opportunities.

### 4.2 Nutrition interventions in schools can improve health and well-being

For many years, evidence has supported that, well managed nutrition education programmes can, at relatively low cost, bring about behavior changes that contribute to improved nutritional well-being. Globally, students in behaviorally-based health and nutrition education programmes have shown significant favorable changes in blood cholesterol, blood pressure and body fat. Therefore, focus on behavior is considered a key determinant in the success of nutrition education programmes.

Cost effective interventions in schools can prevent or greatly reduce health problems and consequences of malnutrition and foster the positive effects of nutrition.

Compared to various public health approaches, school health programmes that provide safe and low-cost health service interventions, such as screening and health education, are shown by research to be one of the most cost–effective investments a nation can make to improve health. In Seychelles, the school health programme that has been in existence for decades now, provides the opportunity for health officials to be present on school premise at any given time. This can facilitate and strengthen nutrition education/ messages and programmes and provide for more effective consultations and communication on the school premise.

Good nutrition education also serves as a means to inform families and other community members about ways to promote nutritional wellbeing and prevent diet related disorders. For instance, educating children about good eating habits has the potential to enhance the nutrition and health status of their younger siblings whom they may care for as well as of other family members that learn parallel from their children.

Schools can be the center for community enhancement projects that include programmes to improve the health and nutritional status of the community. The establishment of school canteens offering healthy food choices and practicing good food safety is a way to demonstrate how to improve facilities in communities.

## 5. Policy Values and Guiding Principles

### Guiding principles
In developing this policy, the following principles were adopted:
*   **Rights-based approach:** Every child has a right to wholesome food, to be free from hunger and to have adequate nutritious food according to the constitution of the Republic of Seychelles 1996.
*   **Equity and gender focused:** Planning and implementation of the NSNP with a comprehensive nutrition education program shall address the specific needs of children from different communities and backgrounds and promote gender equality.
*   **Nutrition sensitivity:** The nutrition programme should increase nutritional intake and promote healthy food consumption habits. As such, all institutions should adapt the laid-down nutrition standards and requirements for the sale of nutritious snacks and beverages, school meal preparation and service.
*   **Cultural sensitivity:** The NSNP should take locally and seasonal availability of foods into consideration while doing its level best to promote culturally sensitive and healthy dietary habits among school going children.
*   **Integrity and accountability:** All stakeholders involved in the nutrition programme should commit to act in an honest, accountable and transparent manner in all their program undertakings.
*   **Multi-sectoral partnerships, coordination and collaboration:** A multi-sectoral approach is critical for the successful and sustainable implementation of school nutrition programme and nutrition interventions. In particular, strong partnerships amongst school communities, relevant ministries and other prime stakeholders are necessary to reduce risks and provide room for programme scale-up. Effective coordination of different sectors and actors involved must harmonize, so as to synergize resources and yield multiple benefits for those involved. This implies the clear allocation of roles and responsibilities for the programme’s planning, resource mobilization and management at all levels.
*   **Sustainability, shared responsibility and community participation:** Stable Government budget is required to support the implementation of the policy. The NSNP will be a shared responsibility between the DoH and the Ministry of Education and Human Resource and Development. School community participation, training and empowerment is crucial to the implementation of the programme.

### Policy Values
Good and adequate nutritious foods are the fundamental rights of every child of the Seychelles, therefore should be sustained throughout their life cycles. The DoH remains committed to ensure that the nutritional needs of every Seychellois child are met adequately.

Every child should have access to nutritious foods and should be given adequate nutrition education to be able to thrive and develop healthily. Those who are disadvantaged, vulnerable and from low socio-economic background will receive support from dedicated funds by the Ministry of Education and other necessary support from the Agency for Social Protection (ASP).

## 6. Policy direction on school nutrition

The NSNP 2020 represents a joint effort between the DoH and the Ministry of Education and Human Resource Development. However, to achieve successful implementation, all parties concerned with the policy have to work together.

### The school community
Schools and communities have a shared responsibility to provide children access to high quality foods and positive nutrition experiences that will have a lifelong impact on their health and education. Family and community involvement is vital in supporting and reinforcing nutrition education in the schools. The policy document will serve as a tool to guide the roles and actions of the school community.

### Parents and Care Givers
Children need support from their parents to implement nutrition knowledge acquired in schools. Changes in positive behavior last longer if interventions are aimed at family attitudes and habits rather than individual alone. Furthermore, parents can benefit from the same information to improve their own health and nutrition behavior. Parental involvement is crucial as it enhances the effectiveness of programmes by stimulating awareness about food and nutrition so that the variety in the diet and the availability of healthy foods at home can be increased.

Children’s food preferences change through observation of peers and elders. Research indicates that children not only model their parents’ food intake, but also their attitudes to food and body dissatisfaction.

The objectives set within the policy document will have direct impact on school children and their parents, and will be implemented through a collective approach by all parties.

### Teachers Role Model / collaborations
The school environment is a powerful influence on the student’s attitudes, performances and behavior. Classroom lessons alone might not be enough to effect lasting changes in student’s eating behavior. Aside from availability of a healthy variety of foods, children also need to be influenced by role models around them. All teachers and other school staff members shall at all-time be complying with the NSNP. The influence of school goes beyond the classroom and includes normative nutrition messages from both peers and adults. In that respect, teachers and other school personnel have a major role in shaping the eating habits of children. Effective implementation of specific objectives laid out within the policy will require full participation and guidance from teachers and other school support staff.

## 7. Policy implementation

To achieve its main goal, the NSNP shall be implemented jointly by DoH and MoEHRD and other relevant stakeholders. This policy is for a period of 10 years after which it will be evaluated and revised. To guide all school children and staff to adopt healthy eating behaviors, all schools are required to take ownership and implement this policy. However, strong commitment and collaboration from all partners are necessary and a collective approach need to be taken to ensure that the objectives are being correctively and effectively implemented.

**The DoH and MoEHRD will be responsible for:**

I. Bringing together all concerned parties through the setting up of a multi-sectoral committee which will be responsible for overseeing the implementation of the policy nationally.
II. The committee will be represented by members from health, education, agriculture, sports and family affairs ministries. Additionally, the committee will also have representatives from SC and PTA. A focal person will be appointed to represent their relevant ministry or organizations.
III. Training in terms of capacity building for school personnel, students and food caterers on the NSNP;
IV. Sensitization of parents, students, school councils and other school community members on the NSP;
V. Reviewing of school curriculums (state and private schools) to integrate nutrition education;
VI. Sensitization of importers, farmers and wholesalers;
VII. Mass media sensitization of the general public on the NSNP;
VIII. The development of a school nutrition action plan to guide and support the implementation of the NSNP accordingly;
IX. Effective periodic review of nutrition related activities will be carried out;
X. Ensure that nutrition issues are adequately incorporated in the curriculum;
XI. Advocate and lobby for adoption of the NSNP;
XII. Coordinate the implementation of NSNP;
XIII. Adopt national school nutrition strategies and guidelines;
XIV. Mobilize partners and resources to support activities related to research, advocacy, coordination of nutrition programmes;
XV. Capacity enhancement for coordination and implementation of nutrition activities;
XVI. Develop communication and advocacy strategies for nutrition;
XVII. Establish a national operational program for research, monitoring and evaluation of nutrition programmes;
XVIII. Organize coordination meetings to strengthen collaboration with partners;
XIX. Develop appropriate monitoring and evaluation instruments / tools;
XX. Periodic monitoring and process evaluation of the implementation of the nutrition policy;
XXI. Submission of annual reports on the implementation of nutrition programmes to different ministries and partners.

## 9. Institutional Arrangements

### The Health Sector
The public health sector has a structure that consists of the health ministry and three public bodies.

**Health Department:** headed by a Principal Secretary and responsible to formulate health sector policy development, planning, monitoring and evaluation, and oversees the implementation of health strategies by the three public bodies for health care provision and training in health care.

**The three public bodies for health care provision and training in health care:**
I. **Health Care Agency:** An autonomous agency that manages the provision of primary, secondary and tertiary care. Oversees the development of integrated health care services, strengthens community-based care), and improves the efficient use of Seychelles Hospital services.
II. **Public Health Authority:** An independent entity that regulates the health sector and provides for the protection of the population's health. PHA regulates health services, health premises, health practitioners as well as the environmental and commercial activities that impact on health.
III. **National Institute of Health and Social Services:** Being the academic arm, NIHSS is an autonomous body that provides pre-service education for continuous in-service education of health workers and the institutionalization of high level health research.

**The DoH shall be responsible to:**
I. Spearhead the nutrition programmes and initiatives in schools,
II. Take the lead in capacity building of other stakeholders,
III. Provide technical support on the implementation of core nutrition activities including nutrition education and promotion,
IV. Ensure that the NSNP is being implemented correctly,
V. Ensure that school communities fully participate in nutrition related activities.

### The Education Sector
The policy is the product of the close collaboration between the DoH and MOEHRD which takes into account that proper nutrition is important for the growth and development of every child. It also reflects the advocacy and commitments towards ‘Health in all Policies’ declaration endorsed by the cabinet of ministers of Seychelles.

The MOEHRD will be the main ministry implementing the policy with DOH spearheading the programme. MOEHRD team for implementation will be led by:
*   Principal Secretary for Early childhood, Primary and Secondary Education
*   Principal secretary for Tertiary Education
*   School Councils
*   Institute for Early Child Development (IECD)
*   Parents Teachers Association (PTA)
*   Private schools

### Other sectors
The policy will be implemented using a whole Government approach and will include the following Ministries:
*   Ministry for Sports
*   Ministry for Family Affairs
*   Ministry for Agriculture and Fisheries

Other organizations will also be involved and these are namely:
*   Faith based organizations

At ministerial level- Department of Health, Ministry of Family affairs and Ministry of Education should take the lead, and at administrative level – Principal Secretaries and relevant technicians.

## 10. Capacity Building / Development

To facilitate implementation of the NSNP, relevant trainings are required for different staff members involved with the policy. The following cadres require appropriate trainings:
1. School nurses
2. Dental therapists
3. PSE teachers and other school teachers
4. School support staff
5. Tuck shop operators

### 1. Engage stakeholders on capacity development
An effective capacity building process to encourage participation by all those involved. Stakeholders involved should share ownership in the process of development and feel more responsible for the outcome and sustainability of the development. Engaging stakeholders who are directly affected by the situation allows for more effective decision-making, it also makes development work more transparent.

### 2. Assess capacity needs and assets
Assessment of pre-existing capacities through engagement with stakeholders to allow trainers to see what areas require additional training, what areas should be prioritized, and in what ways capacity building can be incorporated into local and institutional development strategies.

### 3. Formulate a capacity development response
An assessment needs to be done and once completed, a capacity building response must be created based on four core issues:
*   **Institutional arrangements** – increased work to solve problems associated with institutional arrangements by developing human resource frameworks covering policies and procedures for recruitment, incentives systems, skills development, performance evaluation systems, ethics and values.
*   **Leadership** - strong leadership is required to allow for easier adaption to changes. Strong leaders can also influence people. Coaching and mentoring programmes will help encourage the development of leadership skills such as priority setting, communication and strategic planning.
*   **Knowledge** – knowledge is the foundation of capacity. Greater investment should be made in establishing strong education systems and opportunities for continued learning and the development of professional skills.
*   **Accountability**- implementation of accountability measures facilitates better performance and efficiency.

### 4. Implement a capacity development response
Implementing a capacity building program involves the inclusion of multiple systems institutions and trainees concerned with the nutrition related issues. It will also involve continual re- assessment and expect change depending on changing situations. It will include evaluative indicators to measure the effectiveness of initiated programs.

### 5. Evaluate capacity development
Evaluation of capacity building will promote accountability. Measurements are based on changes that will be observed in schools and in the behavior of school children. Evaluations are based on changes in performance based around the four main issues: institutional arrangements, leadership, knowledge and accountability.

## 11. Advocacy and Community Mobilization

Advocacy and communication form the foundation of driving and empowering nutrition outcomes. It is through advocacy and communication that policy changes are influenced and communities are sensitized.

Advocacy targeted at high-level decision-makers can lead to government commitments that have the potential to improve nutrition programming and accountability. Through strategic lobbying, increased resources (financial and otherwise) are allocated to nutrition interventions. For nutrition programmes to be properly implemented, there must be a good understanding of nutrition among the implementers and the general public. Advocacy and communication play the unique role of creating demand and building consciousness about the importance of nutrition at all school levels and among all key stakeholders involved with nutrition.

For successful advocacy and community engagement, all schools should have a nutrition representative to act as ambassador for their communities. The ambassadors will then work directly with the DoH to facilitate nutrition messages to their schools and communities.

### Community collaboration
The school community plays a vital role in supporting the NSNP. To improve children’s nutritional behavior patterns and knowledge, the community has to be involved in health promotion through collaboration with different sectors and stakeholders.

Partnership that include staff, students, parents and others from the community are important to create a social and physical environment conducive to health reflecting the diverse range of needs. With local involvement, communities become a valuable resource for the school nutrition programme.

### Media and publicity
Generating publicity for school nutrition programme encourages involvement of the community in school nutrition related activities. In addition, it is crucial to keep parents up to date on the actions and changes taking place within school in order to maintain their support.

### School curriculum
Nutrition education that includes consumer rights and understanding food labels, is crucial to developing healthy, responsible and sustainable lifestyle habits in children. Nutrition messages must be consistent, mutually reinforcing and reflected throughout the curriculum.

Children should get engaged in school gardens and cooking classes to reinforce classroom activities, thereby promoting the ‘farm to fork’ approach. The curriculum needs to cover several broad categories:
*   Stages of food production to consumption, or from ‘farm to fork’
*   Nutrition and personal health
*   Food preparation, preservation and storage
*   Consumer awareness and rights
*   Food and emotional development (body image, self-esteem, children’s own responsibilities)
*   Food production, processing and distribution and sustainability
*   Eating habits and sociocultural influences

## 13. Monitoring and Evaluation

The NSNP will be followed by the development of a nutrition action plan.
To ensure effective implementation of planned activities, monitoring and evaluation is essential in all development programs. In addition, periodic evaluations are necessary for establishing level of objective achievement.

In order to follow up implementation of the school nutrition policy, a process evaluation will be carried out to obtain data on the implementation progress. This will be supported by an impact assessment which will be done at the end of the lifespan of this policy. Monitoring will also involve evaluating the level and quality of stakeholder engagement and documentation of the process of policy development in the form of a report.

Operational research will also be carried out to address specific problems identified during the implementation of nutritional activities.
