## Executive Summary

For patients with a disability, rehabilitation sits at the interface of a range of necessary services, from acute care to ongoing community care and disability services. With established expertise in multidisciplinary care planning and delivery, rehabilitation services have the potential to play a key role in better coordinating the patient journey across these streams of care and ensuring that each patient receives the right services at the right time. That potential can only be realised through the effective implementation of a national rehabilitation strategy.

### Objectives for the Strategy Document

- **Clarify and map** the role of rehabilitation services as an integral and essential component within the healthcare continuum in Ireland.
- **Develop an appropriate policy framework** that can lead to the common understanding and leadership for the future development of rehabilitation services.
- **Develop a strategy for quality service provision** and a preferred model of care.
- **Recognise and support** the importance of rehabilitation data collection, management, and research.
- **Bring forward clear recommendations** on how rehabilitation services should be organised, configured, developed, and managed.
- **Develop a role for a senior healthcare individual** to take overall responsibility for the Strategy.

### Key National Agreements Required

- **Bed Capacity:** Minimum of 30 beds per 100,000 designated rehabilitation beds across adult and care of the elderly specialties.
- **Therapy Levels:** Agreeing minimum allied health therapy levels (internationally recognised at 12–15 hours per week).
- **Models of Care:** Early commencement of rehabilitation before medical stability is achieved.
- **Ambulatory Programs:** Establish comprehensive outpatient and community rehabilitation programs to allow rapid discharge.
- **Benchmarking:** National reporting and benchmarking of rehabilitation access, quality, and outcomes.

## Section A: What is Rehabilitation?

### A.1 Definition of Rehabilitation

- **British Society of Rehabilitation Medicine:**
    - *Conceptual:* A process of active change by which a person who has become disabled acquires the knowledge and skills needed for optimal physical, psychological and social function.
    - *Service:* The use of all means to minimise the impact of disabling conditions and to assist disabled people to achieve their desired level of autonomy and participation in society.
- **World Health Organisation (WHO):** A process aimed at enabling people with disabilities to reach and maintain their optimal physical, sensory, intellectual, psychological and social functional levels.

### A.2 Types of Rehabilitation Services

#### A.2.1 General Rehabilitation
All services caring for patients with disabling conditions have a responsibility to provide a rehabilitative approach.

#### A.2.2 Specialist Rehabilitation Service (SRS)
Total active care of patients with a disabling condition and their families by a multi-professional team with recognised specialist training.

#### A.2.3 Complex Specialised Rehabilitation Services (C-SRS)
Services for patients with severe complex disabilities whose needs are beyond the scope of SRS. Examples include:
- Acquired Brain Injury
- Severe neurological illness (MS, GBS, MND)
- Spinal Cord Injury
- Amputation or limb loss

## Section B: About the National Rehabilitation Hospital (NRH)

Founded in 1961 by the Sisters of Mercy, the NRH provides C-SRS and SRS across four main programmes:
1. **Brain Injury**
2. **Spinal Cord System of Care**
3. **Prosthetic, Orthotic and Limb Absence Rehabilitation (POLAR)**

## Section C: NRH Recommendations

### C.1-C.3 Core Strategic Recommendations
- **National Strategy:** Strong support for a comprehensive National Strategy for Rehabilitation in Ireland.
- **Integration:** All rehabilitation services must be recognised as integral components of the health system.
- **Needs Assessment:** Immediate requirement for objective service mapping and needs assessment.

### C.4 Proposed Model System of Rehabilitation Services

The NRH proposes a four-stage continuum of care:
1. **Acute Specialist Rehabilitation Services (A-SRS):** Focus on reducing impairment and preventing secondary complications.
2. **Post Acute Specialist Rehabilitation Services (PA-SRS):** Transition between hospital and community (includes C-SRS and R-SRS).
3. **Community Based Specialist Rehabilitation Services (CB-SRS):** Focus on enhancing participation, social integration, and return to work.
4. **Continuing Care Services (CCS):** Sustaining services for independent or supported living.

### C.5 Regional and National Network
- **Hub and Spoke Model:** Central hubs providing administration, training, and research, with links to regional spokes.
- **Bed Targets:** Minimum of 60 Acquired Brain Injury (ABI) beds per million population (252 for Ireland).
- **Therapy Intensity:** National agreement on 12–15 hours of therapy per week.

### C.6-C.12 Systemic Improvements
- **Community Focus:** Shift focus from mere maintenance to active community rehabilitation.
- **Management:** Proposals for a clear reporting structure under the Minister for Health & Children.
- **Expertise:** Appointment of international independent experts to guide implementation.
- **Quality:** Creation of a National Framework for Quality and Accreditation (via HIQA).
- **Workforce:** Development of a National Rehabilitation Services Workforce Plan.
- **Data:** Development of coordinated, rehabilitation-specific data collection systems.

### Appendix 4: Brain Injury Programme
- **Incidence:** Estimated 4,600 – 6,300 TBI survivors needing rehab annually in Ireland.
- **Standard:** Aiming for a capacity of 150 beds in the new NRH to meet national needs.
- **Care:** Comprehensive services including comma-arousal, cognitive retraining, and neuropsychology.

### Appendix 5: Spinal Cord System of Care (SCSC)
- **Partnership:** Integrated with Mater Misericordiae University Hospital (Acute phase).
- **Key Services:** Urology clinics, Spinal Liaison Nursing, and Vocational Rehabilitation.
- **Observation:** High rates of "delayed discharges" (65%) due to community transition barriers.

### Appendix 6: POLAR Programme
- **Scope:** Only Consultant-led Prosthetic rehab service in Ireland.
- **Model:** Advocates for a Supra-regional Centre of Excellence hub linked to regional clinics.
- **Focus:** Moving beyond "limb fitting" to holistic rehabilitation.

### Appendix 7: Paediatric Rehabilitation
- **Focus:** Family-centred care for children/adolescents with ABI, SCI, or limb absence.
- **Integration:** Strong focus on school reintegration and transition to adult services.
- **Need:** Advocates for a dedicated paediatric Outreach service to prevent discharge delays.
