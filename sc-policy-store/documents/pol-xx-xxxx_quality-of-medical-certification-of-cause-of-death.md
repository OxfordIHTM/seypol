# QUALITY OF MEDICAL CERTIFICATION OF CAUSE OF DEATH (MCCOD): Rapid Assessment Tool

This form contains a set of simple questions for checking common errors to assess the cause of death certification practices. The assessors of the death certificates are required to go through the eight questions and verify whether there are any of the mentioned errors in the certificate completed by the certifier. This tool is adapted from ‘Assessing the quality of death certificates: Guidance for the rapid tool’ (University of Melbourne) available at https://crvsgateway.info/

## 1. General Instructions

| Field | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Age at death** | Age of the deceased at birth. Remember to include units (hours, days, months, years). Insert ‘not recorded’ if unknown. |
| **Sex of the deceased** | Male or female. Insert ‘not recorded’ if unknown. |
| **Hospital/Health facility** | Name of hospital/health facility where the certificate was completed. |
| **Place of death** | For example; hospital, other health facility, home. Insert ‘not recorded’ if unknown. |
| **Certifier** | For example; hospital. |
| **Date of certification** | Write the date the certificate was completed (DD/MM/YYYY). Insert ‘not recorded’ if unknown or blank. |
| **Error types** | Detailed instructions on how to assess the quality of the death certificate against each error type are provided in the document ‘Assessing the quality of death certificates: Guidance for the rapid tool’. |

---

***Impossible underlying causes:**
- **a. Symptoms or signs** (e.g. fever, backache, headache, enlarged liver etc.)
- **b. Intermediate causes** (e.g. septicaemia, secondary hypertension, pneumonitis, pathological fracture)
- **c. Modes of dying** (e.g. cardiac arrest, respiratory arrest, organ failure)
- **d. Unspecified causes within a larger death category** (e.g. cardiovascular disease, congenital anomaly etc.)

*Note: If two or more causes are reported in the lowest used line, and each condition is separated by a space, comma or a similar punctuation, consider the first condition as underlying. If two or more causes are reported on the lowest used line and the conditions are reported as ‘due to’, ‘secondary to’, ‘as a consequence of’ or a term having similar meaning, consider the last condition as underlying.*
