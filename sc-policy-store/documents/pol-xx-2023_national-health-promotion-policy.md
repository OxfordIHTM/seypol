## Key Policy Statements
1. **Provide National leadership and coordination for health promotion.**
    a) Improve coordination through establishment and leadership of an inter-sectoral coordination body for health promotion.
    b) Provide technical support, practical tools, technologies and guides for health promotion actors.
    c) Establish effective monitoring, reporting and evaluation mechanisms for tracking health promotion activities across society.
2. **Ensure relevant supports for successful health promotion: adequate human resources, finance, equipment and infrastructure.**
    a) Work with the Ministries, Departments and Agencies responsible for Education to deliver courses to build health promotion competencies.
    b) Ensuring adequate regular budget allocations for the routine operations of the Health Promotion Unit (HPU).
    c) Ear-mark an agreed fraction of alcohol, cigarette, and sugar taxes to health promotion activities via the MOH Secretariat.
    d) Establish clear mechanisms for access to allocated finance, with accountability.
    e) Leverage on modern Health Promotion Technologies.
3. **Develop and implement healthy public policies.**
    a) Support the implementation of the Health-in-All-Policies approach (HiAP).
    b) Support development of new, and revision of existing legislation to protect and promote health.
    c) Advocate for implementation of strategic taxation and subsidies to promote health.
4. **Advocate for actions across society to address the root social determinants of health.**
5. **Engage communities and build partnerships to mainstream health promotion across sectors.**
6. **Conduct tailored and targeted health promotion.**
    a) By targeting key populations (vulnerable groups) and settings (schools, workplaces, health centres).
    b) Across the life-course and to address priority disease conditions (NCDs, communicable diseases and injuries).
    c) Ensure health promotion is culturally appropriate and delivered in all National languages.
7. **Empower people to take responsibility for health**, by improving personal skills, health literacy and creating supportive environments.
8. **Reorient health services and programmes** to deliver preventive and promotive interventions.
9. **Use research, evidence, modern technologies and innovations** to inform design and delivery.
10. **Support risk communication and community engagement (RCCE)** for health hazards, in partnership with PHA and DRMD.
11. **Guide health promotion across the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).**

## 1. Background
Seychelles has seen health gains but faces increasing health expenditure and risks. Life expectancy at birth (2018) was 73.3 years, while healthy life expectancy (2016) was 65.7 years.

## 2. General Country Context
Seychelles is a high-income SIDS. Key highlights include high HDI (0.797) and strong gender parity. However, it is vulnerable to climate change and relies heavily on tourism and fisheries.

## 3. Seychelles Disease Burden

### 3.1 RMNCAH
High vaccine coverage but concerns over teenage pregnancy (94 per 1000 in 2018).

### 3.2 Communicable Diseases and Injection Drug Use
Increasing trends in HIV and STIs. Significant heroin epidemic (estimated 5,000 users), addressed by the Agency for Prevention of Drug Abuse and Rehabilitation (APDAR/DSAPTR).

### 3.3 Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs)
High prevalence of obesity (57% men, 72% women) and diabetes. Success in tobacco legislation (Tobacco Control Act 2009) and implementation of a sugar tax.

### 3.4 Injuries, Accidents and Intentional Self-Harm
Road traffic deaths and drowning are key concerns. Suicide rate stands at 10.3 per 100,000 population.

### 3.5 Environmental Health, Climate Change and Health Emergencies
Vulnerability to sea-level rise and outbreaks (Dengue, Chikungunya, COVID-19). RCCE capacity strengthened during the pandemic.

### 3.6 Progress towards health-related SDGs

| Indicator | Value | Trend |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Maternal Mortality Rate | 121 | Worsening |
| Births attended by skilled personnel | 99% | Maintaining |
| Under-5 mortality rate | 20.6 | Improving |
| HIV prevalence | 0.1 | Improving |
| Adolescent fertility rate | 94 | Worsening |
| UHC Tracer Index | >80 | Maintaining |

## 4. Health Promotion

### 4.1 Rationale
Addressing the double burden of infections and NCDs is critical for economic growth and population wellbeing.

### 4.2 Definition
Health promotion is the *‘process of enabling people to increase control over, and to improve their health’*.

### 4.3 Global Context (Ottawa Charter Action Areas)
1. **Build healthy public policy**
2. **Create Supportive Environments**
3. **Strengthen Community Actions**
4. **Develop Personal Skills**
5. **Reorient Health Services**

### 4.4 National Policy Context
Aligned with the National Health Strategic Plan (NHSP) 2022-2026, Strategic Direction 4: Healthy Populations.

### 4.5 Situation Analysis (SWOT)

| Building Block | Strengths | Weaknesses | Opportunities | Threats |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Governance** | HPU as coordination body; Tobacco act | HiAP not institutionalized | Integrate into NHSP revision | Competing priorities |
| **HR** | Basic training for all professionals | Inadequate expert capacity | NIHSS training | Changing volunteerism |
| **Finance** | - | Unpredictable budget | Bilateral/UN funds | Social Determinants |
| **Technology** | Basic IEC equipment | - | Modern social media | - |
| **Research** | - | Inadequate data/M&E | Inter-sectoral links | - |
| **Service Delivery** | Health Promoting Schools | Limited community capacity | Reactivate "Health of Our Nation" | Physical infrastructure |

## 5. Strategic Policy Interventions

### 5.1 Vision
*“To mainstream health in all professions, programmes, policies and people, through partnerships.”*

### 5.2 Guiding Principles
Universality, Equity, Participatory, Evidence-based, and Multi-sectoral.

### 5.3 Key Objectives
1. Strengthen coordination systems.
2. Improve physical and mental health.
3. Advocate for determinants of health.
4. Empower communities.
5. Promote multi-sectoral approaches.
6. Advocate for health-promoting environments.
7. Promote evidence-based research.
8. Establish M&E frameworks.

---

## 6. Institutional Arrangements
* **Health Promotion Unit**: Coordination and support.
* **MOH Programmes**: Enhance preventive aspects.
* **Private Sector**: Encouraged to include promotive services.
* **NGOs**: Leveraged for delivery where MOH capacity is limited.

## 7. Conclusion
This policy provides a feasible framework for a healthier population. Review suggested for 2032.
