**Honourable Mr. Jean-Paul Adam**
**Minister of Health, Seychelles**

*The Ministry of Health in collaboration with its key strategic partners, including the Ministry of Education and the Ministry of Family Affairs, have developed the* ***National School Health Policy 2020***.

*Our vision is for every school to be a* ***Health-Promoting School.*** *A school where the environment in which our children study, learn and play, is safe, secure, hygienic, healthy, and health-promoting in all its aspects.*

*Health is defined as “A state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity.” Hence, the* ***Policy*** *recognises that health is not only of the body, but also of the mind and the spirit. As such, psychosocial determinants of health are primordial in the rationale in developing the Policy.*

*It is this perspective that the* ***National School Health Policy 2020*** *takes shape and seeks to promote health in schools in all its facets and dimensions. The document, developed after a series of individual interviews with key stakeholders and inception workshops, places emphasis on long-term and sustained actions that are realistic and achievable, with special attention paid to infrastructure, processes, procedures, programmes and actions undertaken by all actors in the school environment to help ensure that the objectives set are met.*

*It is my belief that the* ***National School Health Policy 2020*** *comes at the right time, when the Ministry of Health and the Ministry of Education are urging patients, parents, teachers and students to take responsibility for their health and education, to realise that many of the outcomes depend on actions that they take every day. They are the ultimate masters of their health and education destinies.*

*Better Education leads to Better Health. And Better Health leads to Better Education. This policy empowers this virtuous circle. The* ***National School Health Policy 2020*** *seeks to promote this attitude and indeed, this way of life.*

*It is my sincere wish that the* ***National School Health Policy 2020*** *serves as a* *guiding light in the collaborative work that the Ministry of Health undertakes with its valued partners in schools. Perhaps even more importantly it will help the children of Seychelles stay safe and healthy, and achieve their full potential as citizens. The country needs them, for a better Seychelles.*

# Executive Summary

The Ministry of Health in collaboration with the Ministry of Education and Human Resource Development has been in consultation with key stakeholders (parents, teachers, students, school health nurses and the community) to develop the Seychelles National School Health Policy 2020. The project funded by WHO recognises the benefits for children at an early age of developing healthy dietary and lifestyle patterns.

The Policy focuses on an examination of the strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats to the school health programme in general and makes recommendations on actions to reverse negative trends and to promote positive policies, regulations, habits and behaviours. The Policy has the vision of “*a school system where parents, teachers, students and other stakeholders are safe and healthy and able to enjoy the highest possible level of holistic health*” and the mission of protecting and promoting the physical and mental well-being of all persons attending and / or working in schools, especially the children, through a holistic and comprehensive school health programme.

# 1.0 Introduction

**Geography and demographics**

1. The Seychelles Archipelago, consisting of 155 islands, is located in the western Indian Ocean, four degrees south of the Equator and approximately 1,600 km off the coast of East Africa. 

2. The multi-ethnic population of 94,000 live mainly on the four largest granitic islands of Mahé, Praslin, La Digue and Silhouette.

## Health and Education Systems

1. The right to health is guaranteed in the ***Seychelles Constitution*** ***Chapter Three: Seychellois Charter of Fundamental Human Rights and Freedoms*** in Article 29.

2. Thus, the main provider of clinical and rehabilitative services is the State.

3. The Ministry of Health provides a comprehensive Maternal and Child Health Programme (MCHP). The School Health Programme takes over from the MCHP and aims to provide ‘*comprehensive, preventive and promotive health services to school going children between the ages of 4 to 17 years*.’

4. The right to education is also guaranteed in the Constitution in Chapter Three in Article 33.

5. Presently, the education system comprises of state schools (26 primary and 11 secondary) and 4 private schools.

## 2.0 Aim

The aim of the ***National School Health Policy 2020*** is to protect and promote the physical, mental and social well-being of children, through a holistic and comprehensive school health programme, in collaboration with key stakeholders.

## 3.0 Objectives

The specific objectives are to:

1. Create and foster sustainable and conducive school environments that protect and promote the physical, mental and social well-being of children.
2. Facilitate and improve access and availability of preventive, promotive and rehabilitative health services to the children.
3. Uphold a culture of healthy living habits in the school community.

# Policy values

1. **Caring**: We consider health as our business and caring as our vocation.
2. **Development**: We shall always develop the health staff to lead our people to healthier lifestyles.
3. **Excellence**: Anything we do now, we shall try to do it better the next time.
4. **Partnership**: We believe in a perpetual partnership with our stakeholders.
5. **People**: We value our health workforce as our most important asset.
6. **Productivity**: We shall strive untiringly to deliver both quality and quantity in health care.
7. **Professionalism**: We believe that skills professionalism is a sine qua non for health improvement.
8. **Reward and Teamwork**: We believe that team work brings about better results.
9. **Service**: We shall serve both with our heart and our mind.

**Policy Principles**

1. *Holistic approach to health*
2. *A whole-school approach*
3. *Targeted and timely interventions and referrals*
4. *Meaningful engagement of parents, students, teachers, the community and other stakeholders*

### Holistic approach to health

The Policy recognises that health is an interaction of physical, mental, emotional and social factors. It seeks to align itself with international commitments and national policies listed in Annex 3.

### Whole school approach

The School Health Programme will be implemented in a comprehensive manner considering the culture of the Seychellois and religious particularities.

### Targeted and timely interventions and referrals

Focused on early identification of health issues. Interventions need to be based on identified needs and evidence-informed decisions.

### Meaningful engagement of all partners

Engaging stakeholders from conception to evaluation. The Programme will ensure that students with physical impairments or mental health issues are included as fully involved participants.

## Policy guidelines

**Objective One**: *Create and foster a sustainable school environment...*

* The School Health Nurse, Dental personnel, and counsellors shall work together to reinforce implementation.
* The Ministry of Education shall maintain the school in the highest possible level of cleanliness.
* All schools shall provide secure facilities for play and physical activities.
* Dining halls and tuck shops shall be maintained for a pleasant eating experience.
* Elimination of physical health hazards (e.g., open gutters, broken handrails).
* Ensure adequate ventilation and access to potable water.

**Objective Two**: *Facilitate and improve access and availability of preventive and promotive health services...*

* A comprehensive health screening programme (see Annex 4) shall be implemented.
* Full-time allocation of one school health nurse per school.
* Periodic review of the health services package.
* Secure funding and provide appropriate physical facilities (offices, screening rooms, sick bays).
* Phase out non-school-based takeaways in accordance with National School Nutrition Policy.

**Objective Three**: *Educate parents/guardians/care-givers, teachers, school children and the community...*

* Use existing school committees to design and implement health behaviour change programmes.
* Integrate awareness programmes into the annual school calendar.

## Roles and Responsibilities

| Partners | Roles and Responsibilities |
| :--- | :--- |
| **National Coordination Committee** | Manage and implement School Health Services; monitor progress; provide guidelines; make policy adjustments. |
| **Ministry of Health** | Provide key services; employ and deploy health professionals; oversee service delivery; provide equipment and materials. |
| **Ministry of Education** | Provide appropriate facilities for health staff; educate school staff on the importance of services. |
| **School Health Programme Manager** | Manage services; coordinate between MoH and MoED; advocate for resources; monitor and report delivery. |
| **School Management Committee** | Develop monitoring mechanisms; facilitate information flow. |
| **Health Professionals** (Nurses, Dental, etc.) | Implement programmes and activities; monitor individual service delivery; alert manager to issues. |

# Monitoring and Evaluation

* The Ministry of Education and Ministry of Health shall oversee implementation.
* The School Health Nurse shall report on implementation annually.
* Implementation will be measured against a set of national indicators.

# Conclusion

 The School Health Programme is an investment in national development. Health and success in school are inextricably linked; when a child experiences good health, it facilitates growth, development, and optimal learning.
