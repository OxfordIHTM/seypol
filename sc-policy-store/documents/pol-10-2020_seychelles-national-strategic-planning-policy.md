# Purpose, Objectives and Scope

1. The National Strategic Planning (NSP) Policy establishes the foundation for the design and implementation of an integrated planning function in Seychelles. The NSP Policy provides the policy framework for the strategic planning pillar of the Results-Based Management (RBM) Policy (2013) and complements the Planning, Monitoring and Evaluation (PME) Policy (2018).

## Purpose

1. The purpose of the NSP Policy is to support inclusive growth and sustainable development by maximizing national outcomes and delivering high-quality services to all citizens.

## Objectives

1. The NSP Policy will be implemented under the four general objectives that underpin the RBM Policy:
1. **Efficiency**: To increase public sector efficiency in order to create the fiscal space required to deliver national priorities.
2. **Effectiveness**: To maximize public sector effectiveness in order to provide accessible, high-quality services and improve government performance.
3. **Transparency**: To enhance transparency in order to strengthen open and accessible government.
4. **Accountability**: To develop a performance-driven planning culture, system, instruments and tools in order to ensure the Government delivers its mandate by achieving planned goals, objectives and outcomes.

2. The NSP Policy is based on an overall objective and specific objectives. Its overall objective is:

> To establish an integrated, evidence-based policy and fiscal planning system that supports the achievement of national and sector outcomes over the short, medium and long term.[[1]](#footnote-1)

### Specific Objectives

1. To provide the policy foundation for implementing the strategic planning pillar of the RBM Policy.
2. To establish the institutional architecture, instruments, standards and tools for the NSP system.
3. To harmonize the instruments, standards and tools of the NSP, PPBB, PME and PMS systems within an integrated, results-driven planning function.
4. To implement portfolio-based planning[[2]](#footnote-2) with clear lines of accountability that focus institutional efforts on achieving national and sector priorities.
5. To institutionalize a results-based, participative and open planning culture across government that continuously monitors, improves and motivates performance.

## Scope

1. The NSP policy applies to all institutions of the executive branch of government.
2. The core NSP instruments will be developed at national and sector levels. To ensure clear accountability, sectors will be portfolio-based (see footnote below). Sectors comprise the lead portfolio institution and the additional departments and agencies that, for strategic planning purposes, report to the head of the lead portfolio institution.[[3]](#footnote-3)
3. In most cases, the lead portfolio institution is a ministry, although different arrangements exist when a department or agency reports to the President, Vice-President or Designated Minister. The National RBM Steering Committee[[4]](#footnote-4) will review and propose for Cabinet approval the composition of and lead portfolio institution for each sector.

# Legal and Policy Foundation

1. The NSP Policy is prepared under the RBM Policy (2013), which established four pillars: strategic planning, programme performance-based budgeting (PPBB), performance monitoring and evaluation, and performance management systems (PMS) and the cross-cutting foundation of open and digital government. The NSP Policy provides the policy framework for the strategic planning pillar of the RBM Policy and complements the PME Policy (2018).
2. With respect to legislation, the linkages between the NSP system, PPBB and the other RBM pillars need to be codified in appropriate statutory instruments, such as the Public Financial Management Act (2012).

It also outlines the coordinating mechanisms used to implement NSP as an RBM pillar across government and elaborates the executive branch’s responsibilities to the legislative and judicial branches.

## Coordinating Mechanisms

1. As national NSP coordinator, EPD serves as the central government institution responsible for implementing the strategic planning pillar under the RBM Policy.
2. The National RBM Steering Committee, formed in 2017 and chaired by the Secretary of State for MFTIEP, oversees and provides guidance to the harmonization of NSP with other RBM pillars. Its main function is to oversee the coordination and implementation of the RBM policy framework through its four pillars thereby providing a more integrated approach for strengthening public sector performance. EPD represents the strategic planning pillar on this committee.
3. At the portfolio level, the portfolio RBM Committee, chaired by the lead portfolio institution, will oversee NSP coordination and harmonization across all portfolio MDAs. At the agency or department level, the internal RBM Committee, RBM Coordinator and focal point responsible for NSP will ensure that NSP coordination and alignment with other RBM pillars occurs.

# NSP System, Instruments and Standards

1. This section outlines the key elements of the NSP system, including the strategic planning cycle and priority setting, and describes the core NSP instruments, tools and standards. Detailed guidance on NSP tools will be included in the NSP Guidelines.

## Planning Hierarchy

1. The NSP system is based on a hierarchy and results chain where higher-level national goals and objectives are translated into increasingly concrete medium and short-term activities, outputs and outcomes at sector (portfolio-based), MDA and budget programme levels. In this way, a roadmap can be provided from the budget in a single MDA programme up to a strategic goal in the NDS.
2. Through the PME system, NSP-related strategies, plans, policies, programmes and projects are then monitored and evaluated along the results chain to assess whether planned outcomes are being achieved and, if not, what corrective actions are required. In future, PME will be implemented through an integrated RBM instrument and, later, an RBM dashboard that brings together the four RBM pillars within the open and digital government foundation. Its quality and success will depend on the development of a robust, realistic PME framework as part of each strategic plan.
3. The NSP system comprises three levels of planning:
    * **Long-term planning** refers to NSP instruments with a duration greater than five years.
    * **Medium-term planning** refers to NSP instruments with a duration of more than one year up to five years.
    * **Short-term planning** refers to NSP instruments (mostly annual plans) with a duration of up to one year.
4. Core long-term NSP instruments include:
    * National Vision.
    * Long-Term Strategies.
5. Core medium-term NSP instruments include:
    * National Development Strategy.
    * Sector Strategic Plans (portfolio-based)[[7]](#footnote-7)
    * Medium-Term Expenditure Strategies (MTES).
6. Core short-term NSP instruments include:
    * Annual Cabinet Work Plan
    * Annual Operational Plans (MDAs)
7. PME instruments, including annual, semi-annual and quarterly progress reports, templates and arrangements, support implementation of the NDS, Sector Strategic Plans and MTES by regularly tracking progress and determining if corrective actions are required.

## Strategic Planning Cycle

1. The strategic planning cycle comprises both fixed and rolling cycles.
    * The **fixed cycle** includes the 15-year National Vision, 5-year NDS and supporting 5-year Sector Strategic Plans.
    * The **rolling cycle** consists of the annual preparation of the 3-year MTES and budget requests, the annual Cabinet Work Plan and MDA operational plans.
2. PME tools and arrangements underpin both the fixed and rolling strategic planning cycles and support successful implementation of long, medium and short-term planning instruments.
3. The current National Vision, *Seychelles Vision 2033*, looks forward fifteen years. It will be delivered by three successive NDS documents: 2019-23; 2024-2028; and 2029-2033. Beginning in 2024, Sector Strategic Plans will cover the same time frames as the NDS. Preparation of the 2024-28 NDS and Sector Strategic Plans will begin in mid 2022 to enable completion before the plans come into effect.
4. Three-year MTES documents are prepared annually during the first half of the year to provide a strategic component to the following year’s three-year budget. For example, the 2022-24 MTES will begin preparation in early 2021. The MTES and PME templates will, in time, be integrated into a joint RBM tool to serve as the implementation plan for the NDS and Sector Strategic Plans.
5. To ensure that NSP-related strategies, plans, policies, programmes and projects are implemented, monitored and evaluated, the PME function deploys a series of instruments and tools across government as per the PME Policy and Guidelines. At quarterly and annual intervals, the PME templates and various progress reports provide government with the opportunity to take stock of progress, measure achievements, identify potential problems and take corrective actions.
6. In future, the evaluation cycle will be integrated into the strategic planning cycle in accordance with the PME Policy (2018).
7. Within the current budget year, the mid-term budget review that takes place after the second quarter offers an opportunity to review progress on underspending, determine if the problem can be remedied and, if not, reallocate underspending (e.g., because of potential delays in launching a public investment project) to national or sector priorities.

## Priority Setting

1. Priority setting underpins successful implementation and performance in any planning system. Priorities are set at national and sector levels. National priorities represent those established in the NDS. Sector priorities represent those established in Sector Strategic Plans. In addition, select national priorities, drawing from the NDS, Sector Strategic Plans or emerging issues, may be identified annually by Cabinet to inform the PPBB process.
2. Two key statements of national priorities are the annual State of the Nation Address (SONA) by the President and the Budget Speech by the Minister responsible for Finance. When providing potential proposals for inclusion in these priority statements, lead portfolio institutions and MDAs need to identify and prioritize those initiatives that support delivery of national and sector priorities.
3. The NDS and Sector Strategic Plans cover five years. On an annual basis, different national and sector priorities may be highlighted through the MTES and PME processes, which take into account the sequencing of strategic interventions over the five-year period. In this way, the MTES document and PME templates (in future, to be merged into a single, integrated RBM instrument) will serve as an implementation plan for national and sector priorities by focusing available resources at the tangible projects and activities aimed at delivering a higher-level goal or objective.
4. MFTIEP may propose mechanisms and procedures through the PPBB process to ensure that funding is being directed from lower-priority expenditure areas to national and sector priorities. Once implemented, e-government initiatives may also free up funding for redeployment to priorities.

## Core NSP Instruments

1. NSP instruments follow a hierarchy where the National Vision informs the National Development Strategy which, in turn, informs portfolio-based Sector Strategic Plans, which in turn inform MTES submissions, budgets, PME instruments and MDA operational plans. In this way, the delivery of national and sector priorities is directly linked to MDA budget allocations.
2. The *National Vision* presents the vision for Seychelles, describing where the nation aims to be in 15 years. The vision is expressed for the country and its citizens generally and across a range of sectors.
3. Other *long-term strategies* may be developed when a crosscutting issue or the policy challenges facing a sector require a long-term perspective (e.g., pension policy). See section below on initiating a strategy.
4. *International commitments* with policy implications, such as the Sustainable Development Goals, represent another type of long-term strategy that sets similarly forward-looking goals, but from a global perspective. Wherever feasible, these commitments need to be mainstreamed into the NSP system to avoid duplication and ensure that efforts to achieve national goals and international commitments are coherent and mutually reinforcing.
5. The five-year *NDS* outlines the roadmap for the National Vision and serves as the primary statement of national priorities.
6. *Sector Strategic Plans* cover five years and are portfolio-based. They describe how the sector will deliver relevant NDS commitments and set the strategic outcomes, contributing indicators, baselines and targets that will serve as the policy roadmap for that sector. These plans also identify the strategic interventions needed to achieve the goals.
7. The portfolio-based approach to developing Sector Strategic Plans does not preclude individual MDAs from preparing strategic plans at the MDA level to guide internal planning and inform contributions to their MTES and Sector Strategic Plans. However, it is intended that the MTES serve as the primary strategic document at the MDA level.
8. Although developed in parallel, the NDS and Sector Strategic Plans must be fully consistent before final approval. Any discrepancies should be resolved and removed before final approval and publication.
9. The three-year MTES and PME templates (in future, to be integrated into a joint integrated RBM instrument) translate the higher-level strategic goals, objectives and targets in the NDS and Sector Strategic Plans into a more concrete set of short, medium and long-term outcomes and priority actions linked to the budget. This enables decision makers to review portfolio and MDA budgets from the perspective of the results they will achieve and the degree of alignment with national and sector priorities.
10. Two key instruments supporting Cabinet decision making need to take NSP commitments into account: preparation of the *Cabinet Work Plan* by Cabinet Office and preparation of *Policy Memoranda* by MDAs.
    1. In preparing annual proposals for inclusion in the Cabinet Work Plan, MDAs need to clearly identify the national and/or sector priorities that the Policy Memoranda are supporting. These will be prioritized by Cabinet Office when scheduling for Cabinet.
    2. Policy Memoranda need to identify whether and how the proposal aligns with and delivers national and/or sector priorities. They also need to contain a rigorous fiscal impact assessment and require a mandatory review and opinion by MFTIEP.
11. The PME templates and other quarterly, semi-annual and annual results and progress reports described in the PME Policy and Guidelines measure progress towards achieving planned outputs and outcomes. This performance information serves as an accountability instrument and as an early warning system for potential problems, thus permitting the necessary corrective actions to be taken in order for the desired outcomes to be achieved.
12. The integrated RBM dashboard will help bring together NSP, PPBB, PME, PMS and other performance information in one single digital instrument to ensure that strategies, plans, policies, programmes and projects are monitored with a view to driving implementation.

## Initiating a Long or Medium-Term Strategy

1. If an MDA is planning on preparing a medium or long-term strategy that will require Cabinet approval, other than the core NSP instruments, the initiation of the strategy must first be approved by Cabinet.
2. Before being scheduled at Cabinet, EPD’s opinion on the need for the strategy and the standards to be applied must accompany the proposal.
3. This requirement only applies to strategies requiring Cabinet approval and does not limit an MDA’s capacity to develop such strategies for internal use.

## Contingency Planning and Crisis Response

1. During periods of national emergency or crisis, the NSP system may need to be adapted to address urgent planning requirements, including:
   1. Developing a range of policy options and identifying their respective socio-economic and fiscal impacts to address immediate challenges.
   2. Applying and refining those options to fit a range of macroeconomic/fiscal scenarios.
   3. Preparing a medium to longer-term national recovery plan.
   4. Recalibrating existing NSP instruments to align with new policy and fiscal environments.
2. During such periods, NSP officials will need to work closely with central or sector crisis-response teams.
3. To strengthen crisis response capability, contingency planning should be regularly undertaken during non-crisis periods drawing on NDS and Sector Strategic Plan risk assessments to anticipate various scenarios.

# Implementation

1. This section outlines key elements related to implementation of the NSP Policy.

## Open Government: Citizen and Stakeholder Engagement
1. From an NSP perspective, open government depends on the sustained involvement of citizens as core contributors to the planning function and the expansion of social accountability mechanisms. *Seychelles Vision 2033* and the 2019-23 NDS were developed through a participatory process that engaged citizens, civil society, the private sector and other stakeholders across Seychelles. Similarly, the NSP Policy will reinforce the government’s commitment to open government and the principles of transparency, accountability and good governance outlined in the President’s inaugural address.
2. To embed transparency and social accountability, tools and mechanisms will be developed that present the government’s planned versus achieved results. The following core NSP & PME reports will be publicly-provided through a range of channels:
    1. Annual Progress Report on the implementation of the National Development Strategy
    2. Annual Progress Report on the implementation of portfolio-based Sector Strategic Plans.
    3. National and sector-level evaluations and related studies.
    4. Performance information generated from the PME system, including the integrated RBM instrument and RBM dashboard (under development).
    5. Results from feedback mechanisms through which citizens and stakeholders will be able to assess the quality of government policies, programmes and services.
3. By formalizing citizen and stakeholder engagement, a cornerstone is put in place for realizing the NSP Policy’s commitment to efficiency, effectiveness, transparency and accountability.

## Human and Financial Resources

1. In alignment with other RBM pillars, the NSP function across government must be appropriately staffed and resourced to ensure successful implementation. Although some MDAs may already have a planning function in place, this is not uniformly applied. To ensure adequate staffing of each institution, an overall capacity assessment, including a costing exercise, needs to be completed and job descriptions, a career path and relevant schemes of service developed. In the interim, MDAs are required to either designate an NSP Focal Point among existing staff or recruit a planning officer from within their existing staffing ceiling to fulfill these duties. This assessment must be undertaken in the context of implementing all RBM pillars.
2. Beyond resourcing levels, effective implementation depends on a capacity strengthening plan and training modules being developed and coordinated with related efforts by the other RBM pillars. EPD will be leading this initiative with respect to NSP capacity development.

## Incentives and Sanctions

1. To reinforce accountability and motivate positive individual performance, incentives and sanctions need to be developed. A range of non-monetary incentives and sanctions need to be considered.
2. At the institution level, budgetary mechanisms may provide a vehicle for rewarding or sanctioning institutions based on their performance in implementing the NSP Policy and achieving the results set out in relevant NSP instruments. Such mechanisms can be designed in a way that reinforces the need to redirect resources from lower to higher priority budget programmes and maximize results through greater cost efficiency and effectiveness.

## Change Management, Risk Management and Strategic Communications

1. A change management plan, including stakeholder engagement, strategic communications and risk management, will be developed to support implementation of the NSP Policy and to engage key stakeholders in reducing resistance to change and enhancing ownership and sustainability of the planning function.

## Performance Monitoring and Evaluation

1. To ensure that the NSP system is implemented effectively, it will be regularly monitored. To avoid conflicts of interest, this will be overseen by the National RBM Steering Committee and submitted to Cabinet for review and adoption. The results framework for assessing NSP Policy implementation will be set out in the NSP Guidelines.
2. An evaluation of NSP Policy implementation will take place every five years.

## NSP Guidelines

1. Following approval of this policy, the NSP Guidelines will be developed to guide MDAs, planners and other stakeholders in the implementation of this policy. The guidelines will include detailed information on each section of the NSP Policy and guidance on how to develop and use the various instruments, tools, templates and user-friendly how-to guides of the NSP system and how to align these with those used by other RBM pillars.

1. National outcomes refer to the planned results to be achieved through implementation of the National Development Strategy. Sector outcomes refer to the planned results to be achieved through implementation of portfolio-based Sector Strategic Plans. In most instances, sector outcomes will align with higher-level national outcomes, but, in some cases, they will refer to sector-specific planned results.
2. **Portfolios** are organized by the government’s administrative structure and, most often, report to a Minister. The composition of the portfolio changes whenever a new government structure is implemented by the President. From an accountability and budgetary perspective, organizing by portfolio is simpler since all the functions and budget programmes in the portfolio report to a single position (usually a Minister),

   **Sectors** are organized by related functions, tend to be stable, but change when the government adds a new function or divests or eliminates an existing one. From an accountability and budgetary perspective, organizing by sector is more challenging since individual sectors often include functions and budget programmes from more than one portfolio i.e., multiple institutions.

   **Portfolio-based Sectors**: In Seychelles, sectors will be portfolio-based; i.e., the grouping of related functions will, wherever feasible, be aligned with a portfolio. However, when a portfolio includes clearly unrelated functions, it may be divided into two sectors. Each sector will be required to prepare a portfolio-based Sector Strategic Plan (abbreviated to Sector Strategic Plan). The portfolio-based approach will be introduced to support the next round of strategic planning supporting preparation of the next NDS.
3. In some cases, departments and agencies report to a Minister. In other cases, they report through a Board of Directors and not to a Minister. For strategic planning purposes, Cabinet, in establishing the portfolio-based sectors, may designate a lead portfolio institution to which all departments and agencies in that sector will report for strategic planning purposes. This does not otherwise affect the established legal reporting relationship that exists for any agency or department.
4. The **National RBM Steering Committee**, chaired by the State Secretary, MFTIEP, oversees implementation of the RBM Policy and harmonizes policy and implementation across the four RBM pillars.
5. NBS focuses on collecting, compiling, analyzing and publishing statistical information and supervising the National Statistical System. This role needs to be differentiated from the role of MDAs in collecting and analysing administrative and service delivery data related to their operations.
6. The internal RBM Committee in MDAs should be derived from the existing PPBB Committee model. Small MDAs may be exempted from this requirement. In some MDAs, depending on size and mandate, NSP focal point responsibilities may be assigned to the RBM Coordinator.
7. The term Sector Strategic Plans is used within the context of a portfolio-based NSP system. Accordingly, all Sector Strategic Plans are portfolio-based with clear accountability to the head of the lead institution (usually a Minister).
