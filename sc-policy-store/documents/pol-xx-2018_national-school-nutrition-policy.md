## Executive Summary

The need for the revision of National School Nutrition Policy comes at an opportune time, now that there is a huge number of products available on the market and consumers often make unhealthy choices face to these situations. Adequate knowledge on healthy eating pattern is crucial among the population now that the rates of obesity is on the rise in both children and adults in the Seychelles. Based on latest results, approximately **22% boys and girls are overweight and obese in the Seychelles**.

In order to prevent or reverse the adverse health effects of overweight and poor eating habits, interventions targeted at healthy nutrition need to occur early in childhood and adolescence. Schools provide the most effective way to reach large portions of the population, including young people, school personnel, families and community members.

---

## Foreword

Actions carried out in the school are widely known to improve children’s lifestyles. However, further commitments are needed by the Government in order to ensure adequate school environment for all children of the Seychelles.

The National School Nutrition Policy was first developed in 2008 and has been revised in 2018 by the Nutrition Unit from the Ministry of Health in collaboration with the Ministry of Education.

# 1 Introduction

### 1.1 What is the policy about?

A National School Nutrition Policy represents efforts to utilize schools as sites for improving the nutritional well-being of all school children. The policy will be used to coordinate all aspects of school nutrition including the curriculum, the school tuck shop, provision of school meals, and training of school personnel.

### 1.2 Goals, objectives and outcomes

#### 1.2.1 Goal

The main goal is to improve the nutritional well-being of school children in the Seychelles through a school environment conducive to healthy eating.

#### 1.2.2 Objectives
- To implement nutrition education from crèche through to secondary schools as part of a sequential comprehensive school curriculum;
- To harmonize the school’s food provision and environment with nutrition education in the curriculum;
- To build the capacity of staffs involved in nutrition education and provision in the principles of healthy eating;
- To involve the Parent’s Teachers Association (PTA), School Council and the community in supporting and reinforcing nutrition education and
- To provide a tool to ensure the school’s compliance to promoting healthy eating.

#### 1.2.3 Outcomes
- Integration of nutrition education that is in line with the Seychelles Dietary Guidelines in a sequential and comprehensive school curriculum
- Improved school’s food provision and environment that is harmonized with nutrition education in the curriculum;
- Staff involved in nutrition education and provision trained on the principles of healthy eating
- Nutrition education supported and reinforced by the PTA and the community
- Tools to ensure the school’s compliance to promoting healthy eating;
- Increase in nutrition-related activities at school level;
- Reduction in diet-related problems amongst school children
- Advertisement/ promotion of food or drink in accordance with the Seychelles Dietary Guidelines

# 2 Health and Nutrition of school children

### 2.1 Obesity
Overweight and obesity have increased drastically among the general population and is no exception among children and adolescents. Overweight and obese children are likely to stay obese into adulthood and more likely to develop non-communicable diseases such as; diabetes and cardiovascular diseases at a younger age.

### 2.2 Dental carries
Tooth decay is the most common oral disease affecting children and adolescents. There is enough evidence to show that sugar is the most important factor in dental decay/caries.

### 2.3 Lack of physical activity
Physical inactivity has been defined as the fourth leading risk factor for global mortality. Sedentary behaviours such as playing electronic games and watching TV are directly associated with obesity.

### 2.4 Eating habits
There has been an abundance of restaurants and take away outlets across the country leading to an increase in the consumption of fast foods and take away meals and a decrease in fruits and vegetable intake.

### 2.5 Good nutrition in schools
Healthy nutrition improves child well-being and learning ability, leading to better academic performance. A balanced and nutritious diet feeds the body as well as the mind.

---

# 3 The roles of nutrition in schools and community

### 3.1 The school community
Schools and communities have a shared responsibility to provide children access to high quality foods and positive nutrition experiences.

### 3.2 Parents and caregivers
Parental involvement is crucial as it enhances the effectiveness of programs by stimulating awareness about food and nutrition.

### 3.3 Teachers Role Model /collaborations
Teachers and other school personnel have a major role in shaping the eating habits of children by also consuming high nutritional value foods from the GREEN category.

### 3.4 Dedicated funds
All schools hold a ‘Dedicated Fund’ for the purposes of assisting students whose families are experiencing financial difficulties.

### 3.5 School curriculum
#### 3.5.1 Educational materials
All nutrition education IEC materials developed for schools need to be in line with the Seychelles Dietary Guidelines.

---

# 4 Guidelines for Food Provision

### 4.1 School Meal standard
Healthy school meals can provide children with a third of their daily nutritional requirements. 

**Objectives:**
- To see an increase availability of items categorised as Green, a reduction of items from Amber group and a complete elimination of food items from the Red category.
- Staffs serving meals to have increased skills and knowledge.

### 4.2 Guidelines for school lunches from external sources (Take away/ Home)
Guidelines aim at providing healthier choices and reducing the incidence of foodborne illnesses among school children.

### 4.3 Tuck shop Guidelines
#### The Traffic Light Classification System
- **Green**: Healthier choices. Should make at least **70%** of the tuck shop menu.
- **Amber**: Should be selected carefully. Should make about **30%** of the tuck shop menu.
- **Red**: Should **NOT** be provided at all on the school premises.

---

# 5 Children with medical conditions

- **5.1 Children with Diabetes**: Can eat the same school meals but should have a meal plan prepared by a Dietician/Nutritionist.
- **5.2 Children Suffering from Food Allergies**: Parents should try to provide their lunch and snacks to accommodate specific requirements.
- **5.3 Nutritional needs of exceptional children**: Institutions should have a policy for delivering medically prescribed diets in partnership with parents and nutritionists.

---

# 6 Social School Events

- **6.1 Fund-Raising Activities**: Foods sold should only be from the **GREEN** category.
- **6.2 Classroom parties**: Only foods from **GREEN** and **AMBER** categories will be served.
- **6.3 Field Trips**: Foods should be from the **GREEN** classifications.
- **6.4 Instructional Purposes**: Must be in line with Tuck Shop Guideline and Food Act.
- **6.5 Vending machines**: Not permitted on any school premises.

---

# 7 Food Safety

Key requirements include:
- Basic food hygiene and hand washing.
- No storage of leftover food.
- Use of licensed sources.
- Personnel must have medical certificates of fitness valid for six months.

---

# 8 Physical activity

Physical Education (PE) occupies 80 minutes on school time table every week. Regular physical activity helps reduce risks for cardiovascular diseases and diabetes.

---

# 9 Access to water

Water should always be adequate and easily accessible. Water fountains are encouraged on school premises.

---

# 10 Coordination and monitoring

### 10.1 The National School Nutrition Committee
Multi-sectorial committee providing leadership in implementation. Includes representatives from MOH, MOE, and other stakeholders.

### 10.2 School Nutrition Action Groups (SNAG)
Action-oriented group responsible for advocacy, awareness, education, and monitoring of compliance at the school level.

### 10.3 Evaluation tools
Designed to assist with monitoring the policy in schools. Evaluations should be conducted at the start and end of every school term.

### 10.4 Roles and responsibilities of MOH and MOE
- **MOE**: Assertively implement the policy, ensure SNAG is functional, and monitor tuck shops.
- **MOH**: Draft the policy, provide nutritional guidelines, and facilitate training.

---

# 11 Implementation

### 11.1 School Nutrition Award
An incentive for schools and operators implementing the policy correctly, awarded every two years.

### 11.2 Violation of policy
SNAG monitors adherence; National Committee informs MOE for corrective actions.

### 11.3 Review of the Policy
Advised to be reviewed after 5 years.
