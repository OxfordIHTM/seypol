# 1. Chapter 1

## 1.1. Acknowledgement
We would like to acknowledge the support and input of the staff of the Ministry of Health and the WHO Liaison Office for Seychelles in the development of this document.

We would like to thank all those who agreed to participate in the pretesting of the messages and ad-hoc assessment of the knowledge, attitude and practices on plague conducted during the development of the plan.

## 1.2. Background
Madagascar is experiencing a severe outbreak of plague affecting major cities and other non-endemic areas since August 2017. This current outbreak is of significant public health concern since both pneumonic and bubonic plagues are reported, with pneumonic the most virulent form of the disease. This outbreak carries a moderate risk of spread to neighbouring Indian Ocean islands, including Seychelles.

At the time of drafting, there have been no confirmed cases of plague in Seychelles. However, events in September and October 2017 including a death in Madagascar and a suspected case in Seychelles created panic and rumours on social media. The Ministry responded by releasing daily updates via the Commissioner of Public Health and the Ministry of Health website, and established a hotline (141) on 12th October 2017.

## 1.3. Introduction
There are different types of plague:
- **Pneumonic plague**: Lung-based, most virulent, can be transmitted between humans via inhalation of droplets. Almost always fatal if not treated early.
- **Bubonic plague**: Most common form, caused by the bite of an infected flea. Causes "buboes" (inflamed lymph nodes).
- **Septicaemic plague**: Occurs when infection spreads through the bloodstream. Can cause bleeding and tissue necrosis.

Effective risk communications is vital for prevention and control of outbreaks, ensuring a regular exchange of information between health officials and the public.

## 1.4. Guiding Principles
The plan is based on five essential practices identified by WHO:
1. **Build trust**: Creating effective two-way communications.
2. **Announce early**: Communicating fast to win public confidence.
3. **Be transparent**: Communication that is candid, complete, and accurate.
4. **Respect public concerns**: Treating concerns as legitimate and engaging in dialogue.
5. **Plan in advance**: Having protocols and materials ready for dissemination.

## 1.5. Risk Communication areas
a) Public awareness and information
b) Behaviour change and protective action
c) Dynamic listening and rumour management
d) Coordination and partnerships

## 1.6. Purpose
This communication strategic plan will contribute to maintain a zero local infection rate and 100% detection rate for imported cases of plague in Seychelles.

# 2. Chapter 2

## 2.1. Rapid Assessment
An emergency helpline was established in October 2017. Concerns recorded have been used to inform this strategy. A summary of key responses is in Annex 3.

## 2.2. Consideration of outrage
Risk perception is a function of hazard and outrage. The public often responds more to outrage than to hazard data, and this must be addressed in the communication strategy.

## 2.3. Coordination and Partnerships
Success depends on the involvement of health partners, government agencies, and communities. Information must be presented in a coordinated and consistent way.

## 2.4 Media landscape
Mass media can reach large numbers of people quickly. In Seychelles, print, radio, and TV are popular, while Facebook is increasingly used by younger generations. 90% of the population owns a mobile phone, making SMS an effective tool.

# 3. Chapter 3

## 3.1. Communication Goal
To increase the level of knowledge on plague and promote protective action among the general population to prevent its importation and spread.

## 3.2. Primary Communication Strategy
Develop and implement a comprehensive communication plan to raise awareness on plague risks and promote preventive measures in Seychelles.

## 3.3. Communication Objectives
1. Increase percentage of the population aware of origin, symptoms, and transmission of plague.
2. Increase uptake of protective measures among at-risk groups.
3. Increase collaboration among target groups and stakeholders.
4. Ensure rapid and proactive communications in the event of an outbreak.

## 3.4. Target Audiences
- **Primary**: Travellers, Health Workers, Travel authorities, Responders, Enforcement Agencies, Ministry of Health/DRDM, General public.
- **Secondary**: Schools, Religious organisations, Community Leaders, National Assembly, Alternative Medicine practitioners.
- **Tertiary**: Media, Health partners/NGOs, Businesses, Other Ministries.

## 3.5. Settings
Homes, Communities, Schools, Workplaces, Health facilities, Points of entry, Places of worship.

## 3.9. Communication Approaches
- **Advocacy**: Engaging influential organizations/individuals.
- **Interpersonal Communication**: One-to-one, workshops, meetings.
- **Community engagement**: Dialogue in schools, churches, etc.
- **Mass media**: Broad awareness and credibility.
- **Digital media**: Facebook and SMS for rapid dissemination.
- **IEC materials**: Fliers, FAQs, and spot messages.
- **Capacity Building**: Trainings for responders and stakeholders.
- **Active coordination**: Information sharing among partners.
- **Accessible public information**: Crisis centres and response lines.

## 3.8. Communication Tools
1. **Mass Media**: Press conferences, releases, interviews.
2. **Digital/Social Media**: SMS, Facebook, Website, WhatsApp, Instagram.
3. **Print/IEC Materials**: Guidelines, posters, fliers, murals.
4. **Internal Communication**: Teleconferences, IDSR meetings, emails.
5. **Information Points**: Health facility corners, Crisis Information Center, phone line (141).
6. **Trainings**: Media houses, spokespeople, community influencers, health workers.
7. **Community Engagement**: Door-to-door sensitization, mass gathering briefings.
8. **Partnerships**: Red Cross, SEPS, District Councils, Interfaith Council.
9. **Media Monitoring**: Logging public concerns and monitoring all publications.

## 3.12.1. Establishment of a Crisis Information Center
Located through DRDM, this center will pre-empt rumours, monitor local and international media (SBC, CNN, BBC, etc.), and serve as an internal communication mechanism.

## 3.12.2. Communication focal points
Identifying focal points ensures consistent information flow and provides a direct line between the Ministry of Health and partners.

## 3.12.3. National spokespeople
Designation of a lead and alternate spokesperson for the Ministry of Health to ensure a credible and official source of information.

## 3.13 Communication Activity Plan
A dynamic plan modified based on the progression of the outbreak and available resources. Detailed in Annex 1 and 2.

## 3.14 Contingency Crisis Communication Planning
Scenarios for worst-case events, including priority access to national media, bull horns, and legal action against misinformation.
