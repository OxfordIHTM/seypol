## 1. Introduction

Nutrition is recognized as a key determinant of health and well-being. The 2012 World Health Assembly adopted a comprehensive implementation plan on maternal, infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) with six global targets for 2025.

### 1.1 Background and Purpose
Action 5 of the MIYCN plan calls for a monitoring framework. The **Global nutrition monitoring framework (GNMF)** consists of 20 core indicators: six primary outcomes, five intermediate outcomes, six process indicators, and three policy environment indicators.

### 1.2 Data Sources for the Report
Main sources include WHO and UN agency databases (DHS, MICS, SMART) and the WHO Nutrition Landscape Information System (NLiS).

### 1.3 Data Extraction
Data were captured using an Excel template and cross-checked with UNICEF/WHO/World Bank estimates, then verified by WHO country offices.

## 2. Nutrition Status in the WHO African Region

### 2.2 Primary Outcome Indicators

#### 2.2.1 Stunting
*   **Target:** 40% reduction in number of children under five who are stunted.
*   **Status:** Median prevalence is 31.3%. 10 countries have very high prevalence (≥40%).

#### 2.2.2 Anaemia
*   **Target:** 50% reduction in women of reproductive age.
*   **Status:** Median prevalence in pregnant women is 47.3%; in non-pregnant women, it is 39.8%.

#### 2.2.3 Low Birth Weight (LBW)
*   **Target:** 30% reduction.
*   **Status:** Median prevalence is 11.4%.

#### 2.2.4 Childhood Overweight
*   **Target:** No increase.
*   **Status:** Median prevalence is 4.1%, but numbers are increasing.

#### 2.2.5 Exclusive Breastfeeding
*   **Target:** Increase to at least 50%.
*   **Status:** Median rate is 41.9%. Only 35% of countries have reached the 50% target.

#### 2.2.6 Wasting
*   **Target:** Maintain at less than 5%.
*   **Status:** Median prevalence is 6.3%. 17 countries meet the target.

### 2.3 Intermediate Outcome Indicators
Includes diarrhoea incidence (median 16.0%), maternal underweight (median 10.9%), adolescent fertility (median 106 per 1000), and overweight/obesity in women (median 23.8%).

### 2.4 Process Indicators
Includes minimum acceptable diet (median 8.6%), access to safe water (median 73.9%), and safe sanitation (median 21.1%).

### 2.5 Policy Environment and Capacity Indicators
Focuses on the ratio of nurses/midwives per 1000 population (median 0.60) and the implementation of the International Code of Marketing of Breast-milk Substitutes.

## 4. Policy Environment

### 4.1 Status of Nutrition-related Policies
Analysis of 194 documents: 80 comprehensive nutrition policies, 27 topic-specific plans, 10 NCD plans, 41 health plans, 13 agriculture plans, and 23 development plans.

### 4.2 Global Nutrition and NCD Targets
Indicators most integrated in policies: Exclusive breastfeeding (38 countries), wasting/stunting (37), and LBW (30).

---

## Conclusion

Undernutrition persists in the WHO African Region. There is a critical need for upgraded surveillance systems, real-time data, and multisectoral coordination to achieve the 2025 global targets.
