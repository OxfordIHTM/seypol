## Day 2 - Capacity Building

### Session 1: Innovation and Innovation Policy

The objective was to develop a common understanding of innovation and the centrality of firms, understand the key challenge of uncertainty as a driver for STI policy, the shift from linear innovation
Thinking to innovation systems concepts, and common challenges faced by developing countries implementing STI policies. Firms and entrepreneurship are central elements in the exercise.

* Definition of and introduction to innovation and innovation policy
* Definition of innovation and invention

### Session 2: Creative destruction, disruption innovation and the role of change

The goal was to bring together three themes that are common in policy discussions on innovation – Schumpeter's creative destruction, Christensen’s disruptive innovation, and societal change, on STI policy challenges and opportunities

* Definition of creative destruction, disruptive innovation and societal change.
* Group work: creative destruction, disruptive innovation and societal change.

### Session 3: National systems of innovation - NSI: their evolution and role in STI policy, the challenges and opportunities

The objective was to broaden the discussion on national systems of innovation by considering the key challenges and opportunities for policymakers embracing an NSI approach and therefore to prepare participants for taking up the case study exercise on innovation policy in Norland.

* Definition of National Innovation System (NIS).
* Overview of NIS models used by different countries and their impact/effectiveness. Information gatekeepers (located at each point of interaction), can hinder interaction between stakeholders. NIS provides a framework to develop the STI policy. It goes beyond market failure problems and moves STI past funding of R&D as a priority goal – opens more policy space.

## Day 3 - Capacity Building

### Session 4: Entrepreneurship and Innovation

The objective of this session was to reinforce the understanding of innovation as an entrepreneurial activity and to highlight the role of innovation in addressing sustainable development challenges. The session discussed policy and public sector support and financing to start-ups innovative firms and entrepreneurs.

* Group discussion: discuss and present 4 case studies on entrepreneurship and innovation.

### Session 5: Technology transfer

STI-led development in developing countries is strongly influenced by their ability to access, adapt and diffuse technology. The session will review concepts underlying technology and knowledge transfer theory and practice and present some concerns that may inform the policy choices of developing countries. The discussion will address definitions, transfer channels, IP and technology transfer as an innovation process.

* Definitions, channels, IP, and innovation on technology transfer
* Exercise – case study: Onion oil production in Norland
* Final reflections – handouts of certificates

# Implications for the health sector

Overall, a science innovation and technology policy can foster transformative advancements in the health sector, leading to improved patient outcomes, enhanced healthcare delivery, and more efficient public health systems. However, it is crucial to address ethical, legal, and regulatory aspects to ensure responsible development and equitable access to these innovations.

* **Advancing Medical Research:** A science and technology policy can accelerate advancements in understanding diseases, discovering new treatments, and developing innovative medical technologies.
* **Improved Healthcare Delivery:** Technology-driven innovations can enhance healthcare delivery by improving access, efficiency, and quality of care. Digital health platforms and mobile applications can help patients manage their health, access medical information, and receive personalized care.
* **Enhanced Disease Surveillance and Prevention:** Science and technology policies can support the development of advanced disease surveillance systems. These systems can employ data analytics, artificial intelligence, and machine learning to detect disease outbreaks early, track the spread of infectious diseases, and facilitate targeted interventions. This proactive approach can help prevent the rapid escalation of epidemics and pandemics.
* **Health Information Systems and Data Analytics:** An effective science and technology policy can support the development of robust health information systems and data analytics capabilities. This enables the collection, integration, and analysis of health data, leading to evidence-based decision-making, improved public health strategies, and more efficient resource allocation. Data-driven approaches can also help identify trends, patterns, and risk factors, enabling proactive interventions and health promotion initiatives.
* **Partnership:** The policy may encourage collaboration between researchers, universities, and private sector entities, fostering a more robust research ecosystem.

## Way forward

* MOH should continue to consult with MIEI on the STI Policy to ensure policy coherence.
* MOH should continue to assess the maturity and capacity of its digital health systems to identify key opportunities to implement and scale-up standard-based digital health solutions that can contribute to strengthened digital transformation.
* MOH should continue to implement the eHIS.
* MOH should continue to integrate STIs in the health system to have better patient care, improved health outcomes and the overall advancement of healthcare practices.
