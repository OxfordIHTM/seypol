# DATA PROTECTION BILL, 2023
**(Bill No 9 of 2023 / Bill No 19 of 2023)**

## OBJECTS AND REASONS

The object of this Bill is to provide for the protection of individuals with regards to the processing of personal data and to recognise the right to privacy envisaged in Article 20 of the Constitution.

The Bill seeks to strengthen the control and personal autonomy of data subjects over their personal data in line with current relevant international standards and best practice.

The Bill also seeks to promote and facilitate responsible and transparent flow of information by private and public entities while ensuring respect to individual's privacy.

# A BILL
**FOR**

AN ACT FOR THE PROTECTION OF INDIVIDUALS WITH REGARDS TO THE PROCESSING OF PERSONAL DATA, TO RECOGNISE THE RIGHT TO PRIVACY ENVISAGED IN ARTICLE 20 OF THE CONSTITUTION, TO PROMOTE AND FACILITATE RESPONSIBLE AND TRANSPARENT FLOW OF INFORMATION BY PRIVATE AND PUBLIC ENTITIES AND TO PROVIDE FOR OTHER RELATED MATTERS.

*ENACTED by the President and the National Assembly.*

## PART I - PRELIMINARY

### Short title
**1.** This Act may be cited as the **Data Protection Act, 2023**.

### Interpretation
**2.** In this Act unless context otherwise requires —

*   **“biometric data”** means personal data resulting from specific technical processing relating to the physical, physiological or behavioral characteristics of a natural person, which allows or confirms the unique identification of that natural person;
*   **“Chief Executive Officer”** means the Chief Executive Officer appointed under section 41 of the Access to Information Act;
*   **“Commission”** means the Information Commission established under section 36 of the Access to Information Act;
*   **“consent”** means an informed, freely given and unambiguous agreement to the processing of personal information by the data subject;
*   **“data controller”** means an individual or legal entity which alone, or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data and has decision making power with respect to the processing;
*   **“data processor”** means any natural or legal person who processes personal data for or on behalf of the data controller;
*   **“data protection impact assessment”** means a tool to identify and assess privacy risks arising from the development life cycle of a program or system;
*   **“data subject”** means any natural person whose personal data is being collected, processed, stored or further distributed;
*   **“designated officers”** means officers or individuals tasked by the Commission to conduct an investigation or audit;
*   **“genetics”** means personal data relating to the inherited or acquired genetic characteristics of a natural person which give unique information about the physiology or the health of that natural person and which result, in particular, from an analysis of a biological sample from the natural person in question;
*   **“Minister”** means the Minister responsible for information;
*   **“personal data”** means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person;
*   **“processing”** means an operation involving collection, recording, structuring or storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation or use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment or combination, or restriction, erasure or destruction;
*   **“personal data breach”** means an unauthorized access and retrieval of personal information by an individual, group of persons, or software system or a breach of security leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of or access to personal data transmitted, stored or otherwise processed;
*   **“profiling”** means automated processing of personal data consisting of the use of personal data to evaluate certain personal aspects relating to a natural person, and in particular to analyse or predict aspects concerning that natural person's performance at work, economic situation, health, preferences, interests, reliability, location or movements;
*   **“sensitive personal data”** means personal data related to the most private areas of the data subject's life, or whose misuse might lead to discrimination or involve a serious risk for the data subject;
*   **“third party”** means a natural or legal person, public authority, agency or body that is authorised to process personal data; and
*   **“traffic data”** means any computer data relating to a communication by means of a computer system generated by a computer system that formed a part in the chain of communication, indicating communication's origin, destination, route, time, date, size, duration, or type of underlying service.

### Application
**3.** This Act applies to the processing of data through automatic or semi-automatic means, and to the processing of data through non-automatic means within Seychelles, which data forms part of the filing system whether managed by a private or public data controller.

### Exemptions
**4.** This Act does not apply to —
(a) the processing of personal data by relevant authorities in the course of a criminal investigation;
(b) matters that pertain to national security; or
(c) processing of personal data by a natural person for a personal activity.

## PART II - INFORMATION COMMISSION

### Information Commission
**5.** 
(1) The Information Commission is the competent authority to enforce and implement this Act.
(2) Any function of the Commission under this Act and under any regulations made under this Act may, to the extent authorised by the Commission be performed by any officer of the Commission.
(3) In order to implement this Act, the Commission shall appoint its own officers and staff who may be public servants, consultants or such other persons as the Commission may consider necessary.

### Powers and duties of the Commission
**6.** 
(1) The Powers and duties of the Commission under this Act include, among others —
(a) enforcement of this Act and any related regulations;
(b) promoting public awareness;
(c) handling complaints from data subjects;
(d) conducting investigations;
(e) imposing fines; and
(f) perform such other functions as may be conferred upon it under any other written law.

(2) Notwithstanding the generality of subsection (1), the Commission shall —
(a) issue and adopt the rules and procedures of the Commission...
(b) determine the nature, process and undertakings necessary to enable the Commission to discharge its mandate...
(c) promote awareness of data controllers and data processors of their obligations...
(d) promote self-regulation among data controllers and data processors...
(e) exercise control on all data processing operations...
(f) promote public awareness, including of children and the youth...
(g) provide information to data subjects on the exercise of their rights and remedies...
(h) advise other public institutions on measures and tools to protect personal data;
(i) issue opinions to the National Assembly or the Government...
(j) undertake research and monitor relevant developments...
(k) examine any proposal for automated decision making or data linkage...
(l) cooperate with supervisory authorities of other countries...

(3) The Commission shall in consultation with the relevant public and private sector stakeholders issue guidelines to further facilitate the implementation of this Act.

### Investigations and audits
**7.** 
(1) The Commission has the powers of investigation and audit for complaints received by a data subject in relation to offences under this Act.
(2) The power of the Commission under subsection (1) includes the power to investigate complaints or information that an offence may have been, is being or is about to be, committed under this Act.
(3) In the performance of the duties under this section, the designated officers, shall have the authority of a law enforcement agent.
(4) For the purposes of an investigation under this section, the designated officers shall have the right to obtain the information needed to carry out their duties...
(5) The right to obtain information under subsection (4) includes the right of the designated officers to issue an order to —
(a) request the receipt or inspection of any required documentation...
(b) obtain a copy of any documents or information...
(c) inspect equipment, systems or place used to process the data...
(d) access the premises of an investigated party...
(e) order a person to attend... for the purpose of being examined orally...
(f) order a person to furnish a statement in writing made under oath...
(6) A person whom an order has been served under this section shall comply with the order.
(7) Any person who fails to comply with an order under this section commits an offence and shall be liable to an administrative fine not exceeding SCR200,000.
(8) Subject to this section, the Commission shall regulate the handling of complaints, investigations and conduct of hearings...
(9) Where the Commission is unable to arrange... the amicable resolution... the Commission shall notify in writing the individual who made the complaint...
(10) The Commission shall plan pre-emptive audits to address data processing in any given sector...
(11) If the investigation or audit is performed pursuant to a complaint, the Commission shall keep the complainant informed of the results.

### Enforcement notice and corrective powers
**8.** 
(1) If the Commission has information or receives a complaint that a data processor or data controller is contravening this Act, the Commission may... serve that person with a written enforcement notice...
(2) An enforcement notice under subsection (1) shall —
(a) contain a statement of the provision of this Act that has been contravened;
(b) specify the measures that shall be taken to remedy the situation;
(c) specify a period which shall not be less than 21 days...
(d) state that a right of appeal is available under section 13...
(3) If by reason of special circumstances... the Commission may include a statement [of urgency]...
(4) On complying with an enforcement notice, the relevant data controller or data processor shall... notify the data subject concerned and any person to whom the data was disclosed...
(5) Where the Commission considers that any provision... may not be complied with... the Commission may vary the notice...
(6) The Commission may cancel an enforcement notice by written notification...
(7) Any person who fails to comply with an enforcement notice commits an offence and shall be liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding level 5 on the Standard Scale.
(8) In addition, the Commission shall have the power to —
(a) issue reprimands...
(b) order the data controller or data processor to comply with the data subject's requests...
(c) order the data controller to communicate a personal data breach to the data subject;
(d) impose a temporary or definitive limitation including a ban on processing;
(e) order the rectification or erasure of personal data...
(f) impose an administrative fine...
(g) order the suspension of data flows to a recipient in another country...

### Preliminary injunction
**9.** 
(1) The Commission may apply to a court for a preliminary injunction to preserve data...
(2) Where the court is satisfied... the court shall issue the injunction specifying a period for preservation which shall not be more than 90 days...
(3) The court may... extend the 90 day period...

### Disclosure
**10.** 
(1) The Commission and every person acting on behalf... shall be bound by the duty of secrecy...
(2) The Commission may disclose... information —
(a) that is necessary to carry out an investigation or establish grounds for findings;
(b) in the course of a prosecution for an offence or an appeal.

### Liability
**11.** No criminal or civil proceedings shall be pursued against the Commission... for anything done, reported or said in good faith...

### Powers and duties of the Chief Executive Officer
**12.** 
(1) Subject to the direction... of the Commission, the Chief Executive Officer shall —
(a) keep internal records and publicize infringements...
(b) fulfil any other tasks related to the protection of personal data...
(c) bring infringements of this Act to the attention of the judicial authorities...
(2) The Chief Executive Officer shall produce an annual general report... and publish it in the Gazette.
(3) The Chief Executive Officer may produce other reports relating to the Commission's functions...

### Right of appeal
**13.** Any person aggrieved by a decision of the Commission... may, within 21 working days... appeal to the courts.

### Cooperation
**14.** The Commission has the right to obtain, on request, assistance from any other public bodies as well as natural or legal persons...

## PART III - DATA PROTECTION

### Processing personal data
**15.** 
(1) Every data controller and data processor shall adhere to the principles... for processing data quality, purpose limitation, use and further disclosure limitation, transparency, data subject's participation, proportionality and accountability.
(2) Personal data shall be processed if —
(a) the data subject has provided consent;
(b) necessary for the performance of contractual obligations;
(c) a specific law requires the processing;
(d) necessary to protect vital interests;
(e) conducted in the context of public interest;
(f) responds to the legitimate interests of the data controller or a third party;
(g) necessary for the administration of justice or public function in a state of emergency.
(3) Where consent is required... consent shall be freely given, explicit and informed.
(4) Data controllers shall develop policies... that enable the data subject to provide consent in an intelligible and user-friendly manner.
(5) When combining data from different sources... data controllers shall develop a layered approach to consent...
(6) The layered approach... shall not apply when limiting data items can affect the purpose of the processing or when categories are complementary.
(7) Data controllers shall provide data subjects all necessary information... to allow for informed consent.
(8) Data subjects may withdraw their consent... at any time.
(9) Data controllers shall be able to provide evidence of data subject consent.
(10) For credit reporting systems, the credit information laws... shall regulate the specific circumstances for consent.
(11) Data controllers shall not... require a data subject to consent to the processing of personal data beyond that required to fulfil the specified and legal purposes.
(12) Where consent is not required... a data controller shall still provide sufficient information...
(13) When data is not obtained directly from the data subject, a data controller shall inform the data subject about the processing by a third party.
(14) In certain circumstances personal information may be collected... without knowledge and consent for protecting vital interests.
(15) When data is not obtained from the data subject, such data shall remain confidential.

### Data retention
**16.** 
(1) Personal data shall be stored for the necessary period... after which it shall be anonymized or archived and if necessary erased.
(2) Data controllers shall adopt procedures... such as data masking, pseudonymization, encryption...
(3) Further retention shall be lawful where it is necessary for freedom of expression, legal obligation, public interest, archiving, scientific/historical research or legal claims.
(4) The Minister may issue additional regulations related to specific data retention periods.

### Data minimization
**17.** 
(1) Personal data processed shall be kept to the minimum necessary.
(2) Every data controller shall disclose the specific purpose... unless additional legal basis for such disclosure is established.

### Data quality
**18.** 
(1) Personal data shall be processed free of error, complete and up to date.
(2) Personal data may be collected used and disclosed only for purposes —
(a) that are specific and appropriate;
(b) that the individual has been informed of under section 15.
(3) Data controllers... shall adequately inform data subjects of —
(a) the purposes for the collection of information;
(b) the name of the organisation and the procedures to follow to exercise rights.

### Access, requests and correction of data
**19.** Data Subjects shall access, request correction or deletion of data, block access to their data and oppose the usage of their data for specific purposes...

### Data security and confidentiality
**20.** 
(1) Data controllers and data processors shall implement technical, organisational and physical measures to manage security risks.
(2) Personal data processed under this Act shall be confidential.
(3) The Minister may issue additional regulations relating to specific security standards.

### Accountability for data processing activities
**21.** 
(1) Data controllers and data processors are responsible for the processing of personal data and shall be accountable.
(2) They shall implement measures that guarantee data protection and adoption of privacy by design.
(3) Data controllers shall maintain appropriate documentation and record keeping...

## PART IV - PROCESSING SPECIAL CATEGORIES OF PERSONAL DATA

### Sensitive data
**23.** 
(1) Processing of personal data relating to race, ethnic origin, biometrics, genetics, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs or for the purpose of identifying a person's health or sex life is prohibited.
(2) Subsection (1) shall not apply where —
(a) the data subject has given explicit consent;
(b) necessary for obligations in the field of employment and social security;
(c) necessary to protect vital interests where the subject is incapable of giving consent;
(d) carried out by a not-for-profit body relating solely to its members;
(e) data is manifestly made public by the data subject;
(f) necessary for the establishment, exercise or defense of legal claims;
(g) necessary for reasons of substantial public interest;
(h) necessary for medical diagnosis, preventive medicine, or health/social care;
(i) necessary for public interest in the area of public health;
(j) necessary for archiving purposes in the public interest, scientific or historical research;
(k) necessary to cover data relating to misconduct or inadequate behavior.

### Data related to children
**24.** 
(1) No person shall process the personal data of a child below the age of 18 years unless consent is given by the child's parent or legal guardian.
(2) The data controller shall obtain consent... or verify that consent has been given.

### Processing of personal data relating to offences and criminal convictions
**25.** Processing of personal data relating to criminal convictions... shall be carried out only under the control of official authority or when authorised by law.

## PART V - DATA SUBJECT'S RIGHTS

### Rights of data subject
**26.** 
(1) Rights may be exercised personally or through a representative.
(2) A holder of parental or custodian authority may exercise rights on behalf of a minor or person with mental disabilities.
(3) Information provided to the data subject must be in intelligible and easily accessible form.
(4) Information may be provided in electronic form.
(5) Information should be provided in the same form it was originally provided if practicable.
(6) For data portability, the data controller shall enable their API to be accessed by a third party.
(7) If identity is doubtful, the controller may request additional information or delay the request.
(8) Information shall be provided free of charge, unless the request is manifestly unfounded or excessive.
(9) A reasonable fee may be charged for unfounded or excessive requests.
(10) The data controller shall facilitate rights both in person and through remote access.
(11) The data subject shall have the right to lodge a complaint with the Commission.
(12) The data subject may lodge a complaint if rights have not been sufficiently responded to.
(13) The Commission shall inform the complainant on the progress and outcome.
(14) The Minister may make regulations prescribing limits to fees and timelines.

### Right to be informed
**27.** 
(1) The data controller shall give the data subject information about: legal basis, storage period (or criteria), categories of recipients, and other necessary information.

### Right to access data
**28.** 
(1) A data subject shall have the right to obtain confirmation of processing, communication of data, list of recipients (past 6 months), purposes, legal basis, categories of data, and storage periods.
(2) A data subject shall have the right to obtain: rectification, cancellation, objection to processing, data portability, and information on the right to lodge a complaint.
(3) The data controller may restrict the provision of information to avoid obstructing official inquiries, prejudicing criminal prosecutions, or protecting public/national security and the rights of others.
(4) If restricted, the data controller shall inform the data subject in writing of the reasons and the right to appeal.
(5) The data subject may request the Commission to verify if the restriction is lawful.

### Right of rectification or correction
**29.** 
(1) The data subject shall have the right to request correction of inaccurate or incomplete data.
(2) The data controller shall evaluate the request and justify any rejection.
(3) Complaints may be filed with the Commission if unsatisfied with the justification.
(4) When corrected, the controller shall inform the subject and all users who accessed the data in the previous 6 months.

### Right to cancel or delete data
**30.** 
(1) Data shall be deleted immediately if processing infringes sections 26-28 or if there is a legal obligation to delete.
(2) If data must be maintained for evidence, the controller shall restrict its processing.
(3) If accuracy is contested but not ascertainable, the controller shall restrict processing.
(4) Deletion or restriction shall be performed whether or not the subject makes a request.

### Right to object the processing of data for a specific purpose
**31.** 
(1) The data subject may restrict processing when it has served its purpose, the legal ground is no longer in force, or it is contrary to law.
(2) The Minister may make regulations regarding significant decisions based solely on automated processing.

### Right to data portability
**32.** 
(1) The data subject may request data in a structured, machine-readable format or transfer it to another controller.
(2) Systems shall be designed to enable data portability.
(3) The Commission shall provide guidance on the scope of data and security measures.
(4) The Commission shall approve consent mechanisms for portability scenarios.

### Right to compensation
**33.** 
(1) Any person suffering material or non-material damage from a contravention has a right to compensation.
(2) The data controller is generally liable; the processor is liable if they fail their specific obligations or act outside instructions.
(3) Exemption from liability if the party proves it is not responsible for the event.
(4) Where multiple parties are involved, each is liable for the entire damage to the subject.
(5) A party who has paid compensation may claim the corresponding portion from other responsible parties.
(6) Proceedings shall be brought before the competent courts of Seychelles.

## PART VI - OBLIGATIONS OF DATA CONTROLLERS AND DATA PROCESSORS

### Safe custody
**34.** 
(1) A data controller is responsible for personal data in its possession.
(2) A data controller shall designate individuals to ensure compliance.
(3) Designated individuals shall not delegate their responsibility.
(4) Business contact information of at least one designated individual shall be public.

### Transparency
**35.** 
(1) Controllers/processors shall make information publicly available and develop a privacy policy.
(2) The policy shall include: types of information, identity of controller, purposes, security measures, how to exercise rights, and retention policies.

### Privacy by design
**36.** 
(1) Controllers shall implement technical and organisational measures to integrate safeguards into processing.
(2) Implementation shall consider state of technology, cost, risk to rights, and likelihood of harm.
(3) By default, only necessary data for each specific purpose shall be processed (amount, extent, storage, accessibility).
(4) Data shall not be accessible to an indefinite number of people without intervention.
(5) Joint data controllers are those who jointly determine purposes and means.
(6) Joint controllers shall transparently determine their respective responsibilities.

### Data processors
**37.** 
(1) Controllers may only use processors who provide sufficient guarantees of technical/organisational measures.
(2) Processors shall not engage another processor without prior written authorisation.
(3) In case of general authorisation, the processor must inform the controller of any additions or replacements.
(4) Processing shall be governed by a written contract specifying subject-matter, duration, nature, purpose, data types, and rights/obligations.
(5) The contract shall provide that the processor: acts only on instructions, ensures confidentiality, assists with subject rights, deletes/returns data at the end of service, and demonstrates compliance.
(6) Transfers to third countries may only occur if instructed by the controller.
(7) If a processor determines purposes/means, they are treated as a data controller.
(8) A processor acting in their own name without disclosing they act for a controller is treated as a data controller.
(9) Processors may not process data except on instructions or to comply with a legal obligation.
(10) Processors may keep data blocked as specified under section 39.

### Records of processing activities
**38.** 
(1) Each data controller shall maintain a record of all processing activities.
(2) Data protection officers shall be informed of modifications to records.
(3) Controller records shall contain: contact details, joint controllers, DPO, purposes, recipients, data categories, profiling details, transfers, legal basis, erasure limits, and security measures.
(4) Each data processor shall maintain a record of activities carried out on behalf of a controller.
(5) Processor records shall contain: contact details, the controller's details, DPO, activities carried out, and transfers.
(6) Records shall be made available to the Commission upon request.

### Logging
**39.** 
(1) Controllers/processors shall keep logs for all processing operations.
(2) Consultation logs shall establish justification, date, time, and identity of the person.
(3) Disclosure logs shall establish justification, date, time, identity of the discloser, and identity of recipients.
(4) Logs may be used for: verifying lawfulness, self-monitoring/disciplinary proceedings, ensuring integrity/security, or criminal proceedings.
(5) Logs shall be available to the Commission upon request.

### Blocking data
**40.** 
(1) Access to data shall be blocked when subject to rectification or erasure.
(2) Blocking shall follow Part III principles, including technical measures and mechanisms for data subjects to exercise rights.

### Data protection impact assessment (DPIA)
**41.** 
(1) Where processing is likely to result in high risk, a DPIA must be carried out prior to processing.
(2) DPIA is required for: systematic/extensive profiling, large scale processing of special categories, large scale monitoring of public areas, or other circumstances specified by the Commission.
(3) A DPIA shall include: description of operations, risk assessment, measures to address risk, and safeguards.
(4) Nature, scope, context, and purposes shall be considered when determining high risk.

### Prior consultation
**42.** 
(1) Applies where a controller intends to create a filing system.
(2) Consultation with the Commission is required if a DPIA indicates high risk.
(3) The controller shall provide the DPIA and other requested information to the Commission.
(4) If the Commission finds an infringement, it shall provide written advice to the controller/processor.
(5) Advice shall be provided within 6 weeks.
(6) The period may be extended by 1 month for complexity.
(7) Extensions must be communicated with reasons before the end of the initial period.

### Security of processing
**43.** 
(1) Controllers/processors shall implement measures such as pseudonymization, encryption, ensuring confidentiality/resilience, timely restoration of access, and regular testing.
(2) Implementation shall consider state of the art, cost, and risk to the data subject.
(3) Risks of accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, or alteration shall be considered.
(4) Measures shall prevent unauthorised processing, ensure system functionality (restoration within 72 hours), and prevent data corruption.
(5) Persons with authorised access must process data in a diligent manner.

### Notification of a personal data breach to the Commission
**44.** 
(1) Controllers shall notify the Commission of a breach within 72 hours of awareness, or provide reasons for delay.
(2) Notification shall include: nature of breach, contact details of DPO, likely consequences, and mitigation measures.
(3) Information on the breach shall be recorded to enable investigation.
(4) Parties shall collaborate with the Commission and other authorities to investigate.

### Communication of a personal data breach to the data subject
**45.** 
(1) If a breach is likely to affect a significant number of individuals and their rights, the controller shall promptly inform the subject.
(2) Information shall include: nature of breach, DPO details, consequences, and mitigation measures.
(3) Notification is not required if: appropriate protection was already applied, risk is no longer likely to materialise, or it would involve disproportionate effort (replaced by public communication).
(4) The Commission may require notification if a high risk is considered.

### Data protection officer (DPO)
**46.** 
(1) A DPO shall be designated when: core activities involve large scale systematic monitoring or large scale processing of special categories/criminal data.
(2) For other activities, a DPO may be optionally designated.
(3) Designation shall consider professional qualities and expert knowledge of data protection law.
(4) The same person may be a DPO for several controllers.

### Tasks of the data protection officer
**47.** 
(1) Monitor compliance with policies and this Act.
(2) Act as the contact point for the Commission.
(3) Act as the focal point for data subject complaints and establish dispute handling mechanisms.

## PART VII - TRANSFERS OF DATA TO THIRD PARTIES

### Cross-border data flows
**48.** 
(1) Processing special categories of Seychellois citizens' data is subject to: a designated accountant in Seychelles, intra-group schemes with overseas HQ, subject notification of location, or necessity for vital interests.
(2) The Commission may specify circumstances for public interest transfers or restrictions on specific categories.
(3) The Commission may authorise transfers part of a cross-border privacy rules system that is legally enforceable and risk-based.
(4) The Commission may prohibit transfers in the public interest.

## PART VIII - OFFENCES AND PENALTIES

### Unlawful disclosure of personal data
**49.** 
(1) Controllers disclosing data incompatible with original purpose without justification commit an offence.
(2) Processors disclosing data without authority of the controller commit an offence.
(3) Anyone obtaining unauthorised access or disclosing data to unauthorised persons commits an offence.
(4) Does not apply to employees/agents acting within their mandate.
(5) Offering to sell data obtained in breach of subsection (1) is an offence.

### Obstruction
**50.** 
(1) Obstructing the Commission or its agents is prohibited.
(2) Contravention is liable to a fine not exceeding SR200,000.

### Offence for which no specific penalty provided
**51.** 
(1) General contraventions are liable to imprisonment not exceeding 2 years or a level 5 fine.
(2) The court may order forfeiture of equipment or prohibit continuing contraventions.

### General conditions for imposing administrative fines
**52.** 
(1) Fines shall be effective, proportionate and dissuasive.
(2) Fines may be in addition to or instead of corrective measures.
(3) Factors considered for fines: nature/gravity/duration, intent/negligence, mitigation efforts, degree of responsibility, previous infringements, cooperation, categories of data, how the breach became known, compliance with previous orders, adherence to codes, and financial gain/loss.
(4) For multiple infringements in one operation, the total fine shall not exceed the amount for the gravest infringement.
(5) Obligations of controller/processor infringements: fines up to SCR 200,000.
(6) Basic principles, subject rights, third country transfers, and non-compliance with orders/access are subject to fines up to SCR 200,000.
(7) Non-compliance with a corrective order: fines up to SCR 200,000.
(8) Regulations may specify if/how fines apply to public authorities.
(9) Fines are subject to procedural safeguards and judicial remedy.
(10) The Commission shall lay down rules on other penalties.

## PART IX - MISCELLANEOUS

### Cooperation with other authorities
**53.** 
(1) The Commission shall develop collaborative mechanisms with government agencies.
(2) The Commission shall cooperate with international organisations and supervisory authorities.
(3) The Commission shall provide international mutual assistance (notification, referral, investigative assistance).
(4) The Commission shall cooperate to solve jurisdictional conflicts.

### Compliance audit
**54.** The Commission may carry out periodical audits of controllers or processors.

### Regulations
**55.** The Minister, in consultation with the Commission, may make regulations for the better carrying into effect of this Act.

### Repeal
**56.** The Data Protection Act (Cap 57) is repealed.
