## Foreword

Today, rehabilitation is considered integral in universal health coverage services, which reflects the importance of the following two aspects: the multidisciplinary approach in providing rehabilitation services which guarantees a delicate process of diagnosis, treatment plan and follow up to achieve the best outcomes in terms of quality of life. The integration between the specialty of rehabilitation and other specialties to maximize the treatment outcomes and minimize time and money loss.

The Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan was one of the first countries in the region to adopt the WHO tools to develop a national rehabilitation strategy. With the support of Humanity & Inclusion (HI), a rehabilitation platform was initiated. The process entailed situation analysis (STARS), developing GRASP and FRAME and eventually the Strategy was developed.

The Strategy sheds light on the role of Ministry of Health, being pivotal in providing rehabilitation services and integrating rehabilitation services into the primary, secondary and tertiary services. Another important aspect is the adoption of regulations, practices and rules to enhance the rehabilitation governance as well as regulating assistive technology to ensure the provision of high quality rehabilitation services. Moreover ; it focuses on capacity building and training of rehabilitation professionals.

## 1. Introduction

Rehabilitation is a fundamental health service and social care centres managed by the Ministry that is relevant to people with a wide range of health conditions and disabilities, throughout all stages of the life-course, and during all phases of their care. Rehabilitation is an increasingly important health service considering the ageing populations and a rising prevalence of chronic and non-communicable diseases (NCD). Furthermore, as access to healthcare interventions expands, rehabilitation is needed to maximize their effectiveness and impact.

Currently, however, the need for rehabilitation globally greatly exceeds its availability, and this is the situation as well in Jordan. Accessible and affordable rehabilitation is necessary for achievement of Universal Health Coverage goals as well as the Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 3, ‘Ensure healthy lives and promote well-being for all at all ages. Jordan is committed to achieve Universal health Coverage by 2030.

The Ministry of Health, Jordan, has led the development of rehabilitation for more than 35 years and the country has quite a diverse and established workforce. Together, with the Royal Medical Services and the private sector, they deliver most of the rehabilitation in Jordan. Two University hospitals also provide rehabilitation; Jordan University Hospital and King Abdullah University Hospital. In addition to the health sector, rehabilitation is provided in disability and social care centres managed by the Ministry of Social Development and to a small extent in the educational system under the Ministry of Education’s supervision. Furthermore, the non-governmental sector is providing rehabilitation and assistive products, emphasizing on filling the gap at community level, including 10 community-based rehabilitation centres across the Palestinian refugee camps in Jordan.

In 2018, after concerted actions of key actors working in the rehabilitation sector in Jordan, the Ministry of Health agreed to conduct a comprehensive assessment of rehabilitation in the health sector. WHO and Humanity & Inclusion assisted this process which coincided with WHO’s development of the ‘Rehabilitation in Health Systems – Guide for Action’ package. Jordan thus became one among the first countries to implement the Guide for action – systematic assessment of rehabilitation situation (STARS) between May and September 2018, followed by a report that was approved by the Ministry of Health beginning of 2019.

In July 2019, a strategic planning workshop was successfully conducted, and the first draft strategic plan was developed. The draft plan underwent consultation and during a consensus workshop in November 2019, the strategic plan and its monitoring and evaluation framework was discussed and finalised.

## 2. Rehabilitation

### 2.1 Rehabilitation concepts that inform Jordan rehabilitation strategic plan

Rehabilitation is about functioning. Rehabilitation addresses the impact of a health condition on the person’s life by improving and maintaining functioning and reducing the experience of disability. It does this through a strong emphasis on educating and empowering people to manage their health conditions, adapt to their situation and remain active. Rehabilitation improves people’s quality of life.

During rehabilitation, the person identifies his/her needs and goals and the rehabilitation interventions are then tailored considering the individual and the environment.

Rehabilitation is for all the population. There is an extensive array of health conditions that benefit from rehabilitation. It is relevant during all phases of care and treatment, regardless of age.

Rehabilitation is highly person-centred, individualised, and strongly involves the user. It is goal orientated, time-bound and outcome focused.

Rehabilitation engages the person in the treatment process and, where appropriate, the family, and carers. It requires a multi-professional workforce including physiatrists, physiotherapists, occupational therapists, speech and language therapists, prosthetists and orthotists, psychologists and rehabilitation nurses.

### 2.2 Rehabilitation needs

The need for rehabilitation is large and growing due to: 
* **Demographic shifts:** Increasing number of elderly people.
* **Disease Burden:** Rise in Non-communicable diseases (NCDs) and injuries.
* **Conflicts:** Jordan hosts approximately 700,000 to 1.5 million refugees from Iraq, Yemen, and Syria.
* **Traffic Accidents:** 13.5% of all injuries lead to permanent disabilities.
* **Disability Prevalence:** Estimated at 11.2% for the population aged five years and above.

### 2.3 The value of rehabilitation
* **Better Health Outcomes:** Restores function and enables better well-being.
* **Economic Savings:** Reductions in re-admission rates and allows people to return to work/education.
* **Human Rights:** Supports the realization of the right to health for persons with disabilities.

## 3. Rehabilitation situation in Jordan

### 3.1 Rehabilitation in Jordan
* **Service Delivery:** Mostly delivered by MoH and Royal Medical Services (RMS) hospitals, alongside University hospitals.
* **Governance:** Leadership structures are still to be defined. There is limited inter-sectoral coordination.
* **Workforce:** Good technical capacity via university training, but issues with professional status (categorized as "technicians"), motivation, and retention.
* **Access:** Reasonable in urban areas but not integrated into primary healthcare. Lack of rehabilitation wards prevents complex care.
* **Assistive Products:** Provision is limited, uncoordinated, and often unaffordable for the general public.

### 3.2 Linkages between the rehabilitation strategic plan and other policies
Aligned with:
* National Strategy for Health Sector in Jordan (2016-2020).
* Law no 20 (2017) on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities.
* Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD).
* Jordan 2025: A National Vision and Strategy.

### 3.3 Priorities for strengthening rehabilitation
1. **Establish stronger leadership and planning** at the national level.
2. **Expand the availability of rehabilitation** across all levels (Primary to Tertiary).
3. **Increase government support for assistive products** and cross-ministerial coordination.
4. **Expand the workforce** and strengthen professionals’ status.
5. **Improve quality** and use of evidence-based practice.
6. **Expand specialized rehabilitation**, including mental health.
7. **Increase information and generation of data** (Hakeem system integration).

### 3.4 Framework for the future development of rehabilitation in Jordan

| Type | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| Specialised high-intensity | In-patient stay facilities, rehabilitation centres, specialized beds |
| Integrated Tertiary/Secondary | Integrated across specialties (orthopaedics, neurology, etc.) in general hospitals |
| Integrated Primary | Delivery in Comprehensive Health Centres (CHCs) |
| Community delivered | Educational facilities, day care centres, NGO run centres |
| Informal/Self-managed | Combined efforts of person, family, and peers |

## 4. Strategic Plan 2020-2024

* **Vision:** All people in Jordan enjoy quality of life and function to their fullest and effectively participate to the sustainable development of Jordan.
* **Goal:** To increase access for all Jordanians to equitable and quality rehabilitation services, delivered by qualified multi-disciplinary professional teams.

### Strategic Objectives
1. Enhance leadership and effective governance for rehabilitation.
2. Increase the availability of reliable data on rehabilitation at national and governorate levels.
3. Integrate quality rehabilitation services in all health care levels and in the community.
4. Expand the development of specialized rehabilitation.
5. Expand and strengthen a comprehensive rehabilitation workforce.
6. Increased availability and quality of assistive products with appropriate provision at an affordable cost.

### Strategic Action Matrix (Summary)

| Objective | Key Actions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Governance** | Define leadership structure, create Technical Advisory Group (TAG), establish monitoring framework. |
| **2. Data** | Identify key indicators, improve Hakeem usage, encourage rehabilitation research. |
| **3. Integration** | Pilot rehab in 5 CHCs, train family doctors, improve referral systems, expansion in secondary hospitals. |
| **4. Specialization** | Plan for National Rehabilitation Centre of Excellence, develop mental health services. |
| **5. Workforce** | Assess workforce gaps, develop competency frameworks, improve attraction/retention, continuous education. |
| **6. Assistive Products** | Cross-ministerial working group, prioritize capital investment for P&O in the south, develop priority list. |

### 5.1 Monitoring, Evaluation, and Review
* **Process:** Annual evaluation meetings to assess operational plans.
* **Structure:** Mid-term review after 3 years and final external review at completion.

### 5.2 Monitoring Framework (Indicators)

| Objective | Key Indicator |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1. Governance | MoH rehabilitation expenditure, Number of steering committee meetings. |
| 2. Data | % of hospitals reporting quarterly, Number of published research projects. |
| 3. Integration | Number of rehab beds, % of general hospitals providing rehab, Number of CHCs with PT/OT. |
| 4. Specialization | Number of tertiary hospitals with 3+ rehab professions. |
| 5. Workforce | Number of rehab personnel in MoH, Number of annual graduates/residents. |
| 6. Assistive Products | Number of working group meetings, Waiting time for P&O products. |

### 5.3 Implementation Process
Follows a **"Plan $\rightarrow$ Do $\rightarrow$ Evaluate"** cycle.
* **Plan:** Joint annual strategic planning meeting.
* **Do:** Implementation by MoH and contributing actors.
* **Evaluate:** Joint annual evaluation meeting.
