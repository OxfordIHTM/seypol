## Preface

Nutrient-based dietary guidelines has been found to be ineffective in promoting and establishing healthy eating habits of the target population. Food-based dietary guidelines have now taken precedence as a means of ensuring the individual attains a high nutritional status.

The new food-based dietary guidelines for Seychelles consist of a series of statements derived from scientific evidence into simple layman terms. The main burden in Seychelles over the last few decades is over-nutrition and its related co-morbidities, mainly **non communicable diseases (NCDs)**.

**Key Statistics:**
* Seychelles has the highest rate of overweight and obesity in Africa.
* 57% of men and 72% of women were either overweight or obese in 2013.
* In 2016, 20% of boys and 24% of girls were overweight.

## Introduction

The current food based dietary guidelines are a series of evidence-based statements aimed at promoting the consumption and enjoyment of healthy, local foods. 

**The overarching principles of these guidelines:**
1. Encourage local and minimally processed foods
2. Promotion of a predominantly plant-based diet
3. Establish healthy eating habits by understanding social, economic, psychological and environmental factors.
4. Overcome barriers that prevent healthy dietary patterns (cooking skills, social events, advertising, cost)
5. Creation of meals from food ingredients portraying local culinary practices

## The set of Guidelines

### Guidelines for the general population above 5 years
1. Enjoy a variety of foods from different food groups every day
2. Eat plenty of fruits, vegetables and pulses (beans, lentils and peas) of different colours every day
3. Choose more whole grains, local tubers and other high-fibre starchy foods instead of rice
4. Include more fish, including oily fish like fresh tuna, bonito and mackerel instead of fatty and processed meats
5. Include milk & milk products every day
6. Choose water as your preferred drink to have every day instead of sugar-sweetened beverages
7. Reduce salt and salty foods such as sauces, canned foods and processed meat
8. Remain physically active on most days of the week
9. If you choose to drink alcohol do so within the recommended limits

### Guidelines for infants and young children (0 – 5 years)
1. Give your child only breast milk for the first 6 months of life
2. Introduce safe and nutrient-rich foods & plain water to complement breastfeeding from 6 months
3. Give your child water instead of sugar-sweetened beverages from 6 months
4. Allow your child to enjoy a variety of foods from different food groups
5. Give young children whole, fresh and local foods instead of processed foods
6. Give young children Iron- and Vitamin A-rich foods
7. Above 12 months children can consume other types of milk
8. Children should remain physically active throughout the day

### Guidelines for pregnant and lactating women
1. Eat plenty of fruits, vegetables and pulses every day
2. Choose more whole grains, local tubers and other high-fibre starchy foods
3. Eat a variety of fish
4. Include iron-rich foods daily to prevent anaemia
5. Include pasteurized milk and milk products every day
6. Reduce intake of salt and salty foods
7. Folic acid supplementation is compulsory in the first three months of pregnancy
8. Alcohol is unsafe during pregnancy & lactation
9. Remain physically active but avoid strenuous exercise
10. Choose water over caffeinated and sugar-sweetened beverages

# Section 1: Guidelines for the general population above 5 years

### 1. Enjoy a variety of foods from different food groups every day
No single food contains all essential nutrients. A balanced diet should include a variety of foods. The **Seychelles Food Guide (SFG)** depicts six food groups. Over half of the plate should be plant-based foods.

### 2. Eat plenty of fruits, vegetables and pulses every day
Fruits, vegetables, and pulses are packed with phytonutrients and fibre. WHO recommends a minimum of **400 grams** (5 portions) a day.

**What is a portion? (approx. 80g):**
* 1 medium fruit (banana, orange)
* 2-3 small fruits (grapes, plums)
* 2 tablespoons of raw or cooked vegetables
* 1 cup of green leafy vegetables

### 3. Choose more whole grains, local tubers and other high-fibre starchy foods instead of rice
Whole grains contain the bran, germ, and endosperm, providing more nutrients than refined grains. Replace white rice with cassava, sweet potato, yam, and taro.

### 4. Include more fish instead of fatty and processed meats
Processed meat is linked to certain forms of cancer. Oily fish (tuna, bonito, mackerel) provide Omega-3, which lowers the risk of heart disease.

### 5. Include milk & milk products every day
These provide calcium, protein, and vitamins. 

**Table 1: Calcium recommendation by age group**

| Age group | Calcium recommendations | Servings of milk/milk products |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1-3 years | 350mg/day | 1 1/2 |
| 4-6 years | 450mg/day | 1 1/2 |
| 7-10 years | 550mg/day | 2 |
| 11-18 years | 1000mg/day | 3 1/2 |
| Adult over 19 years | 700mg/day | 2 ½ |
| Pregnancy | No increment | Depends on age |
| Lactation | +550mg/day | +2 extra servings |

### 6. Choose water as your preferred drink
An average adult requires a minimum of 2.5 litres of fluids daily, with 1.5 litres being plain water.

### 7. Reduce salt and salty foods
WHO recommends adults consume $\le 5$g of salt per day. 

**Salt Guide:**
* **HIGH Salt:** $\ge 1.25$g salt per 100g (0.5g sodium)
* **LOW Salt:** $\le 0.25$g salt per 100g (0.1g sodium)

### 8. Remain physically active
Moderate physical activity is recommended for at least **30 minutes per day**. 

**BMI Formula:** $\text{Weight (kg)} / (\text{Height (m)}^2)$
* Underweight: $< 18.5$
* Normal: $18.5 - 24.9$
* Overweight: $25.0 - 29.9$
* Obese: $\ge 30.0$

### 9. Alcohol limits
* Max 2 units per day / 14 units per week.
* Abstain at least 3 days per week.

**Table 2: Alcohol Units and Calories**

| Type of drink | Units | Calories |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Small shot spirits (25ml) | 1 | 50 |
| Alcopop (275ml) | 1.5 | 228 |
| Glass of wine (125ml) | 1.5 | 85-95 |
| Heineken (250ml) | 1.3 | 105 |
| SeyBrew (280ml) | 1.4 | 150 |
| Guinness (300ml) | 2.3 | 192 |

# Section 2: Guidelines for Infants and young children (0 – 5 years)

### 1. Exclusive Breastfeeding
Exclusive breastfeeding for the first 6 months provides ideal nutrition and the baby's first immunization.

### 2. Complementary Feeding
Starting at 6 months, introduce safe, nutrient-rich foods while continuing to breastfeed.

**Table 3: Feeding Guidance (6-23 months)**

| Age (months) | Texture | Frequency | Amount per meal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 6–8 | Thick mashed/pureed | 2–3 meals + snacks | 2-3 tbsp to 1/2 cup |
| 9–11 | Finely chopped/mashed | 3-4 meals + snacks | 1/2 cup/bowl |
| 12–23 | Family foods | 3–4 meals + snacks | 3/4 to full cup/bowl |

### 3. Water vs Sugar-sweetened beverages
Avoid juices and sodas to prevent dental caries and obesity. Use boiled and cooled water for infants under 12 months.

### 4. Variety of Foods
Responsive feeding is key: feed slowly, patiently, and avoid distractions.

### 5. Whole, Fresh, and Local Foods
Avoid tinned vegetables, sugary cereals, and processed meats (nuggets, sausages).

### 6. Iron and Vitamin A
Crucial for mental and motor development. Sources: liver, eggs, dark green leafy vegetables, and fortified cereals.

### 7. Milk after 12 Months
Pasteurized full cream milk can be introduced. Avoid soy or almond milk as primary replacements before 2 years.

### 8. Physical Activity
Children above 12 months should have at least 3 hours of activity daily. Limit screen time to $< 1$ hour for ages 2-5, and zero for under 2.

# Section 3: Guidelines for pregnant and lactating women

* **Fruits, Veg, Pulses:** Essential for collagen production and fetal development.
* **Whole Grains:** Prevent constipation (common due to progesterone).
* **Fish:** Source of DHA/Omega-3 for brain and eye development.
* **Iron:** Prevent anaemia; combine non-haem iron with Vitamin C for better absorption.
* **Pasteurized Milk:** Essential for baby's bone mineralisation. **Avoid unpasteurized milk** to prevent Listeriosis.
* **Salt:** Reduce to avoid high blood pressure/stroke risk.
* **Folic Acid:** Compulsory 400µg/day in the first 3 months to prevent Neural Tube Defects.
* **Alcohol:** Absolutely unsafe (Foetal Alcohol Syndrome).
* **Activity:** 30 mins/day; avoid strenuous exercise.
* **Hydration:** 2 litres/day (pregnancy), 3 litres/day (lactation).

# Section 4: The Seychelles Food Guide

**Rationale for revisions:**
* **Oils & Healthy Fats:** Removed "Sugar" from the group name to avoid promoting sugar intake.
* **Separate Fruits & Veg:** To encourage higher vegetable intake (3 portions veg, 2 portions fruit).
* **Grains, Tubers, Breadfruit, Plantains:** Reduced plate proportion from 1/3 to 1/4 to fight obesity.
* **Fish, Meat, Eggs, and Plant-based Alternatives:** Clarified the inclusion of eggs and legumes.

* **Colostrum:** thick, yellow fluid high in antibodies (first 2-3 days).

### Appendix 6: Sugar Implications
* **Free Sugars:** Should be $< 10\%$ of total energy intake ($\approx 12$ teaspoons/day). Optimal is $< 5\%$ ($\approx 6$ teaspoons).
* **Calculation:** 1 teaspoon $\approx 4$ grams of sugar.
