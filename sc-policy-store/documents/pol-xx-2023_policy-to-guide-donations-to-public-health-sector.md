## 1 INTRODUCTION

Global health inequalities impact access to healthcare in most low-and middle-income countries (LMICs), as well as affecting deprived population groups and/or communities in high-income countries (HICs). These may often mean shortages of the necessary health equipment and health-related supplies. The health sectors of many developing nations are assisted by more economically developed nations or international organisations providing aid, through donations of medical equipment, supplies, or technical assistance in a range of health-related aspects.

Whilst donations of such medical supplies are greatly welcomed and appreciated by the recipients, a quick study of the literature on medical donations will inform any reader of the many challenges that may be brought on by donations, such as poor quality control/standards, weak communication between donor and recipient, and lack of expertise for maintenance.

Many recipient countries may not be able to cope with the incurred costs of medical equipment throughout their life span as the costs of purchasing amount to a mere 20% of the overall costs incurred in the equipment life cycle. Additionally, a problematic idea sometimes noted with donors, is that the recipient countries’ circumstances mean that anything is better than nothing. This may lead to the dumping of unwanted equipment and supplies to recipient countries. Several estimates suggest that between 40% to 70% of donated medical devices and equipment in low-resource settings are broken, unused, or unfit for purpose.

Many developing countries lack regulations and policies for the donation of medicines and medical equipment. In efforts to guide donations, the World Health Organization [WHO], has developed several guidelines to ensure that donations of medical supplies are more efficient and effective. These guidelines have been widely recognised as the international standard for donations and they are intended to serve as a basis for national or institutional guidelines.

The WHO guidelines for Medicine Donations and guidelines for Health Care Equipment Donations are formed around four underlying core principles of **Good Donations Practice**:

1.  Donations of medicine and health-related products should benefit the recipient to the maximum extent.
2.  Donations should be given with respect for the wishes and authority of the recipient.
3.  No double standards in quality: the standards of donations should be acceptable for use in the donor country/organisation.
4.  Effective communication between donor and recipient with all donations made according to a plan formulated and agreed to by both parties.

The Paris Declaration on Aid Effectiveness, adopted by over 100 countries in 2005, commits signatories to improving the quality of aid and its impact on development. The declaration is not officially endorsed by Seychelles to-date, but it is by most global and bilateral partners. It sets out five principles (Table 1). These principles form the basis of guiding donor-recipient roles and responsibilities in donations.

### Table 1: Principles of the Paris Declaration on Aid Effectiveness

| PRINCIPLE | DESCRIPTION |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Ownership** | Developing countries set their own development strategies, improve their institutions and tackle corruption. |
| **Alignment** | Donor countries and organisations bring their support in line with these strategies and use local systems. |
| **Harmonisation** | Donor countries and organisations co-ordinate their actions, simplify procedures and share information to avoid duplication. |
| **Managing for Results** | Developing countries and donors focus on producing and measuring results. |
| **Mutual Accountability** | Donors and developing countries are accountable for development results. |

The mere existence of guidelines and standards on donations are insufficient. Enforcement is required to ensure that such guidelines, whether international or domestic, are respected and adhered to by both donors and recipients. Therefore, while it may seem prudent to develop policies guiding health-related donations, adequate recognition and buy-in from stakeholders is key to successful implementation.

The WHO suggests the following methods to aid in the successful implementation of donations policies:

*   The effective and efficient management of donations.
*   Ensuring appropriate actions from donor agencies – Donors should respect the four core principles of Good Donations Practice and national guidelines set by the recipient.
*   The avoidance of donations of drugs (or other consumables) with short expiry dates.
*   Informing the public – the public in the donor country is not always aware of the problems associated with donations. Governments of donor countries should make effort to create more public awareness of Good Donation Practices.
*   Establishing donor coordination.

For the purposes of this policy, gifts given to individuals (or groups thereof) in public service are not covered. For acceptance of gifts, refer to Article 61, Order 119 of the Public Service Procedures Manual (2011).

### 1.1 Seychelles & Assistance in Healthcare

Seychelles is a small island state of 115 islands, located in the Indian Ocean. The Seychellois people enjoy access to a wide range of healthcare services, with a Service Coverage Index of 71, the highest in the African Region, according to the World Health Statistics Report of 2020. The right to health care is incorporated in Article 29 of the Seychelles constitution.

The State, through the Ministry of Health (MOH), is the key health service provider. Three entities exist under the MOH:

1.  **Health Care Agency (HCA)**: providing healthcare services.
2.  **Public Health Authority (PHA)**: for delivering on public health functions and regulation of the health sector.
3.  **National AIDS Council (NAC)**: for coordinating and leading the National response to HIV/AIDS epidemic.

**Health Sector Goals:**
*   Increase life expectancy and healthy life expectancy.
*   Achieve and sustain all dimensions of UHC.
*   Prepare, prevent, detect early, and respond to health emergencies.
*   Promote healthy populations.

### 1.2 The Need for a Policy to Guide Donations

A lack of formal guidelines at National level and the fact that Seychelles has not endorsed the Paris declaration on aid effectiveness, a gap remains in terms of guidance around donations, in particular to government entities.

The development of this policy was recommended in the Health Task Force Report (2013) and the NHSP 2022-2026. In the absence of formal donation guidelines, challenges have included donations not tailored to sector needs, lack of quality controls, and lack of local technical expertise for maintenance.

According to the National Health Policy (2015), almost 90% of medical equipment in use at that time in the sector were donations. Some of these lacked specifications and standardisation, creating challenges for operation, maintenance, repair, and acquisition of spare parts.

### 1.3 Purpose and Objectives of the Policy

This is the first policy to guide donations to the health sector. For the purposes of this policy, donations will refer to cash or in-kind donations to the MOH Seychelles, irrespective of value.

**Objectives:**
*   To formalise and standardise the procedures on donations to the MOH;
*   To improve transparency and accountability in the process; and
*   To improve aid effectiveness.

## 2 RESPONSIBILITIES

### 2.1 Responsibilities of Ministry of Health

*   Ensure all relevant actors and donors are aware of this policy and follow stated procedures.
*   Ensure all offer of donations are carefully considered based on proposed criteria before acceptance.
*   Involve relevant experts in verifying proposed donations of specialised medical equipment or medicines.
*   Ensure effective communication with the donor and intermediary actors.
*   Keep records of the type and value of all donations and report annually.

### 2.2 Responsibilities of the Donor

*   Establish direct contact with the **International Co-operation Unit (ICoU)** of the MOH at the earliest stage.
*   Respect core principles of good donation practices and the guidance set by this policy.
*   Ensure donations are based on stated needs of MOH and are relevant to Seychelles health services.
*   Align medicine donations with the MOH Essential Drugs List and equipment with designated specifications.
*   Dispatch donations only with the consent of MOH.
*   Ensure donations are obtained from legitimate, reliable sources complying with quality standards.
*   Offer installation assistance, aftercare, and training for medical equipment.
*   Ensure medicines/supplies have a shelf-life of at least one year and/or 80% remaining.
*   Ensure donated medical equipment has a useful life of at least two years after arrival.
*   Label items in English, French, or Creole with support documents.
    *   **Medicine/Vaccine labels must include:** Generic name, Dosage form, Manufacturer details, Quantity, Storage conditions, and Expiry date.
*   Provide Certificates of Analysis (CoA) and potentially GMP certificates.
*   Pack items according to international regulations with a detailed packing list.

## 3 PROCEDURES

### 3.1 General Procedures

*   All donations (monetary or in-kind) must follow these procedures.
*   Applicable to all donations regardless of value.
*   Donations must be channelled through the International Cooperation Unit [ICoU].
*   ICoU and respective entities will maintain accurate records.

### 3.2 Procedures for MOH

**Requesting Donations:**
*   ICoU shall guide relevant entities throughout the process.
*   A written request must be submitted to the ICoU Unit.
*   Requests must be guided by MOH experts to ensure alignment with needs.
*   Requests must be approved by the Minister or Principal Secretary.
*   Foreign entity donations must be channelled through the Ministry responsible for Foreign Affairs.

**Accepting Donations:**
*   ICoU/Donor must complete a pre-donation checklist (Annex 1).
*   Donations must be reviewed by experts upon receipt (Annex 2).
*   A written letter of acceptance or refusal must be issued to the donor.
*   For cash donations, ensure currency is exchangeable in Seychelles.
*   Upon successful receipt, a letter of receipt and appreciation will be issued.

### 3.3 Procedures for Donors

**Unsolicited Donations:**
*   Donors must contact the ICoU Unit of the MOH.
*   ICoU will provide this policy and other relevant documents (e.g., Essential List of Medicines).
*   The offer will be reviewed by MOH experts to determine acceptability.

## 4 ACCOUNTING

*   Records must specify the nature of the donation (cash, fixed assets, inventory, etc.).
*   Provide registered cash equivalent for in-kind donations.
*   Follow proper accounting procedures in ledger systems.
*   Maintain clear records of receipt and keep supporting documents in files.
*   A centralised archiving mechanism for donations should be created.
*   Annual reports on donations will be produce and disseminated by the ICoU.

## ANNEX 1: PRE-DONATION ASSESSMENT

**Ministry of Health - International Cooperation Unit**

**Checklist Components:**
1.  **Donor Information:** Agency name, Contact person, Contact details, Address, Conflict of interest check.
2.  **Donation Type:** Cash (currency), Item/Product, Service, or Other.
3.  **Assessment Criteria (Yes/No/NA):**
    *   Based on expressed need?
    *   MOH has expertise/resources to handle?
    *   Adequate storage/distribution capacity?
    *   Approved/registered in donor country and Seychelles?
    *   Energy efficient/environmentally friendly?
    *   Personnel qualified to use/maintain? (If no, is there a training plan?)
    *   Safe receipt and storage conditions?
    *   Meet local minimum standards?
    *   Site preparation required?
    *   Evidence of maintenance (if second-hand)?
    *   Comply with national policies?
    *   Sufficient shelf-life?
    *   Features in Essential Medicines List?
    *   CoA provided in English/French?
    *   GMP certificate provided?
4.  **Beneficiary Entity/Unit.**
5.  **Reason/Purpose for donation.**
6.  **Conditions/restrictions.**
7.  **Approval:** Office of the Principal Secretary (Accept/Decline).

## ANNEX 2: CHECKLIST: RECEIPT OF DONATIONS

**Assessment Categories (Yes/No/NA):**

**All Products:**
*   Pre-donation assessment completed?
*   Pre-donation approval from Office of PS?
*   Properly packaged?
*   Detailed packing list attached?
*   Labelled in English or French?
*   Certified energy efficient/environmentally friendly?

**Medical Equipment:**
*   Meet technical specifications?
*   Storage conditions appropriate/safe?
*   Usable and safe?
*   Environment for equipment provided?
*   Resources to operate available?
*   On standardized lists?
*   Operational and service manuals included?
*   Essential accessories and supplies available?

*   Compliant with national policy/regulations?
*   Listed in current Essential Medicines list?
*   Meet shelf-life requirements?
*   Labels/inserts in English/French?
