## THE PURPOSE OF THIS DOCUMENT

This document sets out how all agencies should work together to promote children’s welfare and protect them from abuse.

It sets out the role and responsibilities of different agencies involved in child protection work.

It also outlines the procedures for joint working arrangements, providing guidance on child protection in specific circumstances. It is intended to provide a national framework within which agencies agree on ways of working together.

---

## INTRODUCTION

Good practice calls for effective cooperation between agencies.

This guidance document reflects the principles contained in the **United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child**, ratified by the Seychelles Government in 1990. It also takes into account other relevant legislation and is particularly informed by the **Children Act 1982**.

Those procedures are based on the determination of all agencies to uphold the right of each child to be protected from abuse. They are designed to ensure that, as far as possible, all serious actual or likely harm to children is identified and to prevent further and possibly more serious injury or suffering.

In order to do this, the established procedures for coordination and collaboration must be adhered to.

It should be noted that the protection and overall welfare of the child is of paramount consideration. Where there is a conflict of interest, the needs of the child should take precedence over those of the parent or carer or the interests of an organisation. A child, whatever his/her race, sex, religion, language, cultural background, ability or disability, has the right to be protected by services which are sensitive to the child’s individual needs.

The main responsibility for the protection of children rests with the parents or carers. All agencies have a responsibility for the prevention, identification and reporting of child abuse. The Social Services have the main statutory duty to investigate alleged or suspected child abuse, and to provide appropriate support and child care services. The specific duties of other agencies to cooperate in this work, and the roles of voluntary organisations amongst others, give all these agencies a responsibility for the safety and welfare of children. These, however, in no way diminish the responsibilities of neighbours, relatives, the wider community and all other agencies which have contact with children.

## PRINCIPLES OF CHILD PROTECTION
### Working Together to Support Children and Families

*   The welfare of the child is the paramount consideration.
*   Wherever possible, children should be brought up and cared for by their own families.
*   Parents of children in need should be helped to bring up their children.
*   Help should be:
    *   provided in a spirit of partnership.
    *   responsive to any special needs created by any disability.
    *   drawn from effective collaboration between different agencies.
*   Children and young people should be consulted and kept informed about what is to happen to them.
*   Parents have the prime responsibility for the care and protection of their children.
*   Time is a crucial element in child care and there should be no undue delay.
*   Children’s welfare must be protected by prompt, positive and pro-active attention.
*   Issues of confidentiality and access to records need to be addressed by all agencies.
*   Registers and records must be accurately maintained and kept up to date.
*   Cooperation between organisations, departments and individuals is essential to promote effective communication.
*   Patterns of family life vary and there is no one perfect way to bring up children. Good parenting involves caring for children’s needs, showing them warmth and love and providing the stimulation needed for their development, within a stable environment where they experience consistent guidance and boundaries.
    *   Parents must be able to make sure that their children grow up, cared for and safe from harm.
    *   They have to promote their children’s health and development and help them to achieve their full potential.
*   Parents themselves require support and there are both statutory and voluntary services available to assist them.
*   All work with children and families should retain a clear focus on the welfare of the child.

Safeguarding children’s well-being and protecting them from significant harm depends crucially upon effective information sharing, collaboration and understanding between agencies. Awareness and appreciation of the role of other agencies are vital for effective collaboration.

## CATEGORIES OF CHILD ABUSE

### PHYSICAL ABUSE
Physical abuse may involve hitting, throwing, poisoning, burning or scalding, drowning, suffocating, or otherwise causing physical harm to a child. Physical harm may also be caused when a parent or carer feigns the symptoms of, or deliberately causes ill health to a child who they are looking after. This situation is commonly described using terms such as *factitious illness by proxy* or *Munchausen syndrome by proxy*.

### EMOTIONAL ABUSE
Emotional abuse is the severe and persistent adverse effects on the child’s emotional development. It may involve conveying to children that they are worthless or unloved, inadequate, or valued only insofar as they meet the needs of another person. It may feature age or developmentally inappropriate expectations being imposed on children. It may involve causing children frequently to feel frightened or in danger, or the exploitation or corruption of children. Some level of emotional abuse is involved in all types of ill-treatment of a child, though it may occur alone.

### SEXUAL ABUSE
Sexual abuse involves forcing or enticing a child or young person to take part in sexual activities, whether or not the child is aware of what is happening. The activity may involve physical contact, including penetrative (e.g. rape or buggery) or non-penetrative acts. They may include non-contact activities, such as involving children in looking at, or in the production of, pornographic material or watching sexual activities, or encouraging children to behave in sexually inappropriate ways.

### NEGLECT
Neglect is the persistent failure to meet a child’s basic physical and/or psychological needs, likely to result in the serious impairment of the child’s health or development. It may involve a parent or carer failing to provide adequate food, shelter and clothing, failing to protect a child from physical harm or danger, or the failure to ensure access to appropriate medical care or treatment. It may also include neglect of, or unresponsiveness to, a child’s basic emotional needs.

## INDICATORS OF ABUSE

### PHYSICAL ABUSE
Some signs which are due to physical abuse are:

#### (i) BRUISES
*   One or both eyes are bruised.
*   Bruises or other injuries to the face of a non-mobile child.
*   Bruises or other injuries, including to the frenulum, in or around the mouth (especially in small babies).
*   Grasp marks on limbs or finger tip bruises on one or both side of the face.
*   Symmetrical bruising.
*   Bruising to the ear.
*   Outline bruising (e.g. belt marks, hand prints, shoe marks).
*   Linear bruising (particularly on the buttocks or back).
*   Bruising on soft tissue with no obvious and verifiable explanation.
*   Different age bruising (especially in the same area e.g. buttocks).

**Uncommon sites for accidental bruising:** Mouth, cheeks, behind the ear, neck, abdomen, chest, under the arm, back of legs, buttocks (except occasionally along the bony protuberances of the spine), genital area, rectal area, soles of feet.

#### (ii) BITES, BURNS AND SCARS
*   Clear impressions of teeth.
*   Oval or crescent shaped bites. Bites more than 3 cm across are likely to have been caused by an adult or older child.
*   Burns or scalds with clear outlines.
*   Burns of uniform depth over a larger area.
*   Splash marks above the main scald area caused by hot liquid being thrown.

**Note on Scalding:** A responsible adult checks the temperature of the bath before a child gets in. A mobile child is unlikely to sit down voluntary in too hot a bath and cannot accidentally scald his/her bottom without also scalding the feet.

**Scars to note:**
*   An exceptionally large number of differing age scars (especially if coupled with current bruising), or unusual shaped scars.
*   Small round burns which may be cigarette burns.
*   Large scars that are from burns or lacerations that did not receive medical treatment.

#### (iii) FRACTURES
*   Pain, swelling and discoloration over a bone or joint (common sites: long bones of arms, legs, and collar bones).
*   Swelling to the side of the head may indicate a fractured skull. Drowsiness may also be a feature.
*   Fractured ribs (often only identified on x-ray).
*   **Unnoticed fractures:** Fractures in young children heal quickly; the absence of painful symptoms a short time after a report may mask the existence of a fracture injury.

#### (iv) OTHER INJURIES
*   Poisoning, injections, ingestion or other application of damaging substances administered by a carer.
*   **Female genital mutilation**, which includes female circumcision, excision and infibulation, is physical abuse and an offence regardless of cultural or other reasons (except medical grounds).
*   Intentional lacerations and other bodily harm.

### EMOTIONAL ABUSE
Children suffering emotional abuse may show:
*   Abnormally passive, lethargic or attention seeking behavior.
*   Specific habit disorders e.g. soiling with faecal smearing, excessive drinking, inappropriate appetite (eating inedible substances), self-mutilation.
*   Severely delayed social development; poor language and speech development.
*   Weight and height which is disproportionately low that cannot be explained by medical reasons.
*   Nervous behavior e.g. excessive rocking, hair twisting.
*   Low self-esteem e.g. excessive self-criticism.

### SEXUAL ABUSE
#### Physical Signs:
*   Sexually transmitted diseases.
*   Recurrent urinary infections.
*   Genital and rectal itching and soreness.
*   Unexplained bleeding and discharges.
*   Bruising in genital region.
*   Sexual play and/or masturbation inappropriate to age and development.
*   Sexually explicit behavior.
*   Young children with inappropriate sexual knowledge.

#### Accompanying Signs:
*   Sleeping and eating disturbances.
*   Recurrent abdominal pains, headaches.
*   Social withdrawal, restlessness, and aimlessness.
*   Sexually abusive behavior towards other children.
*   Inexplicable school failure.
*   Poor trust and secretiveness.
*   Unexplained pregnancy.
*   Indiscriminate and careless sexual behavior.
*   Drug abuse, running away, self mutilation, suicide attempts.
*   Hysterical fits, faints etc.

**N.B:** In many cases, a sexually abused child presents no physical symptoms. The most important indicator is what the child says.

### NEGLECT
Signs include children who are:
*   Not receiving adequate food consistent with growth.
*   Exposed through lack of supervision to injuries or toxic substances.
*   Exposed to an unconducive, dirty, and hot or cold environment.
*   Left in circumstances without appropriate adult supervision (including abandonment).
*   Whose parents/carers are failing or refusing to seek medical advice or treatment.
*   Left alone below the age of 12 years old.

#### Neglect and Medical Treatment
Where parents refuse commonly available food/fluids or fail to cooperate with medical treatment resulting in significant harm or death, this is neglect. If religious or cultural reasons prevent life-saving treatment, the duty of staff is to institute Child Protection Procedures.

## THE IMPACT OF ABUSE

Sustained abuse or neglect can have major long-term effects on health, development, and well-being, often extending into adulthood (difficulty in relationships, workforce entry, and parenting).

### PHYSICAL ABUSE
Can lead to neurological damage, physical injuries, disability, or death. Linked to aggressive behavior, emotional/behavioral problems, and educational difficulties.

### EMOTIONAL ABUSE
Impacts mental health, behavior, and self-esteem. Especially damaging in infancy. Often occurs alongside domestic violence or parental substance misuse.

### SEXUAL ABUSE
Linked to self-harm, inappropriate sexualised behavior, sadness, and depression. Impact increases with the duration of abuse and the degree of coercion or sadism. Support from a non-abusive adult is critical for coping.

### NEGLECT
Associated with major impairment of growth and intellectual development, social functioning, and in extreme cases, death.

## GENERAL CONSIDERATION

*   Child abuse occurs across all sexes, ages, cultures, religions, and social classes.
*   Identification usually requires both social and medical assessment.
*   Previous records of abuse or multi-generational abuse are significant indicators.
*   Always listen to the child; pay attention to spontaneous statements or non-verbal communication (signing, play).
*   Delay in seeking medical assistance may be an indicator of abuse.
*   Be wary of vague or inconsistent explanations of "accidents."
*   Observe the child’s interaction with parents (e.g., "frozen watchfulness").
*   Any history of unexplained injury/illness requires careful scrutiny, regardless of how attentive the parent appears.
*   Injury inconsistent with the child’s development/mobility is a red flag.
*   Give particular attention to domestic violence cases.

## ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES: INTER-AGENCY PROCEDURES

### SOCIAL SERVICES
Statutory responsibility to promote and safeguard children under the Children Act. Acts as the coordinating role.

#### Response Procedure
(a) Director Social Services (or Principal Social Worker, Child Protection) receives referrals.
(b) Social Worker completes initial assessment.
(c) Recorded information: Full name, DOB/age, reason for concern, parents' names/addresses, and other household members.
(d) Investigations are carried out and detailed reports submitted. All investigations must be joint with the Police.
(e) Police take the lead in investigation to avoid contamination of evidence.
(f) Medical examination requires parent consent; otherwise, Director Social Services applies for a Court order.
(g) Inter-agency consultation meeting follows to decide on action (e.g., therapy).
(h) Case conference determines if the child should be registered and agrees on a Child Protection Plan.
(i) Social Worker monitors and submits periodic follow-up reports.

### EDUCATION DEPARTMENT
Provides a learning environment free of abuse and a pastoral care system.

#### Response Procedure
(a) Students contact School Counsellor, Deputy Head, or Head Teacher.
(b) Staff collect basic info (DOB, parents, account) for referral.
(c) Notification sent to Director Social Services and Head Teacher.
(d) Matter reported to Schools Division, Student Support Services Section.
(e) Head teacher reports the matter to the Student Support Services Section.
(f) Staff may attend case conferences.
(g) Staff assist in continuing work for the child.
(h) Steps taken to secure student safety while at school.
(i) Information treated with utmost discretion.

### HEALTH DEPARTMENT
Designated Child Protection Health Officer (Senior Nurse), alongside a paediatrician and gynaecologist.

#### Management of a Case
(a) First contact health worker reports to Child Protection Health Officer (CPHO).
(b) CPHO determines initial management (examination, admission).
(c) Physician’s examination (paediatrician/gynaecologist) collects evidence. CPHO stays present. Detailed interrogation is left to Police/Social Worker.
(d) Parental permission required; otherwise, Director Social Services obtains a court order.

#### Notification
(a) CPHO informs Director Social Services by telephone.
(b) Director Social Services assumes responsibility for further investigation/management.
(c) CPHO attends Inter-Agency meetings and acts as liaison.
(d) CPHO maintains a confidential register of abuse cases.

### THE POLICE
#### Immediate Actions
If a child is in immediate danger or needs medical treatment, the officer takes the child to a Police Station or medical centre.

#### Family Support Squad Procedure
(a) Protect child from immediate danger.
(b) Inform Director Social Services.
(c) Ensure child is not interfered with (not washed) before medical examination; obtain parent consent.
(d) Take ill/injured child to hospital immediately (parent permission not necessary).
(e) Discuss need for evidence-gathering examination with Social Worker.
(f) Obtain magistrate/judge order if parents refuse examination.
(g) Request immediate medical examination even before court order.
(h) Provide medical officer with case details.
(i) Assess need for examination of suspects according to Seychelles law.
(j) Joint interviews with Social Worker.
(k) Take leading role in investigation.
(l) Provide statements to Social Services for civil court actions.
(m) Keep Social Worker informed; discuss results at Inter-Agency meetings.
(n) Submit case file to senior officer, then to Attorney General’s office (within 3 months).
(o) Monitor progress and inform Social Services of outcome.

### NATIONAL COUNCIL FOR CHILDREN (NCC)
#### Procedure
(a) Intake Officer collects full child/family details and the reporting issue.
(b) Refers information to the Child Protection Team.
(c) Briefs NCC Inter-Agency representative.
(d) Social Services may refer child for therapy once safe.
(e) Psychology/counselling team assess needs weekly.
(f) Case allocated to psychologist/therapist.
(g) Initial family assessment and goal setting.
(h) Case management with Social Services may be requested.
(i) Development of treatment plan; preparation for Court.
(j) Refer back to Social Worker if parents fail to attend therapy.
(k) Provide feedback to Social Worker on progress or new issues.
(l) Plan disengagement and submit psychological report to Director Social Services.

## GUIDELINES FOR INITIAL CASE CONFERENCES

### Introduction
Case conferences provide a forum for sharing information, analyzing risk, and recommending responsibility for action on an inter-agency basis.

### Convening a Conference
Convened by the agency with statutory powers. A review conference typically takes place within 3 months of registration.

### Aims and Purpose
To decide whether to register the child and to formulate a **Child Protection Plan**. A key worker from Social Services is appointed as the lead for the case.

### Organisation
Limited to those who need to know for efficiency and confidentiality. Requires proper administrative arrangements and minutes.

### Involvement of Children/Parents/Carers
Partnership with family is important, though parents may be excluded if they are the alleged abuser. If a child cannot attend, their views are provided by professionals.

## THE SOCIAL SERVICES CHILD PROTECTION INTERNAL REGISTER

### Purpose
*   Record unresolved child protection issues and active Plans.
*   Maintain records of enquiries for the last three years.
*   Provide a central point for speedy professional enquiry.
*   Facilitate inter-agency coordination.
*   Provide management information on national activity.
Records are kept until the child's 18th birthday.

### Contacting the Register
Only professional staff from designated agencies may enquire. Information provided includes current registration status, category, and key worker identity.

### Criteria for Registration
Registration occurs if there is a likelihood of significant harm based on:
(A) One or more identifiable incidents (acts of commission or omission).
(B) Professional judgment/research evidence suggesting significant harm is expected.

## CHILD PROTECTION PLAN

### Social Worker's Role
*   Coordinate inter-agency activity.
*   Regular face-to-face contact with the child.
*   Notify Register Custodian of changes/de-registration.
*   Ensure assessment, monitoring, and support.
*   Maintain detailed records.

### The Initial Child Protection Plan
First plan drawn up within short timescales. Remains "initial" until the first Review Conference.

**Plan Details:**
*   Focal point contact details.
*   Objectives and time-scales related to the identified risk.
*   Day-to-day protection, contingency plans, and monitoring.
*   Review dates.
*   Communication arrangements in key worker's absence.

### Comprehensive Child Protection Assessment
An inter-disciplinary process to understand:
*   The nature of the concern.
*   Parental attitude and acceptance.
*   Child’s needs and feelings.
*   Family behavioral patterns.
*   Future risk level.

**Process Areas:**
*   Social and family circumstances.
*   Health and development.
*   Education.
*   Environmental circumstances.
*   Relevant other family members.

**Recording Findings:**
Must include conclusions, recommendations for future actions, objectives for family change, time scales, and resources required.

## CHILD PROTECTION REVIEWS, PLANS AND DE-REGISTRATION

### Purpose of Review
*   Review protection arrangements and risk levels.
*   Ensure inter-agency coordination is functioning.
*   Review the Protection Plan.

### De-Registration
Occurs when risks are reduced to a minimum or acceptable level. Requires an updated comprehensive assessment.

#### Criteria for De-Registration:
1.  **Original factors no longer apply:**
    *   Risk reduced through work with family.
    *   Child placed away from home/abuser.
    *   Abuser no longer in household.
    *   Analysis shows registration no longer required.
2.  **No longer a child in the eyes of the law:**
    *   Reaches 18 years of age.
    *   Gets married.
    *   Death.

## CONCLUSION

Child protection is a complex, evolving process. All agencies share the responsibility for maintaining close working arrangements and must identify specific individuals for these duties. The Government continues to prioritize the protection and development of children for the betterment of society.
