# National Human Resource Development Strategy 2018-2022

## Foreword by the Minister

Seychelles as a Small Island Developing State with limited natural resources has been one of the leaders in environment conservation and management internationally. The competitiveness and productivity of the industries, the quality and effectiveness of the public services, and the welfare of the families and communities all depend on the availability of a well-educated and highly skilled populace. For these reasons, investment in education and training has been a national priority. As a result, the Government of Seychelles has long recognised the importance of its human resources by continuously investing in education, training and professional development of its human capital to remain competitive and successful in the modern global economy. To date, the continuous advancement of its human resource development through the development of a training system is entrusted to the Agency for National Human Resource Development (ANHRD).

ANHRD, in consultation with its stakeholders, has embarked on this project to formulate the National Human Resource Development Strategy for Seychelles. This strategy is both crucial and timely, as it builds on the inextricable linkages that exist between economic development, human resource development and the realization of decent work for all, particularly young people within the context of Seychelles.

With the rapid development in the different sectors, the trend now is for the society to move towards a knowledge-based economy, in which the application of knowledge is the principal factor of production. The essential ingredient of products and services is the inherent knowledge versus knowledge capacity, a critical factor for any organisation in achieving a competitive advantage. The ability to gather information, generate and disseminate new knowledge, apply the knowledge to achieve performance improvement and innovation, is therefore the key to any organisation’s success.

The rationale for including ‘knowledge management’ in the strategy for balancing economic demand and supply of human resources, lies in the transformation of the economy into a ‘knowledge-based economy’. Individuals, teams and organisations need to develop the necessary competencies to be able to participate in a working life that incorporates knowledge productivity and usage. To transform the Seychellois workforce into knowledge workers for the achievement of the knowledge-based society envisioned by the Government, ANHRD in liaison with training providers will raise awareness on life-long learning, learning organisations and knowledge management. It will be therefore necessary to change peoples’ attitude from knowledge hoarding to knowledge creation, sharing, research and documentation.

Within the next five years (2018-2022), the following short and long-term strategies will be pursued to strengthen Seychelles’ HRD capacity to compete successfully in the global, knowledge-based economy:

*   Interventions for education, employment, training and development focused on managerial, technical and professional areas in all sectors of the economy;
*   A mechanism for tooling, re-skilling and lifelong learning to cater for changes in the labour market;
*   Integration of HRD in all aspects of national, social and economic development;
*   Processes for HRD impact assessment;
*   More focus upon demand-related issues and reform to workplace practices.

The success of the integrated National HRD Strategy depends on the active participation and commitment of all stakeholders. Let us be guided along the path by the vision, mission, and underlying principles of this strategy, to ensure that the potential of every Seychellois is truly realised.

We look forward to the partnership from all sectors, in the implementation of the National Human Resource Development Strategy 2018-2022.

**Mrs. Jeanne Simeon**
Minister for Education and Human Resource Development

## Executive Summary

The Government of Seychelles first developed a HRD policy in 2007. A new HRD policy and strategy is now required to support the country’s Sustainable Development Strategy 2012-2020, moving the economy from middle-income to high-income status.

**Vision:** "A workforce, especially youth, that is empowered with the necessary skills, attitudes and competencies as well as work ethics for sustainable national socio-economic development".

**Mission:** "The establishment of a necessary framework to monitor and assess human resource development needs in the context of national development, and to create mechanisms to ensure that these needs are met."

### Key Challenges
*   **Youth Non-participation:** High unemployment levels among youth (above 13% for males, 22% for females in 2015).
*   **TVET Status:** Limited status of TVET and low enrollment numbers.
*   **Responsiveness:** Lack of soft skills and poor work-related attitudes among new employees.
*   **Lifelong Learning:** Limited commitment to workforce development; a culture of "poaching" skilled employees rather than training.
*   **Institutional Linkages:** Weak linkages between government departments and minimal research guiding decisions.

### Strategic Responses (Pillars)
*   **Pillar 1: Youth Empowerment:** Career counselling, entrepreneurship programs, and workplace-based programs.
*   **Pillar 2: TVET Strengthening:** Flexible systems, occupational standards, and teacher training.
*   **Pillar 3: Market Responsiveness:** Targeted funding for high-demand occupations and industry engagement.
*   **Pillar 4: Lifelong Learning:** Promoting a culture of continuous learning and HRM reforms to retain staff.
*   **Pillar 5: Institutional Development:** Standardising information gathering and adopting a sector-based approach.

## 1. Introduction

Seychelles is characterized by high GDP and HDI but remains vulnerable to external shocks. Despite economic growth, the labour market faces low youth participation and a skills mismatch where locals lack the expertise for high-paying technical positions.

### 1.1 Definition of Human Resource Development
HRD is the integrated use of training, organisation and career development efforts to improve individual, group and organisational effectiveness. It includes planned learning activities to support the economy of Seychelles.

### 1.2 Scope of the HRD Strategy
*   Education, employment, and training in managerial, technical, and professional areas.
*   Mechanisms for re-skilling and lifelong learning.
*   Integration of HRD into national social and economic development.
*   Impact assessment and reform of workplace practices.

### 1.3 Rationale
Despite investments, gaps persist at technician and technologist levels, leading to increased expatriate employment. The ANHRD was established in 2014 to better match demand and supply.

### 1.4 Alignment with National Context
Existing themes include transforming into a knowledge economy, raising awareness of lifelong learning, and linking HRD to priority economic sectors.

### 1.5 Purpose
To tackle persistent challenges, build the knowledge base, diversify economic activity, and address expertise gaps.

### 1.6 Methodology
*   **Data Gathering:** Literature reviews, supply/demand data collection, and extensive stakeholder consultations (workshops and one-on-one interviews).
*   **Analysis:** Linking to policy principles, analyzing pillars, and framing responses as outputs/outcomes.

### 1.7 Framework
Focused on adding value to existing activities and ensuring a validated consultation process involving Government, Private Sector, Academia, and NGOs.

## 2. Challenges and Responses

### 2.1 Youth Participation
Youth unemployment is significantly higher than the national average. The government is moving toward a coordinated approach involving ANHRD, MEICS, MEHRD, and MYSC.

### 2.2 Education Foundation and TVET
There is a critical need for competency-based curricula. TVET is often viewed as a "second-rate" option, leading to a reliance on foreign workers for technician roles.

### 2.3 Responsiveness to Labour Market
*   **Provision:** Tertiary education often fails to provide necessary employability and soft skills.
*   **Employer Engagement:** Limited industry influence on policy and curriculum.
*   **Expatriate Dominance:** Growth in expatriate employment across all occupational groups, especially Professional/Technical (+39%) and Managerial (+40%).

### 2.4 Lifelong Learning and Productivity
There is a culture of "poaching" in the private sector. Labour market rigidities and poor retention of talent hinder overall productivity.

### 2.5 Government Linkages and Research
Lack of reliable data on unemployed/underemployed youth and a disconnect between macro-economic policies and HRD planning.

### 2.6 SWOT Analysis

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- |
| Good fiscal framework | Poor educational outcomes (Maths/Science) |
| Sector-based approach to planning | Lack of employer engagement |
| Establishment of ANHRD | Low productivity |
| Increased attention to TVET | Lack of work readiness in graduates |

| Opportunities | Threats |
| Centres of Excellence | Low youth labour force participation |
| Standardised data gathering | Negative perception of TVET |
| New TVET Division | Limited workforce development |
| Broadening training funds | High expatriate employment in mid-level jobs |

## 3. Vision, Mission and Goals

**Vision:** The workforce especially youths is empowered with the necessary skills, attitudes and competencies as well as work ethic for sustainable national socio-economic development.

**Mission:** To establish the necessary framework to monitor and assess human resource development needs in the context of national development, and to establish mechanisms to ensure that these needs are met.

**Goals:**
*   HRD at the centre of development.
*   Investment in Human Capital (Academic and TVET).
*   Framework for continuous learning.
*   Demand-led approach.
*   HRM and workplace development.
*   HRD Integration across sectors.
*   Clarified roles and responsibilities.
*   Systematic approach to HRD actions.
*   Partnerships in priority areas.

## 4. HRD Strategic Priorities

### Pillar 1: Youth Empowerment
*   **HRD Initiatives:** Strengthening career guidance and leadership programs.
*   **Internships:** Aligning internships to labour market needs and focusing on technical/work-readiness skills.
*   **Education:** Improving literacy, numeracy, and ICT in general education; creating Centres of Excellence.
*   **Entrepreneurship:** Introducing enterprise education in primary/secondary schools and supporting self-employment through mentoring.

### Pillar 2: TVET Expansion
*   **Flexibility:** implementing RPL (Recognition of Prior Learning) and credit systems.
*   **Occupational Standards:** Shifting to demand-driven, industry-led curricula.
*   **Image:** Promotional campaigns and "TVET champions".
*   **Teacher Training:** Mandatory pre-service training and "Train-the-Trainer" programs.
*   **Industry Currency:** Implementing the Teachers Industry Placement Scheme (TIPS).

### Pillar 3: Market Responsiveness
*   **Targeted Funding:** Linking government funding to high-demand qualifications.
*   **Funding Access:** Broadening access to training funds for workforce training and internships.
*   **Scholarships:** Expanding scholarships to include mid-level technician/technologist courses.
*   **Industry Engagement:** Establishing industry sector forums and using TVET/UniSey as coordination hubs.

### Pillar 4: Lifelong Learning & Productivity
*   **Culture:** Creating clearer articulation pathways between institutions.
*   **HRM Reforms:** Developing legislation to encourage workforce development and improve retention.
*   **PPPs:** Using the sector-based approach to create Public-Private Partnerships.
*   **Alternatives to Scholarships:** Group training, workplace-based qualifications, and local/international hybrid degrees.

### Pillar 5: Institutional Development
*   **Data Standardization:** Developing standard proformas and MoUs for data sharing between agencies.
*   **Sector-Based Approach:** Implementing the HRD Planning Guide across line ministries.

Bi-annual reviews by the ANHRD Board. Monitoring will track changes in national development plans, structural shifts in the labour market, and demographic changes.

## 9. Stakeholder Mapping
*   **Government:** MEICS (Labour Relations and Employment Promotion), MEHRD (Tertiary institutions like SIT, SITE, SMA, etc.), MFTIEP.
*   **Academia/NGOs:** Range from early childhood to UniSey; need for more formal industry-education cooperation.
*   **Private Sector:** Over 2,000 companies (mainly SMEs) in tourism, manufacturing, and construction.

## 10. Strategic Communications
*   Official launch of Policy, Strategy, and Planning Guide.
*   Support for sector-based approach implementation.
*   Expansion of ANHRD online services and publications.
