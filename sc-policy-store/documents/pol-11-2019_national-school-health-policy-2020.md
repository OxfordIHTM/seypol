**Minister of Health, Seychelles**

*The Ministry of Health in collaboration with its key strategic partners, including the Ministry of Education and the Ministry of Family Affairs, have developed the* ***National School Health Policy 2020***.

*Our vision is for every school to be a* ***Health-Promoting School.*** *A school where the environment in which our children study, learn and play, is safe, secure, hygienic, healthy, and health-promoting in all its aspects.*

*Health is defined as “A state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity.” Hence, the* ***Policy*** *recognises that health is not only of the body, but also of the mind and the spirit. As such, psychosocial determinants of health are primordial in the rationale in developing the Policy.*

*It is this perspective that the* ***National School Health Policy 2020*** *takes shape and seeks to promote health in schools in all its facets and dimensions. The document, developed after a series of individual interviews with key stakeholders and inception workshops, places emphasis on long-term and sustained actions that are realistic and achievable, with special attention paid to infrastructure, processes, procedures, programmes and actions undertaken by all actors in the school environment to help ensure that the objectives set are met.*

*It is my belief that the* ***National School Health Policy 2020*** *comes at the right time, when the Ministry of Health and the Ministry of Education are urging patients, parents, teachers and students to take responsibility for their health and education, to realise that many of the outcomes depend on actions that they take every day. They are the ultimate masters of their health and education destinies.*

*Better Education leads to Better Health. And Better Health leads to Better Education. This policy empowers this virtuous circle. The* ***National School Health Policy 2020*** *seeks to promote this attitude and indeed, this way of life.*

Perhaps even more importantly it will help the children of Seychelles stay safe and healthy, and achieve their full potential as citizens. The country needs them, for a better Seychelles.*

# Executive Summary

The Ministry of Health in collaboration with the Ministry of Education and Human Resource Development has been in consultation with key stakeholders (parents, teachers, students, school health nurses and the community) to develop the Seychelles National School Health Policy 2020. The project funded by WHO recognises the benefits for children at an early age of developing healthy dietary and lifestyle patterns. These can have profound impact on their health status in their adult lives, and enhance the productivity of the individual citizens and the nation, as a whole.

The Policy focuses on an examination of the strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats to the school health programme in general and makes recommendations on actions to reverse negative trends and to promote positive policies, regulations, habits and behaviours. The Policy has the vision of “a school system where parents, teachers, students and other stakeholders are safe and healthy and able to enjoy the highest possible level of holistic health” and the mission of protecting and promoting the physical and mental well-being of all persons attending and / or working in schools, especially the children, through a holistic and comprehensive school health programme.

The holistic and comprehensive school health programme has four main principles: a holistic approach to health, a whole school approach encouraging the engagement of all who work and learn there, the use of targeted and timely interventions as and when needed and the meaningful engagement of all stakeholders.

There are three key objectives: to create and foster sustainable and conducive school environments that protect and promote the physical and mental well-being of all persons attending and / or working in schools, with special focus on key issues of concern; to facilitate and improve access and availability of preventive and promotive health services to the children and to educate parents, teachers, school children and the community about key health concerns.

The Policy also includes Implementation and Monitoring and Evaluation frameworks to help guide the work of all engaged in the process of developing the holistic and comprehensive school health programme.

# 1.0 Introduction

**Geography and demographics**
1. The Seychelles Archipelago, consisting of 155 islands, is located in the western Indian Ocean, four degrees south of the Equator and approximately 1,600 km off the coast of East Africa. The islands are scattered over some 500,000km2 of sea. While the land mass is approximately 455.3 km2, with about 491 km of coastline, the Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) is approximately 1.374 million km2. The country is quite isolated geographically and the islands are highly dispersed.

2. The multi-ethnic population of 94, 000 live mainly on the four largest granitic islands of Mahé, Praslin, La Digue and Silhouette. Eighty percent of the population, however, lives on the main island of Mahé which accounts for approximately 35% of the total land area and is the economic hub of the country. The capital, Victoria - also the major port - and the international airport are also located on Mahé. Most of the other smaller islands have no permanent population. The national languages are Kreol (a French based creole or *patois*), English and French. The population is also predominantly Christian.

## Health and Education Systems

3. The right to health is guaranteed in the ***Seychelles Constitution*** ***Chapter Three: Seychellois Charter of Fundamental Human Rights and Freedoms*** in Article 29 which reads in part: “*The state recognises the right of every citizen to protection of health and to the enjoyment of the highest attainable standard of physical and mental health...*” Furthermore, the Constitution in the same Article (29. a) the State engages itself to undertake “*to take steps to provide for free primary health care in State institutions for all its citizens.*” Other promised actions, amongst others, include taking “*appropriate measures to prevent, treat and control epidemic, endemic and other diseases*;” to reduce infant mortality, and “*to promote individual responsibility in health matters*.”

4. Thus, the main provider of clinical and rehabilitative services is the State. The existing health facilities consist of a central referral hospital, 1 rehabilitation centre, 3 cottage hospitals (1 rehabilitative hospital, 1 mental hospital, 1 youth health centre) and 17 decentralized district health centres located throughout the country. These are further complemented by the non-public health facilities, which cover, amongst others, services such as outpatient care services, emergency and trauma care, mother and child services, as well as laboratory and investigative services. There are some specialised services offered at the central referral hospital, and dental health services in some state schools.

5. The Ministry of Health provides a comprehensive Maternal and Child Health Programme (MCHP) an all-inclusive and free antenatal and post-natal care, through maternal and child health programmes in the central referral hospital and the district health centres, on the four main islands with permanent populations. All births occur in healthcare facilities, assisted by health professionals. The School Health Programme takes over from the MCHP and aims to provide ‘*comprehensive, preventive and promotive health services to school going children between the ages of 4 to 17 years*.’ However, services are presently offered to three and a half /four year olds, at crèche enrolment. Services include health assessments, including basic screening procedures, are carried out at five stages in the children’s school life: upon crèche entry, primary 4, secondary 1, secondary 4, and secondary 5. There are presently at least 15 school health nurses, deployed in all primary and secondary state schools, taking a leading role with the support of other relevant health professionals. The health professionals seek parental consent at each stage of their interventions and they also provide counselling and social support including home visits.

6. The right to education is also guaranteed in the Constitution in Chapter Three in Article 33 which “*recognises the right of every citizen to education.*” The key features are free compulsory education for 10 years conducted in a holistic and comprehensive manner, with curricula catering to all types of learning styles and abilities. The Education Act (2004) is the principal law that guides education provision in primary and secondary schools. The Ministry of Education’s vision for education is “*to empower future citizens to contribute positively to the process of building a sustainable, peaceful and harmonious Seychelles society, whilst safeguarding and promoting our unique traditions and cultural values*.”

7. Presently, the education system comprises of state schools (26 primary and 11 secondary) and 4 private schools. There are 11 professional centres and one university. The focus of the ***National School Health Policy 2020*** will be on primary and secondary state and private schools.

## 2.0 Aim

The aim of the ***National School Health Policy 2020*** is to protect and promote the physical, mental and social well-being of children, through a holistic and comprehensive school health programme, in collaboration with key stakeholders.

## 3.0 Objectives

The specific objectives are to:

1. Create and foster sustainable and conducive school environments that protect and promote the physical, mental and social well-being of children, with special focus on key health issues of concern.
2. Facilitate and improve access and availability of preventive, promotive and rehabilitative health services to the children.
3. Uphold a culture of healthy living habits in the school community.

### Policy Principles

The policy rests on principles that will help to implement it with all partners. These are as follows:

1. *Holistic approach to health*
2. *A whole-school approach* (based on the Health Promoting School Initiative)
3. *Targeted and timely interventions and referrals*, and
4. *Meaningful engagement of parents, students, teachers, the community and other stakeholders*.

### **Holistic approach to health**

The Policy recognises that health is an interaction of physical, mental, emotional and social factors. Thus, its approach is improve social determinants of health and to focus on all aspects of health; hence, the need for the school health nurse to work closely with the school counsellor, social worker and other actors and school staff to ensure that health is seen and treated in its widest sense possible.

The Policy recognises and seeks to align itself with the international commitments made by the Government through the ratification of human rights instruments, and regional economic groups’ protocols, as well as the various sectoral national policies from different ministries and agencies. These international and national obligations are guidelines for the Policy. These are listed in Annex 3. It is essential that proposed guidelines are coordinated and that each national policy finds its place in the overall picture of ensuring that health in the schools is seen, treated and implemented in a comprehensive manner.

### **Whole school approach**

The School Health Programme will also ensure that it is developed and implemented in a comprehensive manner that takes into account the variety of needs of the various partners and stakeholders. Piece-meal programmes do not fully cover the needs of its intended beneficiaries and end up responding to only some of the needs identified and expressed.

Programmes need to consider issues such as the culture of the Seychellois, the religious particularities and sensitivities of the community to ensure success in its implementation.

### **Targeted and timely interventions and referrals**

A well-designed School Health Programme is one that is mostly concerned with early identification of health issues in all its dimensions and thus, is preventive in nature. When there are rehabilitative interventions to undertake, these are done where and when necessary and as needed, to avoid a one size fits all approach. It is expected that timely referrals will be done in a two-way manner, with regular feedbacks from all parties. Thus, interventions need to be based on identified needs and evidence-informed decisions and they are just enough of what the community needs, rather than an overkill.

### **Meaningful engagement of all partners**

Lastly, the well-designed School Health Programme seeks to meaningfully engage all stakeholders in the whole process, from conception to design, from implementation to monitoring and evaluation. It is important to especially engage the parents, teachers, students and the community in the complete process and procedures used to develop programmes and interventions. Stakeholders must be just that: individuals and organisation that have a ‘stake’ and own the programmes.

Meaningful engagement can be at different levels, from providing technical, material and financial support to the Ministry of Education and the Ministry of Health and to the schools directly. Stakeholders may choose to donate whatever they are able to: time, effort, funds, human resources and others to ensure that their roles are truly participatory.

The actions and interventions in the School Health Programme must be inclusive and

“leave no-one behind.” All students, staff of the school, parents, the community and other stakeholders need to be included in programmes. The Programme will ensure that students with physical impairments or mental health issues will be included as fully involved participants. Programmes will be developed together with them and their inputs are considered primordial to the whole process.

## **Policy guidelines**

**Objective One**: *Create and foster a sustainable school environment that protects and promotes the physical, social and psychological well-being of all persons attending and / or working in schools*, with *special focus on the school children*

The policy focuses on the following issues: personal hygiene, cleanliness of the school compound, availability and access to facilities for play and exercise, ensuring that children and staff are protected from vehicles on their way and from school, improving the eating experiences of the children and promoting nutrition, proactive and prosocial measures to maintain optimal mental well-being of all persons in the school, especially school children.

***Therefore;***

* The School Health Nurse, the School Dental personnel, Public Health officer, the school-based counsellor and the Personal, Social and Civic Education Teachers, social workers, amongst others shall work together to reinforce implementation of the existing programme in all schools.
* The Ministry of Education shall maintain the school in the highest possible level of cleanliness.
* All schools shall endeavour to find, build and organise facilities for children to play securely, safely, and to engage in physical activities, under the adequate supervision of staff.
* All schools shall endeavour to build, improve and maintain the physical and aesthetic aspects of the dining halls, tuck shops and other facilities where food is sold and served to make the eating experience of children more pleasant so that they develop healthy eating habits.
* The overall experience of the children and staff shall be an extension of what the children have been taught in class, incorporating both the theoretical and practical aspects of health.
* All schools shall eliminate all physical health hazards such as, amongst others, open gutters, broken handrails, protruding metal bars left over from construction of the schools, sidewalks, physical conditions of playgrounds and other facilities.
* To prevent injury, all schools shall seek to provide appropriate and sufficient furniture and equipment for children to use.

* All classrooms shall have adequate ventilation for air circulation and freshness at all times so that students, teachers and staff can enjoy the highest quality of air.

* Students and staff have access to potable water through water fountains which are maintained in hygienic and pristine conditions.

### **Objective Two**: *Facilitate and improve access and availability of preventive and promotive health services to school-age children*

The Policy focuses on the development and delivery of a comprehensive package of school health services.

***Therefore;***

* The complete School Health Services package shall be a comprehensive health screening programme to ensure the holistic growth and development of the children. The complete health screening programme is presented in Annex 4.
* Schools shall be encouraged to:
    1. Initiate and adopt health promoting programmes, as and when necessary, to complement the health screening programme;
    2. Increase the number of assessments, e.g., BMI, done by school personnel, such as the P.E. Teachers, counsellors, PSCE Teachers and others.

* One school health nurse shall be allocated per school on a full-time basis, assisted by a Health Promotion Officer and or a Health Care Assistant.

* The Ministry of Health in collaboration with the Ministry of Education, shall periodically review the package of School Health Services and together, they will develop an adapted and adopt up-to-date package to meet current trends in health and disease pattern.

* The comprehensive School Health Services package shall be promoted to parents, students, teachers and the community to increase uptake and improve level of personal responsibility for health and health-seeking behaviour in students.

* Funding shall be secured to implement the School Health Services.

* The Ministry of Education shall ensure that there are appropriate and conducive facilities (offices for administrative duties, one-to-one counselling, safe storage, well-equipped screening rooms, separate sick bays, furniture, adequate lighting and ventilation, toilets and washing facilities, amongst others) in place for the implementation of the School Health Services.

* The Ministry of Education shall phase out all non-school-based takeaways and refreshments in accordance with the National School Nutrition Policy.

* The Ministry of Education, Seychelles Licensing Authority, the Ministry of Health and other relevant authorities shall regulate the distance between mobile fast food vendors/hawkers and schools.

### **Objective Three**: *Educate parents/guardians/care-givers, teachers, school children and the community about key health issues and how to address these effectively*

The Policy focuses on health promotion programmes for schools, which include parents, teachers, school children and the community, as well as key partners.

***Therefore;***

* Schools shall use their existing school committees, including students and parents, at school level to conceptualize, design, implement and monitor health behaviour change programmes for parents, teachers, students and the community to address key issues of physical and mental health, as and when necessary.

* A schedule for the implementation of the awareness and health behaviour change programmes shall be integrated within the school’s calendar of activities annually.

# Monitoring and Evaluation

* The Ministry of Education through the Health Promotion Coordinator in collaboration with the Family Health and Nutrition section in the Ministry of Health shall oversee implementation of the School Heath Policy.
* The School Health Nurse shall monitor, evaluate and report on implementation of the School Health Policy annually. A copy of the report shall also be send to the Health Information Systems and Education Management Information System (EMIS) for statistical purposes.
* The M&E framework shall propose a set of national indicators that will be used to monitor the school health services.
# Conclusion

The School Health Programme is an investment in national development, as it provides for strong and healthy citizens right from the beginning – in their childhood. Indeed, School Health Programmes are considered one of the most efficient strategies a nation might use to prevent major health and social problems. Schools already play a powerful role in influencing students' health-related behaviours, through the kinds of in-class instruction received, but perhaps, more importantly, through the experiences the children have when they interact with their peers and the adults in authority in terms of customs and habits they learn outside of the classroom.

In addition, health and success in school are inextricably linked. When a child is experiencing good health, it facilitates his or her growth, development, and optimal learning. Education on the other hand contributes to children's knowledge about being and staying healthy through their lifespan. It is in recognition of the roles of education and health that the Seychelles National School Health Policy has been drafted and it is hoped that all stakeholders will play their own respective role in helping the nation to be healthy.
